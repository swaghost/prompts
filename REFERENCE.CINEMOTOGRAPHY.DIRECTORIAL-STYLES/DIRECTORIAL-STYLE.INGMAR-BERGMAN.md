# Directorial Style: Ingmar Bergman

## Genre

Use the director's established genres as a starting point, then specify the original scene's genre and dramatic objective.

## Writing and Story Logic

Stories turn inward toward faith, death, marriage, identity, silence, shame, and emotional cruelty. Dialogue functions as confession, attack, evasion, or failed communication.

## Directorial Evolution

Early chamber dramas develop into increasingly concentrated psychological and spiritual studies, with later work folding memory, theater, dream, and autobiography into intimate forms.

## Visual Grammar

## Camera and Lens

Choose camera height, movement, focal length, depth of field, and coverage to serve the scene's emotional information.

Legacy overview evidence: Slow push-ins, locked close-ups, minimal movement, carefully timed shifts toward the eyes.

## Composition and Blocking

Use bodies, thresholds, screen direction, foreground layers, and group geometry to make power and attention legible.

Legacy overview evidence: Sparse rooms, confrontational two-shots, faces isolated against darkness, bodies arranged like psychological diagrams.

## Lighting and Color

Motivate the source, contrast, palette, practicals, and weather through the scene's emotional and material conditions.

Legacy overview evidence: Stark monochrome, candlelight, pale interiors, deep shadows, restrained red accents in selected works.

## Production Design

Select locations, objects, costume relationships, and surfaces that reveal the story rather than decorate it.
