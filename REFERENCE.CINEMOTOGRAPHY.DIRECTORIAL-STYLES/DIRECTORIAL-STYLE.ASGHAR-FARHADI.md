


## Nationality
Iranian.

## Working Decades and Evolution by Decade
1980-2024; active across the 1980s through the 2020s.

# Directorial Style: Asghar Farhadi

## Genre

Iranian domestic drama and moral thriller built from ordinary disputes.

## Location Selection Styles

Apartments, schools, courthouses, and cramped streets where privacy constantly collapses.

## Writing and Story Logic

A small omission triggers intersecting obligations; truth arrives through contradictory testimony.

## Directorial Evolution

Moved from ensemble domestic realism toward larger transnational systems without abandoning ethical ambiguity.

## Visual Grammar

Restrained handheld observation, blocked sightlines, and offscreen causality.

## Camera and Lens

Naturalistic medium lenses, shoulder-height coverage, and motivated reframing during confrontations.

## Composition and Blocking

Doorways, corridors, and groups split by thresholds make allegiance visible.

## Lighting and Color

Available interior light, muted beige-gray rooms, and practical daylight that refuses glamour.

## Production Design

Lived-in apartments, damaged objects, work uniforms, and bureaucratic spaces encode class pressure.

## Editing

Cause-and-effect continuity with withheld information and tense ellipses.

## Sound and Scoring

Overlapping speech, traffic, phones, and abrupt silence; sparse score.

## Casting and Performance

Naturalistic ensemble behavior; actors conceal motives inside practical tasks.

## Collaborators and Collaborations

Cinematographers Mahmoud Kalari and Mahmoud Kolari; recurring Iranian ensemble performers support social density.

## Notable Evidentiary Works and Scenes

The elevator dispute in _A Separation_; the theater aftermath in _The Salesman_; the missing girl in _About Elly_.

## Recognition Profile

- A Separation* won the Academy Award and Berlin Golden Bear, proving intimate Iranian drama could command global attention; *The Salesman* won Cannes acting and screenplay honors; *A Hero\* became a late-career moral-thriller touchstone.

## Themes

Responsibility, class, reputation, gendered risk, and the instability of partial truth.

## Usage and When to Reference

Use for social thrillers, domestic investigations, and scenes where every choice harms someone.

## Prompt Translation

`Farhadi-informed original domestic moral thriller: cramped lived-in location, shoulder-height observation, obstructed sightlines, overlapping testimony, muted practical light, escalating ethical ambiguity.`

## Hard Negatives

Avoid melodramatic villains, omniscient explanations, glossy lighting, and tidy moral verdicts.
