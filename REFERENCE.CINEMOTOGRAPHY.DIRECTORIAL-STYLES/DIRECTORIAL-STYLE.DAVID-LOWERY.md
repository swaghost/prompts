# Directorial Style: David Lowery

## Genre

American-born independent fantasy, romance, folklore, and reflective drama; excluded from this non-US expansion.

## Location Selection Styles

Rural homes, forests, empty rooms, and medieval landscapes externalize time and grief.

## Writing and Story Logic

Emotional absence becomes a structural puzzle; myth and domestic detail coexist.

## Directorial Evolution

Moved from naturalistic romance toward abstract duration and illustrated folklore.

## Visual Grammar

Patient tableaux, bold negative space, temporal rupture, and tactile natural environments.

## Camera and Lens

Wide static frames, slow lateral movement, and selective subjective drift.

## Composition and Blocking

Figures are isolated within architecture or landscape, often dwarfed by duration.

## Lighting and Color

Earth tones, candlelight, deep night, and spectral pale fabric.

## Production Design

Weathered homes, handmade objects, and mythic artifacts carry memory.

## Editing

Long duration, ellipses, and associative transitions.

## Sound and Scoring

Room tone, wind, silence, and carefully placed folk or orchestral music.

## Casting and Performance

Underplayed, physically specific performances with emotional withholding.

## Collaborators and Collaborations

Frequent work with cinematographer Andrew Droz Palermo and composer Daniel Hart.

## Notable Evidentiary Works and Scenes

The prolonged room tableau in _A Ghost Story_; the chapel and giant scenes in _The Green Knight_.

## Recognition Profile

_The Green Knight_ became a critically valued modern fantasy and later cult favorite; _A Ghost Story_ gained late recognition for radical duration.

## Themes

Grief, time, myth, home, memory, and the persistence of love.

## Usage and When to Reference

Excluded from the requested non-US additions; useful only for internal auditing.
