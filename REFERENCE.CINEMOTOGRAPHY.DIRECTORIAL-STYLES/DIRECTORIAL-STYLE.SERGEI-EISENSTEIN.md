


## Nationality
Russian-Soviet.

## Working Decades and Evolution by Decade
1923-1948; active across the 1920s through the 1950s.

# Directorial Style: Sergei Eisenstein

## Genre

Historical epic, political montage, and revolutionary drama.

## Writing and Story Logic

Ideas emerge from collision between images, bodies, crowds, and opposing social forces.

## Directorial Evolution

Theoretical silent montage expands into synchronized sound, spectacle, and state-scale historical narrative.

## Visual Grammar

Graphic contrast, typage, emblematic objects, crowd movement, and intellectual juxtaposition.

## Camera and Lens

Aggressive angles, low perspectives, close detail, and dynamic compositions built for collision.

## Composition and Blocking

Crowds, machines, stairs, and bodies form legible political diagrams.

## Lighting and Color

Sculpted monochrome, sharp backlight, smoke, and selected color as ideological punctuation.

## Production Design

Monuments, tools, uniforms, banners, machinery, and material symbols of power.

## Editing

Metric, rhythmic, tonal, and intellectual montage; edit images to create a new idea.

## Sound and Scoring

Percussive effects, collective voices, alarms, and music structured against visual rhythm.

## Casting and Performance

Emblematic faces, physical types, collective gestures, and concentrated reaction.

## Collaborators and Collaborations

Cinematographers, designers, composers, and montage theorists are structural partners.

## Notable Evidentiary Works and Scenes

_Battleship Potemkin_, _October_, _Strike_, and _Ivan the Terrible_.

## Themes

Revolution, collective force, class, violence, history, and ideological power.

## Usage and When to Reference

Use for protest, political montage, historical crowds, machines, and image-driven argument.

## Prompt Translation

`Eisenstein-informed original political montage: graphic crowd geometry, emblematic tool inserts, sharp monochrome, percussive cutting, collective force.`

## Hard Negatives

Avoid propaganda simplification, arbitrary rapid cuts, and direct recreation of iconic stair imagery.
