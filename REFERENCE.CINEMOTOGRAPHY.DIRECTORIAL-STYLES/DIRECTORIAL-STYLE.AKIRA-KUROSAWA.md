# Directorial Style: Akira Kurosawa

## Genre

Use the director's established genres as a starting point, then specify the original scene's genre and dramatic objective.

## Writing and Story Logic

Moral conflict is expressed through action, weather, social duty, and competing codes of honor. Characters face decisive choices rather than abstract dilemmas.

## Directorial Evolution

Early work favors disciplined genre structure and sharp black-and-white contrast. Later films expand into monumental color, painterly landscapes, mythic scale, and elemental weather.

## Visual Grammar

Translate the director's recurring visual decisions into original choices of scale, duration, contrast, texture, and spatial attention.

Legacy overview evidence: Monumental, legible compositions; weather and architecture externalize conflict.

## Camera and Lens

Choose camera height, movement, focal length, depth of field, and coverage to serve the scene's emotional information.

Legacy overview evidence: Dynamic lateral tracking, decisive pans, telephoto compression for group movement, sudden reframing.

## Composition and Blocking

Use bodies, thresholds, screen direction, foreground layers, and group geometry to make power and attention legible.

Legacy overview evidence: Strong diagonals, layered staging, geometric group formations, characters crossing through depth.

## Lighting and Color

Motivate the source, contrast, palette, practicals, and weather through the scene's emotional and material conditions.

Legacy overview evidence: High-contrast black and white in early work; elemental color, rain, wind, dust, and snow in later work.

## Production Design

Select locations, objects, costume relationships, and surfaces that reveal the story rather than decorate it.

## Editing

Set shot duration, transitions, ellipsis, repetition, and revelation rhythm according to the dramatic beat.

Legacy overview evidence: Rhythmic action cutting, abrupt transitions, weather sound, silence before impact, percussion-like movement.
