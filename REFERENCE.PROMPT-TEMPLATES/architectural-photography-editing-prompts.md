## Overview

### What This Guide Covers

This comprehensive reference system documents **professional architectural photography AI editing prompts** for image expansion, object removal, model transformation, people addition, collage creation, and virtual staging. Each prompt pattern maintains architectural integrity while achieving specific editing goals.

### Core Editing Categories

- **Frame Expansion**: Reveal more space while preserving architecture
- **Object Removal**: Clean spaces by removing furniture/items
- **Transformation**: Convert 3D models to photorealistic images
- **People Addition**: Add human scale and life to spaces
- **Collage Creation**: Multiple crops showcasing different aspects
- **Virtual Staging**: Furnish empty spaces based on design references

### Universal Principles

✅ **Preserve Architecture** - Building geometry, proportions, structure never change  
✅ **Maintain Materials** - Original textures, finishes, surfaces preserved  
✅ **Keep Lighting** - Original light quality, direction, shadows maintained  
✅ **Respect Camera Angle** - Original perspective and framing preserved  
✅ **Editorial Quality** - Professional architectural photography standards  
✅ **No CGI Appearance** - True photographic look, not rendered

## Frame Expansion Prompts

### Purpose

Expand the visible area of a photograph beyond original frame edges, revealing more of the space while maintaining architectural accuracy and photographic quality.

### Master Template

```
Expand the framing while preserving the original architecture and materials, revealing [specific element/direction]. Editorial architectural photography, warm natural light, full-frame DSLR, [XX]mm lens, with a true photographic look and no CGI or rendered appearance.
```

---

### Variant 1: Expand to Reveal Specific Object/Area

**Use Case:** Show furniture, architectural features, or spaces just outside original frame

**Template:**

```
Expand the framing while preserving the original architecture and materials, revealing the [sofa on the right / window on the left / staircase behind / etc.]. Editorial architectural photography, warm natural light, full-frame DSLR, [35mm] lens, with a true photographic look and no CGI or rendered appearance.
```

**Example:**

```
Expand the framing while preserving the original architecture and materials, revealing the sofa on the right. Editorial architectural photography, warm natural light, full-frame DSLR, 35mm lens, with a true photographic look and no CGI or rendered appearance.
```

**Key Parameters:**

- **Original architecture preserved**: Walls, ceiling, floor, windows, doors unchanged
- **Materials maintained**: Wood, stone, paint colors, textures identical
- **Lighting consistent**: Same light quality, direction, time of day
- **Camera angle preserved**: Perspective lines continue naturally
- **Photographic realism**: No CGI look, true camera capture aesthetic

**Best For:**

- Real estate photography (showing more of room)
- Interior design portfolios (revealing full furniture layout)
- Architectural documentation (capturing full space)
- Editorial publications (better composition with more context)

## Collage Generation Prompts

### Purpose

Create multi-frame collages from single photographs, showcasing different crops, details, and perspectives of the same space while maintaining visual consistency.

### Master Template

```
Generate an editorial-style [4×4 / 3×3 / 2×2] collage using multiple crops from the same photograph. Highlight the overall space, architectural details, materials, furniture, and exterior views while preserving the original architecture, lighting, materials, and visual consistency across every frame.
```

---

### Variant 1: 4×4 Detail Collage

**Template:**

```
Generate an editorial-style 4×4 collage using multiple crops from the same photograph. Highlight the overall space, architectural details, materials, furniture, and exterior views while preserving the original architecture, lighting, materials, and visual consistency across every frame.
```

**Suggested Frame Breakdown (16 frames):**

**Row 1 (Top):**

1. Overall room view (wide establishing shot)
2. Ceiling detail or architectural molding
3. Window with exterior view
4. Corner or wall detail

**Row 2:** 5. Furniture grouping (sofa area) 6. Material close-up (wood grain, stone, fabric) 7. Decorative detail (art, plant, accessory) 8. Lighting fixture

**Row 3:** 9. Another furniture angle 10. Floor material/pattern detail 11. Architectural element (fireplace, built-in, etc.) 12. Texture close-up

**Row 4 (Bottom):** 13. Wide alternate angle 14. Window frame detail 15. Material transition (floor to wall) 16. Atmospheric detail (light, shadow, reflection)

**Consistency Requirements:**

- ✅ Same lighting across all frames
- ✅ Same time of day (consistent light color/quality)
- ✅ Consistent color grading
- ✅ Unified photographic style
- ✅ Same level of sharpness/focus
- ✅ Coherent overall narrative

**Best For:**

- Editorial magazine spreads
- Portfolio presentations
- Social media multi-image posts
- Detailed project documentation
- Design process storytelling

---

**Row 1:**

1. Detail (material/texture)

## Technical Photography Parameters

### Camera Equipment Specifications

**Full-Frame DSLR:**

- Professional camera body (Canon 5D, Nikon D850, Sony A7 series equivalent)
- Full-frame sensor (35mm equivalent)
- High resolution (24-50 megapixels)
- Professional quality output

**Lens Focal Lengths by Space Type:**

**24mm Wide-Angle:**

- Small rooms, tight spaces
- Full room views in compact areas
- Dramatic perspective
- More architectural distortion (acceptable for interiors)

**35mm Standard Wide:**

- Most versatile for interiors
- Natural perspective
- Good for medium-sized rooms
- Minimal distortion
- **Most common for editorial work**

**50mm Standard:**

- Detail shots
- Furniture groupings
- More intimate spaces
- Natural eye-level perspective

**Tilt-Shift Lenses:**

- Correcting vertical lines
- Large spaces, tall ceilings
- Professional architectural work
- Maintaining parallel verticals

---

### Photography Settings & Qualities

**Aperture:**

- f/8 to f/11 most common (deep depth of field)
- Everything in focus (foreground to background)
- Sharp throughout space
- Professional architectural standard

**ISO:**

- Low ISO (100-400)
- Clean images, minimal noise
- High quality detail
- Professional standard

**Shutter Speed:**

- Tripod-based (allows longer exposures)
- 1/15s to several seconds
- Captures full ambient light
- Sharp images (no camera shake)

**White Balance:**

- Accurate color representation
- Natural warm light (3000-4000K for interiors)
- Daylight balanced (5500K) with window light
- No color casts

**Dynamic Range:**

- Balanced exposure (windows and interior both visible)
- HDR techniques common (multiple exposures blended)
- Detail in highlights and shadows
- Professional tonal range

## Editorial Architectural Photography Style

### Defining Characteristics

**1. Composition:**

- Clean, balanced, intentional
- Rule of thirds or centered symmetry
- Leading lines guide eye through space
- Thoughtful framing of architectural elements
- Breathing room around subjects
- Professional polish

**2. Lighting:**

- Natural light emphasized (windows, daylight)
- Warm inviting quality (not cold/clinical)
- Soft shadows (not harsh)
- Balanced exposure (no blown highlights)
- Atmospheric depth
- Time of day appropriate (usually morning/afternoon)

**3. Color Grading:**

- Warm natural tones
- Slightly desaturated elegance (not overly vibrant)
- Consistent color palette
- Professional color correction
- Film-like quality (not digital/harsh)
- Magazine-ready appearance

**4. Focus & Sharpness:**

- Deep depth of field (everything sharp)
- Tack-sharp critical focus
- Detail visible throughout
- Professional clarity
- No soft/blurry areas (unless intentional)

**5. Perspective:**

- Vertical lines parallel (not converging)
- Horizontal alignment accurate
- Professional level composition
- No Dutch angles (unless artistic intent)
- Proper lens correction

**6. Mood & Atmosphere:**

- Inviting, warm, livable
- Sophisticated, refined
- Editorial elegance
- Professional polish
- Timeless quality (not trendy)

---

### True Photographic Look vs. CGI/Rendered

**Photographic Qualities:**

- ✅ Natural lens characteristics (slight vignetting)
- ✅ Realistic depth of field
- ✅ Natural grain structure (film-like)
- ✅ Organic light falloff
- ✅ Realistic material reflections
- ✅ Natural shadow softness
- ✅ Atmospheric perspective (depth haze)
- ✅ Lens flare (when backlit)
- ✅ Natural color transitions

**Avoid CGI Appearance:**

- ❌ Overly perfect/clean (too sterile)
- ❌ Artificial lighting (no natural falloff)
- ✅ Synthetic material appearance
- ❌ Unrealistic color saturation
- ❌ Perfect reflections (too mirror-like)
- ❌ Hard-edged shadows (no softness)
- ❌ Flat dimensionless quality
- ❌ Video game aesthetic
- ❌ 3D render look

**Prompt Keywords for Photographic Realism:**

- "True photographic look"
- "No CGI or rendered appearance"
- "Full-frame DSLR quality"
- "Editorial architectural photography"
- "Natural camera characteristics"
- "Film-like quality"
- "Professional photography"
- "Authentic camera capture"

## Lighting Systems

### Natural Light (Most Common)

**Characteristics:**

- Window light as primary source
- Soft directional quality
- Warm color temperature (morning/afternoon)
- Natural shadows
- Atmospheric depth
- Inviting warmth

**Time of Day:**

- **Morning Light** (8am-11am): Fresh, clean, slightly cool
- **Afternoon Light** (2pm-5pm): Warm, golden, inviting (most common)
- **Overcast**: Soft, even, minimal shadows
- **Golden Hour** (sunset): Very warm, dramatic, special occasions

**Prompt Keywords:**

- "Warm natural light"
- "Soft window light"
- "Natural daylight"
- "Afternoon sun"
- "Diffused natural light"

---

### Mixed Lighting (Natural + Artificial)

## Composition & Framing Rules

### Architectural Photography Composition

**1. Vertical Lines:**

- Keep vertical lines parallel (not converging)
- Use tilt-shift or correction
- Professional architectural standard
- Walls appear straight, not leaning

**2. Horizontal Alignment:**

- Keep horizons and horizontal lines level
- No tilted compositions (unless intentional)
- Clean professional appearance

**3. Symmetry vs. Rule of Thirds:**

**Symmetry:**

- Centered compositions
- Formal, classical spaces
- Grand architectural elements
- Balanced and orderly

**Rule of Thirds:**

- Off-center focal points
- More dynamic composition
- Modern editorial approach
- Visual interest and movement

**4. Leading Lines:**

- Use architectural elements to guide eye
- Floor patterns, ceiling beams, furniture arrangement
- Create depth and dimension
- Draw viewer through space

**5. Framing Within Frame:**

- Doorways, windows, architectural openings
- Creates depth and layers
- Professional compositional device
- Adds visual interest

**6. Breathing Room:**

- Don't crowd frame edges
- Leave space around key elements
- Comfortable visual balance
- Professional polish

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

### Issue 1: Architecture Changes Unintentionally

**Symptoms:** Walls move, ceiling height changes, proportions shift

**Solutions:**
✅ Add "EXACTLY preserving the original architecture" multiple times  
✅ Specify "no changes to building geometry or volume"  
✅ Add "maintaining exact room proportions"  
✅ Use "walls, ceiling, floor positions unchanged"  
✅ Try more explicit architecture preservation language

---

### Issue 2: Materials Change Color or Texture

**Symptoms:** Wood becomes different species, floor color changes, wall finish alters

**Solutions:**
✅ Specify "preserving original materials EXACTLY"  
✅ List specific materials: "keep oak flooring, white walls, marble counters"  
✅ Add "no material changes"  
✅ Reference preservation of "textures, colors, and finishes"  
✅ Be more descriptive about original materials

---

### Issue 3: Lighting Changes Unrealistically

**Symptoms:** Light direction shifts, time of day changes, unnatural lighting

**Solutions:**
✅ Specify "preserve original lighting direction and quality"  
✅ Add "maintain time of day and light color"  
✅ Use "keep natural light characteristics unchanged"  
✅ Reference "same window light and shadows"  
✅ Specify "no new light sources"

---

### Issue 4: CGI/Rendered Appearance Instead of Photographic

**Symptoms:** Result looks 3D rendered, not real camera photo

**Solutions:**
✅ Emphasize "true photographic look"  
✅ Add "NO CGI or rendered appearance" explicitly  
✅ Specify "full-frame DSLR camera quality"  
✅ Use "editorial architectural photography aesthetic"  
✅ Add "natural camera lens characteristics"  
✅ Reference "film-like quality"

---

### Issue 5: Perspective/Camera Angle Shifts

**Symptoms:** View changes, different perspective, camera moves

**Solutions:**
✅ Specify "preserve exact camera angle and framing"  
✅ Add "maintain original perspective and viewpoint"  
✅ Use "no changes to camera position"  
✅ Reference "keep vertical and horizontal alignment"  
✅ Add "same focal length characteristics"

---

### Issue 6: Added Furniture Poorly Scaled

**Symptoms:** Furniture too large/small, doesn't fit space naturally

**Solutions:**
✅ Add "maintaining accurate scale and proportions"  
✅ Specify "appropriately scaled furniture for the space"  
✅ Use "realistic furniture sizing"  
✅ Add architectural reference dimensions if possible  
✅ Regenerate with scale emphasis

---

### Issue 7: Frame Expansion Creates Seams or Artifacts

**Symptoms:** Visible blending lines, inconsistent areas, unnatural extensions

**Solutions:**
✅ Specify "seamless natural extension"  
✅ Add "architectural elements continue logically"  
✅ Use "no seams or blending artifacts"  
✅ Request "smooth continuation of existing space"  
✅ May need manual touch-up in Photoshop

---

### Issue 8: Object Removal Leaves Ghosts or Artifacts

**Symptoms:** Outlines of removed items visible, unnatural floor/wall patterns

**Solutions:**
✅ Specify "complete removal with natural background revealed"  
✅ Add "no ghosting or artifacts"  
✅ Use "seamless floor and wall restoration"  
✅ Request "natural empty space appearance"  
✅ May need inpainting refinement

## 30 Pro Tips

### Prompt Writing Tips (1-10)

**1. Be Explicit About Preservation**
Always clearly state what must be preserved—architecture, materials, lighting, camera angle.

**2. Repeat Critical Requirements**
Mention preservation multiple times in prompt for emphasis and AI attention.

**3. Use Negative Constraints**
"No CGI appearance", "no changes to geometry", "no material alterations" reinforces boundaries.

**4. Specify Photography Style**
"Editorial architectural photography" sets professional tone and aesthetic expectations.

**5. Include Technical Camera Details**
"Full-frame DSLR, 35mm lens, f/8" adds photographic realism and precision.

**6. Describe Desired Lighting Explicitly**
"Warm natural light" vs "cool overcast" vs "mixed lighting"—be specific about mood.

**7. Reference Professional Standards**
"Editorial quality", "magazine-worthy", "professional photography" raises output bar.

**8. List Specific Materials When Critical**
If oak flooring or marble counters matter, name them explicitly in prompt.

**9. Use Architectural Terminology**
"Geometry", "proportions", "volume", "scale"—professional language yields better results.

**10. Separate Preservation from Addition**
First state what must stay same, then describe what should be added/changed.

---

### Execution Tips (11-20)

**11. Start with Simple Edits**
Test frame expansion or simple object removal before complex transformations.

**12. Generate Multiple Variations**
Create 3-5 versions of same prompt, select best result—AI varies each time.

**13. Iterate and Refine**
If first result isn't perfect, refine prompt based on what went wrong.

**14. Use High-Quality Source Images**
Better input = better output—start with sharp, well-lit, high-res photos.

**15. Check Architecture First**
Before evaluating aesthetics, verify architectural preservation—non-negotiable.

**16. Verify Scale and Proportions**
Look for furniture that's too large/small, doors that don't fit people, odd spatial relationships.

**17. Examine Material Consistency**
Zoom in on materials—wood grain should be consistent, tile patterns unbroken.

**18. Review Lighting Logic**
Shadows should align, light direction consistent, no impossible lighting scenarios.

**19. Compare to Original Side-by-Side**
Put original and edited images next to each other to catch subtle unwanted changes.

**20. Accept Minor Imperfections for Major Wins**
Tiny texture inconsistencies acceptable if overall architecture and aesthetic are preserved.

---

### Platform & Workflow Tips (21-30)

**21. Match Platform to Task**
Photoshop best for extensions/removals, Midjourney for full transformations.

**22. Use Photoshop for Final Touch-Ups**
Even AI-generated results often benefit from minor manual refinements.

**23. Save Intermediate Versions**
Keep original and each iteration—allows rollback if needed.

**24. Build Prompt Library**
Save successful prompts for reuse—create personal template collection.

**25. Test on Low-Stakes Projects First**
Practice on personal projects before client work to understand platform behavior.

**26. Combine Multiple Edits Carefully**
Frame expansion + object removal + staging = complex—may need sequential steps.

**27. Provide Reference Images When Possible**
For staging, mood boards or reference photos significantly improve results.

**28. Use Consistent Photography Style**
For multi-image projects, maintain same camera/lighting approach across all edits.

**29. Consider Output Usage**
Web use vs. print affects resolution and quality requirements—generate accordingly.

**30. Stay Updated on Platform Capabilities**
AI tools evolve rapidly—new features may handle old problems better.
