# Action Sequence Template: Superhero Landing

## Overview

Precise timing, camera work, and choreography for a cinematic superhero landing action sequence.

**Total Duration:** 1.8-15 seconds across two shots  
**Style:** High-speed action, continuous camera movement, realistic impact physics  
**Key Elements:** Vertical drop, taxi impact, aerial pursuit launch

---

## SHOT ONE: The Landing (0-1.8 Seconds)

### Initial Setup

**Camera:** Fixed, low-angle 24mm wide-lens camera aimed at a completely stationary yellow taxi parked in the middle of a city street.

**Duration:** Frame remains quiet for a brief moment.

### The Drop (Timing Critical)

**Action:** The protagonist suddenly drops vertically into the frame from above at extremely high speed and crashes heavily onto the taxi's hood in a one-knee landing pose.

### Impact Sequence

**Physics Requirements:**

- The landing must communicate realistic body weight and powerful impact
- The taxi body suddenly sinks downward
- Suspension compresses visibly
- Hood dents slightly
- Windshield windows shake violently from the impact
- Tires briefly compress downward while dust and small fragments burst outward in every direction

### Camera Transition

**After stabilizing his body**, the protagonist quickly raises his head.

**Camera Movement:** The camera transitions from the locked-off position into a rapid low-angle push-in.

### Launch Sequence

**Action:** The protagonist forcefully launches himself upward from the taxi hood toward the buildings above.

**Physics:**

- The taxi sinks and shakes violently under the force of his jump
- Dust and a strong burst of air explode beneath his feet as he shoots upward toward the surrounding skyscrapers

---

## Critical Requirements for Shot One

### Mandatory Rules:

1. ✓ The taxi must be **completely stationary** at the beginning
2. ✓ The protagonist must **suddenly fall into the frame from above**
3. ✓ He must **never already be crouching** on the taxi hood when the video begins
4. ✓ Only **one camera cut** occurs at exactly 1.8 seconds

---

## SHOT TWO: The Pursuit (1.8-15 Seconds)

### Camera Setup

**Cut to:** A high-speed aerial tracking camera positioned behind and slightly to the side of the protagonist.

### Continuity Rule

**No additional cuts are allowed after this point.**

All subsequent action must be captured through continuous camera movement:

- Smooth orbiting
- Diving
- Close tracking
- Controlled camera throughout

### Camera Restrictions

**Do not use:**

- Additional edits
- Jump cuts
- Camera resets

---

## FINAL SEQUENCE: Glass Wall Breach (1.8-3 Seconds)

### Action

The protagonist launches from the taxi toward a tall building and performs one rapid forward flip in mid-air, clearing the glass curtain wall and rooftop edge.

### Impact & Entry

- Shattering glass
- Explosive entry through building facade
- Continuous camera follows through breach
- No cuts or resets

---

## Technical Specifications

### Timing Breakdown:

- **0-1.8s:** Shot One (fixed → push-in)
- **1.8s:** Single camera cut
- **1.8-3s:** Glass wall breach
- **3-15s:** Continuous aerial tracking (no cuts)

### Camera Movements:

- **Shot 1:** Fixed → Low-angle push-in
- **Shot 2:** High-speed aerial tracking, smooth orbiting, diving, close tracking

### Physics Requirements:

- Realistic body weight on impact
- Suspension compression
- Environmental response (dust, debris, glass)
- Violent shaking and deformation
- Air burst effects

### Environmental Details:

- City street setting
- Yellow taxi (completely stationary initially)
- Tall buildings/skyscrapers
- Glass curtain wall
- Rooftop edge

---

## Adaptation Guide

To adapt this template for other action sequences:

1. **Identify the impact moment** (replaces taxi landing)
2. **Define the launch point** (replaces upward jump)
3. **Specify camera transition timing** (keep single cut at ~1.8s)
4. **Detail continuous tracking requirements** (no additional cuts)
5. **Describe environmental interactions** (glass, debris, surfaces)

### Key Principles to Maintain:

- Sudden entry into frame (not pre-positioned)
- Realistic physics and weight
- Environmental response to action
- Single camera cut at precise moment
- Continuous movement after cut
- No additional edits, jumps, or resets

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Subject already in position at start  
❌ Multiple camera cuts after initial transition  
❌ Lack of environmental response (no dust, no impact)  
❌ Unrealistic physics (too light, too slow)  
❌ Breaking continuous camera movement  
❌ Adding edits or resets after the 1.8s mark

✓ Subject enters dramatically  
✓ Single clean cut at specified time  
✓ Full environmental interaction  
✓ Realistic weight and impact  
✓ Smooth continuous tracking  
✓ No breaks in action flow
