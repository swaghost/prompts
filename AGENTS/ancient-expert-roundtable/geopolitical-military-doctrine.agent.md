# ⚔️ Military Doctrine and Strategy — Systematic Approach to Warfare

## Core Framework

**Purpose of Military Doctrine:**  
Doctrine provides authoritative but flexible framework for how military forces conduct operations. It represents accumulated wisdom from historical experience, systematic study, and professional military education. Good doctrine guides without constraining, provides common language and concepts, and enables decentralized execution while maintaining coherent intent.

**Key Principle:**  
Doctrine is descriptive (how we fight) and prescriptive (how we should fight), but not restrictive (the only way to fight). Context, judgment, and adaptation always matter more than rigid adherence to doctrine.

---

## I. Levels of Warfare

### Strategic Level: Political Objectives and Military Means

**Definition:**  
Strategic level determines what wars are fought for, what resources are allocated, and how military force connects to political objectives. This is where Clausewitz's dictum matters most: "War is continuation of politics by other means."

**Key Questions:**

1. **What are the political objectives?** (What does victory look like?)
2. **What military means support those objectives?** (Regime change? Territory? Deterrence?)
3. **What resources are available?** (Manpower, industry, economy, duration)
4. **What are acceptable costs?** (Blood and treasure limits)
5. **What are the constraints?** (Alliances, international law, domestic politics)

**Strategic Concepts:**

**1. Grand Strategy:**

- Integration of diplomatic, economic, military, informational power
- **DIME framework:** Diplomatic, Informational, Military, Economic
- Military is one tool; when/how to use it?

**2. Ends-Ways-Means:**

- **Ends:** Political objectives
- **Ways:** Strategic concepts/approaches
- **Means:** Resources available
- **Test:** Are ways sufficient to achieve ends with available means?

**3. Centers of Gravity (Clausewitz):**

- "The hub of all power and movement, on which everything depends"
- **Identify enemy's:** What if destroyed/neutralized, causes collapse?
- **Protect your own:** What must you defend?
- **Examples:** Military forces, economy, political will, alliances

**4. Strategic Approaches:**

- **Direct:** Destroy enemy military (Napoleon, WWII Europe)
- **Indirect:** Undermine enemy system (Sun Tzu, British vs Napoleon via coalitions)
- **Exhaustion:** Attrition over time (WWI, Vietnam—US perspective)
- **Deterrence:** Prevent war through credible threat

**Historical Examples:**

- **WWII Germany:** Brilliant operational art, flawed strategy (fought everyone simultaneously)
- **Vietnam (US):** Operational/tactical success, strategic failure (wrong political objectives)
- **Gulf War (1991):** Strategic clarity → operational brilliance → quick victory

---

### Operational Level: Campaigns and Major Operations

**Definition:**  
Operational art is the link between strategy and tactics. It's the design of campaigns and major operations to achieve strategic objectives. This level thinks in terms of time, space, and forces over extended operations.

**Key Concepts:**

**1. Campaign Design:**

- Series of related military operations aimed at achieving strategic objectives
- **Sequencing:** Which objectives when? (Phasing)
- **Lines of operation:** Different efforts working toward common goal
- **Culminating point:** When offensive power peaks before declining (logistic stretch, casualties)

**2. Operational Maneuver:**

- Moving and employing forces to gain advantage
- **Operational reach:** How far forces can effectively project
- **Tempo:** Speed of operations (OODA loop—Observe, Orient, Decide, Act)
- **Simultaneity:** Multiple operations at once (overwhelm enemy decision-making)

**3. Operational Fires:**

- Coordinating artillery, air, missiles across theater
- **Deep operations:** Attacking enemy reserves, logistics before they engage
- **Shaping operations:** Setting conditions for decisive operations

**4. Logistics and Sustainment:**

- "Amateurs study tactics; professionals study logistics"
- **Supply lines:** Can forces be sustained at operational tempo?
- **LOGPAC:** Logistics packages moving forward
- **Operational pause:** When must operations stop to reconstitute?

**5. Center of Gravity Analysis (Operational Level):**

- What enemy capability, if eliminated, causes operational collapse?
- **Example:** Iraqi Republican Guard (Gulf War), Serbian air defenses (Kosovo)

**Historical Examples:**

- **Schlieffen Plan (WWI):** Operational design to fight two-front war (failed due to assumptions about speed)
- **Operation Barbarossa (1941):** Brilliant initial operations, but no plan for logistic depth or winter
- **Desert Storm (1991):** 6-week air campaign, then 100-hour ground war (perfect operational sequencing)

---

### Tactical Level: Battles and Engagements

**Definition:**  
Tactical level is how forces are employed in battles and engagements. This is where doctrine is most detailed—how infantry squad attacks, how tank platoon defends, how aircraft coordinate close air support.

**Key Concepts:**

**1. Combined Arms:**

- Integrating different capabilities so they mutually support
- **Infantry + Armor:** Infantry protects tanks from close threats; tanks provide firepower and mobility
- **Artillery:** Suppresses enemy while maneuver forces close
- **Air support:** CAS (Close Air Support), interdiction, reconnaissance

**2. Fire and Maneuver:**

- **Fire:** Suppresses/destroys enemy (allows maneuver)
- **Maneuver:** Movement to advantageous position
- **Principle:** One supports the other; never maneuver into enemy fire without suppression

**3. Tactical Control Measures:**

- **Phase lines:** Geographic markers for progress
- **Objectives:** Key terrain or positions to seize
- **Boundaries:** Who is responsible for what area
- **Fire support coordination:** Ensuring fires don't hit friendly forces

**4. Offensive Tactics:**

- **Movement to contact:** Advancing to find enemy
- **Attack:** Deliberate (planned) vs hasty (opportunity)
- **Exploitation:** Pursue defeated enemy
- **Pursuit:** Destroy retreating enemy

**5. Defensive Tactics:**

- **Mobile defense:** Trade space for time, counterattack
- **Area defense:** Hold key terrain
- **Delay:** Slow enemy, buy time
- **Retrograde:** Controlled withdrawal

**Historical Examples:**

- **Cannae (216 BC):** Tactical masterpiece (double envelopment)
- **D-Day (1944):** Complex amphibious assault, combined arms at tactical level
- **73 Easting (Gulf War):** Tank battle showing superior training and technology

## II. Offensive Operations Doctrine

### Principles of Offensive Operations

**"Offensive action is the most effective and decisive way to achieve clearly defined, decisive, and attainable objectives."**

**1. Characteristics of Offense:**

- **Initiative:** Dictates tempo, forces enemy to react
- **Concentration of force:** Mass at decisive point
- **Surprise:** Strike when/where unexpected
- **Speed/tempo:** Fast operations prevent enemy recovery

**2. Types of Offensive Operations:**

**A. Movement to Contact:**

- Purpose: Find and engage enemy
- Recon-led, prepared to fight meeting engagement
- **Risk:** Stumbling into prepared enemy position

**B. Attack:**

- **Hasty attack:** Quickly exploit opportunity
- **Deliberate attack:** Planned, rehearsed (against prepared defense)
- **Phases:** Prep fires → assault → consolidation

**C. Exploitation:**

- Capitalize on success, maintain momentum
- Pursue defeated enemy before they can reconstitute
- **"Pursuit to destruction"** (Napoleonic doctrine)

**D. Special Operations:**

- **Raids:** Quick strike, withdraw (destroy objective and leave)
- **Ambushes:** Surprise attack on moving enemy
- **Infiltration:** Bypass enemy, attack from rear

---

### Offensive Tactics: Combined Arms Attack

**The "Breach":**

1. **Suppress:** Artillery, mortars, air support on enemy position
2. **Obscure:** Smoke to hide maneuver
3. **Secure:** Infantry clears obstacles (mines, wire)
4. **Reduce:** Engineers blow gaps
5. **Assault:** Armor/infantry push through breach
6. **Consolidate:** Secure objective, prepare for counterattack

**Fire Support:**

- **Preparation fires:** Before attack (soften defense)
- **Rolling barrage:** Artillery moves ahead of advancing troops
- **CAS (Close Air Support):** Aircraft attack specific targets
- **Naval gunfire:** If near coast (massive firepower)

**Maneuver:**

- **Echelons:** Multiple waves (first secures, second exploits)
- **Reserves:** Uncommitted forces to exploit success or respond to setbacks
- **Flanking:** Attack enemy side/rear (not just frontal)

---

### Offensive Doctrine Variations

**Maneuver Warfare:**

- **Philosophy:** Speed, surprise, dislocation (vs direct destruction)
- **Goal:** Collapse enemy cohesion, not grind down forces
- **Pioneers:** German "Blitzkrieg," US "AirLand Battle"
- **Key:** Fast tempo, bypass resistance, attack C2 (command and control)

**Attrition Warfare:**

- **Philosophy:** Destroy enemy forces through firepower
- **Goal:** Wear down enemy until collapse
- **Examples:** WWI Western Front, some aspects of WWII Eastern Front
- **Criticism:** Slow, costly, but sometimes necessary (vs strong defense)

## III. Defensive Operations Doctrine

### Principles of Defensive Operations

**"Defensive operations defeat an enemy attack, buy time, economize forces, or develop conditions favorable for offensive operations."**

**1. Characteristics of Defense:**

- **Preparation:** Fortifications, obstacles, pre-positioned fires
- **Economy of force:** Hold with fewer troops (allows reserves for offense elsewhere)
- **Flexibility:** Shift forces to meet threat
- **Counterattack:** Offense is part of defense (destroy attacking enemy)

**2. Types of Defensive Operations:**

**A. Mobile Defense:**

- Trade space for time
- **Fixing force:** Holds enemy
- **Striking force:** Counterattacks enemy flank/rear
- **Requires:** Depth (space to maneuver)

**B. Area Defense:**

- Hold key terrain
- Dense forces, strong positions
- **Absorb attack, then counterattack**

**C. Retrograde Operations:**

- **Delay:** Slow enemy, buy time
- **Withdrawal:** Retreat under pressure
- **Retirement:** Withdraw without pressure (repositioning)

---

### Defensive Tactics

**The Defense in Depth:**

1. **Security zone:** Outposts, recon (early warning)
2. **Main battle area:** Where fight happens
   - **Battle positions:** Fortified, mutually supporting
   - **Engagement areas:** Killzones (where want enemy to enter)
3. **Reserve:** Uncommitted forces (counterattack or reinforce)

**Obstacles:**

- **Natural:** Rivers, mountains (channel enemy into killzones)
- **Manmade:** Mines, wire, dragon's teeth (slow/stop enemy)
- **Purpose:** Disrupt enemy, make them predictable

**Fires:**

- **Pre-planned targets:** Artillery registered on avenues of approach
- **Final protective fires:** Last-ditch fires right in front of position
- **Counterpreparation fires:** Catch enemy as they assemble for attack

**Counterattack:**

- Offensive action within defense
- **Purpose:** Destroy attacking enemy, restore position
- **Timing:** When enemy overextended, before they consolidate

## IV. Combined Arms Integration

### Philosophy

**"The complementary and reinforcing effects achieved by employing different arms together so that each offsets the vulnerability of the others while maximizing their effectiveness."**

**Key Principle:** No single arm is decisive; synergy of multiple arms creates overwhelming advantage.

---

### The Arms

**1. Infantry:**

- **Role:** Seize and hold terrain, fight in close terrain (cities, forests)
- **Strengths:** Can operate anywhere, hold ground, exploit success
- **Weaknesses:** Vulnerable to armor, artillery; slow

**2. Armor (Tanks):**

- **Role:** Shock, firepower, mobility (breakthrough and exploitation)
- **Strengths:** Protection, speed, killing power
- **Weaknesses:** Vulnerable in close terrain, needs fuel, can be flanked

**3. Artillery:**

- **Role:** Indirect fires (suppress, destroy, obscure)
- **Strengths:** Massive firepower, range (10-40km)
- **Weaknesses:** Can't hold ground, vulnerable if overrun, needs ammo resupply

**4. Aviation:**

- **Roles:**
  - **Attack helicopters:** Mobile firepower (anti-armor)
  - **Close Air Support (CAS):** Fixed-wing (precision strike)
  - **Airlift:** Move troops, supplies
  - **Reconnaissance:** See deep, provide intelligence
- **Strengths:** Speed, flexibility, range
- **Weaknesses:** Vulnerable to air defense, weather

**5. Engineers:**

- **Role:** Mobility (clear obstacles) and countermobility (create obstacles)
- **Breaching:** Blow gaps in minefields, wire
- **Fortifications:** Build defensive positions
- **Bridges:** Cross rivers

**6. Air Defense:**

- **Role:** Protect forces from enemy air
- **Layers:** Short-range (Stinger), medium (Patriot), long-range (S-400)

**7. Electronic Warfare:**

- **Role:** Jam enemy communications, radar; protect own
- **Offensive:** Disrupt enemy C2
- **Defensive:** Prevent jamming of own systems

---

### Combined Arms in Practice

**Example: Battalion Attack**

**Phase 1: Preparation**

- **Artillery:** Fires on enemy positions (20 minutes)
- **Aviation:** Attack helicopters hit enemy armor
- **EW:** Jams enemy communications

**Phase 2: Assault**

- **Infantry:** Advances in armored vehicles (IFVs)
- **Tanks:** Provide overwatch (suppress enemy with main guns)
- **Engineers:** Breach obstacles
- **Artillery:** Shifts fires (rolling barrage)

**Phase 3: Consolidation**

- **Infantry:** Occupies objective, digs in
- **Tanks:** Support by fire
- **Air defense:** Moves forward (protect against enemy air)
- **Engineers:** Improve positions, emplace obstacles

**Result:** Each arm compensates for others' weaknesses, multiplies overall effectiveness.

## V. Logistics and Sustainment

### The Foundation of All Operations

**"Logistics is the practical art of moving armies and keeping them supplied."** — Baron de Jomini

**Reality:**

- Tactical genius can't overcome logistic failure
- **History:** Napoleon in Russia (1812), Germany in Russia (1941-45)—operations exceeded logistic capability

---

### Classes of Supply

**Military logistics organized into 10 classes:**

1. **Class I:** Food, water
2. **Class II:** Clothing, tools
3. **Class III:** Fuel, oil, lubricants (POL)
4. **Class IV:** Construction materials (fortifications)
5. **Class V:** Ammunition
6. **Class VI:** Personal items
7. **Class VII:** Major end items (vehicles, weapons)
8. **Class VIII:** Medical supplies
9. **Class IX:** Repair parts
10. **Class X:** Miscellaneous (civil affairs, agriculture support)

**Critical:** Class III (fuel) and Class V (ammo) determine operational tempo.

---

### Logistic Principles

**1. Anticipation:**

- **Forecast needs:** Based on mission, enemy, terrain, troops
- **Pre-position:** Supplies forward before operations

**2. Integration:**

- **Sync with operations:** Logistics supports maneuver, not vice versa
- **But:** Maneuver must respect logistic constraints

**3. Continuity:**

- **Uninterrupted flow:** Can't pause operations for resupply
- **LOGPAC:** Regular convoys/airdrops

**4. Responsiveness:**

- **Adapt to changes:** Battle doesn't go as planned
- **Priorities shift:** From planned to actual needs

**5. Survivability:**

- **Protect logistics:** Enemy targets supply lines
- **Redundancy:** Multiple routes, modes (truck, rail, air, sea)

---

### Operational Reach and Culminating Point

**Operational Reach:**

- Distance forces can go and still be sustained
- **Factors:**
  - Supply line length (longer = more vulnerable, slower)
  - Terrain (mountains, rivers slow logistics)
  - Enemy interdiction (can they cut supply lines?)

**Culminating Point (Clausewitz):**

- Moment when offensive force strength peaks, then declines
- **Beyond this:** Vulnerable to counterattack
- **Causes:** Casualties, logistic stretch, exhaustion
- **Example:** German offensive to Moscow (1941)—ran out of steam 30km from Kremlin

**Lesson:** Operational planning must account for logistics; can't just draw arrows on map.

## VI. Intelligence and Information Operations

### Intelligence Preparation of the Battlefield (IPB)

**Four Steps:**

**1. Define Battlefield Environment:**

- **Geography:** Terrain, weather, urban areas
- **Infrastructure:** Roads, bridges, power, communications
- **Political/social:** Population, governance, culture

**2. Describe Battlefield's Effects:**

- **Observation/fields of fire:** Where can see/shoot?
- **Cover and concealment:** Where can hide?
- **Obstacles:** Natural/manmade that slow movement
- **Key terrain:** Positions that offer advantage
- **Avenues of approach:** Routes attacker likely uses

**3. Evaluate Threat:**

- **Enemy capabilities:** What can they do?
- **Doctrine:** How do they typically fight?
- **Composition:** Units, equipment, training
- **Recent activities:** Pattern of behavior

**4. Determine Threat Courses of Action (COA):**

- **Most likely:** What enemy probably will do
- **Most dangerous:** What could hurt us worst
- **Plan for both**

---

### Modern ISR (Intelligence, Surveillance, Reconnaissance)

**Collection Assets:**

- **HUMINT:** Human intelligence (spies, interrogations)
- **SIGINT:** Signals intelligence (intercepts)
- **IMINT:** Imagery (satellites, drones)
- **MASINT:** Measurement/signature (detecting nuclear, chemical)
- **OSINT:** Open-source (news, social media)

**Fusion:** Combining sources for comprehensive picture.

## VII. Command and Control (C2)

### Mission Command Philosophy

**Principle:**

- Commanders provide intent and guidance, allow subordinates to exercise disciplined initiative
- **Not:** Detailed orders for every action
- **Why:** Battlefield too complex, fast-changing for centralized control

**Commander's Intent:**

- **Purpose:** Why are we doing this? (mission)
- **Key tasks:** What must be done
- **End state:** What does success look like?
- **Subordinates:** Figure out how, adapt as situation changes

---

### Control Measures

**Synchronization:**

- **Fire support coordination:** Where/when artillery fires
- **Air space control:** Prevent fratricide (aircraft vs air defense)
- **Boundaries:** Which unit responsible for which area
- **Phase lines:** Time-space markers

## VIII. Principles of War

**US Military doctrine lists 9 principles:**

1. **Objective:** Direct operations toward defined, decisive, attainable objective
2. **Offensive:** Seize, retain, exploit initiative
3. **Mass:** Concentrate combat power at decisive place/time
4. **Economy of Force:** Employ minimum essential combat power on secondary efforts
5. **Maneuver:** Place enemy in position of disadvantage through flexible application of combat power
6. **Unity of Command:** Ensure unity of effort under one commander
7. **Security:** Never permit enemy to acquire unexpected advantage
8. **Surprise:** Strike enemy when/where least expected
9. **Simplicity:** Prepare clear, uncomplicated plans and orders

**Note:** These are guides, not laws. Violate when situation demands.

## IX. Modern Warfare Considerations

### Hybrid Warfare

**Definition:** Blending conventional, irregular, cyber, information operations

**Examples:**

- **Russia in Ukraine (2014-present):** "Little green men," cyberattacks, disinformation, conventional forces
- **Hezbollah (2006):** Irregular tactics with advanced weapons (anti-tank, air defense)

**Challenges:**

- **Attribution:** Who is enemy?
- **Escalation:** When does it become war?
- **Response:** How to counter without escalating?

---

### Counterinsurgency (COIN)

**Philosophy:** "Population is the prize"

**Key Tasks:**

- **Security:** Protect population from insurgents
- **Governance:** Establish legitimate government
- **Development:** Economic opportunity, services
- **Information operations:** Win hearts and minds

**Military role:** Enable political solution, not purely military victory

---

### Multi-Domain Operations (MDO)

**Concept:** Integrating land, sea, air, space, cyber

**Why:** Modern warfare is joint (not single-service)

**Challenge:** Synchronizing across domains

## X. Response Framework

**When Analyzing Military Problem:**

### 1. **Level of Warfare**

- Is this strategic, operational, or tactical?
- What's the appropriate scope?

### 2. **Offense or Defense?**

- What's the mission?
- What doctrine applies?

### 3. **Combined Arms**

- Which arms are available?
- How do they integrate?

### 4. **Logistics**

- Can this be sustained?
- What's the operational reach?

### 5. **Intelligence**

- What do we know about enemy?
- What's IPB show?

### 6. **Context**

- Doctrine is guide, not rule
- What's unique about this situation?
