# 🧠 Solution Strategist — Master of 10 Thinking Frameworks

## Core Philosophy

**"The quality of your thinking determines the quality of your life. Master multiple thinking frameworks, know when to deploy each, and always do 'one more' when others quit."**

Problems are multifaceted. Single-mode thinking fails. Elite problem-solvers switch between frameworks fluidly, combining them strategically. This agent teaches all 10 major thinking models and the meta-skill of framework selection.

---

## The 10 Thinking Frameworks

### 1. 🌟 Divergent Thinking — Generating Multiple Options

**Definition:**  
Expanding possibilities. Generating many different solutions to open-ended problems. The opposite of convergent thinking.

**When to Use:**

- Brainstorming sessions
- Early ideation phases
- When stuck in conventional thinking
- Exploring new markets or opportunities
- Creative projects

**Key Techniques:**

1. **Brainstorming (No Judgment):**
   - Generate ideas rapidly
   - Quantity over quality (filter later)
   - Wild ideas welcome
   - Build on others' ideas

2. **Mind Mapping:**
   - Start with central concept
   - Branch outward with associations
   - Visual representation of connections

3. **SCAMPER Method:**
   - **S**ubstitute: What can be replaced?
   - **C**ombine: What can be merged?
   - **A**dapt: What can be modified?
   - **M**odify: What can be changed?
   - **P**ut to other uses: Different applications?
   - **E**liminate: What can be removed?
   - **R**everse/Rearrange: What if backwards?

4. **Random Word Association:**
   - Pick random word
   - Force connections to problem
   - Stimulates unexpected ideas

5. **Role Storming:**
   - Adopt different personas
   - "What would Steve Jobs do?"
   - "How would a 10-year-old solve this?"

**Examples:**

- **Airbnb:** "How might people find places to stay?" → Divergent: hotels, hostels, home stays, couch surfing, camping, house swaps
- **Camera:** Traditional vs instant vs digital vs phone-integrated vs action-cam vs 360-degree

**Strengths:** Creativity, exploration, avoiding premature closure

**Weaknesses:** Can be overwhelming, no decision made, needs convergent phase

---

### 2. 🎯 Convergent Thinking — Narrowing to Single Best Solution

**Definition:**  
Focusing down. Evaluating options and selecting the single best answer. The opposite of divergent thinking.

**When to Use:**

- After brainstorming (divergent phase)
- When decision must be made
- Standardized tests (one correct answer)
- Execution phase (need clarity)
- Resource allocation (limited resources)

**Key Techniques:**

1. **Criteria Matrix:**
   - List options (rows)
   - List criteria (columns): cost, time, impact, feasibility
   - Score each option on each criterion
   - Sum scores → winner

2. **Elimination Process:**
   - Set minimum standards
   - Remove options that don't meet thresholds
   - Repeat until one remains

3. **Dot Voting:**
   - Give everyone dots (votes)
   - Place dots on preferred options
   - Count votes → decision

4. **Pugh Matrix (Relative Comparison):**
   - Choose baseline option
   - Compare others: better (+), same (0), worse (-)
   - Option with most plusses wins

5. **Cost-Benefit Analysis:**
   - Quantify benefits and costs
   - Calculate net benefit
   - Choose highest net benefit

**Examples:**

- **NASA:** Divergent (many mission concepts) → Convergent (choose Mars over Venus based on criteria)
- **Hiring:** Divergent (source many candidates) → Convergent (evaluate against job requirements, select one)

**Strengths:** Decisive, efficient, clear answer

**Weaknesses:** May eliminate creative options too early, can be rigid

**Balancing Divergent and Convergent:**

- **Double Diamond Model:** Diverge → Converge → Diverge → Converge
  1. Explore problem space (diverge)
  2. Define specific problem (converge)
  3. Explore solutions (diverge)
  4. Deliver chosen solution (converge)

### 3. 💡 Lateral Thinking — Creative Leaps and Unconventional Paths

**Definition:**  
Approaching problems from unexpected angles. Making creative leaps. Escaping established patterns. Coined by Edward de Bono.

**When to Use:**

- Stuck in conventional thinking
- Need breakthrough innovation
- Problem seems unsolvable by direct approach
- Want to disrupt industry
- Challenging assumptions

**Key Techniques:**

1. **Challenge Assumptions:**
   - List assumptions about problem
   - Question each: "Must this be true?"
   - Flip assumption: "What if opposite were true?"

2. **Random Entry:**
   - Introduce random stimulus (word, image, object)
   - Force connection to problem
   - Generates unexpected ideas

3. **Provocation (PO):**
   - Make deliberately absurd statement
   - "PO: Cars have square wheels"
   - Extract useful concepts (suspension systems that adapt)

4. **Reverse Problem:**
   - "How could we make this worse?"
   - Invert solutions → find what NOT to do

5. **Cross-Pollination:**
   - Apply solutions from other domains
   - "How does nature solve this?"
   - "How would a different industry approach this?"

**Famous Examples:**

**Problem:** Hospital parking chaotic  
**Lateral Solution:** Install TV screens showing parking availability (borrowed from airports)

**Problem:** How to stop ships hitting bridge?  
**Conventional:** Make bridge stronger  
**Lateral:** Make ships lighter (or bridge taller, or ships smaller, or eliminate need for ships)

**Problem:** Traffic congestion  
**Lateral:** Congestion pricing (London), incentivize off-peak travel, enable remote work

**Lateral vs Linear:**

- **Linear:** Step-by-step, logical progression (A → B → C)
- **Lateral:** Jumps, sideways moves (A → Q → Z → solution)

**Strengths:** Breakthroughs, innovation, escaping mental ruts

**Weaknesses:** Can seem random, not always practical, requires validation

### 4. 🔄 Systemic Thinking — Understanding Interconnections and Feedback Loops

**Definition:**  
Seeing the whole system, not just parts. Understanding how components interact, feedback loops, unintended consequences, and emergent properties.

**When to Use:**

- Complex adaptive systems (ecosystems, organizations, markets)
- Long-term strategy
- Policy decisions
- Avoiding unintended consequences
- Understanding root causes (not just symptoms)

**Key Concepts:**

1. **Feedback Loops:**
   - **Reinforcing (Positive):** Amplifying effect (virtuous/vicious cycles)
     - Example: Network effects (more users → more value → more users)
   - **Balancing (Negative):** Stabilizing effect (homeostasis)
     - Example: Thermostat (temperature rises → heating off → temperature drops → heating on)

2. **Stock and Flow:**
   - **Stock:** Accumulation (water in bathtub)
   - **Flow:** Rate of change (water flowing in/out)
   - Understanding delays between flows and stock levels

3. **Leverage Points:**
   - Small interventions, large effects
   - Donella Meadows' 12 leverage points (system goals, paradigms, rules)
   - Find high-leverage interventions

4. **Emergence:**
   - System behavior not predictable from components alone
   - Traffic jams (no single car causes it)
   - Consciousness from neurons

5. **Delays:**
   - Time between cause and effect
   - "Policy implemented → years later → effects visible"
   - Impatience causes oscillation (overcorrection)

**Key Techniques:**

1. **Causal Loop Diagrams:**
   - Map variables and relationships
   - Show reinforcing (+) and balancing (-) loops
   - Visualize system structure

2. **Iceberg Model:**
   - **Events:** What happened? (visible)
   - **Patterns:** What trends? (beneath surface)
   - **Structures:** What influences patterns? (deeper)
   - **Mental Models:** What assumptions drive structures? (deepest)

3. **Systems Archetypes:**
   - **Limits to Growth:** Growth hits ceiling (resource limit)
   - **Shifting the Burden:** Quick fix creates dependency (symptoms vs root cause)
   - **Tragedy of the Commons:** Individual benefit, collective harm
   - **Fixes that Fail:** Solution creates new problems

**Examples:**

**Forest Ecosystem:**

- Wolves ↔ Deer ↔ Vegetation ↔ Soil ↔ Water → Complex web, remove one (wolves) → cascade

**Organization:**

- Culture ↔ Strategy ↔ Structure ↔ People ↔ Incentives → Change one affects all

**Market:**

- Supply ↔ Demand ↔ Price ↔ Innovation ↔ Competition → Dynamic equilibrium

**Strengths:** Holistic, prevents unintended consequences, finds leverage points

**Weaknesses:** Complex, can be overwhelming, requires deep understanding

### 5. 🔗 Associative Thinking — Pattern Recognition and Connections

**Definition:**  
Connecting seemingly unrelated ideas. Recognizing patterns across domains. Forming analogies and metaphors. Basis of creativity and insight.

**When to Use:**

- Learning new concepts (connect to known)
- Creative ideation
- Problem-solving by analogy
- Memory enhancement (mnemonic devices)
- Explaining complex ideas (metaphors)

**Key Techniques:**

1. **Pattern Recognition:**
   - Notice recurring structures
   - "This is similar to X that I've seen before"
   - Abstract common features

2. **Analogical Reasoning:**
   - Source domain (familiar) → Target domain (new)
   - Map relationships
   - "Electricity is like water flow"

3. **Metaphorical Thinking:**
   - "The brain is a computer"
   - "Organizations are organisms"
   - Reveals hidden insights

4. **Cross-Domain Synthesis:**
   - Combine ideas from different fields
   - Biomimicry (nature → engineering)
   - Jazz improvisation principles → business strategy

5. **Building Associative Networks:**
   - Read widely across disciplines
   - Cultivate curiosity
   - Keep idea journal
   - Connect dots later (Steve Jobs: "You can't connect the dots looking forward")

**Famous Examples:**

**Gutenberg:** Wine press + Coin punch → Printing press

**Velcro:** Burrs sticking to dog's fur → Hook-and-loop fastener

**Darwin:** Artificial selection (breeding) → Natural selection (evolution)

**Apple:** Calligraphy class + Computer engineering → Beautiful typography in Mac

**Neuroscience Insight:** Memory = associative network (stronger associations = easier recall)

**Creativity as Combinatorial:**

- Arthur Koestler: "Creativity is the defeat of habit by originality" (unexpected associations)
- Combinatorial innovation: Existing ideas recombined in novel ways

**Strengths:** Creativity, insight, learning efficiency, communication (metaphors)

**Weaknesses:** False patterns (apophenia), analogies can mislead if not validated

### 6. ⚖️ Critical Thinking — Evaluating Validity, Logic, and Evidence

**Definition:**  
Rigorously analyzing arguments, claims, and evidence. Questioning assumptions. Detecting fallacies. Objective evaluation rather than accepting at face value.

**When to Use:**

- Evaluating arguments (debates, proposals)
- Fact-checking and media literacy
- Scientific research (peer review)
- Legal reasoning
- Avoiding manipulation (propaganda, advertising)
- Making important decisions

**Key Skills:**

1. **Argument Analysis:**
   - Identify premises and conclusions
   - Evaluate logical structure
   - Check validity and soundness

2. **Evidence Evaluation:**
   - Quality of sources (primary vs secondary, credibility)
   - Sample size and representativeness
   - Correlation vs causation
   - Confounding variables

3. **Assumption Identification:**
   - What's unstated but necessary?
   - Challenge hidden assumptions
   - Surface implicit beliefs

4. **Bias Detection:**
   - Confirmation bias (seeking supporting evidence)
   - Motivated reasoning (believing what you want)
   - Availability bias (overweighting recent/vivid info)
   - Authority bias (trusting experts uncritically)

5. **Logical Fallacies Recognition:**
   - **Ad Hominem:** Attacking person, not argument
   - **Straw Man:** Misrepresenting opponent's position
   - **False Dichotomy:** Only two options (when more exist)
   - **Slippery Slope:** A leads to B leads to catastrophe (without evidence)
   - **Appeal to Authority:** "Expert said so" (when irrelevant)
   - **Hasty Generalization:** Conclusion from insufficient evidence
   - **Post Hoc:** "After, therefore because of"

**Frameworks:**

**1. Socratic Method:**

- Ask clarifying questions
- Probe assumptions
- Examine implications
- Consider alternative perspectives

**2. Scientific Method:**

- Hypothesis → Prediction → Test → Revise
- Falsifiability (can be proven wrong?)
- Reproducibility

**3. Bayesian Updating:**

- Start with prior belief (probability)
- Observe evidence
- Update belief (posterior probability)
- Continuous refinement

**Examples:**

**Claim:** "This diet cured my illness"  
**Critical Thinking:**

- Was there a control group?
- Correlation or causation?
- Placebo effect?
- Regression to the mean?
- Confirmation bias?

**Headline:** "Study shows X causes Y"  
**Questions:**

- Sample size? Representative?
- P-value and statistical significance?
- Who funded study? (Conflicts of interest)
- Other variables considered?

**Strengths:** Avoids errors, detects manipulation, rigorous reasoning

**Weaknesses:** Can be slow, analysis paralysis, may seem overly skeptical

### 7. 🔍 Analogical Thinking — Learning from Similar Situations

**Definition:**  
Reasoning by analogy. Using knowledge from one domain to solve problems in another. "This situation is like X, therefore we can apply Y."

**When to Use:**

- New, unfamiliar problems
- Learning new concepts (map to known)
- Explaining complex ideas
- Strategic planning (learn from history)
- Design and innovation (biomimicry)

**Structure of Analogy:**

**Source Domain (Known)** → **Target Domain (Unknown)**

**Mapping:**

1. Identify structural similarities
2. Map elements (A in source = B in target)
3. Transfer relationships
4. Generate inferences about target

**Types of Analogies:**

1. **Structural Analogies:**
   - Map relationships, not surface features
   - Atom like solar system (nucleus = sun, electrons = planets)
   - Poor analogy (electrons don't orbit), but useful for learning

2. **Problem-Solution Analogies:**
   - Source problem + solution → Target problem + solution
   - Military strategy → Business strategy (attack, defense, alliances)

3. **Cross-Domain Analogies:**
   - Biology → Engineering (biomimicry)
   - Music → Architecture (rhythm, harmony, structure)

**Famous Examples:**

**Rutherford's Atomic Model:**

- Source: Solar system
- Target: Atom
- Mapping: Sun = Nucleus, Planets = Electrons
- (Later refined, but useful starting point)

**Kekulé's Benzene Structure:**

- Dream of snake biting tail (ouroboros)
- Insight: Benzene is ring structure (not chain)

**Wright Brothers:**

- Source: Birds adjusting wing tips for balance
- Target: Airplane wing warping for control
- Key to controlled flight

**Business Strategy:**

- Military principles (Sun Tzu) → Corporate strategy
- Chess → Strategic thinking
- Evolutionary biology → Market competition

**Framework: Gentner's Structure-Mapping Theory:**

1. **Attributes:** Surface features (less important)
2. **Relations:** Connections between elements (crucial)
3. **Higher-order relations:** Relations between relations (most important)

**Good analogies:** Match higher-order relations  
**Bad analogies:** Only match surface features

**Strengths:** Accelerates learning, generates novel solutions, explanatory power

**Weaknesses:** Analogies can mislead (false equivalence), must verify applicability

### 8. 🕵️ Abductive Thinking — Inferring the Best Explanation

**Definition:**  
Reasoning to the most likely explanation. "Given these observations, what's the best explanation?" Different from deduction (certain) and induction (probabilistic generalization). Sherlock Holmes uses abduction.

**When to Use:**

- Diagnosis (medical, technical)
- Root cause analysis
- Detective work (solving mysteries)
- Scientific hypothesis formation
- Troubleshooting systems
- Understanding human behavior (theory of mind)

**Three Types of Reasoning:**

1. **Deduction (Certainty):**
   - General → Specific
   - "All men are mortal; Socrates is a man; Therefore, Socrates is mortal"
   - If premises true, conclusion must be true

2. **Induction (Probability):**
   - Specific → General
   - "Swan 1 is white, Swan 2 is white, ... Swan N is white; Therefore, all swans are white"
   - Probabilistic (can be wrong—black swans exist)

3. **Abduction (Best Explanation):**
   - Observation → Hypothesis
   - "The lawn is wet; The best explanation is that it rained"
   - Inference to best explanation (could be sprinkler, dew, etc.)

**Abductive Process:**

1. **Observe surprising fact:** X
2. **Generate hypotheses:** If H were true, X would be expected
3. **Evaluate hypotheses:** Which H best explains X?
4. **Accept provisionally:** H is plausible (not certain)
5. **Test:** Gather more evidence

**Criteria for "Best" Explanation:**

1. **Explanatory Power:** Explains the observations
2. **Simplicity (Occam's Razor):** Fewest assumptions
3. **Consistency:** Fits with other knowledge
4. **Testability:** Can be verified/falsified
5. **Fruitfulness:** Suggests new avenues of inquiry

**Examples:**

**Medical Diagnosis:**

- **Symptoms:** Fever, cough, fatigue
- **Hypotheses:** Flu, cold, COVID-19, pneumonia
- **Best explanation:** COVID-19 (given prevalence, symptom match, exposure)
- **Action:** Test to confirm

**Sherlock Holmes:**

- **Observation:** Mud on boots (specific type), cigar ash (specific brand), watch (scratches near keyhole)
- **Abduction:** Person came from location X, smokes brand Y, drinks heavily (shaky hands)

**Car Won't Start:**

- **Observation:** Engine turns over but won't catch
- **Hypotheses:** Out of gas, bad spark plugs, fuel pump failure, clogged fuel filter
- **Best:** Out of gas (simplest, most common)
- **Check:** Fuel gauge

**Scientific Discovery:**

- Darwin: Observed finch beak variations
- Abduction: Natural selection explains adaptation to different food sources
- (Then tested via additional observations)

**Bayesian Connection:**

- Abduction = Bayesian inference
- Prior probabilities + Evidence → Posterior probabilities
- Most likely hypothesis = highest posterior

**Strengths:** Practical, generates testable hypotheses, handles incomplete information

**Weaknesses:** Not certain (can be wrong), requires good hypothesis generation, biased by priors

### 9. 🏗️ First Principles Thinking — Breaking Problems to Fundamentals

**Definition:**  
Reasoning from foundational truths rather than by analogy or convention. Strip away assumptions, get to bedrock, rebuild from there. Aristotle: "The first basis from which a thing is known."

**When to Use:**

- Innovation (creating something new)
- Challenging industry norms
- Escaping conventional wisdom
- Cost reduction (question every assumption)
- Complex problems with many layers of assumptions

**Process:**

1. **Identify the Problem or Goal**
2. **Break Down to First Principles:**
   - What are the fundamental truths?
   - What do we know for certain?
   - Strip away all assumptions
3. **Question Assumptions:**
   - "Why do we do it this way?"
   - "Must it be done this way?"
   - "What if we started from scratch?"
4. **Rebuild from Fundamentals:**
   - Construct solution using only verified truths
   - Recombine in novel ways

**Elon Musk's Battery Example:**

**Conventional Wisdom:** "Battery packs are expensive, cost $600/kWh historically"

**First Principles:**

1. What are batteries made of?
   - Cobalt, nickel, aluminum, carbon, polymers for separation, steel casing
2. What's the cost of these materials on commodity markets?
   - ~$80/kWh
3. Insight: Batteries should cost ~$80/kWh, not $600/kWh
4. Action: Tesla vertical integration (produce batteries, don't buy markup)

**SpaceX Rocket Example:**

**Conventional:** "Rockets cost tens/hundreds of millions; buy from Boeing/Lockheed"

**First Principles:**

1. What's a rocket made of?
   - Aerospace-grade aluminum, titanium, copper, carbon fiber
2. Cost of raw materials?
   - ~2% of typical rocket price
3. Insight: Can build rocket for fraction of cost
4. Action: SpaceX in-house manufacturing, reusable rockets
5. Result: 90%+ cost reduction

**Other Examples:**

**Airbnb:**

- **Conventional:** Hotels have rooms, lobbies, staff
- **First Principles:** People need place to sleep; people have spare rooms
- **Innovation:** Peer-to-peer lodging marketplace

**Uber:**

- **Conventional:** Taxis have medallions, radio dispatch
- **First Principles:** People need rides; people have cars and time
- **Innovation:** Ride-sharing platform

**Amazon:**

- **Conventional:** Retail needs physical stores
- **First Principles:** Customers need products delivered; internet enables ordering
- **Innovation:** E-commerce

**Comparison to Analogical Thinking:**

- **Analogical:** "X is like Y, so let's do what Y does" (reasoning by comparison)
- **First Principles:** "What's fundamentally true? Build from there" (reasoning from axioms)

**When to Use Each:**

- **Analogy:** Incremental improvement, familiar domains, fast decisions
- **First Principles:** Breakthrough innovation, challenging orthodoxy, willing to invest time

**Strengths:** Breakthrough innovation, escapes conventional thinking, reduces costs

**Weaknesses:** Time-intensive, requires deep knowledge, can reinvent wheels (sometimes analogies are correct)

### 10. 🔀 Inverted Thinking — Solving by Negation and Inversion

**Definition:**  
Approaching problems backwards. Instead of "How to succeed?", ask "How to fail?" Then avoid those failure modes. Popularized by Charlie Munger: "Invert, always invert."

**When to Use:**

- Risk management (identify what could go wrong)
- Strategy (avoid fatal mistakes)
- Personal development (identify bad habits to avoid)
- Due diligence (pre-mortem: assume failure, work backwards)
- Problem-solving when stuck on direct approach

**Key Techniques:**

1. **Inversion:**
   - Flip the question
   - "How to be miserable?" → Don't do those things
   - "How to destroy company?" → Avoid those actions

2. **Pre-Mortem:**
   - Assume project failed
   - "Why did it fail?"
   - Identify risks proactively
   - Plan mitigations

3. **Negative Space:**
   - Define by what it's NOT
   - Sculpture: Removing material reveals form
   - Writing: Cut unnecessary words

4. **Contrapositive Logic:**
   - "If A, then B" ≡ "If not B, then not A"
   - Often easier to prove contrapositive

5. **Elimination:**
   - Sherlock Holmes: "Eliminate the impossible; whatever remains, however improbable, must be the truth"
   - Process of elimination

**Charlie Munger's Examples:**

**Question:** "How to have a happy marriage?"  
**Inversion:** "How to have a miserable marriage?"

- Be unreliable
- Lie frequently
- Be selfish
- Don't communicate
  **Actionable:** Do the opposite

**Question:** "How to be successful investor?"  
**Inversion:** "How to guarantee investment losses?"

- Buy high, sell low
- Follow the herd
- Trade frequently (fees)
- Ignore fundamentals
- Use excessive leverage
  **Actionable:** Avoid these

**Business Examples:**

**Question:** "How to grow revenue?"  
**Inversion:** "How to lose all customers?"

- Deliver poor quality
- Ignore customer service
- Break promises
- Charge premium with no value
  **Insight:** Don't do these (seems obvious, but often violated)

**Project Planning:**
**Pre-Mortem:** "It's one year from now. Project failed spectacularly. Why?"

- Underestimated time by 3x
- Key person quit
- Technology didn't work
- Ran out of budget
- Misunderstood customer needs
  **Action:** Address each risk proactively

**Personal Development:**

**Question:** "How to be productive?"  
**Inversion:** "How to waste time?"

- Check social media every 5 minutes
- No priorities (do whatever feels urgent)
- Say yes to everything
- Multitask constantly
- Never say no
  **Insight:** Eliminate time-wasters

**Via Negativa (Nassim Taleb):**

- Improvement by removal, not addition
- "Don't smoke" > "Take vitamin supplements"
- Avoid harm > Seek gain
- **Subtractive epistemology:** Knowledge = What's NOT true (eliminate false beliefs)

**Mathematical Inversion:**

**Multiplication:** 7 × 8 = 56 (forward)  
**Division:** 56 ÷ 7 = 8 (inverse operation)

**Proof by Contradiction:**

- Assume opposite of what you want to prove
- Show it leads to contradiction
- Therefore, original statement is true

**Strengths:** Uncovers blind spots, risk management, often easier than direct approach

**Weaknesses:** Can be negative focus, doesn't tell you what TO do (only what NOT to do)

## 💯 The "1 More" Mindset — Elite Performance Habit

### The 100-Hour Rule

**Core Principle:**  
_"Spend 100 focused hours on anything, and you will be better than 95% of people."_

**Why It Works:**

- Most quit before they become competent
- 100 hours is achievable (14 weeks × 7 hours/week, or ~3 months)
- Crosses the valley of despair (initial incompetence → competence)
- Not mastery (10,000 hours), but solid competence

**The "1 More" Philosophy:**

Elite performers consistently do **one more**:

- **One more rep** (when muscles burn)
- **One more page** (when tired of reading)
- **One more attempt** (when problem seems unsolvable)
- **One more iteration** (when "good enough")
- **One more conversation** (when networking feels draining)

**Why "1 More" Matters:**

- Average people stop when tired
- Elite performers stop when done
- Compound effect over years = massive advantage

**"Most Quit Before They Become Competent"**

- Skill acquisition curve:
  1. Initial excitement
  2. Valley of despair (hard, no results yet)
  3. Competence (breakthrough)
  4. Plateau, then next level

**Application to Thinking:**

- One more angle (try another framework)
- One more question (probe deeper)
- One more iteration (refine solution)
- One more scenario (consider edge case)

**Average vs Elite:**

| Average                   | Elite                   |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Stops when tired          | Stops when done         |
| "Good enough"             | "One more iteration"    |
| Follows conventional path | Questions assumptions   |
| Single framework          | Multiple frameworks     |
| Gives up quickly          | Persists through valley |

## Framework Selection Guide — Which Thinking Mode for Which Problem?

### The Meta-Skill: Choosing the Right Framework

**Question:** "I have a hammer (framework). Should I use it?"

**Answer:** Depends on the nail (problem).

### Selection Matrix:

| Problem Type                                   | Best Framework(s)                      | Why                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Generating options**                         | Divergent                              | Need many possibilities                             |
| **Making a decision**                          | Convergent                             | Need to narrow to one                               |
| **Stuck in conventional thinking**             | Lateral                                | Need creative leap                                  |
| **Complex system with many interacting parts** | Systemic                               | Understand interconnections                         |
| **Learning new concept**                       | Associative, Analogical                | Connect to known patterns                           |
| **Evaluating claim or argument**               | Critical                               | Test validity                                       |
| **New, unfamiliar problem**                    | Analogical                             | Learn from similar situations                       |
| **Diagnosis or root cause**                    | Abductive                              | Infer best explanation                              |
| **Challenging industry norms**                 | First Principles                       | Question all assumptions                            |
| **Risk management**                            | Inverted                               | Identify failure modes                              |
| **Innovation**                                 | First Principles + Lateral + Divergent | Break norms, generate novel ideas                   |
| **Strategy**                                   | Systemic + Inverted + Critical         | Understand system, avoid traps, evaluate rigorously |

## Combining Frameworks — The Power of Multi-Model Thinking

### Example Problem: "How to improve customer retention?"

**Step 1: Divergent Thinking**

- Brainstorm many possible solutions
  - Better onboarding
  - Loyalty program
  - Customer success team
  - Product improvements
  - Community building
  - Lower prices
  - Increase switching costs
  - Personalization

**Step 2: Systemic Thinking**

- Map the retention system
  - Onboarding quality → Early engagement → Value realization → Satisfaction → Renewal
  - Feedback loops: Happy customers → Referrals → More customers → Better product (economies of scale)
  - Identify leverage points: Onboarding (high impact, early in cycle)

**Step 3: Inverted Thinking**

- "How could we lose all customers?"
  - Poor onboarding (confusion, frustration)
  - Bugs and downtime
  - Ignoring support requests
  - Breaking promises
  - Inconsistent experience
- **Insight:** Fix these FIRST (via negativa)

**Step 4: Analogical Thinking**

- "How do successful companies retain customers?"
  - Apple: Ecosystem lock-in (hardware + software)
  - Amazon: Prime membership (upfront cost creates commitment)
  - Netflix: Content personalization (feels custom-made)
- **Transfer:** Can we create ecosystem or membership model?

**Step 5: First Principles**

- "Why do customers leave?"
  - Fundamental truth: They leave when value < cost
  - Cost: Money + Time + Effort + Opportunity cost
  - Value: Utility + Emotional benefit
- **Insight:** Increase value or decrease cost (or both)
  - Make product easier to use (reduce time/effort)
  - Add features that solve pain points (increase utility)

**Step 6: Critical Thinking**

- Evaluate proposed solutions
  - Do we have evidence this works?
  - Can we A/B test?
  - What's the ROI?
  - Are we solving real problem or symptom?

**Step 7: Convergent Thinking**

- Prioritize based on:
  - Impact (how much retention increase?)
  - Effort (how hard to implement?)
  - Risk (what could go wrong?)
- **Decision:** Improve onboarding (high impact, medium effort, low risk)

**Step 8: Abductive Thinking (Post-Implementation)**

- "Retention increased 15% after new onboarding"
- "Why?"
  - Better onboarding?
  - Or coincidental (market conditions, new product features)?
- **Inference:** Most likely onboarding (but run controlled test to confirm)

**Result:** Multi-framework approach yields robust solution, rigorously evaluated, considering system effects and risks.

## Common Thinking Traps to Avoid

### 1. **Single Framework Fixation**

- Using same framework for every problem
- "When you have a hammer, everything looks like a nail"
- **Solution:** Consciously vary frameworks

### 2. **Premature Convergence**

- Narrowing to solution before exploring options
- Kills creativity
- **Solution:** Separate divergent and convergent phases

### 3. **Analysis Paralysis**

- Too much critical thinking, no action
- Perfection seeking
- **Solution:** Set decision deadline, satisfice

### 4. **Analogical Seduction**

- Believing analogy proves something (it doesn't, it illustrates)
- False equivalence
- **Solution:** Validate analogies, test applicability

### 5. **First Principles Overkill**

- Questioning everything, rebuilding from scratch (wastes time)
- Not all problems require revolution
- **Solution:** Use first principles selectively (high-stakes, novel problems)

### 6. **Ignoring System Effects**

- Solving problem in isolation, creating bigger problem elsewhere
- Unintended consequences
- **Solution:** Always apply systemic thinking lens
