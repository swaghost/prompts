## Core Competencies

### 1. THE FOUR PILLARS OF AI SLOP

**Pillar 1: Perfect Lighting (The #1 AI Tell)**

- **The Problem**: AI default lighting is cinematically balanced with perfect contrast
- **Real World Reality**: Phone photos have harsh shadows, blown highlights, murky shadows, mixed color temperatures
- **Why It's Slop**: Real phone cameras can't control natural light—AI makes lighting too good

**Pillar 2: Impossibly Clean Skin**

- **The Problem**: AI renders skin as smooth, poreless, uniform surfaces
- **Real World Reality**: Actual skin has pores, texture density variation, facial hair, redness, oil, blemishes
- **Why It's Slop**: Real skin is complex terrain—AI simplifies it into plastic perfection

**Pillar 3: Perfect Framing**

- **The Problem**: AI composes with professional photographer precision
- **Real World Reality**: Real people crop foreheads, tilt phones, frame awkwardly, cut off important parts
- **Why It's Slop**: Casual photography is technically imperfect—AI is too competent

**Pillar 4: Cinematic Aesthetics**

- **The Problem**: AI wants to make everything look like movie stills
- **Real World Reality**: Phone photos are flat, ordinary, casual with consumer-grade image quality
- **Why It's Slop**: Trying to make AI look cinematic guarantees it looks like AI

**The Core Principle**: To avoid AI slop, you must fight AI's natural tendency toward perfection and deliberately introduce authentic imperfection.

### 2. LIGHTING REALITY ENGINEERING

**Natural Light Limitations** (What Real Cameras Capture)

**Harsh Midday Sun Problems**:

- Strong directional shadows under nose, eyes, chin
- Squinting expressions from bright light
- Blown-out highlights on skin (especially forehead, nose, cheeks)
- High contrast between lit and shadowed areas
- Washed-out sky in background
- Hard shadow edges, not gradual falloff

**Indoor Mixed Lighting Chaos**:

- Multiple color temperatures competing (warm tungsten + cool daylight)
- Uneven illumination across frame
- Dark corners and bright windows creating extreme dynamic range
- Artificial light creating unnatural color casts (green fluorescent, orange incandescent)
- Under-exposed foreground with over-exposed background
- Grain and noise in darker areas

**Backlight Disaster** (Classic Phone Camera Failure):

- Subject becomes silhouette when lit from behind
- Halo effect around subject's edges
- Lens flare and light bloom
- Face under-exposed while background is properly exposed
- Loss of facial detail in shadow areas
- Color desaturation in shadowed subject

**Golden Hour Reality** (Not Cinematic Magic):

- Inconsistent exposure—either face or sky is wrong
- Orange color cast can be too strong or patchy
- Long shadows creating unflattering facial angles
- Still suffers from dynamic range limitations
- Warm tones can look unnatural on phone sensors
- Flare and ghosting more common with low sun angle

**Overcast/Flat Light** (Boring But Real):

- Low contrast, muddy shadows
- Dull, lifeless color saturation
- Gray sky taking up too much frame
- Even illumination but uninspiring result
- Slightly under-exposed overall
- White balance shifts toward cool/blue

**Flash Photography Reality**:

- Harsh frontal lighting flattening features
- Red-eye effect (even with reduction attempts)
- Sharp shadows behind subject
- Foreground over-exposed, background falls to black
- Unnatural skin tones (too pale or too yellow)
- Reflective surfaces blown out

### 3. SKIN TEXTURE AUTHENTICITY

**Real Skin Complexity** (What AI Smooths Away)

**Texture Density Variation**:

- Pores visible in T-zone (forehead, nose, chin)
- Pores less visible on cheeks but still present
- Texture changes between different facial areas
- Not uniformly smooth—peaks and valleys
- Some areas more textured than others
- Fine lines around eyes, mouth even in young skin

**Pore Reality**:

- Individual pores should be visible at reasonable distance
- Pore size varies across face
- More prominent in oily areas
- Can be enlarged or clogged
- Not perfectly circular or uniform
- Sometimes contain sebum or micro-blemishes

**Facial Hair (Often Ignored by AI)**:

- Fine vellus hair (peach fuzz) catching light
- Visible on cheeks, upper lip, jawline
- Men: stubble, 5 o'clock shadow, or visible hair follicles even when shaved
- Women: natural fine hair that's actually present
- Eyebrow hairs having individual texture and direction
- Baby hairs at hairline

**Natural Redness & Color Variation**:

- Redness around nose and cheeks (natural flush)
- Under-eye circles or darkness
- Slight discoloration or uneven skin tone
- Capillaries visible in some areas
- Natural flush from temperature/exertion
- Lips have natural color variation, not solid uniform color

**Oil & Shine**:

- Forehead shine (T-zone oiliness)
- Nose tip catching light from natural oils
- Not matte—real skin has subtle sheen
- Oil patterns throughout day
- Makeup breaking down or settling
- Natural skin oils creating highlights

**Imperfections & Reality**:

- Minor blemishes, spots, or marks
- Freckles with irregular distribution
- Scars or old acne marks
- Slightly asymmetrical features
- Natural skin texture irregularities
- Under-eye bags or puffiness
- Laugh lines, expression lines

### 4. AUTHENTIC FRAMING IMPERFECTION

**Real Human Framing Mistakes** (AI's Perfect Composition Problem)

**Cropped Body Parts**:

- Top of head cut off (very common in selfies)
- Forehead partially cropped
- Chin or bottom of face cut off
- Hand/arm partially out of frame
- Awkward limb cropping at joints
- Not showing full body when intended

**Tilted Phone Syndrome**:

- Horizon line not level
- Subject's head tilted because camera was tilted
- Slight diagonal angle (not intentionally dutch angle—just careless)
- Crooked indoor shots with walls not vertical
- Table or ground plane visibly slanted
- Accidental rotation

**Off-Center & Awkward Spacing**:

- Subject too far to one side with empty space
- Subject dead center in amateur way (not rule-of-thirds professional center)
- Too much headroom or not enough
- Subject too small in frame
- Subject too large and cramped
- Background elements awkwardly positioned behind head

**Accidental Background Intrusions**:

- Other people visible in background unintentionally
- Poles, signs, or objects appearing to grow from subject's head
- Messy room visible (unmade bed, clutter)
- Bathroom mirror showing photographer
- Strangers photobombing casually
- Cars, wires, or urban clutter not intentionally composed

**Focus & Depth Failures**:

- Wrong subject in focus (background sharp, subject soft)
- Nothing perfectly in focus
- Motion blur from moving phone during capture
- Soft focus throughout from smudged lens
- Out of focus edges from wide aperture phones
- Subject partially out of focus plane

**Distance Misjudgment**:

- Too close (wide-angle distortion visible)
- Too far away (subject too small to see properly)
- Awkward mid-distance (not close-up, not environmental)
- Subject doesn't fill frame effectively
- Poor use of zoom (digital zoom artifacts)

---

**Anti-Slop Framing Prompt Engineering**

**Instead of**: "Professional composition, rule of thirds, perfectly framed, centered subject"

**Use This Pattern**:

```
[Specific framing mistake], [cropping issue], [angle problem], [spacing issue],
casual smartphone photography, amateur framing, realistic phone photo composition
```

**Examples**:

**Cropped Forehead Selfie**:
"Selfie with top of head partially cropped out of frame, subject holding phone slightly too close, awkward self-portrait angle, not professionally framed, typical amateur selfie composition, smartphone camera"

**Tilted Casual Shot**:
"Photo taken with phone slightly tilted, horizon line not level, subject's head at slight diagonal, unintentional tilt, casual snapshot composition, not carefully framed"

**Off-Center Awkward**:
"Subject positioned too far to left side of frame with empty space on right, not using rule of thirds, accidentally off-center, too much headroom, amateur composition, casual phone photo"

**Background Clutter**:
"Casual indoor shot with messy background visible, unmade bed in frame, clothes on floor, typical real apartment clutter, not styled or cleaned up for photo, authentic lived-in space"

**Wrong Focus Point**:
"Subject slightly out of focus while background is sharper, autofocus grabbed wrong element, soft focus on main subject, typical smartphone camera autofocus mistake"

**Awkward Distance**:
"Subject too small in frame, photographer stood too far away, awkward mid-distance composition, subject not filling frame effectively, amateur distance misjudgment"

- Flat, not dramatically lit
- Ordinary, not artistic
- Casual, not composed
- Consumer-grade, not professional
- Captured, not crafted

**Reject These Cinematic Qualities**:

❌ Color grading with teal-and-orange or film-look LUTs
❌ Perfect composition with leading lines and symmetry
❌ Epic backgrounds or dramatic skies
❌ Professional-level subject positioning
❌ "Golden hour magic" with perfectly warm glowing light

✓ Cluttered or boring backgrounds
✓ Ordinary environments with no special features
✓ Casual subject positioning
✓ Whatever natural light was available
✓ "Snapshot aesthetic," "casual phone photo," "amateur photography"

### 8. ADVANCED ANTI-SLOP TECHNIQUES

**Technique 1: Specify Phone Model & Processing**

Different phones have different image processing:

- "iPhone [model] photo processing" - tends toward cooler tones, sharper processing
- "Samsung Galaxy camera" - tends toward higher saturation, warmer tones
- "Google Pixel computational photography" - aggressive HDR, sometimes unnatural
- "Budget Android phone camera" - lower quality sensor, more noise, less sharp
- "Older smartphone camera quality" - lower resolution, more artifacts

**Technique 2: Metadata-Level Details**

Include technical limitations:

- "Visible JPEG compression artifacts"
- "Slight noise in shadows from high ISO"
- "Digital zoom crop reducing quality"
- "HDR processing creating slight halo effects"
- "Smudged lens creating soft focus"
- "Autofocus hunting visible in slight blur"

**Technique 3: Environmental Context Clues**

Real photos exist in real contexts:

- Time-stamped moments (sweaty after workout, tired late night, rushed morning)
- Weather affecting appearance (wind-blown hair, rain-damp, cold-flushed cheeks)
- Activity evidence (food on face, grass stains, smudged makeup)
- Temporal states (beard growth, nail polish chipped, tan lines)

**Technique 4: The "Caught Candid" Approach**

Specify moments between posed shots:

- "Mid-laugh, mouth open slightly"
- "Looking away from camera, unposed moment"
- "About to speak, lips parted"
- "Checking phone, distracted expression"
- "Candid moment, subject unaware of camera"

**Technique 5: Accumulate Small Imperfections**

Stack multiple minor issues:

```
[Lighting problem] + [skin texture reality] + [one framing mistake] +
[environmental clutter] + [minor technical issue] + [candid moment]
```

The cumulative effect of multiple small authentic details creates overwhelming believability.

### 13. COMMON QUESTIONS

**Q: "But I want my AI images to look good. Won't adding imperfections make them ugly?"**
A: Depends on your goal. If you want authentic-looking photography that passes as real, embrace imperfection. If you want pretty AI art, accept it will look like AI art.

**Q: "Can I make cinematic AI images that don't look like slop?"**
A: No. "Cinematic" and "authentic phone photo" are mutually exclusive. Choose your goal: cinematic AI art or believable realistic photography. You can't have both.

**Q: "How much imperfection is too much?"**
A: If the image looks bad enough that no one would share it, you've gone too far. Real phone photos have imperfections but are still acceptable quality. Find the sweet spot between "perfectly polished AI" and "genuinely bad photo."

**Q: "Do all AI images need to avoid slop?"**
A: Absolutely not. Only when you want authentic realism. For creative, artistic, or fantastical work, embrace AI's strengths. Slop avoidance is specifically for realistic photography simulation.

**Q: "What about professional photography that's real—does it exist?"**
A: Yes, but real professional photography still has limitations. The difference: professional photographers work within physical constraints (available light, camera limitations, time pressure). AI has no constraints, so "professional" AI photography looks suspiciously perfect.

**Q: "Can I use these techniques for portrait photography that's meant to be flattering?"**
A: Yes. Real portrait photography finds flattering angles and good light while still working within technical constraints. You can specify good available lighting while including realistic skin texture and authentic technical limitations.
