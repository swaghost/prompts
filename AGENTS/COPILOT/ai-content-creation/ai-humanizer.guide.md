# AI Humanizer Guide

Use this guide as a final editorial pass after the content's strategy, facts, and structure are already sound. The goal is not to make writing casual, imperfect, or artificially "human." The goal is to remove predictable patterns and restore a clear point of view.

## The Ten Fingerprints

### 1. The Em Dash Epidemic

One em dash is normal punctuation. Several in a paragraph can become a fingerprint. Replace some with a period, comma, colon, or a sentence rewrite. Keep an em dash when the pause is genuinely useful.

### 2. The Sparkle

Watch for decorative enthusiasm, stacked adjectives, and emphasis in every sentence. Cut the decoration until the strongest sentence can stand by itself.

### 3. Unlock and Game-Changer

These phrases are usually vague claims. Replace them with the exact capability, result, or change. Name what became possible, faster, cheaper, clearer, or different.

### 4. The Contrast Crutch

Limit repeated constructions such as "It's not just X, it's Y." Use the sharper idea directly unless the contrast is essential to the argument.

### 5. Academic Connectors

In trust-oriented or conversational writing, remove habitual connectors such as "Moreover," "Furthermore," and "Additionally." Use a natural transition, a short sentence, or no connector.

### 6. The Frictionless Tone

Prose becomes suspicious when every sentence flows smoothly, agrees politely, and has no texture. Add a real judgment, limitation, doubt, concrete example, or natural pause when it reflects the writer's experience.

### 7. The Over-Explanation

Ask whether the point can survive in two lines. Delete repeated setup, obvious interpretation, and conclusions that merely restate the paragraph. Keep explanation when the reader truly needs it.

### 8. Forced Analogies

Delete metaphors that need a paragraph of explanation or do not make the idea easier to see. Prefer a concrete example over a decorative comparison.

### 9. Phrase Recycling

Scan for repeated sentence shapes, transitions, and paragraph endings. Combine duplicate ideas and vary structure only where the meaning remains precise. Do not use random synonyms to hide repetition.

### 10. No Point of View

Information alone does not create authority. Add the writer's judgment, preference, risk, stake, or belief when one exists. Never invent a personal experience or opinion; mark the passage as neutral when no point of view is available.

## Editing Sequence

1. Read the draft aloud once without editing.
2. Mark repeated patterns, not isolated words.
3. Delete bloat before adding personality.
4. Replace vague claims with concrete details or outcomes.
5. Add the writer's real judgment, tension, or stake where supported.
6. Re-read for voice consistency and factual accuracy.
7. Stop when the writing sounds natural, not when every sentence contains a stylistic quirk.

## Final Check

- Are repeated em dashes doing real work?
- Does every enthusiastic phrase earn its place?
- Are claims such as "unlock" or "game-changer" concrete?
- Is the contrast structure occasional rather than automatic?
- Do transitions sound like this writer?
- Is there enough texture to avoid press-release smoothness?
- Can any explanation be cut without losing meaning?
- Does each analogy clarify immediately?
- Are repeated sentence shapes hiding a loop?
- Does the piece contain a defensible point of view?

Humanization is successful when the reader notices the idea and the person behind it, not the editing technique.
