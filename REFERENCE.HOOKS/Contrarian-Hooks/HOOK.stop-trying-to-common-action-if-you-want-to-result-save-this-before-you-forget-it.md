# Stop trying to [COMMON ACTION] if you want to [RESULT]. Save this before you forget it.

## Hook Group

Contrarian Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as someone who questions conventional wisdom and offers counterintuitive insight that challenges their current approach. This hook positions the creator as an authority figure who has identified flawed conventional methods and possesses superior alternative strategies.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations

**Emotional:** Curiosity mixed with defensiveness, followed by openness to alternative perspectives. Creates mild anxiety about current methods being ineffective, then relief that better approaches exist.

**Intellectual:** Cognitive dissonance as the viewer reconciles their current practices with the possibility they've been misguided. Activates critical thinking about why common actions might be counterproductive.

**Psychological:** Pattern interruption that disrupts automatic thinking. Triggers reassessment of learned behaviors and creates receptivity to new mental models.

**Physical:** Attention spike manifesting as forward lean, pause in scrolling behavior, possible note-taking or screenshot action.

## Psychology

This hook leverages **reactance theory** and **contrarian positioning** to create immediate engagement through cognitive disruption. When someone states that a common practice should be stopped, it triggers multiple psychological responses simultaneously.

The template works because it directly challenges the viewer's **status quo bias**—our tendency to prefer current states and resist change. By explicitly telling viewers to "stop" something they're already doing, it creates productive tension. This isn't merely suggesting an alternative; it's declaring the current path wrong, which demands attention and justification.

The phrase "if you want to [RESULT]" adds **conditional framing** that makes the contrarian advice feel less like opinion and more like cause-and-effect reality. It implies: "Continue your current behavior and fail, or adopt this alternative and succeed." This binary framing activates **loss aversion**—the fear of missing desired results becomes more motivating than the promise of gaining them.

Adding "Save this before you forget it" creates **urgency** and **perceived value**. It implies the information is so counterintuitive and valuable that our normal memory can't be trusted to retain it. This metacognitive nudge suggests the content will challenge deeply ingrained patterns that our brain might resist or rationalize away.

The hook activates **the Einstellung effect**—when familiar patterns prevent better solutions from being recognized. By explicitly stating that common actions are blocking results, it gives permission to abandon learned approaches that viewers may have already sensed weren't working optimally.

There's also powerful **social proof reversal** at work. Instead of saying "everyone does this," it says "everyone does this wrong." This repositions the majority as misguided rather than wise, allowing viewers to see themselves as part of an enlightened minority if they engage with the alternative approach.

The template creates **cognitive curiosity loops**—viewers need to know which common action is problematic and what alternative approach exists. This dual gap (what to stop + what to do instead) makes scrolling past psychologically difficult.

For viewers already frustrated with their progress, this hook provides **attribution relief**—it's not that they lack ability or effort, it's that they've been following misguided conventional wisdom. This reframing protects ego while motivating change.

The "save this" element also triggers **implementation intentions**—by suggesting the viewer should preserve the information, it primes them to not just consume but apply the contrarian insight, increasing the likelihood of behavioral change and deeper engagement.

## Example

"Stop trying to post consistently if you want to grow your audience. Save this before you forget it."

This challenges the universal advice to "post every day," suggesting that consistency without quality or strategy may be counterproductive. It gives frustrated creators permission to abandon exhausting posting schedules that aren't yielding results.

## Why it works

Works because it gives language and permission to doubt widely accepted practices that may not be delivering promised results. Humans crave contrarian insights that explain why their standard efforts haven't succeeded—it absolves them of failure while providing a new path forward. The combination of challenging convention with actionable correction creates both intellectual satisfaction and practical value.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

Fails when the contrarian position is contrarian for contrarian's sake without legitimate merit, or when the common action being challenged is actually effective for most people. If the alternative approach requires significantly more effort or resources than the common action, viewers feel deceived. Also fails if the creator doesn't clearly articulate what should be done instead, leaving viewers with criticism but no alternative.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

Can backfire by creating cynicism toward all advice, including sound conventional wisdom that actually works. May attract perpetual contrarians who resist any guidance, not just flawed conventional approaches. The "stop trying" framing could be misinterpreted as discouragement rather than strategic redirection. For viewers who have invested significant time in the challenged common action, the hook can trigger defensive resistance rather than openness, causing them to dismiss the content entirely to protect their prior investments.
