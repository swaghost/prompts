# Stop Trying to [Common Action] If You Want to [Result]

## Hook Group

Contrarian Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should feel that they've been following conventional wisdom that's actually counterproductive. This hook positions you as someone willing to challenge the status quo and offer a fresh perspective that goes against popular advice.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

- **Intellectual:** Cognitive dissonance - questioning their current approach
- **Emotional:** Relief (they haven't been failing, just following bad advice), curiosity, validation
- **Psychological:** Pattern interrupt, authority recognition, openness to alternative methods
- **Physical:** Pause scrolling, lean in, prepare to reassess current strategies

## Psychology

The mind pays attention to contrarian statements because they challenge existing beliefs and create cognitive dissonance. When someone has been struggling despite "doing everything right," a contrarian perspective offers hope that there's a better way. This hook exploits the gap between effort and results, suggesting that conventional wisdom may be the problem, not the person's execution.

## Example

"Stop trying to post consistently if you want to grow your audience."

## Why it works

This hook works because it directly challenges widely accepted advice, creating immediate curiosity about what alternative approach you're suggesting. It positions conventional wisdom as the obstacle rather than the solution, which resonates with people who have been following that advice without success. The contrast between "stop" and "if you want" creates tension that demands resolution. It also provides psychological relief by suggesting that failure wasn't due to personal inadequacy but to following flawed advice. The hook implies exclusive knowledge that goes against mainstream thinking, elevating your authority.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails if the contrarian take is too extreme or obviously wrong, making you seem ignorant rather than insightful. It can backfire if you don't quickly follow up with credible reasoning - leaving viewers angry at the contradiction without payoff. If the "common action" you're challenging isn't actually common or widely believed, the hook loses relevance. Overuse of contrarian hooks can make you seem like a perpetual contrarian without substance, eroding trust. The hook also fails if your alternative isn't meaningfully better or is just semantic wordplay rather than genuine strategic difference.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can alienate viewers who have found success with the traditional approach, making them feel attacked or dismissed. It can attract an audience that only seeks contrarian content without substance, leading to shallow engagement. The hook may position you as needlessly combative or arrogant, especially if the traditional advice has merit in certain contexts. It can create unrealistic expectations that your alternative approach is a magic bullet, leading to disappointment when nuance is required. Over-reliance on contrarian hooks can box you into always having to take opposing stances, limiting authentic content creation.
