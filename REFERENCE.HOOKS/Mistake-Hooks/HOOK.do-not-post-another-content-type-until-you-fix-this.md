# Do Not Post Another [Content Type] Until You Fix This

## Hook Group

Mistake Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should see you as someone issuing an urgent warning to prevent continued waste of effort. You're positioned as protective and directive, stopping them from repeating a critical error that's sabotaging their content.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

- **Intellectual:** Immediate halt and assessment, critical error identification, urgency recognition
- **Emotional:** Alarm (stop now), gratitude (caught before more waste), urgent correction desire
- **Psychological:** Pattern interrupt, loss aversion activation, immediate fix-seeking
- **Physical:** Literal stopping, full attention engagement, correction readiness

## Psychology

The directive "do not... until" creates a compelling stop-and-fix command that's hard to ignore. It suggests that continuing without the fix is wasteful at best, destructive at worst. This hook leverages loss aversion: the fear of wasting effort on content that's doomed to underperform. By positioning the fix as mandatory before proceeding, you elevate its importance to critical-path status. The urgency implies they're currently at a decision point where they could waste another post or fix it now.

## Example

"Do not post another video until you fix this: Your hook happens at second 3, but people decide to scroll at second 1. Every video is bleeding 70% of potential viewers in the first second. Move your hook to literally the first frame or accept that 7 out of 10 people will never see your value."

## Why it works

This hook works because it creates a decisive intervention moment - stop doing what you're doing until you fix this. The directive format feels protective rather than critical: you're preventing waste, not judging past work. By connecting the fix to all future content ("another"), you multiply the stakes - this isn't about one post, it's about every post going forward. The urgency is justified: every content piece created without the fix is a missed opportunity. It positions you as someone confident enough in this diagnosis to issue a stop order, demonstrating conviction.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails if the "fix" isn't actually critical, making your urgency seem exaggerated or manipulative. It can backfire if viewers have posted without the fix and feel attacked rather than helped. If the directive is too strong without justification, it feels pushy rather than protective. The hook loses credibility if the issue you identify isn't universally important for that content type. It also fails if viewers don't understand how to implement the fix, leaving them stuck rather than empowered.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can create paralysis where viewers are afraid to post anything without checking multiple critical factors. It may generate anxiety about content creation, making the process feel like navigating a minefield. The strong directive can come across as authoritarian rather than helpful, damaging relationship with audience. If overused, it creates boy-who-cried-wolf syndrome where viewers stop responding to urgency. The hook might also pressure perfection-seeking that prevents learning-through-doing, as viewers endlessly fix rather than ship and iterate.
