# Main character energy

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Ego humor.

## Example

Putting on sunglasses like I'm in a movie—main character energy.

## Why it works

Main-character hooks work by identifying moments when you feel like protagonist of your own life story, creating humor through self-awareness about treating mundane moments as cinematically significant. The phrase "main character energy" has become internet shorthand for feeling like or acting as if you're the central figure in a movie, where everything revolves around you and mundane actions take on narrative significance. This concept creates humor through recognition that we all have moments of feeling like our lives are performance or story worth documenting. The format celebrates rather than mocks these moments of self-importance, positioning them as fun indulgence rather than delusional narcissism. The structure works by identifying specific mundane actions that somehow feel cinematically significant when you're doing them. The example about putting on sunglasses captures a specific moment that can feel unexpectedly cool or movie-like: the simple act of sliding on sunglasses can make you feel like action hero or cool protagonist, especially with right context (walking away from something, stepping into sunlight, making entrance). The awareness that this is absurd (it's just sunglasses, you're not actually in a movie) doesn't diminish the feeling, creating humor through that gap between reality and subjective experience. The hook works because it validates moments of feeling special or significant, celebrating rather than diminishing those feelings. It builds community through recognition that we all have moments of feeling like main characters despite knowing we're not, creating permission to enjoy those feelings without shame. The self-aware framing keeps it from feeling like genuine narcissism while still allowing celebration of personal moments that feel significant.
