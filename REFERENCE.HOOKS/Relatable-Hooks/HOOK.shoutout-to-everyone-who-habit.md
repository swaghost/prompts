# Shoutout to everyone who [habit]

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Recognition + celebration.

## Example

Shoutout to everyone who starts 10 projects and finishes 0.

## Why it works

Recognition hooks work by explicitly celebrating or acknowledging groups defined by specific behaviors, creating belonging through being named and seen. The phrase "shoutout to" gives public recognition that validates the group's existence and experiences, making members feel acknowledged rather than invisible or alone. This format creates strong identification opportunity—if you match the description, you've been specifically called out for recognition, creating that "this is me!" feeling that drives engagement. The structure also normalizes behaviors that might create shame by treating them as worthy of celebration rather than hiding, transforming potential weaknesses into community identity markers. The format works particularly well for patterns that are common but typically hidden or not discussed, making the recognition feel particularly validating. The example about starting many projects but finishing none addresses specific pattern that creates shame for many people: enthusiastic project initiation driven by inspiration and optimism, followed by abandonment as challenges arise, interest wanes, or new ideas emerge. This pattern creates guilt (unfinished projects, broken commitments to yourself) and questioning whether you're capable of completion. Yet it's extremely common among creative people who generate ideas faster than they can execute them. The recognition validates that this pattern doesn't make you uniquely flaky but part of community of enthusiastic starters. The hook works because it creates belonging through recognition of patterns people often experience alone, transforming personal struggles into group identities. It celebrates rather than condemns these patterns, removing shame through positive framing that positions the behavior as characteristic worth claiming rather than flaw worth hiding.
