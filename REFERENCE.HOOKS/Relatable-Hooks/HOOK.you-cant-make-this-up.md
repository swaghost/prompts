# You can't make this up

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Absurdity relatability.

## Example

You can't make this up—my reel hit 1k views in 2 minutes, then froze for 3 days.

## Why it works

Absurdity hooks work by highlighting situations so bizarre or ironic that they seem invented, yet they actually happened, creating humor through the gap between expected and actual reality. The phrase "you can't make this up" emphasizes the story's authenticity despite its improbability—if someone were inventing a story, they wouldn't create something this specific or frustrating. This framing builds trust through apparent honesty about absurd realities rather than embellished tales. The structure also creates engagement through relat ability if others have experienced similar absurdities or curiosity if they haven't. The format works particularly well for frustrating situations where reality seems deliberately cruel or ironic. The example about a reel rapidly gaining traction then mysteriously freezing captures a specific creator frustration with social media algorithms: experiencing what seems like viral breakthrough (1,000 views in 2 minutes suggests strong momentum), only to have the algorithm apparently stop showing it to anyone for days. This creates frustration, confusion, and helplessness—you can't control or understand why momentum suddenly stopped. The specificity (exact numbers, exact timing) makes it feel authentic rather than exaggerated. The absurdity is that the same platform that pushed the content aggressively suddenly abandoned it for no visible reason. The hook works because it validates experiences with unpredictable systems that seem designed to frustrate, creating community among people dealing with similar absurd situations. It builds bonding through shared bewilderment at realities that are too bizarre to predict or prevent, creating humor through collective recognition that sometimes reality is absurdly disappointing in ways fiction wouldn't dare attempt.
