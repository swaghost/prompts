# Raise your hand if [thing you hate]

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Interactive engagement.

## Example

Raise your hand if you hate group projects.

## Why it works

Interactive-engagement hooks work by directly requesting physical or verbal participation, transforming passive viewing into active engagement that algorithms reward. The phrase "raise your hand if" is a participatory prompt borrowed from classroom and group settings, creating familiar call-and-response dynamic that people naturally want to complete. Even though viewers can't literally raise hands in most contexts, the phrase still creates impulse to respond through comments, reactions, or mental agreement. The format also serves as filtering mechanism—it identifies who shares the specific feeling or experience, building community among those who do while creating curiosity in those who don't about why others feel this way. The focus on negative shared experience ("hate") creates stronger bonding than positive experiences because complaining together is powerful social glue. The example about hating group projects is universally relatable for anyone who's been through school, as group projects are notoriously frustrating: unequal work distribution, scheduling conflicts, grade dependency on others' effort, social loafing, conflicting work styles, and getting credit or blame for collective output rather than individual contribution. The hook works because it invites collective catharsis around shared frustrations, creating community through commiseration. The participatory format drives comments (people explaining why they hate group projects, sharing nightmare experiences, or tagging friends who relate), which boosts algorithmic distribution. It positions the creator as someone who validates shared frustrations rather than toxic-positivity forcing, building trust through acknowledgment of genuinely annoying experiences.
