# If you know, you know

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Exclusivity bonding.

## Example

If you know, you know—closing 25 tabs just to reopen them all later.

## Why it works

Exclusive-knowledge hooks work by referencing experiences that require specific context or lived experience to fully appreciate, creating strong in-group bonding among those who understand while maintaining mystery for outsiders. The phrase "if you know, you know" (often abbreviated IYKYK) explicitly divides audience into insiders who immediately understand versus outsiders who lack the reference point. This creates pride and belonging among insiders through exclusive shared knowledge while making outsiders curious about what they're missing. The format celebrates insider status without requiring explanation, making the understanding itself proof of membership. The structure works well for behaviors or experiences that seem nonsensical without context but make perfect sense to those who've lived them. The example about closing browser tabs only to reopen them later captures specific digital behavior: periodically closing accumulated tabs to regain control and clear mental space, only to immediately reopen the same tabs because you actually do need them or can't let go of "I might need this later" thinking. This is both futile (you end up back where you started) and compulsive (you keep doing it anyway). People who do this immediately recognize the exact behavior and irrational logic, while others might find it bizarre. The hook works because it builds exclusive community through shared experiences that bond members without needing external validation or explanation. It positions certain knowledge as earned through experience rather than learnable through description, creating strong in-group identity. The format drives engagement as insiders confirm membership while outsiders request explanation, both valuable for algorithmic distribution and community building.
