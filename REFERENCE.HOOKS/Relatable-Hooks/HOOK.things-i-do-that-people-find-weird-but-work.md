# Things I do that people find weird (but work)

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Permission to be unconventional.

## Example

Things I do that people find weird: I wake up at 5am and journal for an hour.

## Why it works

Unconventional-methods hooks work by sharing approaches that deviate from norms but produce results, giving permission for viewers to experiment with unconventional methods if they're effective. The phrase "people find weird" acknowledges that these behaviors are outside normal patterns and might attract judgment or confusion, but the crucial addition "but work" validates them through results regardless of social approval. This structure creates permission to prioritize effectiveness over conformity. The hook also creates curiosity about what unusual practices the creator has adopted and why they're effective despite being unconventional. By openly sharing strange-seeming behaviors, the creator demonstrates confidence and authenticity—they're not performing normal but sharing their actual practices even when they're socially unusual. This vulnerability and honesty build trust. The example about waking at 5am to journal is effective because it's specific and detailed (not just "morning routine" but exact timing and activity) while being something many people might judge as extreme or unnecessary. However, early morning journaling has strong evidence supporting it: morning is when the mind is fresh and less cluttered, journaling provides clarity and emotional processing, and an hour allows deep reflection rather than rushed thoughts. The practice is "weird" only in that it requires discipline and prioritizes internal work over jumping into activity. The hook works because it challenges viewers to evaluate practices by results rather than social approval, which is liberating for people who've avoided unconventional but potentially effective approaches due to fear of judgment. It positions the creator as someone who prioritizes what works over what's comfortable or socially normal, building respect for their pragmatism.
