# POV: You think [thing] is easy… until you try

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Naïve expectation humor.

## Example

POV: You think making Reels is easy… until you try.

## Why it works

Assumption-correction POV hooks work by capturing the moment when easy assumptions meet difficult reality, creating humor through the gap between expectation and experience. The structure "you think [thing] is easy... until you try" perfectly captures the before/after realization that something is harder than it appears from the outside. The ellipsis creates pause that emphasizes the coming correction, building anticipation for the reality check. The word "until" marks the exact moment when naive confidence meets actual difficulty, creating that specific "oh no" feeling when you realize you underestimated something. The POV format makes it first-person immersive, placing viewers inside the experience of discovering unexpected difficulty. The example about making Reels (Instagram's short video format) is perfect because it looks deceptively simple when scrolling—just quick clips with music. But actually creating them involves multiple challenges: filming compelling content, syncing to audio beats, editing smoothly, creating hooks that stop scrolls, understanding trends, looking comfortable on camera, and somehow making it look effortless despite the effort involved. First-time creators typically drastically underestimate the time and skill involved. The hook works because it validates the difficulty of things that appear easy, removing shame from struggling with tasks that seem simple when others do them. It creates community among people who've had the humbling experience of discovering something is harder than it looks, building bonding through collective humility. The format also educates outsiders about hidden complexity in things they might be underestimating, creating respect for skills they might not have valued.
