# I spent [amount] on [thing] so you don't have to

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Sacrifice for community benefit.

## Example

I spent $5k on courses so you don't have to—here's what actually works.

## Why it works

Sacrificial-testing hooks work by positioning the creator as having absorbed financial or time costs to test something so viewers can benefit from the findings without paying the same price. The specific amount spent creates credibility through concrete investment—not vague "I researched this" but quantified resource commitment that proves genuine testing. The phrase "so you don't have to" is powerful because it frames the creator's spending as service to the community, transforming what could be seen as personal expense into generous investment in shared knowledge. This creates reciprocity pressure (they invested on my behalf) that increases engagement and trust. The structure also promises to share distilled findings, filtering signal from noise after extensive testing. The example about spending $5k on courses is particularly effective because online courses are a major category where people waste money buying based on marketing promises rather than actual quality or fit. By aggregating learning from multiple courses, the creator can identify patterns about what actually produces results versus what merely sounds good in sales copy. The promise "here's what actually works" suggests the creator will share actionable insights without viewers needing to spend thousands of dollars and many hours working through multiple courses to extract the same lessons. The hook works because it positions the creator's expensive experience as shortcut for viewers, providing decision-making guidance (which courses/approaches are worth it) and synthesized knowledge (key lessons across multiple sources). It builds trust through demonstrated investment and generosity, creating perception of value exchange that makes viewers more receptive to content and recommendations.
