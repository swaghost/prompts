# POV: You promised yourself [thing] but…

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Broken promises create laughs.

## Example

POV: You promised yourself one episode, but Netflix says otherwise.

## Why it works

Broken-promise POV hooks work by placing viewers inside the specific moment of realizing they're breaking a promise to themselves, creating humor through recognition of weak willpower. The POV format makes it immersive—you're not observing someone else's failure but experiencing your own, which heightens relatability. The phrase "you promised yourself" establishes clear intention and self-imposed rule, creating expectation of following through. The immediate contradiction "but..." signals the promise is already broken or about to be, creating humor through rapid collapse of resolve. The example blaming Netflix ("Netflix says otherwise") adds humor by personifying the platform as having agency that overrides your willpower, which is funny because it shifts "blame" from yourself to the streaming service while everyone knows you're the one hitting "next episode." The hook captures the specific experience of telling yourself you'll watch one episode before bed, then the episode ends on a cliffhanger or you're not ready to stop, and suddenly it's 2am and you've watched an entire season. The promise never stood a chance against compelling content and the low friction of autoplay. The hook works because it creates humor and community around failures of self-control that are simultaneously disappointing and predictable. It positions giving in to temptation as nearly inevitable rather than personal failing, removing guilt while acknowledging the pattern. The POV format makes it more visceral and personal than observational humor, creating stronger connection through

first-person experience. The specific modern example (streaming binging) makes it timely and universally relatable to modern media consumption habits.
