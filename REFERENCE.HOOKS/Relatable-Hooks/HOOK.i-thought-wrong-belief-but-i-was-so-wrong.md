# I thought [wrong belief], but I was so wrong

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Belief correction (shares growth through mind shift).

## Example

I thought posting more would fix everything—I was so wrong.

## Why it works

Belief-correction hooks work by sharing how the creator's thinking evolved, demonstrating intellectual humility and growth. The phrase "I thought" establishes the creator's previous belief system, making them relatable because everyone has held beliefs that later proved incorrect. The strong emphasis "I was so wrong" creates curiosity about what the correct understanding is and what evidence changed the creator's mind. The dramatic admission also builds trust through vulnerability—openly admitting being significantly mistaken demonstrates honesty and lack of ego. This structure creates a powerful teaching moment because it highlights a specific mental model shift that likely represents a breakthrough in the creator's journey. The example about posting more is particularly resonant for content creators who fall into the quantity trap—believing that more posts automatically means better results. This is intuitive logic (more exposure = more success) but often wrong because quality, targeting, and strategy matter far more than volume. The corrected belief might be that posting strategically and optimizing for engagement is more effective than high-volume posting with poor targeting. The hook works because it identifies common wrong beliefs that lead people astray, then promises to share the correct understanding that creates better results. It positions the creator as someone who learned through experience what works versus what seems like it should work, which is valuable wisdom for audiences trying to avoid wasting time on ineffective approaches. The hook validates that the wrong belief is understandable (the creator believed it too) while promising to share the better understanding that leads to actual results.
