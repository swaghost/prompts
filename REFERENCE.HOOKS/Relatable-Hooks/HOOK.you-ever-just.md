# You ever just…

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Open-ended relatability.

## Example

You ever just open Instagram, forget why, then close it?

## Why it works

Trailing-question hooks work by starting a thought then leaving it unfinished with an implied "..." creating invitation for viewers to complete the thought through their own recognition. The phrase "you ever just" is deliberately casual and open-ended, suggesting behavior that's almost unconscious or automatic rather than deliberate. The ellipsis (whether spoken through pause or written) creates space for viewers to fill in whether they share the experience before it's fully described. This format works well for behaviors people engage in mindlessly or without clear reason, where the questioning itself highlights the irrationality. The structure also creates intimacy through the casual, almost mid-thought way of speaking, like you're thinking out loud rather than crafting formal content. The example about opening Instagram, forgetting purpose, then closing it captures a specific modern digital behavior: automatic app-opening that's become so habitual it doesn't even require conscious intention or purpose. You open the app reflexively, spend a moment scrolling or staring, realize you didn't actually have a reason to be there, and close it—only to potentially repeat the cycle minutes later. This behavior reveals how deeply app-checking is embedded as automatic behavior disconnected from conscious intent. The hook works because it highlights mindless patterns that people don't usually consciously notice until someone names them, creating that "oh my god, I do that constantly" recognition. It builds community through acknowledgment of shared unconscious behaviors, creating bonding through discovery that quirks you thought were personal are actually collective patterns of modern digital life. The casual format makes it feel like conversation rather than content, creating intimacy and connection.
