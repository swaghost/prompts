# I stopped [common action] and everything changed

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Subtraction revelation (what you remove > what you add).

## Example

I stopped checking email first thing—everything changed.

## Why it works

Subtraction hooks work by highlighting how removing something created dramatic improvement, which is counterintuitive because we typically focus on what to add or do more of rather than what to eliminate. The phrase "I stopped" signals a deliberate decision to quit a behavior, creating curiosity about why and what motivated that change. The dramatic claim "everything changed" creates strong curiosity about the cascade of positive effects that resulted from this one subtraction. This hyperbole suggests the change was transformative, not just marginally helpful, making the lesson feel high-value. The structure also appeals to people feeling overwhelmed because subtraction is easier than addition—you're removing burden rather than adding more to do, which is psychologically relieving. The example about stopping email checking first thing in the morning is particularly powerful because it targets a nearly universal habit that's actually destructive to productivity and wellbeing. Starting the day with email means beginning in reactive mode, allowing others' priorities to hijack your attention before you've done your most important work. It sets a stressed, reactive tone for the entire day. By stopping this habit, the creator likely experienced better focus on priority work, more proactive rather than reactive work style, and reduced stress from starting the day on their own terms. The hook works because it challenges the default assumption that success comes from doing more by suggesting that strategically doing less (or eliminating specific negative patterns) can be more transformative than addition. It positions subtraction as powerful lever for change, which is especially appealing to overwhelmed people who can't add more to their plates but can eliminate what's actively harmful.
