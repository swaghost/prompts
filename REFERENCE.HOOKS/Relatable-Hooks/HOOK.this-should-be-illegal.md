# This should be illegal

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Hyperbolic outrage = relatability.

## Example

Getting zoom fatigue should be illegal.

## Why it works

Hyperbolic-outrage hooks work by expressing exaggerated frustration about genuinely annoying situations, creating humor through the gap between actual severity and proposed response. The phrase "should be illegal" applies criminal-justice language to minor frustrations, suggesting they're so annoying they warrant legal prohibition. This exaggeration is funny because it vastly overstates the case while emotionally capturing how frustrating the situation genuinely feels. The format validates strong negative reactions to minor annoyances by giving voice to that disproportionate frustration in clearly playful way. The structure also creates engagement as viewers agree, share their own candidates for "should be illegal" status, or playfully debate whether the frustration is truly warranted. The example about Zoom fatigue captures specific modern exhaustion from video meetings: the cognitive load of constant video presence, inability to look away without seeming disengaged, self-consciousness about appearance, artificial nature of virtual interaction, and cumulative drain of back-to-back virtual meetings without physical transition time. The fatigue is real and measurable yet difficult to explain to those who don't experience it, and complaining about it can feel ungrateful when remote work offers flexibility. The "should be illegal" framing validates that the exhaustion is genuine and significant while keeping it humorous. The hook works because it gives permission to have strong reactions to frustrations that seem too minor to complain about seriously, providing outlet for that frustration through clearly exaggerated proposals. It builds community through recognition of shared annoyances, creating bonding through collective expression of disproportionate outrage that's funny precisely because it's clearly overblown yet emotionally accurate.
