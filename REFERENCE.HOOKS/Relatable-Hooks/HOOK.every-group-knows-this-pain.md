# Every [group] knows this pain

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Community-specific empathy.

## Example

Every freelancer knows the pain of "just one quick revision."

## Why it works

Community-specific hooks work by identifying struggles that are unique to particular groups, creating in-group recognition and bonding while educating outsiders about that community's experience. The phrase "every [group]" is inclusive within the specified community—if you're part of this group, you definitely know this experience, creating belonging through shared understanding. The word "pain" acknowledges that this is genuinely difficult or frustrating, validating the struggle rather than dismissing it. The specificity to a community makes it feel like insider knowledge that creates kinship among members. The example about freelancers and "just one quick revision" is perfect because it captures a specific client behavior that freelancers universally experience and find frustrating: clients requesting "just one quick change" that's actually neither quick nor small, often reopening entire project scope after work is considered complete. The quotation marks around "just one quick revision" signal that this is client language that freelancers know to be misleading—what clients call quick is rarely quick. This phrase represents scope creep, undervaluation of work time, and the difficulty of enforcing boundaries when clients frame additional work as trivial. The hook works because it validates community-specific frustrations that outsiders might not understand or recognize as significant, creating strong in-group bonding through shared experience. It positions the creator as someone who genuinely understands the specific realities of that profession or identity, building trust through demonstrated lived experience rather than surface understanding. The format naturally generates engagement as community members share their own versions and war stories, strengthening community bonds.
