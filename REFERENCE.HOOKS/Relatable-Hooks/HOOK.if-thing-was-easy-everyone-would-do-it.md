# If [thing] was easy, everyone would do it

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Difficulty validation.

## Example

If building a business was easy, everyone would do it.

## Why it works

Difficulty-validation hooks work by acknowledging that worthwhile pursuits are genuinely challenging, which both validates people's struggles and reframes difficulty as evidence of value rather than personal inadequacy. The phrase "if [thing] was easy" creates a counterfactual that highlights the reality: it's not easy, and that's exactly why it's worth doing and why success is meaningful. The conclusion "everyone would do it" suggests that the difficulty itself is what creates value and differentiation—if something were easy, the results would be common and unremarkable. This reframe is psychologically powerful because it transforms difficulty from discouraging obstacle into validating indicator that you're pursuing something valuable. The structure also creates realistic expectations, protecting people from the disappointment of discovering that significant achievements require sustained effort. The example about building a business is particularly resonant because entrepreneurship is often romanticized in social media with highlights of success while the years of difficulty, uncertainty, and setbacks are compressed or omitted. When people experience how hard it actually is, they may feel like they're doing something wrong or aren't cut out for it. By explicitly stating "if it was easy, everyone would do it," the hook validates that the difficulty is normal and expected, not evidence of personal failure. It also explains why business success is rare and valuable—precisely because it's difficult enough that most people won't persist through the challenges. The hook works because it reframes hardship as filter rather than failure, positioning those who persist through difficulty as earning something valuable rather than simply struggling with what should be easy.
