# I tried [method/tool] and here's the result

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Curiosity gap (audience wants the outcome revealed).

## Example

I tried the Pomodoro technique—my focus went up 3x.

## Why it works

Curiosity-gap hooks work by establishing that an experiment was conducted and promising to reveal the results, creating a knowledge gap viewers want closed. The phrase "I tried" signals direct experience rather than theoretical knowledge, which is more credible and interesting—this is field-tested information about whether something actually works. The structure creates narrative tension: what were the results? Did it work? How dramatic was the change? The hook leverages natural human curiosity about outcomes, especially for methods or tools the viewer might be considering trying themselves. By withholding the full result in the hook (or revealing just enough to intrigue), it creates motivation to watch for the complete story. The example about Pomodoro technique is particularly effective because it's a well-known productivity method many people have heard about but not implemented, making the real-world results highly relevant for decision-making. The specific quantification "3x" makes the result concrete and dramatic—not just "better focus" but measurable, significant improvement. This specificity makes the result feel credible rather than vague claims about improvement. The hook works because it positions the creator as someone who experiments with popular methods and reports honest results, which is exactly what audiences want before investing time or money in tools and techniques. It promises to short-circuit the viewer's own trial-and-error process by sharing what the creator learned through direct testing, providing decision-making information based on real experience.
