# The truth is, [problem] is more common than you think

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Hidden prevalence (reveals universality).

## Example

The truth is, most successful people still feel like frauds.

## Why it works

Hidden-prevalence hooks work by revealing that problems people experience privately are actually widespread, even among those who seem to have it figured out. The phrase "the truth is" signals that what follows contradicts common assumptions or surface appearances, creating curiosity about what's being revealed. The structure "more common than you think" directly addresses the tendency to underestimate how many others share our struggles because we only see their curated external presentation, not their internal experience. This revelation is psychologically powerful because it reframes personal struggles from "something wrong with me" to "common human experience," which dramatically reduces shame and increases willingness to address the issue. The hook also creates intrigue by suggesting the creator has insider knowledge about prevalence that most people don't realize. The example about successful people feeling like frauds (imposter syndrome) is particularly impactful because it targets the exact population people assume don't have this problem. If even successful people experience imposter syndrome, it means success doesn't eliminate the feeling—it's not about actual competence but about the psychological pattern of discounting your achievements. This realization is both relieving (you can succeed despite the feeling) and eye-opening (success won't automatically make it go away, so you need to address it differently). The hook works because it combats the isolation and shame that make problems harder to solve by revealing they're far more widespread than surface appearances suggest, creating permission to acknowledge and address the struggle openly.
