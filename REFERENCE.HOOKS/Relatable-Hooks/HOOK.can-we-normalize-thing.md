# Can we normalize [thing]?

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Permission movement.

## Example

Can we normalize leaving parties without saying goodbye to everyone?

## Why it works

Normalization-campaign hooks work by calling for social permission for behaviors that are currently stigmatized or awkward but actually reasonable, creating community among people who want that permission. The question format "can we normalize" is a call to collective action rather than individual decision, suggesting that changing social norms requires group agreement rather than just personal choice. This frames the desired behavior as needing cultural shift, not just individual courage. The format also invites agreement and discussion, creating engagement as viewers debate whether the behavior should be normalized. The structure works particularly well for behaviors people already do but feel awkward about, offering permission structure through collective agreement. The example about leaving parties without extensive goodbyes (sometimes called "Irish goodbye" or "ghosting a party") is perfect because it addresses a common social anxiety: the extended departure where you try to leave but keep getting pulled into new conversations, making leaving take 30+ minutes and creating pressure to stay longer than wanted. Many people prefer slipping out quietly to avoid this, but it can feel rude. By calling to normalize it, the hook creates permission to do what many people want—leave when ready without elaborate departure process. The hook works because it identifies reasonable behaviors that face social judgment or awkwardness, then mobilizes community support to remove that stigma through collective normalization. It positions social norms as malleable and potentially worthy of updating, empowering viewers to advocate for permission structures that make life easier or more authentic. The question format naturally generates engagement as people debate, agree, or share their own candidates for normalization.
