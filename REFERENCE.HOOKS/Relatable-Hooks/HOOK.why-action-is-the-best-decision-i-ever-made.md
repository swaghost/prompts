# Why [action] is the best decision I ever made

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Endorsement through hindsight.

## Example

Why quitting social media was the best decision I ever made.

## Why it works

Best-decision hooks work by providing strong endorsement of an action through retrospective evaluation, suggesting the benefits were so significant that among all life choices, this one stands out. The phrase "best decision I ever made" is strong superlative language that creates curiosity about what makes this action so transformative that it surpasses all other decisions. This is particularly compelling for decisions viewers might be considering but hesitating on, as it provides strong social proof and validation that taking that step can be life-changing. The structure also promises explanation—not just "I'm glad I did this" but "here's why it was the best decision," suggesting specific, significant benefits that justify the superlative claim. The example about quitting social media is particularly interesting because it's countercultural in a world where social media presence is treated as mandatory for business, personal brand, and staying connected. The hook creates curiosity about how removing something considered essential could actually be the best decision, and what benefits outweighed the assumed costs. The benefits might include: recovered time and attention, reduced comparison anxiety, deeper relationships through direct rather than passive connection, increased focus and creativity, better mental health, or business success through owned platforms like email rather than rented social media. The hook works because it provides retrospective validation for courageous or unconventional decisions viewers might be considering but fear committing to. It suggests that short-term difficulty or social pressure is worth it for long-term benefits, building courage to act on decisions that feel right but scary. The superlative claim creates strong credibility when backed by authentic explanation.
