# Be honest

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Truth-seeking engagement.

## Example

Be honest—how many unread tabs do you have open right now?

## Why it works

Honesty-challenge hooks work by explicitly requesting truth about potentially embarrassing behaviors or numbers, creating engagement through confession invitation while building community through discovery of shared patterns. The phrase "be honest" creates playful pressure to admit truth rather than presenting idealized version, suggesting there's gap between socially acceptable answer and reality. This acknowledgment that people might lie or minimize creates safety to be truthful since dishonesty is expected and anticipated. The format works particularly well for questions about behaviors that are common but slightly embarrassing, where people know they're "bad" but do them anyway. The direct question format creates clear call-to-action for engagement through commenting answers. The example about unread open browser tabs is perfect because it's simultaneously meaningless (number of tabs doesn't matter) and revealing (excessive tabs suggest digital clutter, information hoarding, inability to let go of "might need this," or simply poor tab hygiene). Many people have embarrassingly large numbers of tabs open across multiple windows, telling themselves they'll read or address them eventually despite knowing they probably won't. The question creates opportunity for confession with implicit competition about who has the most, turning potential embarrassment into playful one-upmanship. The hook works because it creates engagement through confession prompts that are low-stakes enough to be safe but revealing enough to be interesting. It builds community through discovery that embarrassing numbers or behaviors are widely shared, often in more extreme versions than your own, creating relief through "at least I'm not as bad as that" comparisons while normalizing the behavior through collective admission.
