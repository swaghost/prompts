# No one talks about [truth], but we should

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Taboo-breaking (addresses silence).

## Example

No one talks about how lonely entrepreneurship is—but we should.

## Why it works

Taboo-breaking hooks work by addressing topics that are experienced widely but discussed rarely, creating relief through validation that breaking the silence is necessary and valuable. The phrase "no one talks about" identifies the gap between private experience and public conversation, suggesting there's a social taboo or discomfort around the topic that prevents open discussion. This creates curiosity about what's being hidden or avoided. The addition "but we should" is a call to action that positions open discussion as necessary rather than optional, suggesting that the silence itself is harmful and maintaining it prevents important understanding or support. This frames the creator as brave enough to discuss what others avoid, building authority and trust. The structure also validates viewers' experiences by acknowledging that if they're experiencing something nobody talks about, they're not alone—others experience it too but the culture of silence makes it seem rare. The example about entrepreneurship loneliness is particularly powerful because entrepreneurship is typically discussed in terms of freedom, success, and achievement, rarely acknowledging the isolation that comes from leaving traditional employment, having few people who understand your struggles, making decisions alone, and spending long periods working independently. This loneliness is widespread but rarely discussed because admitting it might seem like weakness or ingratitude for the privilege of entrepreneurship. By naming it explicitly and calling for discussion, the hook gives permission for entrepreneurs to acknowledge this difficult aspect and seek support. The hook works because it creates permission structures around difficult topics, building community through shared understanding while positioning the creator as willing to have honest conversations others avoid.
