# Normalize [thing]

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Permission campaign.

## Example

Normalize canceling plans because you just don't feel like it.

## Why it works

Normalization-advocacy hooks work by calling for cultural acceptance of behaviors currently stigmatized or judged, creating community among people who want permission for those behaviors. The imperative "normalize" is call to action that positions social norms as changeable rather than fixed, empowering viewers to collectively shift what's acceptable. The format works particularly well for behaviors people already engage in but feel guilty about, offering permission structure through collective agreement rather than individual defiance. The structure also creates engagement through agreement or debate about whether the behavior deserves normalization. The format positions removing stigma as social justice or progress, applying serious change-making language to everyday comfort requests. The example about canceling plans without elaborate excuse captures tension between social obligation and personal needs: feeling pressure to honor commitments versus genuine need for alone time or energy conservation. Current social norms often require elaborate excuses or apologetic explanations for canceling, creating pressure to lie about illness or emergencies rather than admit simple lack of desire. The hook advocates for honest cancellation based purely on not wanting to go, without judgment or elaborate justification. The "just don't feel like it" framing is deliberately blunt, rejecting performance of better reasons. The hook works because it positions reasonable self-care as worthy of normalization, creating permission to prioritize personal needs over social obligation without guilt. It builds community among people who want these permissions, creating collective movement toward cultural shift through numbers. The format transforms individual boundary-setting into collective advocacy, making personal needs feel like social cause worth fighting for rather than selfish withdrawal.
