# Watch this if you're tired of [frustration]

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Call-out empathy (direct address hooks scrollers who relate).

## Example

Watch this if you're tired of scrolling LinkedIn with no results.

## Why it works

Direct-address frustration hooks work by naming a specific pain point that causes people to stop scrolling and self-identify. The phrase "watch this if you're tired of" creates a qualifying statement that helps viewers instantly determine relevance—if they're experiencing that frustration, the content is for them; if not, they can scroll past. This targeting creates strong engagement from the right audience while filtering out those who wouldn't find value. The word "tired" is emotionally resonant because it captures not just the presence of a problem but the exhaustion of dealing with it repeatedly, suggesting this is an ongoing frustration rather than a one-time issue. This accumulated frustration creates stronger motivation to finally find a solution. The direct command "watch this" creates urgency and positions the content as the answer to that specific frustration. The example about scrolling LinkedIn with no results is particularly relatable for professionals and entrepreneurs who invest time in the platform but don't see tangible outcomes like connections, leads, opportunities, or engagement. The hook acknowledges the effort they're already putting in ("scrolling LinkedIn") while validating that lack of results is frustrating and worth addressing. The hook works because it combines precise audience targeting (if you have this specific frustration) with empathetic acknowledgment (I understand you're tired of this) and implied promise (this will help you solve it). It positions the creator as someone who understands the specific pain point and has a solution, which is exactly what frustrated viewers are seeking.
