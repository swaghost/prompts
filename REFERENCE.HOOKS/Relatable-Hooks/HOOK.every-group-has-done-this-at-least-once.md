# Every [group] has done this at least once

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Tribal humor validation.

## Example

Every student has googled "define plagiarism" at least once.

## Why it works

Universal-group-experience hooks work by identifying behaviors that virtually all members of a specific community have engaged in, creating insider bonding through shared acknowledgment of common behaviors. The phrase "every [group]" is deliberately universal—not "most" or "many" but "every," suggesting this behavior is so common it's essentially a rite of passage or inevitable part of membership. The phrase "at least once" acknowledges that some behavior may be embarrassing or questionable but positions it as normal enough that everyone has done it, removing shame through universality. The format creates safety in numbers—if absolutely everyone has done this, it can't be too terrible. The structure also creates humor through self-awareness about group patterns that might seem contradictory or ironic. The example about students googling "define plagiarism" is brilliantly ironic because it suggests students researching what plagiarism is while potentially in the process of plagiarizing, or at minimum needing clarity about boundaries they're approaching. The irony is that understanding plagiarism is important for avoiding it, yet googling for the definition highlights that many students aren't clear on the concept until they need to be. The hook works because it creates insider humor through acknowledgment of behaviors that define group membership even when those behaviors are slightly embarrassing or contradict group values. It builds community through collective admission of shared imperfection, normalizing the gap between ideals and reality. The tribal framing creates belonging—if you've done this, you're confirmed member of the group, and if you haven't, you're probably lying or not really part of the community.
