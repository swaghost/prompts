# Every time I [habit], I regret it

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Repetition humor + self-recognition.

## Example

Every time I skip breakfast, I regret it by 10am.

## Why it works

Repetitive-regret hooks work by acknowledging patterns where we repeatedly make the same choice despite consistently regretting it, creating humor through recognition of persistent irrationality. The phrase "every time" emphasizes the pattern's reliability—this isn't occasional mistake but repeated behavior, making the failure to learn from experience more absurd and funny. The addition "I regret it" creates the classic cycle: make choice, experience negative consequence, regret choice, then somehow make the same choice again next time. This cycle is universally human and creates bonding through shared recognition of our inability to consistently act on lessons we've already learned multiple times. The specificity of the pattern makes it memorable and relatable. The example about skipping breakfast and regretting it by 10am captures a specific self-sabotage cycle many people experience: you wake up not hungry or without time, skip breakfast despite knowing better, then by mid-morning you're irritable, unfocused, hungry, and regretting the choice. Yet next morning, the same factors (not hungry, rushed) lead to the same choice despite yesterday's regret. The 10am timing is specific and resonates because it's that awkward time when you're stuck between breakfast and lunch, unable to concentrate due to hunger but still hours from lunch. The hook works because it validates that humans often don't learn from repeated negative consequences, removing shame from persistent irrationality. It creates community through recognition that we all have choices we repeatedly regret but somehow keep making, building bonding through collective admission of being perpetually unable to apply obvious lessons despite constant evidence.
