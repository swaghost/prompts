# I said I wouldn't, but I did [this]

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Promise broken = "told you so to myself" moment.

## Example

I said I wouldn't buy more books, but here we are with a full cart.

## Why it works

Broken-promise hooks work by admitting failures to stick to self-imposed rules or intentions, creating humor and relatability through honest acknowledgment of weak willpower or changing priorities. The phrase "I said I wouldn't" establishes a clear intention or promise to yourself, creating expectation. The immediate contradiction "but I did" creates humor through the rapid breakdown of that resolve, suggesting the intention never really stood a chance. This pattern is universally relatable because everyone makes commitments to themselves they then break, whether about spending, eating, time management, or behavior. The format creates gentle self-deprecating humor without harsh judgment, acknowledging human weakness with affection rather than criticism. The phrase "but here we are" adds resigned acceptance that makes it funnier—not fighting the failure but accepting it almost proudly. The example about buying more books despite promising not to is perfect because book buying is a specific hobby where enthusiasts constantly promise themselves they'll stop buying until they read what they have, then immediately break that promise when encountering irresistible titles. The "full cart" detail emphasizes that this wasn't a small slip but complete abandonment of the resolution. The hook works because it creates bonding through shared failures of self-control, normalizing that humans are often weak against temptation or changing our minds when confronted with immediate desires versus abstract future consequences. It removes shame from breaking self-promises by framing it as universal human experience rather than personal failing, creating community through collective admission of imperfect willpower.
