# Based on a true story

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Authenticity humor.

## Example

Based on a true story—scheduling 5 tasks but finishing 0.

## Why it works

True-story hooks work by emphasizing authenticity of embarrassing or frustrating experiences, creating trust through admission that this actually happened rather than being hypothetical or exaggerated. The phrase "based on a true story" borrows language from movies and TV shows that claim real-world inspiration, applying it to mundane personal failures or frustrations. This creates humor through treating minor personal embarrassments with gravitas typically reserved for dramatic narratives worth documenting. The format also creates implicit vulnerability—you're admitting real failures or struggles rather than inventing relatable content, building connection through genuine self-disclosure. The structure works particularly well for describing failures, gaps between intentions and outcomes, or moments that are funny in retrospect but were frustrating when they happened. The example about scheduling multiple tasks then completing none captures specific productivity failure: optimistically planning ambitious task list, then ending day having achieved none of it due to distractions, underestimation of time needed, procrastination, or energy depletion. The contrast between intention (5 tasks) and outcome (0 completed) is simultaneously frustrating and humorous, especially when it's recurring pattern rather than one-time failure. The "based on a true story" framing emphasizes this actually happened, validating both the ambition and the failure as real experience. The hook works because it builds community through authentic admission of common failures, creating bonding through honest disclosure of gaps between plans and execution. It normalizes productivity failures by revealing they're common enough to be documented, removing shame through collective recognition that ambitious plans often fail.
