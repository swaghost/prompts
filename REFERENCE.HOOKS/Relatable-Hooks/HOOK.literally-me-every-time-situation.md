# Literally me every time [situation]

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Identity meme hook.

## Example

Literally me every time Netflix asks, "Are you still watching?"

## Why it works

Identity-meme hooks work by using "literally me" format to claim specific behaviors or reactions as defining personal characteristics, creating recognition and community among others who share those patterns. The phrase "literally me" is internet vernacular that signals complete identification with a described behavior, making it feel like a personality trait rather than occasional occurrence. This creates strong belonging among viewers who also identify with the pattern while being humorous about the self-awareness. The phrase "every time" emphasizes the reliability and predictability of the pattern, making it feel like intrinsic characteristic rather than random behavior. The format works well with visual content showing the behavior or reaction, making it highly shareable. The example about Netflix's "Are you still watching?" prompt is perfect because it captures a nearly universal modern experience—binge-watching for hours until Netflix checks if you're actually still there or have fallen asleep. The implied behavior (yes, definitely still watching, often feeling called out for how long you've been watching) is both relatable and mildly self-deprecating. The hook taps into the cultural norm of binge-watching and the sheepish recognition when Netflix essentially asks "are you really still on the couch?" The hook works because it creates identity-based community around shared behaviors, positioning quirks and habits as personality traits worth claiming rather than hiding. It leverages meme culture's embrace of relatable self-deprecation as bonding mechanism, building connection through honest acknowledgment of very human patterns. The format naturally generates engagement through self-identification and sharing.
