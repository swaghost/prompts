# Anyone else or just me: [habit]

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Validation seeking.

## Example

Anyone else or just me: planning conversations that will never happen.

## Why it works

Validation-seeking hooks work by admitting behaviors or thoughts while explicitly asking whether others share them, creating engagement opportunity through direct question format. The phrase "anyone else or just me" expresses vulnerability—the creator is unsure whether their experience is normal or unique, making the question genuine rather than rhetorical. This uncertainty makes viewers want to provide reassurance or validation by confirming they also do this, creating natural engagement. The format also makes individuals feel less alone when they discover others share their quirks or thought patterns. The structure cleverly works whether you're unique or common: if many people share the behavior, you get validating community; if few do, you get interesting insight into diversity of human experience. Either way, engagement happens through people declaring whether they relate. The example about planning conversations that will never happen captures an extremely common but rarely discussed behavior—mental rehearsal of future conversations, arguments you'd win, explanations you'd give, or perfect comebacks to past conversations. This mental habit is common but feels slightly absurd when named explicitly, creating both recognition and gentle humor. The hook works because it exposes internal experiences people assume are private or weird, then creates relief when others validate that the experience is actually common. It builds community by revealing that quirky internal experiences many people assume are uniquely theirs are actually widely shared, creating bonding through mutual recognition. The question format naturally drives comments as people confirm whether they relate, generating engagement that boosts algorithmic distribution while strengthening community through collective self-disclosure.
