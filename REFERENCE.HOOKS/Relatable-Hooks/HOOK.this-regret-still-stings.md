# This regret still stings

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Vulnerability + authority.

## Example

My biggest regret? Not documenting my journey earlier.

## Why it works

Lingering-regret hooks work by sharing mistakes that still cause emotional pain, demonstrating genuine vulnerability while offering cautionary wisdom. The phrase "still stings" indicates the regret remains emotionally active, not a distant memory that's been fully processed and moved past. This raw emotional honesty creates deep connection because it shows the creator is human and experiences lasting consequences from choices, making them relatable and trustworthy. The present-tense pain also emphasizes the significance of the mistake—it mattered enough that it continues to hurt, making the lesson feel important rather than trivial. The structure creates curiosity about what specifically is regretted and why it continues to hurt, driving engagement to learn the story and lesson. The example about not documenting the journey is particularly resonant for content creators and entrepreneurs who built something valuable but failed to capture the process, learning, and progress along the way. This regret is common because people are focused on building in the moment and don't realize until later how valuable that documentation would be—for content, for learning, for inspiration, and for validating how far they've come. The "still stings" emphasis suggests the creator regularly wishes they had those early moments documented, perhaps when they see opportunities they missed or when trying to help others but lacking specific memory of how they solved early problems. The hook works because it shares hard-earned wisdom through personal cost, making the lesson memorable and motivating. It positions mistakes as valuable teachers while encouraging viewers to avoid the same regret by acting on the lesson now while they still can.
