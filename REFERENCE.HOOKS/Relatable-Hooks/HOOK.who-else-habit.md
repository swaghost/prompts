# Who else [habit]?

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Direct community building.

## Example

Who else scrolls through the same 3 apps on repeat?

## Why it works

Community-identification hooks work by directly asking who shares specific behaviors, creating engagement through explicit invitation to self-identify while building community among respondents. The question format "who else" assumes multiple people share the behavior and invites them to reveal themselves, creating safety in numbers—you're not admitting to being the only one but joining others who've already been assumed to exist. This framing creates low-risk identification opportunity because the question format already validates that the behavior is shared. The structure also creates clear call-to-action for engagement through responding, whether through comments, reactions, or mental agreement. The format works particularly well for habits that are common but not typically discussed or acknowledged, making the public questioning of them feel like revelation. The example about repeatedly cycling through the same few apps captures specific modern digital behavior: opening social media apps sequentially (Instagram, Twitter, TikTok, etc.), scrolling each briefly, then cycling back to the beginning despite just checking them minutes ago. This loop can continue indefinitely without conscious intent, creating time sink that feels mindless and slightly compulsive. The "same 3 apps" specificity suggests how limited and repetitive the loop is, while "on repeat" emphasizes the cyclical mindless quality. The hook works because it creates immediate community identification through direct question format that invites public acknowledgment of shared behaviors. It normalizes potentially problematic patterns (mindless phone use, app addiction) through collective revelation that transforms individual struggles into group characteristics. The question format drives engagement through invitation to self-identify while building community through discovery of how many people share the exact same patterns.
