# Here's the tea

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Gossip framing.

## Example

Here's the tea—nobody actually knows what they're doing.

## Why it works

Gossip-framing hooks work by using "spilling tea" (internet slang for sharing gossip or insider information) language to reveal truths that aren't actually gossip but universal realities people pretend not to acknowledge. The phrase "here's the tea" creates anticipation for juicy information or confession, borrowing the engaging format of gossip while applying it to existential truths or widely-shared secrets. This creates humor through treating universal struggles as if they're shocking revelations worth gossiping about. The format also creates insider feeling—you're being let in on information others supposedly don't know, even when the "secret" is actually widely known but rarely stated. The structure positions uncomfortable truths as exciting reveals rather than depressing admissions, making them more palatable. The example about nobody knowing what they're doing captures universal impostor phenomenon where people appear confident and competent externally while internally feeling uncertain and making it up as they go. This disconnect between appearance and internal experience makes everyone feel like they're uniquely faking it while others truly know what they're doing, when reality is most people feel this way. Stating this "secret" validates that competence is often performed rather than genuinely felt, removing isolation from feeling uncertain. The hook works because it frames validating truths as exciting gossip worth sharing, making uncomfortable admissions feel more like juicy reveals than depressing confessions. It builds community by exposing "secrets" that turn out to be universally shared experiences, creating relief through revelation that what felt like personal inadequacy is actually collective human condition. The gossip framing makes serious topics feel lighter and more engaging.
