# This is your reminder to [action/mindset]

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Permission + gentle push.

## Example

This is your reminder to rest without guilt.

## Why it works

Reminder hooks work by providing permission and encouragement for actions or mindsets people know they need but struggle to prioritize or allow themselves. The phrase "this is your reminder" positions the creator as supportive voice providing gentle accountability, acknowledging that people need external prompting for things they intellectually know but emotionally resist. This creates a supportive rather than preachy tone—not "you should" (which creates resistance) but "reminder" (which acknowledges you already know this but may have forgotten or deprioritized it). The structure also creates permission because reminders come from outside authority, making it easier to act on than if it was entirely self-motivated. This is particularly effective for actions that carry guilt or feel indulgent when they're actually necessary for wellbeing. The example "rest without guilt" is particularly powerful because rest is often treated as earned reward rather than fundamental need, especially in hustle culture that valorizes constant productivity. The phrase "without guilt" directly addresses the emotional barrier that prevents many people from resting even when exhausted—the feeling that rest is lazy or unproductive rather than necessary for sustainable performance and health. By explicitly including "without guilt," the hook gives permission to rest AND to feel okay about resting, addressing both the behavior and the emotional judgment. The hook works because it provides external permission structure for self-care or positive actions people struggle to give themselves permission for. It positions the creator as supportive advocate for healthy behaviors rather than pushy advisor, building connection through care rather than judgment while gently pushing toward beneficial actions or mindsets.
