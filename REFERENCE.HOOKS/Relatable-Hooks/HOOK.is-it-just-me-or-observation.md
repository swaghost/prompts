# Is it just me or [observation]?

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Shared reality check.

## Example

Is it just me or does time move differently on the weekend?

## Why it works

Reality-check hooks work by questioning whether perceptions are unique or shared, inviting validation while acknowledging that subjective experiences might not be universal. The phrase "is it just me or" creates inclusive question that expresses uncertainty about whether perception is personal or shared, making it vulnerable admission that invites reassurance. This format creates engagement through direct question that viewers naturally want to answer by confirming or denying shared experience. The structure also validates that subjective perceptions are worth questioning and discussing rather than assuming they're universal or dismissing them as purely personal. The format works particularly well for observations about everyday phenomena that might be cognitive biases or actual patterns worth recognizing collectively. The example about time moving differently on weekends captures specific subjective time experience: weekends often feel like they pass more quickly than weekdays despite having same number of hours, possibly because weekend time is more enjoyable (making it feel shorter), less structured (reducing time markers that make duration feel longer), or because we're not watching the clock the same way. The observation is simultaneously subjective (time objectively doesn't change speed) and shared (many people report this exact perception), making it perfect for reality-check format. The hook works because it validates subjective experiences by revealing they're commonly shared perceptions rather than purely personal distortions. It builds community through discovery that weird observations about reality are actually collective experiences rather than individual misperceptions, creating bonding through validation of shared reality tunnels. The question format drives engagement through invitation to confirm or complicate the observation.
