# This shouldn't be this hard, but here we are

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Expectation vs difficulty validation.

## Example

Waking up shouldn't be this hard, but here we are.

## Why it works

Expectation-difficulty hooks work by acknowledging the gap between how easy something should theoretically be and how difficult it actually feels, validating struggle with tasks that seem like they shouldn't be problems. The phrase "shouldn't be this hard" expresses frustration with difficulty that feels disproportionate—the task seems simple yet remains challenging, creating cognitive dissonance that the hook validates. The resigned conclusion "but here we are" acknowledges reality with humor and acceptance, suggesting we're stuck with this inexplicable difficulty whether it makes sense or not. This combination of frustration and acceptance creates relatability through shared bewilderment at why simple things remain difficult. The format also creates gentle self-deprecating humor without harsh self-judgment. The example about waking up is perfect because it's something humans do daily our entire lives, yet many people find it genuinely difficult every single day—alarm goes off, snoozing happens, dragging yourself out of bed feels like enormous effort, despite having done this thousands of times. The difficulty feels irrational (you're just opening your eyes and standing up, yet it feels like climbing a mountain), making it both frustrating and humorous. The hook works because it validates struggles that feel like they shouldn't be struggles, removing shame around finding basic tasks difficult. It creates community among people who share these inexplicable difficulties, building connection through honest acknowledgment that some things remain hard despite being objectively simple. The resigned humor prevents it from feeling like complaining while still validating genuine struggle.
