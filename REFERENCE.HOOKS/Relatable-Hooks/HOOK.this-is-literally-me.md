# This is literally me

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Self-recognition loop.

## Example

This is literally me—saving audio I'll never use.

## Why it works

Identity-claim hooks work by asserting complete identification with specific behaviors or patterns, creating strong recognition and community among people who share those exact traits. The phrase "this is literally me" uses "literally" for emphasis (despite being used loosely) to claim total identification with described behavior—not "sometimes me" but defining characteristic. This creates belonging through recognition while being humorous about making behaviors into identity markers. The format works well with visual content showing the behavior, making it highly shareable as people tag others or reshare to their stories claiming "this is me." The structure transforms behaviors into identity traits worth claiming rather than hiding, normalizing quirks through public identification. The example about saving audio you'll never use captures specific creator behavior: enthusiastically saving sounds, songs, or audio clips while browsing, imagining future content you'll create with them, yet never actually using most of what you save. The "save" collection becomes graveyard of good intentions rather than useful resource library. This pattern is extremely common among creators who collect more than they execute, creating gap between ambition (all the content you plan to make) and reality (what you actually create). The specificity makes it instantly recognizable to creators while the admission that you'll "never use" them shows self-awareness about the futility of the behavior. The hook works because it creates identity-based community around shared behaviors, building bonding through collective claiming of specific patterns as personality traits. It normalizes behaviors that might seem like personal quirks by revealing they're widely shared enough to be group identity markers, creating comfort through numbers while celebrating rather than hiding those traits.
