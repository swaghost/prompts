# What [achieving goal] actually costs

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Full-cost transparency.

## Example

What building a 6-figure business actually costs: relationships, sleep, and years of uncertainty.

## Why it works

Full-cost hooks work by revealing the complete price of achievement beyond financial costs, including time, relationships, health, and opportunity costs that are systematically excluded from success narratives. The word "actually" signals that commonly understood costs are incomplete or misleading, creating curiosity about what's being omitted. The phrase "actually costs" promises comprehensive accounting of tradeoffs rather than selective highlighting of benefits or obvious financial investments. This transparency is valuable because it enables informed decision-making about whether a goal is worth pursuing given the real total cost, not just marketed benefits and obvious expenses. The example about six-figure businesses is powerful because it lists non-obvious costs that are far more significant than financial investments: relationships strained or ended due to time demands and changed priorities; sleep sacrificed to fit work into available time; and years of uncertainty about whether the business will succeed, creating ongoing stress and anxiety. These costs are real and significant but systematically excluded from entrepreneurship marketing that emphasizes freedom and financial success. By naming them explicitly, the hook provides crucial perspective—yes, six figures is possible, but these are the tradeoffs you should expect and evaluate whether you're willing to make. This isn't meant to discourage but to ensure people pursue goals with eyes open rather than discovering costs after commitment. The hook works because it respects viewers enough to share complete truth, building deep trust through transparency about real costs of success. It positions achievement as absolutely possible but requiring specific sacrifices, allowing informed choice rather than surprised disappointment when hidden costs emerge unexpectedly.
