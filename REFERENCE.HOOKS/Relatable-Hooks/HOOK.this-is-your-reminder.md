# This is your reminder

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Gentle nudge framing.

## Example

This is your reminder to drink water and stop scrolling.

## Why it works

Reminder hooks work by framing advice or encouragement as timely nudge viewers needed at this moment, creating helpful tone without being preachy or demanding. The phrase "this is your reminder" positions the message as gentle prompt about something viewers already know but might have forgotten, making it supportive rather than instructive. This framing assumes viewers have good intentions and knowledge but need occasional prompting, which is both accurate and respectful. The format also creates serendipity feeling—encountering this message "right when you needed it" suggests meaningful timing that makes simple advice feel more personally relevant. The structure works particularly well for self-care basics that people know they should do but often neglect until reminded. The example combining water and stopping scrolling addresses two common neglected self-care areas: hydration (easy to forget when absorbed in activities) and screen time (scrolling can extend indefinitely without conscious intervention). The pairing suggests the reminder itself is breaking the scrolling pattern by prompting awareness and healthier behavior. The message is caring without being judgmental—it doesn't scold for neglecting these things but gently prompts course correction. The hook works because it positions the creator as caring friend providing helpful nudges rather than authority figure demanding compliance. It builds trust through gentle care that respects viewer autonomy while still encouraging better choices. The reminder framing makes basic advice feel timely and personally relevant rather than generic nagging, creating gratitude for the prompt rather than resistance to being told what to do.
