# We've all been there—[relatable problem]—here's how to fix it

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Normalization (reduces shame, increases openness to solutions).

## Example

We've all been there—staring at a blank screen—here's how to fix it.

## Why it works

Normalization hooks work by removing the shame and isolation that often accompanies struggles. The phrase "we've all been there" is psychologically powerful because it universalizes the problem, transforming it from a personal failure into a shared human experience. This normalization reduces the emotional resistance that prevents people from seeking help or admitting difficulty. When someone feels alone in their struggle, they're more likely to hide it or feel inadequate; when they realize it's common, they become more receptive to solutions. The structure also creates psychological safety: the creator isn't positioning themselves as superior but as someone who has also experienced this challenge, building trust through shared vulnerability. The immediate pivot to "here's how to fix it" provides hope and concrete value, transforming the hook from mere commiseration into actionable guidance. The example about staring at a blank screen is universally relatable for anyone who creates content, writes, or needs to be productive—writer's block or creative paralysis affects everyone regardless of skill level. The hook works because it combines emotional validation (your struggle is normal) with practical promise (I have a solution), creating both comfort and anticipation. It positions the creator as an empathetic guide who understands the pain point intimately and has figured out how to overcome it, which builds credibility and motivation to engage with the solution.
