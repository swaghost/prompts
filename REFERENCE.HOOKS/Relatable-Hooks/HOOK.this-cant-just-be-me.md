# This can't just be me

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Validation seeking humor.

## Example

This can't just be me—buying books faster than I can read them.

## Why it works

Reassurance-seeking hooks work by expressing genuine uncertainty about whether an experience is personal quirk or shared pattern, inviting validation while creating engagement through direct question format. The phrase "this can't just be me" expresses hope that others share the experience while acknowledging possibility of being alone in it, creating vulnerability that invites reassurance. The slightly desperate tone ("can't" rather than "isn't") suggests need for validation, making viewers want to provide comfort by confirming they share the experience. The format creates safety for confession by framing it as question rather than assertion, making admission less risky. The structure works particularly well for behaviors that seem irrational or potentially problematic, where confirming others share them provides relief and normalization. The example about buying books faster than reading them captures specific reader behavior pattern: continuously acquiring new books despite having substantial unread backlog, driven by enthusiasm for reading, fear of missing acclaimed titles, sales, or collecting instinct that's separate from actual reading capacity. This creates guilt (unread books staring accusingly) and cognitive dissonance (claiming to be "a reader" while not reading what you own), yet the behavior persists. The pattern is extremely common among book lovers yet feels like personal failing. The hook works because it transforms potentially isolating quirks into community-building confessions by revealing behaviors you thought were personal weaknesses are actually shared patterns. It creates relief through numbers—if many people do this, it's normal rather than personal failure—while building bonds through collective admission of irrational but persistent behaviors that define group identity despite seeming counterproductive.
