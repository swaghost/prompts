# Just me, or does this happen to everyone?

## Hook Group

Relatable Hooks

## Psychology

Question + validation.

## Example

Just me, or does everyone type "lol" in chat without actually laughing?

## Why it works

Universality-check hooks work by questioning whether an experience is unique or shared, inviting validation while creating engagement through direct question format. The phrase "just me, or" expresses genuine uncertainty about whether you're alone in an experience, making it vulnerable admission that invites reassurance. This vulnerability creates connection because viewers naturally want to provide validation if they share the experience. The phrase "does this happen to everyone" opens it beyond just "you and me" to universal experience, suggesting the behavior might be standard human pattern rather than individual quirk. The structure works whether the answer is "yes, everyone" (validating that you're normal) or "actually, just you" (creating interesting insight into diversity). Either way, engagement happens through people declaring whether they relate. The example about typing "lol" without actually laughing captures a specific modern communication pattern: "lol" has evolved from literal "laughing out loud" to general tone softener, indicating friendliness or acknowledging something as mildly amusing without requiring actual laughter. Most people type it constantly while stone-faced, using it as punctuation or social lubricant rather than laughter report. The question invites viewers to recognize this exact behavior in themselves, creating humor through collective admission that we're all pretending to laugh more than we actually are. The hook works because it transforms potentially isolating quirks into bonding opportunities by revealing they're universally shared. It creates comfort through discovery that behaviors you thought might be weird are actually standard, building community through collective confession. The question format naturally drives engagement as people confirm or deny their participation, generating comments that boost algorithmic distribution.
