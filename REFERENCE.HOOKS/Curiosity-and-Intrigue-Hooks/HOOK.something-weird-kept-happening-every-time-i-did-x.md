# Something weird kept happening every time I did [X]

## Hook Group

Curiosity & Intrigue Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as pattern-detector identifying recurring-strangeness. They see you as correlation-finder. You appear as someone whose kept-happening emphasis demonstrates you recognize repeated-anomalies enabling audience to discover hidden-patterns through repetition-observation.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers pattern-recognition curiosity. Intellectually activates repetition-curiosity thinking. Psychologically creates need for pattern-explanation. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "every time" promises revealing that repeated-weirdness indicates discoverable-pattern requiring investigation.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because leverages repetition curiosity - if weird thing repeats, pattern exists requiring discovery. This hook leverages appeal of mystery-solving where consistent-anomaly indicates hidden-cause. The every-time framing creates reliability. By emphasizing recurring-strangeness, you enable pattern-investigation. This satisfies desire for explanation through identifying-causation of repeated-phenomenon.

## Example

Something weird kept happening every time I posted at 3PM. High engagement compared to other times. Consistent pattern emerged. Repeated result indicated cause. Regular occurrence suggested mechanism. Reliable weirdness revealed optimization. Repetition proved pattern not coincidence.

## Why it works

This hook works because it intrigues through repetition-pattern framing teaching correlation-investigation. The every-time creates reliability-curiosity. By showing 3PM consistently performing differently, you demonstrate concrete recurring-anomaly. The hook satisfies desire for pattern-discovery. This combination of intrigue-teaching, reliability-curiosity, anomaly-demonstration, and discovery-satisfaction creates audiences who investigate patterns understanding repeated-occurrences reveal hidden-mechanisms worth understanding.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when pattern is coincidence or when you overstate repetition-reliability. If you constantly claim consistent-patterns but correlations don't hold, you lose credibility. The hook also fails if you use repetition-claiming to suggest causation from correlation. If "every time" is actually selective-memory or pattern is spurious, framing misleads. Too much pattern-emphasis can reduce appreciation for how coincidences happen and create belief all repetitions indicate meaningful-patterns.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who see patterns everywhere confusing correlation with causation. You may attract people who use repetition-excuse to claim spurious-correlations as proven-patterns. The constant every-time emphasis can reduce appreciation for how statistical-coincidences occur regularly. If only seeing patterns never testing causation, followers develop superstitious-beliefs. This approach can also foster apophenia. The correlation emphasis may reduce understanding that while some repetitions indicate patterns, most correlations require causal-testing, and excessive pattern-seeking often produces seeing-patterns-in-randomness replacing rigorous analysis.
