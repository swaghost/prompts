# What I thought was true… wasn't

## Hook Group

Curiosity & Intrigue Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as belief-challenger experiencing paradigm-shifts. They see you as assumption-questioner. You appear as someone whose thought-true-wasn't emphasis demonstrates you undergo belief-revision enabling audience to question their own assumptions through your disconfirmation-journey.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about belief-reversal. Intellectually activates belief-disconfirmation thinking. Psychologically creates openness to revision. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "wasn't true" promises revealing that false-belief got corrected through discovery creating paradigm-shift.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because demonstrates belief disconfirmation - if thought true but wasn't, beliefs require questioning. This hook leverages appeal of intellectual-honesty where admitting-wrong demonstrates credibility. The thought-true-wasn't framing creates humility. By showing belief-reversal, you enable questioning-assumptions. This satisfies desire for truth through honest-admission of previous-error creating trust.

## Example

What I thought about hashtags was true… wasn't. Believed hashtags drove-growth. Reality showed hashtags had minimal-impact. Assumption proved wrong. Belief contradicted data. Previous-understanding required revision. Wrong-belief got corrected. Truth replaced assumption.

## Why it works

This hook works because it humbles through belief-reversal framing teaching intellectual-honesty. The wasn't-true creates credibility. By showing hashtag-belief correction, you demonstrate concrete disconfirmation. The hook satisfies desire for honest-guidance. This combination of humility-teaching, credibility-creation, disconfirmation-demonstration, and honesty-provision creates audiences who trust your guidance understanding admission-of-error indicates intellectual-honesty and commitment-to-truth over ego-protection.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you create false-beliefs to dramatically-correct or when constant-revision suggests unreliability. If you frequently claim belief-reversals, pattern suggests poor-initial-thinking. The hook also fails if you use disconfirmation-claiming to appear humble while actually-displaying-ignorance. If previous-belief was obviously-wrong, admission highlights incompetence not honesty. Too much revision-emphasis can reduce trust and create belief you lack stable-knowledge.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating perception of unreliable-knowledge where constant-belief-changes suggest unstable-understanding. You may attract audiences who distrust all-beliefs using disconfirmation-excuse to avoid commitment-to-positions. The constant wasn't-true emphasis can reduce confidence in your current-beliefs. If only revising-beliefs never maintaining-positions, followers question knowledge-stability. This approach can also foster epistemological-instability. The revision emphasis may reduce understanding that while some belief-corrections demonstrate growth, most expertise includes stable-knowledge-base, and excessive revision-claiming often produces perception-of-unreliability replacing credibility-of-expertise.
