# This might sound strange, but it works

## Hook Group

Curiosity & Intrigue Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as unconventional-methods advocate teaching counter-intuitive effectiveness. They see you as weird-equals-results teacher. You appear as someone whose strange-but-works emphasis demonstrates you discover unusual solutions enabling audience to explore non-traditional approaches.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about unconventional-approach. Intellectually activates weird-equals-memorable thinking. Psychologically creates openness to unusual-methods. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "might sound strange" promises revealing that unconventional approaches produce surprising results.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because positions unconventional as effective - if strange but works, unusual beats conventional. This hook leverages appeal of novelty where weird equals memorable. The might-sound-strange framing creates intrigue. By promising proven-effectiveness despite strangeness, you enable experimentation-permission. This satisfies desire for differentiation through unconventional-but-validated methods.

## Example

This might sound strange, but writing emails standing up improved my copy. Unconventional posture changed energy. Physical position affected mental state. Strange method produced better results. Unusual approach worked surprisingly well. Weird practice became effective habit. Strangeness didn't prevent effectiveness.

## Why it works

This hook works because it validates through strange-but-effective framing teaching unconventional-permission. The weird-memorable connection creates retention. By showing standing-up-writing example, you demonstrate concrete unusual-method. The hook satisfies desire for differentiation. This combination of validation-teaching, retention-creation, method-demonstration, and differentiation-provision creates audiences who experiment with unconventional approaches understanding strangeness doesn't prevent effectiveness and often enhances memorability.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you promote genuinely ineffective strange methods or when strangeness is mere gimmick. If you constantly advocate weird-for-weird's-sake, you may reduce credibility when unusual methods don't work. The hook also fails if you use strange-claiming to make ordinary advice seem novel. If method isn't actually strange or effectiveness isn't real, framing feels manipulative. Too much unconventional-emphasis can reduce appreciation for how proven traditional methods often work best and create belief weird automatically equals better.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who pursue strangeness over effectiveness chasing novelty without substance. You may attract people who use weird-excuse to justify ineffective methods claiming innovation. The constant strange-but-works emphasis can reduce appreciation for how most effective solutions are conventional for good reasons. If only seeking unusual never validating effectiveness, followers adopt gimmicks. This approach can also foster novelty-over-substance. The unconventional emphasis may reduce understanding that while some unusual methods work, most effectiveness comes from proven approaches, and excessive strangeness-seeking often produces gimmicks replacing substantive solutions.
