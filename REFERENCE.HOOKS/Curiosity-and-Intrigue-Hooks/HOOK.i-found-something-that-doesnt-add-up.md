# I found something that doesn't add up…

## Hook Group

Curiosity & Intrigue Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as investigative-analyst revealing inconsistencies. They see you as pattern-detector. You appear as someone whose doesn't-add-up emphasis demonstrates you possess critical-thinking skills enabling audience to trust your analysis of hidden problems.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers investigative curiosity. Intellectually activates curiosity-gap thinking. Psychologically creates need for resolution. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "doesn't add up" promises revealing what's wrong or hidden in familiar systems.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because creates curiosity gap - if something doesn't add up, people want to know what's "off." This hook leverages appeal of problem-detection where inconsistency signals hidden truth. The doesn't-add-up framing creates unresolved tension. By suggesting investigation-discovery, you enable mystery-solving satisfaction. This satisfies desire for understanding through inconsistency-resolution.

## Example

I found something that doesn't add up in Instagram's algorithm. High engagement posts not reaching explore page. Low engagement posts going viral. Numbers contradicted assumptions. Pattern revealed hidden factor. Inconsistency indicated discovery. Investigation uncovered truth.

## Why it works

This hook works because it creates urgency through inconsistency-detection teaching hidden-truth revelation. The doesn't-add-up creates unresolved tension. By showing Instagram algorithm contradiction, you demonstrate concrete mystery. The hook satisfies desire for discovery. This combination of urgency-creation, tension-building, mystery-demonstration, and discovery-satisfaction creates audiences who engage fully understanding resolution reveals important hidden information.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you create false mysteries or when inconsistency is manufactured. If you constantly claim things don't add up, you may lose credibility when revelations disappoint. The hook also fails if you use mystery-claiming without providing satisfying resolution. If "what doesn't add up" is trivial or forced, audience feels manipulated. Too much mystery-emphasis can reduce trust and create belief you manufacture problems for engagement.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who distrust everything seeking problems where none exist. You may attract conspiracy-minded people who use doesn't-add-up-excuse to question legitimate systems. The constant inconsistency-emphasis can reduce appreciation for how most systems work as expected. If only creating mysteries never providing substantive resolutions, followers become cynical. This approach can also foster paranoid-thinking. The investigation emphasis may reduce understanding that while critical analysis matters, most effective learning combines healthy skepticism with trust, and excessive mystery-framing often produces cynicism replacing constructive analysis.
