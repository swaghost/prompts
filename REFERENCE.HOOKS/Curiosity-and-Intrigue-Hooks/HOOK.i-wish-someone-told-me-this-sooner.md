# I wish someone told me this sooner

## Hook Group

Curiosity & Intrigue Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as wisdom-sharer preventing others-mistakes. They see you as time-saver. You appear as someone whose wish-told-sooner emphasis demonstrates you provide valuable-early-knowledge enabling audience to avoid wasted-time through accessing important-information earlier.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers FOMO about missed-knowledge. Intellectually activates FOMO-bias thinking. Psychologically creates urgency for information-access. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "told sooner" promises revealing that important-knowledge would've saved-time making audiences don't want to miss out on valuable-early-insight.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because leverages FOMO bias - if wish told sooner, early-knowledge prevents waste making audiences don't want to miss out. This hook leverages appeal of time-saving where early-information prevents mistakes. The wish-sooner framing creates regret-prevention urgency. By suggesting value-of-early-knowledge, you enable opportunity-seizing. This satisfies desire for advantage through accessing important-information now avoiding future-regret of late-discovery.

## Example

I wish someone told me this sooner about scaling an offer. Start-small beats launch-big. Gradual-build outperforms dramatic-launch. Early-small-wins enable sustainable-growth. Big-bang-launches often fail. Incremental-approach works better. Knowing-sooner would've prevented failed-big-launch. Early-knowledge would've saved time-money-effort.

## Why it works

This hook works because it urgency-creates through regret-prevention framing teaching early-knowledge value. The wish-sooner creates FOMO-motivation. By showing start-small beating launch-big, you demonstrate concrete valuable-early-insight. The hook satisfies desire for time-saving. This combination of urgency-teaching, FOMO-motivation, insight-demonstration, and saving-provision creates audiences who act immediately understanding early-knowledge prevents wasted-effort making now the right-time to learn avoiding future-regret.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when information isn't actually time-sensitive or when you manufacture urgency. If you constantly claim wish-told-sooner but knowledge doesn't require early-access, you lose credibility. The hook also fails if you use FOMO-claiming to pressure-audiences artificially. If timing doesn't actually matter, framing feels manipulative. Too much urgency-emphasis can reduce trust and create belief you manufacture false-time-sensitivity for engagement.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences with chronic-FOMO constantly-worried about missing-information. You may attract people who use wish-sooner-excuse to regret all-past-decisions claiming everything should've been known-earlier. The constant FOMO-bias emphasis can reduce appreciation for how learning-when-ready often works better than premature-information. If only focusing on missed-opportunities never appreciating right-timing, followers develop unhealthy-regret patterns. This approach can also foster chronic-FOMO. The regret emphasis may reduce understanding that while some information has optimal-timing, most knowledge works whenever learned, and excessive FOMO-creation often produces chronic-regret replacing healthy-learning-pace acceptance.
