# Why [trend] could be the future

## Hook Group

Trending Topics & Current Events Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

The viewer perceives you as trend-aware and practical, someone who translates current events into clear action.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally this creates immediacy, curiosity, and social relevance. Intellectually it frames context around what matters now. Psychologically it activates novelty bias, FOMO, and social proof mechanisms. Physically it drives scroll-stopping attention and comment/share behavior.

## Psychology

Future-bias — builds curiosity + authority.

## Example

Why Web3 could be the future of the internet.

## Why it works

This works because humans prioritize novel, socially relevant information tied to current momentum. Trend-linked framing lowers cognitive effort by giving people a familiar context and a clear reason to care now. When paired with a specific angle or stance, it increases discussion and memorability. The hook also taps identity signaling, which boosts engagement through agreement, disagreement, and sharing.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

It fails if the topic is already stale or only weakly related to the audience. If you chase trends without substance, credibility erodes quickly. Overusing controversy can attract low-quality engagement and reduce trust. Vague claims without evidence often look like clickbait and underperform over time.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

Over-indexing on trend content can make your brand feel reactive instead of principled. If predictions or takes age poorly, authority can drop fast. Polarizing angles may increase comments while damaging long-term audience fit. Depending too much on current events can reduce evergreen value and consistency.
