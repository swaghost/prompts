# Three Ways to Make [Trend] Feel Original

## Hook Group

Trend Hooks, Differentiation Hooks, Adaptation Hooks, Creativity Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

Viewers should perceive this as creative guidance for standing out while participating in trends. The creator should appear as someone who balances trend participation with originality.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

**Emotional:** Excitement about creative adaptation, relief at differentiation strategies, confidence in standing out

**Intellectual:** Strategic thinking about trend modification, understanding of differentiation, creativity activation

**Psychological:** Permission to participate in trends without feeling generic, belief in creative capacity, motivation to adapt uniquely

**Physical:** Attention for adaptation strategies, creative ideation, mental application to own niche

## Psychology

This hook addresses the tension creators feel around trends: wanting to participate for reach but not wanting to look like everyone else. The promise of making trends "feel original" offers the best of both worlds—trend participation with personal differentiation. The "three ways" structure creates actionable, digestible guidance rather than vague "be creative" advice. This hook works because it validates trend participation (you're not dismissing trends) while acknowledging the sameness problem (you recognize generic copying is an issue). The number three is psychologically optimal—substantial enough to feel valuable, manageable enough to implement without overwhelm. The hook also works because it reframes trends from constraint (forces you to copy) to canvas (foundation for creative expression). This shift empowers rather than restricts, making trend usage feel creative rather than derivative.

## Example

"Three ways to make any trend feel original: 1) Change the context—apply it to your unique niche angle. 2) Flip the perspective—show opposite side everyone misses. 3) Add your personality—make it unmistakably you."

"Three ways to make this trend feel original: 1) Start differently—same trend, different hook. 2) Unexpected twist—where others zig, you zag. 3) Behind-the-scenes—show your process adapting it."

"Three ways to make viral trends feel original: 1) Combine two trends. 2) Add data or receipts no one else shares. 3) Tell the story behind why it matters to you specifically."

## Why it works

This hook succeeds by offering practical creativity guidance rather than abstract "be yourself" advice. The three-way structure provides multiple options, respecting that different creators will resonate with different differentiation strategies. The hook also works because it acknowledges that originality within trends is both possible and valuable—you don't have to choose between trend participation and authenticity. The "feel original" framing is psychologically sophisticated; it's not about being completely unique but about perception of uniqueness, which is what matters for audience experience. The hook appeals to creators who want to use trends strategically but worry about being lost in the sameness. It also positions you as creative strategist who can articulate how differentiation happens, not just that it should happen. The specific number (three ways) makes the advice concrete and memorable.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails if your three ways are generic creativity advice that applies to all content, not specifically to trend adaptation—viewers need trend-specific tactics. It also fails if the differentiation strategies you recommend are so complex they defeat the efficiency of trend participation, making creators start from scratch anyway. The hook doesn't work if you can't demonstrate examples of each adaptation strategy applied to actual trends—theory without examples leaves viewers confused. Another failure mode: if your adaptations make content so different it no longer benefits from trend association, you've defeated the purpose of trend participation. The hook also falls flat if you present originality as always superior to strategic copying, when sometimes direct trend participation works better than adaptation. Finally, if your own trend content doesn't demonstrate obvious originality or differentiation, the advice lacks credibility.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can accidentally create pressure for forced originality where creators twist trends unnaturally trying to be different, making content feel contrived rather than authentic. It might also discourage straightforward trend participation that could work fine, replacing simple execution with overthinking. The differentiation emphasis could also create competitive differentiation arms race where everyone tries so hard to be original that trends fracture into incomprehensible variations, losing the shared reference that made them powerful. By providing three specific methods, you might also create formulaic differentiation where everyone applies the same "originality" techniques, paradoxically creating new sameness. The hook can also attract audiences obsessed with standing out rather than serving audiences well, prioritizing differentiation over value. Additionally, if the three adaptation strategies don't resonate with certain niches, content types, or creator personalities, viewers might force inappropriate tactics. The hook might also undervalue the legitimate benefit of trend alignment—sometimes being part of a larger conversation matters more than standing out within it, and emphasizing originality might make creators miss that point.
