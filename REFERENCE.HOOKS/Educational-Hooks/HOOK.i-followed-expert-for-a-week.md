# I followed [expert] for a week—here's what happened

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Vicarious experiment lowers risk for the viewer. This hook offers the results of following expert advice without the reader having to commit themselves, satisfying curiosity about whether the advice actually works.

## Example

I followed Tim Ferriss' morning routine for a week—results inside.

## Why it works

Experiment-based hooks are powerful because they satisfy curiosity while removing risk from the reader. By conducting the experiment themselves, the creator answers the "does this actually work?" question that readers have when encountering advice or routines. The time-bound nature ("for a week") makes the experiment feel manageable and replicable—it's not a lifestyle overhaul, it's a contained test. The phrase "here's what happened" creates a story structure with built-in suspense—there will be results, possibly surprising ones. This format also builds credibility because it demonstrates the creator is willing to test claims rather than just repeat them. The example leveraging Tim Ferriss is particularly effective because he's a well-known optimization expert whose routines are often discussed but rarely fully implemented. Readers are curious whether his advice works but hesitant to commit to trying it themselves. By serving as a proxy experimenter, the creator provides valuable market research while also demonstrating whether the routine is practical for normal people, not just optimization enthusiasts.
