# People always ask me: [FAQ]

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Social proof of demand. This hook uses frequent questions as validation that this topic is important and widely wondered about, making the answer feel valuable to share broadly.

## Example

People always ask: "How do I start a podcast?"

## Why it works

FAQ hooks work by leveraging social proof of demand—if people are "always" asking this question, it must be important and relevant to many others. The phrase "people always ask me" positions the creator as an authority who others turn to for guidance, building credibility. It also suggests the creator has answered this many times and has refined their response, so readers will get distilled wisdom rather than off-the-cuff thoughts. The hook makes readers feel less alone if they have the same question—they're not the only one wondering about this, which reduces embarrassment about not knowing. The conversational, quoted format of the question makes it feel authentic rather than manufactured, like the creator is genuinely responding to audience need rather than pushing their own agenda. The example "How do I start a podcast?" is particularly effective because it's a common aspiration that people don't know how to begin—they want to podcast but are overwhelmed by technical and strategic unknowns. The hook promises to answer the most common starting question, which is valuable for beginners. This works because it identifies the highest-demand question in a topic area and promises to address it directly, making the content feel responsive and useful. It positions the creator as a resource who understands what their audience actually needs rather than what they think the audience should need.
