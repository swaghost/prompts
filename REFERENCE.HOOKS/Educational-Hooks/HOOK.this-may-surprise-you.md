# This may surprise you…

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Open loop + counterintuitive claim. This hook creates curiosity by promising information that contradicts expectations or common assumptions, compelling readers to discover what the surprising insight is.

## Example

This may surprise you: breaks grow output, not kill it.

## Why it works

Surprise-based hooks work by creating an expectation gap that the brain wants to close. The phrase "this may surprise you" is a meta-statement that primes readers for unexpected information, which heightens attention and engagement. It works because our brains are wired to notice novelty and contradiction—surprising information is inherently more memorable and engaging than confirming what we already believe. The ellipsis creates an open loop, a psychological technique where incomplete information creates tension that can only be relieved by continuing. The hook works especially well when followed by a genuinely counterintuitive claim. The example about breaks is particularly effective because it contradicts the deeply ingrained productivity belief that more continuous work equals more output. For people raised on hustle culture, the idea that breaks improve rather than diminish productivity feels revolutionary. This creates both surprise and relief—permission to rest justified by performance data. The hook succeeds because it positions the creator as someone who has insider or scientific knowledge that challenges conventional wisdom, which builds authority while satisfying curiosity.
