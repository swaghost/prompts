# My go-to tip for making \_\_\_ easier

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

"Go-to" suggests proven. This hook shares the creator's default, most reliable method for simplifying a challenging task, implying it's been tested repeatedly and consistently works.

## Example

My go-to tip for meal planning: theme nights.

## Why it works

Go-to hooks work by sharing what the creator actually uses rather than theoretical best practices. The phrase "go-to" implies this is the creator's default, reliable solution they return to repeatedly because it works consistently. This provides strong social proof—not "here's something I heard works" but "here's what I personally rely on." The focus on making something "easier" rather than "better" or "perfect" is psychologically smart because it acknowledges the task is legitimately difficult and promises relief rather than excellence. This is more appealing to people who are struggling or overwhelmed. The hook also benefits from specificity—it's not "my tips" (plural) but "my tip" (singular), suggesting focus on the highest-impact intervention. The example about meal planning is particularly effective because meal planning is a classic example of something everyone knows they should do but many find overwhelming. "Theme nights" is brilliantly simple: Taco Tuesday, Pasta Wednesday, etc. This removes the cognitive load of deciding what to cook while providing enough structure to make shopping easier. The concept is immediately understandable and implementable. The hook works because it shares battle-tested practical wisdom rather than untested ideas, making readers feel they're getting insider knowledge about what actually works in real life, not just theory.
