# Read this before you try [tool/service]

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Pre-purchase "safety" increases attention and saves regret. This hook positions itself as essential reading before making a decision, suggesting it contains warnings, tips, or insights that could prevent costly mistakes.

## Example

Read this before you try an all-in-one CRM.

## Why it works

This hook leverages loss aversion—the fear of making a mistake or wasting resources. By positioning the content as essential pre-decision reading, it suggests there are non-obvious pitfalls or considerations that could be costly to overlook. The phrase "before you try" creates urgency without pressure—it's not "don't try this," it's "be informed first," which feels helpful rather than discouraging. This makes the hook feel like protective advice from someone who has your best interests at heart. The hook works particularly well for expensive purchases, time commitments, or complex tools where mistakes are costly to reverse. The example about all-in-one CRMs is effective because these systems typically require significant implementation time, migration effort, and team training—making a wrong choice is expensive. The hook promises to share insights that could either validate the reader's choice, steer them toward better alternatives, or help them avoid common setup mistakes. This advisory tone builds trust and positions the creator as an experienced guide worth listening to before making consequential decisions.
