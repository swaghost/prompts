# Why your [solution] isn't working

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Diagnoses justify a tuned prescription. This hook validates that the reader has tried the right general approach but explains why it's failing, promising corrective adjustments rather than completely new direction.

## Example

Why your time-blocking isn't working—buffers matter.

## Why it works

Diagnostic hooks work by validating existing effort while explaining failure. By focusing on "why your [solution] isn't working" rather than "why you're failing," the hook respects that the reader chose a legitimate approach. This removes shame and positions the problem as technical implementation rather than personal failure. The hook implies the solution itself is sound but requires refinement or missing elements, which is more actionable than "start over with something completely different." The promise of diagnosis creates hope—once you understand what's wrong, fixing it becomes straightforward. The example about time-blocking is particularly effective because time-blocking is widely recommended productivity advice that many people try but abandon when it fails. The insight "buffers matter" identifies a specific, common oversight: people create perfectly optimized schedules with no room for the unexpected, which makes them fragile and demoralizing when reality intrudes. Adding buffer time makes time-blocking sustainable. This hook works because it explains why good advice fails in practice, which is often more valuable than the advice itself. It positions the creator as someone who understands implementation realities, not just theory.
