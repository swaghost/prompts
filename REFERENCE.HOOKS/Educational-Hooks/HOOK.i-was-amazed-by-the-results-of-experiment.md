# I was amazed by the results of [experiment]

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Experiment framing signals rigor. This hook uses the language of experimentation to suggest systematic testing and measurable results, which builds credibility and trust in the findings.

## Example

Four-day workweek trial: output rose, stress fell.

## Why it works

Experiment-based hooks work because they promise empirical results rather than opinions or theories. The word "experiment" suggests controlled testing with before/after measurement, which satisfies our desire for evidence-based information. "I was amazed" adds authentic surprise, suggesting the results were unexpected even to the tester, which makes them more credible—this isn't confirmation bias, it's genuine discovery. The structure implies the creator took a hypothesis, tested it systematically, and measured outcomes, which positions them as scientifically-minded and rigorous. The example about the four-day workweek is particularly compelling because it's a controversial policy change that many companies fear will reduce productivity. By framing it as an experiment with measurable results ("output rose, stress fell"), the hook provides evidence for both economic (productivity) and human (wellbeing) benefits. The parallel structure of the results creates rhetorical balance that makes the findings memorable. The hook works because it transforms a contentious topic from opinion-based debate to data-driven discovery, which is far more persuasive and shareable. It suggests that unconventional approaches deserve testing rather than dismissal.
