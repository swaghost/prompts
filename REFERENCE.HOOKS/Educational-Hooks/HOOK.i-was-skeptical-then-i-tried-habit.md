# I was skeptical, then I tried [habit]

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Authentic reversal. This hook takes readers through a credibility-building journey from initial resistance to eventual adoption, making the endorsement feel earned rather than enthusiastic promotion.

## Example

I was skeptical about daily cold walks—now I'm hooked.

## Why it works

Skeptic-reversal hooks work by acknowledging that the creator's initial position matched the reader's likely current position, creating identification and trust. The phrase "I was skeptical" validates reader doubt—if the creator was skeptical, the reader's skepticism is reasonable, not closed-minded. This reduces defensiveness and opens receptivity to the argument. The phrase "then I tried" marks the pivot point where experimentation overcame theoretical doubt, suggesting direct experience was convincing in ways that arguments weren't. This implies readers should also prioritize trying over analyzing. The progression from skeptical to "hooked" in the example shows complete reversal—not just "it works" but genuine enthusiasm. The example about cold walks (walking outdoors in cold weather) addresses a practice many people dismiss as uncomfortable or unnecessary. The combination of "daily" (consistency), "cold" (perceived hardship), and "walks" (simple activity) creates a practice that sounds both demanding and mundane. The revelation of being "hooked" suggests the experience is actually enjoyable or rewarding in unexpected ways, creating curiosity about what makes it compelling. The hook works because it models the exact transformation the creator wants readers to experience, showing that skepticism can be overcome through direct experimentation rather than just argument.
