# I used to believe [belief], but here's what changed

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Vulnerability + data = credibility. This hook builds trust by admitting the creator once held a different view, then explaining what evidence or experience changed their mind.

## Example

I used to believe multitasking works—then I tracked it.

## Why it works

Belief-change hooks work because they demonstrate intellectual honesty and evolution. By admitting "I used to believe" something different, the creator shows vulnerability and relatability—they weren't always right, which makes them more trustworthy than someone claiming to have always known the truth. This also creates a learning journey that readers can follow: if the creator changed their mind based on evidence, readers can too. The phrase "but here's what changed" promises an explanation, suggesting the shift wasn't arbitrary but based on compelling reasons. This creates curiosity about what evidence was strong enough to overcome the original belief. The structure also subtly validates readers who currently hold the old belief—they're not stupid for believing it, even the creator once thought the same way. The example about multitasking is particularly effective because multitasking is widely practiced despite research showing it's ineffective. The phrase "then I tracked it" suggests empirical self-testing, which is more credible than just reading studies. This implies the creator discovered through personal measurement that multitasking reduced rather than increased productivity. The hook works because it combines relatability, evidence-based thinking, and a clear pivot point that invites the reader to make the same shift.
