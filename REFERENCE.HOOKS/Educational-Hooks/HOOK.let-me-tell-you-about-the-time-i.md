# Let me tell you about the time I \_\_\_

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Narrative stickiness. This hook uses storytelling structure to engage readers, making lessons more memorable and emotionally resonant than straightforward advice.

## Example

The time I pitched with no slides—and won.

## Why it works

Story-invitation hooks work by engaging the brain's natural preference for narrative over instruction. The phrase "let me tell you about the time" creates an intimate, conversational tone that feels like sharing experiences with a friend rather than formal teaching. This lowers defenses and increases receptivity. Stories are more memorable than facts or tips because they create emotional engagement and visual imagery that aid recall. The structure also creates anticipation—there's a story coming with a beginning, middle, and end. The example about pitching without slides is particularly intriguing because it violates conventional business wisdom (professional presentations need polished decks), creating curiosity about how this unconventional approach succeeded. The "and won" ending provides the payoff that makes the rule-breaking worthwhile and suggests there's a valuable lesson about what truly matters in presentations (connection, clarity, confidence) versus what's merely conventional (slides). The hook works because it promises both entertainment (a good story) and education (a lesson from the experience). Stories also provide social proof through personal testimony, making advice feel field-tested rather than theoretical. The narrative structure makes the lesson stickier and more shareable than equivalent advice delivered as tips.
