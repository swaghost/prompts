# I tried [practice]—here's what surprised me

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Unexpected upside invites adoption. This hook creates curiosity by suggesting the practice had surprising benefits beyond the expected outcomes, making it more compelling than straightforward advocacy.

## Example

I tried meal-prepping—Sundays saved me 7 hours/week.

## Why it works

Surprise-outcome hooks work because they promise discovery beyond obvious benefits. The phrase "here's what surprised me" creates an information gap—readers know the basic premise of the practice but are curious about the unexpected finding. This structure is more compelling than "I tried X and it worked" because it suggests there's hidden value that won't be obvious until you try it yourself. The hook acknowledges that the creator had expectations but reality exceeded or differed from them, which feels more credible than unqualified enthusiasm. The example about meal-prepping is particularly effective because it quantifies an unexpected time savings: the practice typically associated with spending time (Sunday meal prep) actually saves much more time during the week (7 hours). This reframes meal prep from a time-consuming chore to a time-investment strategy with positive ROI. The specificity of "7 hours/week" makes the claim concrete and believable. This hook works because it reframes practices that might seem burdensome by revealing counterintuitive benefits. It invites readers to reconsider things they've dismissed or avoided by suggesting their understanding is incomplete.
