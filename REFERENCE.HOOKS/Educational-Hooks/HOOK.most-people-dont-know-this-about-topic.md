# Most people don't know this about [topic]

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Insider tip FOMO. This hook creates curiosity by suggesting there's important information about a topic that isn't widely known, making readers feel they might be missing something valuable.

## Example

Most people don't know pricing psychology basics.

## Why it works

Unknown-information hooks work by creating information FOMO (fear of missing out). The phrase "most people don't know" implies that the reader might be among the uninformed majority, which creates mild anxiety that can only be relieved by learning what's being missed. It also suggests that knowing this information creates competitive advantage—if most people don't know it, those who do have an edge. The phrase is less confrontational than "you don't know" because it's not directly accusatory, making it easier to accept without feeling attacked. It positions the information as specialized or insider knowledge rather than basic facts everyone should know. The example about pricing psychology is particularly effective because pricing decisions have massive business impact yet most entrepreneurs and even marketers don't understand the psychological principles (anchoring, charm pricing, decoy effect, price-quality heuristic) that influence purchase decisions. This gap between importance and awareness makes the hook compelling. The hook works because it identifies knowledge gaps in areas where that knowledge would be valuable, creating urgency to close the gap. It positions the creator as someone with insider or expert knowledge willing to share what usually isn't discussed openly, building both authority and generosity. The specificity of "basics" makes the missing knowledge feel both accessible and foundational rather than advanced.
