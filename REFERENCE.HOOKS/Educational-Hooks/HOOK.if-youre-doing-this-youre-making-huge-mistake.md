# If you're doing this, you're making a huge mistake

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Fear of avoidable loss. This hook creates urgency and concern by suggesting current behavior is actively harmful, motivating immediate attention to understand and correct the error.

## Example

If you're posting and ghosting, you're wasting reach.

## Why it works

Warning hooks work by triggering loss aversion—the psychological principle that people are more motivated to avoid losses than pursue gains. The phrase "you're making a huge mistake" creates immediate concern and urgency: "what am I doing wrong?" The word "huge" escalates the severity, suggesting this isn't a minor optimization but a significant error with real costs. The conditional "if you're doing this" creates a checkpoint where readers assess whether the warning applies to them, and those who recognize the behavior feel compelled to learn why it's wrong. The hook is particularly effective when the mistake is common—if many people are making this error, it validates that it's an easy trap to fall into while also meaning the correction will be widely valuable. The example about "posting and ghosting" (creating content without engaging with respondents) is effective because it's an extremely common behavior, especially among busy creators, yet it demonstrably wastes the algorithmic and social benefits of engagement. The phrase "wasting reach" quantifies the cost, making the mistake tangible rather than vague. This hook works because it combines fear (making a huge mistake) with relief (it's fixable) and community (others make this mistake too).
