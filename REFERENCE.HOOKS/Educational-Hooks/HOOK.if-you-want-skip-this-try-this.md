# If you want ___, skip this and try this

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Loss-avoidance + contrast frames the new tactic as safer. This hook leverages the psychological principle that people are more motivated to avoid mistakes than to pursue gains. By explicitly identifying what to avoid and what to do instead, it provides clear directional guidance.

## Example

If you want focus, skip multitasking and try 50-minute sprints.

## Why it works

This hook works by creating a clear before/after comparison that helps readers immediately identify whether they're on the wrong path. The structure "skip this and try this" provides both warning and solution in one compact statement, which feels like insider advice that could save time and frustration. The psychology of contrast makes the recommended approach seem more effective simply by virtue of being compared to a flawed alternative. By leading with "If you want ___," the hook ensures readers are emotionally invested in the goal before presenting the choice. The word "skip" is powerful because it suggests the reader may currently be wasting effort on something ineffective, creating mild anxiety that can only be resolved by learning the better alternative. The example is particularly effective because multitasking is a widely practiced but scientifically debunked approach, making the advice feel both validating (for those who struggle with it) and authoritative.
