# Here's why [common belief] is a myth

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Belief-violation grabs attention and invites re-evaluation. This hook challenges widely accepted assumptions, creating cognitive dissonance that compels readers to learn why their belief might be wrong.

## Example

Here's why "eight glasses a day" is a myth.

## Why it works

Myth-busting hooks are powerful because they challenge the reader's existing mental models, creating an information gap that demands resolution. When something we believe to be true is called into question, it triggers curiosity and mild anxiety—we need to know if we've been wrong and what the truth actually is. The word "myth" is particularly effective because it doesn't just suggest the belief is incorrect; it implies the belief is widely held but false, which makes readers wonder how many other people are also mistaken. This creates social curiosity ("everyone believes this, but it's wrong") alongside personal curiosity. The example about "eight glasses a day" is especially effective because it's a health belief many people have repeated without questioning, making the revelation both surprising and relevant. The phrase "Here's why" promises not just contradiction but explanation, which builds credibility—the creator isn't just saying something is wrong, they're going to prove it. This hook works particularly well for educational content because it positions the creator as a myth-debunker with authoritative knowledge.
