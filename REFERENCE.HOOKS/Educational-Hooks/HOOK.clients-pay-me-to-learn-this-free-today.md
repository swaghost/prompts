# Clients pay me $\_\_\_ to learn this—free today

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Anchoring value. This hook uses price anchoring to make free content feel exceptionally valuable by revealing what others pay for the same information.

## Example

Clients pay me $500 for this IG system—free today.

## Why it works

Value-anchoring hooks work by establishing a high price point that creates perceived value for free content. When readers learn that others pay $500 for this information, receiving it for free feels like getting exceptional value—a $500 value for $0. This leverages anchoring bias: the first number we hear influences our perception of value. By stating the paid price first, the creator anchors expectations high before revealing it's free. The phrase "clients pay me" adds credibility through social proof—this isn't just theoretical value, actual clients pay real money for this information, validating its worth. The word "clients" also positions the creator as a professional who sells this knowledge, building authority. "Free today" creates urgency through temporal limitation—while it's free now, the implication is that it might not always be, creating scarcity motivation. The example about an "IG system" is effective because "system" suggests comprehensive, implementable methodology rather than single tips. The specificity of $500 makes the value concrete and believable—not $10,000 (too high to be credible for social media advice) but substantial enough to feel significant. The hook works because it reframes free content from "worth nothing" to "exceptionally valuable but being offered free," which dramatically increases perceived value and engagement. It positions the creator as generous expert rather than just another content producer.
