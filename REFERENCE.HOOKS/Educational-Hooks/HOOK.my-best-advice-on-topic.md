# My BEST advice on [topic]

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

"Best" primes attention. The capitalization or emphasis on "BEST" signals that this is the creator's most important insight, cutting through all other advice to deliver what truly matters.

## Example

My BEST time-management advice: work in themes.

## Why it works

Superlative hooks work by claiming to deliver the single most important piece of advice, which is valuable in information-overloaded fields where people suffer from too many options rather than too few. The emphasis (all-caps "BEST") creates a promise that this isn't just good advice but the pinnacle—if you only take one thing away, this should be it. This cuts through noise and demands attention. The first-person "my BEST" makes it personal rather than claiming objective universality, which paradoxically makes it more credible—this is hard-won personal wisdom, not generic platitudes. The structure also suggests the creator has tested many approaches and this rose to the top, implying experience-based validation. The example about time management is effective because it's a category drowning in advice (time blocking, Pomodoro, Getting Things Done, etc.), making a clear "best advice" declaration genuinely valuable. "Work in themes" means dedicating different days to different types of work (creative days, meeting days, admin days) rather than mixing everything daily. This is sophisticated advice that requires understanding but produces significant improvements in cognitive load and context-switching costs. The hook works because it positions one insight as supremely important, giving readers permission to ignore everything else and focus on this one principle, which is psychologically relieving in an age of endless optimization advice.
