# How to [verb] a [topic] in 3 simple steps

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Simplicity + structure. This hook promises to break down a potentially complex process into just three manageable steps, making achievement feel accessible and clearly mapped.

## Example

How to grow a channel in 3 steps.

## Why it works

Three-step hooks work because they combine structural clarity with psychological accessibility. The number three is particularly powerful—it's the minimum needed to feel like a complete system (one step feels trivial, two feels incomplete) while still being simple enough to remember and execute. This "rule of three" is recognized in rhetoric, storytelling, and teaching as optimally memorable. The word "simple" is crucial because it explicitly promises that these steps won't be complex or overwhelming, removing the fear of difficulty as a barrier. The structure "how to [verb] [topic]" provides clear action orientation—readers know exactly what outcome they're working toward. The format also implies that these three steps are sufficient—you don't need a 47-point plan, just these three essentials executed well. The example about growing a channel is effective because channel growth is typically presented as requiring constant content, perfect thumbnails, algorithm understanding, SEO, promotion, collaboration, and more. Distilling it to three steps (perhaps: 1. Choose a focused niche, 2. Optimize first 30 seconds of every video, 3. Be consistent with publishing schedule) makes it feel achievable rather than overwhelming. The hook works because it promises both simplicity and completeness, suggesting readers can achieve meaningful results without complexity or confusion. It positions success as systematic rather than mysterious.
