# My journey from [start] to [win]

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Transformation narrative. This hook uses a hero's journey structure to show progression from starting point to success, making the path feel replicable and inspiring.

## Example

From broke to booked—what changed.

## Why it works

Journey hooks work by providing both relatability (the starting point) and aspiration (the ending point) in one frame. The phrase "from X to Y" creates a clear before/after comparison that humans find compelling—we're wired to recognize and learn from transformation stories. The starting point should be relatable to the target audience (broke, struggling, unknown) while the ending should represent success they desire (booked, profitable, recognized). The phrase "what changed" or similar is crucial because it promises to reveal the inflection point or key decisions that enabled the transformation, which is the information readers actually want. The example "from broke to booked" is particularly effective because it captures a complete economic transformation for freelancers or service providers: from financially struggling with no clients to having a full calendar. The alliteration (broke/booked) makes it memorable and punchy. The hook works because it promises to decode success by showing the actual path between starting and winning, including the key decisions, mistakes, and insights that made the difference. It positions success as achievable through specific actions rather than luck or innate talent, which is empowering. Journey narratives also build trust through vulnerability—sharing the difficult starting point demonstrates honesty and relatability rather than just boasting about the endpoint.
