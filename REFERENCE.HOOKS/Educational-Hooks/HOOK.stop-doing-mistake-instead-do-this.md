# Stop doing [mistake]. Instead, do this

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Replace, don't just remove. This hook addresses a common error by providing an alternative action rather than just warning against the mistake, making the advice more actionable.

## Example

Stop cold emailing. Instead, warm up intent.

## Why it works

Replacement hooks work by addressing behavioral change through substitution rather than elimination. Psychological research shows that replacing bad habits with good ones is more effective than just trying to stop the bad habit, because it fills the void rather than leaving emptiness. The structure "stop X, instead Y" acknowledges what people are currently doing wrong while immediately providing the correct alternative, making action clear and immediate. The word "instead" is crucial—it's not additional work but a different approach to the same goal, which feels manageable. The contrast format makes the difference memorable and actionable. The example about cold emailing is particularly effective because cold outreach is a common business development tactic that has become increasingly ineffective due to oversaturation. "Warm up intent" means engaging with prospects through content, comments, or mutual connections before the ask, creating familiarity that dramatically improves response rates. This isn't just "don't cold email" (which leaves people wondering how to reach prospects) but provides a strategic alternative. The hook works because it corrects course without just saying "don't," which is often ineffective. It respects that people are trying to achieve legitimate goals (reach prospects, make sales) but doing it ineffectively, and provides a better path to the same destination.
