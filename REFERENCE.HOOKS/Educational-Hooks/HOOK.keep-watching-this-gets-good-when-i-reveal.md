# Keep watching—this gets good when I reveal \_\_\_

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Teased payoff increases retention. This hook explicitly promises a valuable revelation coming later, creating an open loop that viewers want to see closed.

## Example

Keep watching—at 0:30 I show the script.

## Why it works

Retention-optimization hooks work by creating explicit expectation for a specific payoff at a defined moment. This leverages the psychological principle of open loops—once a promise is made, our brains want resolution. The phrase "keep watching" acknowledges that viewer attention isn't guaranteed and directly asks for continued engagement. The phrase "this gets good when" implies that what comes before may be setup or context, but there's a clear payoff moment worth waiting for. The specificity of timing ("at 0:30") is crucial for video content because it tells viewers exactly how long they need to wait, making the commitment feel manageable. If the payoff was at 8 minutes, some would bail, but 30 seconds is easy to commit to. The word "reveal" creates anticipation through implying something hidden or exclusive is about to be shown. The example about showing "the script" promises concrete, implementable value—not just talking about principles but showing the actual artifact viewers can use or model. This hook works particularly well for video content where retention directly affects algorithmic distribution. It's psychologically effective because it front-loads the value proposition (I will show you something specific) while creating a mini-commitment device (stick around for 30 seconds). The specificity of both timing and payoff makes the promise feel credible rather than manipulation.
