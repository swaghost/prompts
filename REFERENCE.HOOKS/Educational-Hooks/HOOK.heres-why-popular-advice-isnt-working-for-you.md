# Here's why [popular advice] isn't working for you

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Empathy + promise of a targeted fix. This hook acknowledges frustration with advice that seems to work for others but not for the reader, then promises personalized understanding and solutions.

## Example

Here's why "eat less, move more" isn't working long-term.

## Why it works

This hook is powerful because it validates a deeply frustrating experience: following advice that everyone says works, but not seeing results. This creates a sense of isolation and self-blame—"everyone else can do this, what's wrong with me?" By addressing this directly and saying the advice "isn't working for you," the hook immediately relieves that shame and reframes the problem from personal failing to flawed advice. The word "you" makes it personal and relevant, suggesting the creator understands individual variation and context rather than offering one-size-fits-all solutions. The phrase "isn't working" implies the reader has genuinely tried, which respects their effort rather than assuming laziness. The example about "eat less, move more" is particularly resonant because it's advice that's technically true but practically useless—it ignores metabolic adaptation, hunger signals, sustainability, and behavioral psychology. By targeting advice that's overly simplistic, the hook positions the creator as someone with deeper, more sophisticated understanding. This builds trust and hope that finally, someone will provide advice that actually works for the reader's specific situation.
