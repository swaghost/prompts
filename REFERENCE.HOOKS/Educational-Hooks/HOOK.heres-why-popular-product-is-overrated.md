# Here's why [popular product] is overrated

## Hook Group

Educational Hooks

## Psychology

Counter-trend stance sparks debate and clicks. This hook takes a contrarian position against something widely praised or popular, which creates controversy and makes people want to see the justification.

## Example

Here's why luxury face creams are overrated for anti-aging.

## Why it works

Contrarian takes are psychologically compelling because they challenge social consensus and herd behavior. When everyone seems to love something, a voice saying "actually, it's overrated" creates immediate intrigue—either the creator knows something others don't, or they're going to embarrass themselves, and either outcome is worth watching. The word "overrated" is more sophisticated than "bad" or "doesn't work"—it acknowledges that the product has value but suggests the hype exceeds the reality, which feels like a more nuanced and credible position. This hook particularly resonates with people who have tried the popular product and been disappointed, or with skeptics who suspected the hype was manufactured. The example about luxury face creams is effective because the beauty industry is full of expensive products with bold claims but minimal scientific backing. By targeting a category where price doesn't necessarily equal results, the hook promises to save readers money while delivering potentially better alternatives. The controversy potential also increases shareability—people will forward this to friends to warn them or debate the claim.
