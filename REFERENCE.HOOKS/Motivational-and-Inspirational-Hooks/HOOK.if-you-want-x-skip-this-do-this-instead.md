# If you want X, skip this—do this instead

## Hook Group

Motivational & Inspirational Hooks

## Psychology

Contrast framing (replace bad habit with a better one). This hook leverages the psychological principle of substitution rather than elimination. Instead of just telling people what NOT to do (which creates a void), it immediately offers a superior alternative, making the change feel less like a sacrifice and more like an upgrade.

## Example

If you want consistent fat loss, skip detox teas—track protein.

## Why it works

Contrast framing is effective because it reframes the decision from "should I stop doing this thing I'm currently doing?" to "which of these two options is better?" This subtle shift makes the choice feel less like deprivation and more like optimization. By leading with the desired outcome ("If you want X"), the hook ensures the reader is already emotionally invested before presenting the options. The pattern "skip this—do this instead" creates a direct comparison that highlights the superiority of the alternative. In the example, "detox teas" represents the popular-but-ineffective approach many people are currently using, while "track protein" offers a science-backed alternative. The hook works particularly well because it positions the creator as someone who has insider knowledge—they know what doesn't work and what does—which builds authority and trust. Readers feel like they're being let in on a secret that will save them time and effort.
