# Stop doing this if you want consistent wins

## Hook Group

Motivational & Inspirational Hooks

## Psychology

Loss framing (what you lose by not changing). This hook uses the psychological principle that people are more motivated to avoid losses than to pursue gains. By framing the current behavior as something that's preventing desired outcomes ("consistent wins"), it creates urgency to identify and eliminate the problematic behavior.

## Example

Stop starting five tasks at once; finish one, then move.

## Why it works

Loss framing is one of the most powerful psychological principles in behavioral economics. People feel the pain of loss approximately twice as strongly as the pleasure of gain, making "what you're losing" more motivating than "what you could gain." The command "Stop doing this" creates curiosity (what am I doing wrong?) combined with concern (am I sabotaging myself?). The conditional "if you want consistent wins" is clever because it doesn't assume everyone wants this—it self-selects for people who do, making them feel personally called out. The phrase "consistent wins" is appealing because it promises not just success, but reliable, repeatable success. The example reveals a behavior pattern many people recognize in themselves: task-switching and lack of follow-through. "Stop starting five tasks at once" is specific enough to be immediately relatable, while "finish one, then move" provides a clear alternative. This creates an "aha" moment where readers realize their strategy has been counterproductive, making them eager to learn the better approach.
