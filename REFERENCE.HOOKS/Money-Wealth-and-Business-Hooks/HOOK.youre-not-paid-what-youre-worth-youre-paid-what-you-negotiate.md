# You're not paid what you're worth. You're paid what you negotiate

## Hook Group

Money, Wealth & Business Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as negotiation-empowerment teacher revealing compensation-control. They see you as worth-versus-ask expert. You appear as someone whose negotiation-emphasis demonstrates you understand assertive-asking determines income enabling audience to pursue negotiation over passive-acceptance.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers recognition of passive-acceptance. Intellectually activates control-reframe thinking. Psychologically creates empowerment through agency. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "paid what you negotiate" promises revealing that assertiveness not inherent-worth determines compensation.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because uses control reframe - if paid by negotiation not worth, taking control enables higher income. This hook leverages appeal of agency where assertiveness determines outcomes. The not-worth-but-negotiate framing creates empowerment. By emphasizing negotiation, you enable income-control. This satisfies desire for compensation-agency through asking.

## Example

Same job, higher pay—because I asked. Identical work generated different compensation based on negotiation. Worth was equal. Pay differed because I advocated. Asking determined income. Silence accepted baseline. Negotiation created premium. Assertion beat acceptance.

## Why it works

This hook works because it empowers through negotiation-emphasis teaching compensation-control. The worth-versus-negotiate reframe creates agency. By showing same-job-higher-pay from asking, you demonstrate negotiation-power. The hook satisfies desire for income-control. This combination of empowerment-teaching, agency-creation, power-demonstration, and negotiation-emphasis creates audiences who assertively negotiate understanding asking determines pay regardless of inherent worth.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you dismiss workplace power-dynamics or when you suggest all compensation is negotiable. If you constantly advocate asking, you may ignore contexts where negotiation creates backlash. The hook also fails if you use negotiation-claiming to blame individuals for systemic underpayment. If structural pay-inequity exists, individual-negotiation-only is incomplete. Too much negotiation-emphasis can reduce appreciation for how power-dynamics limit negotiation-effectiveness and create belief all low-pay results from not-asking.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who negotiate aggressively in inappropriate contexts damaging relationships. You may attract people who use worth-excuse to shame those accepting offered compensation. The constant negotiation-emphasis can reduce appreciation for how structural pay-inequity requires systemic solutions. If only negotiating individually, followers may miss collective-action opportunities. This approach can also foster blame-individuals for structural underpayment. The agency emphasis may reduce understanding that while negotiation matters, most fair compensation requires both individual assertiveness AND systemic equity, and pure negotiation-focus often blames workers for employer underpayment.
