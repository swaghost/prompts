# Want more money? Solve bigger problems

## Hook Group

Money, Wealth & Business Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as value-creation teacher linking problem-scale to compensation. They see you as impact-maximizer. You appear as someone whose solve-bigger-problems emphasis demonstrates you understand problem-magnitude determines income enabling audience to pursue high-value problem-solving.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers ambition for bigger impact. Intellectually activates value-creation thinking. Psychologically creates motivation for problem-scale increase. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "solve bigger problems" promises revealing that problem-magnitude determines compensation-level.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because links money to value-creation - if bigger problems mean more money, income follows impact-scale. This hook leverages appeal of merit-based-reward where value determines compensation. The want-money-solve-problems framing creates direction. By emphasizing problem-scale, you enable value-focus. This satisfies desire for income-increase through impact-maximization.

## Example

Solving individual customer problems = decent income. Solving industry-wide problems = significant income. Small impact generated small compensation. Large impact generated large compensation. Problem-scale determined value-scale. Bigger problems commanded bigger rewards. Impact determined income.

## Why it works

This hook works because it directs through value-linkage teaching problem-scale-determines-income. The want-more framing creates motivation. By contrasting individual versus industry problems, you demonstrate scale-effect. The hook satisfies desire for income-increase framework. This combination of direction-teaching, motivation-creation, scale-demonstration, and framework-provision creates audiences who pursue bigger problems understanding impact-magnitude determines compensation-level.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you oversimplify income-determination or when you dismiss structural pay-inequity. If you constantly link compensation purely to problem-scale, you may ignore how market-power and negotiation affect pay. The hook also fails if you use value-claiming to suggest all low-income results from small-impact. If structural factors limit compensation regardless of value-created, only-problem-scale is incomplete. Too much merit-emphasis can reduce appreciation for how compensation reflects power-dynamics not just value and create belief all low-pay means small-contribution.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who overestimate problem-scale-to-compensation link ignoring market-realities. You may attract people who use solve-bigger-excuse to devalue necessary smaller-scale work. The constant bigger-problems emphasis can reduce appreciation for how many high-value contributions involve small-scale precision. If only pursuing big problems never executing well, followers may chase scale without competence. This approach can also foster devalue-small-contributions. The scale emphasis may reduce understanding that while problem-magnitude matters, most fair compensation requires both value-creation AND market-power, and pure value-focus often ignores how power-dynamics determine pay regardless of contribution.
