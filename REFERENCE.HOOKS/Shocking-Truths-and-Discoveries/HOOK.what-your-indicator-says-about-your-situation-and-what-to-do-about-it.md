# What your [indicator] says about your [situation] (and what to do about it)

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as diagnostic about indirect indicators revealing situation status. They see you as analytical about what signals mean. You appear as someone whose indicator-interpretation provides both assessment and guidance.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers self-assessment about own indicators. Intellectually activates diagnostic thinking about what signals reveal. Psychologically creates motivation to address revealed situation. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "what indicator says about situation" promises diagnostic insight plus actionable guidance.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because indirect indicators provide situation assessment audiences can self-diagnose - understanding what signals reveal enables targeted intervention. This hook leverages appeal of diagnostics where interpreting indicators enables early detection and correction. The "what it says" framing treats indicator as revealing information. By adding "what to do," you provide complete guidance. This satisfies desire for both assessment and action direction.

## Example

What your calendar says about your priorities (and what to do about it): if your scheduled time doesn't match stated values, values are aspirations not priorities - priorities are what you actually do not what you wish mattered. Check: compare time-spent to values-stated. Fix: schedule values-aligned time first, treat as non-negotiable, let other things fill gaps.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides diagnostic framework enabling self-assessment plus correction guidance. The indicator-interpretation framing creates indirect revelation. By offering action guidance, you prevent diagnostic paralysis. The hook satisfies desire for assessment and direction. This combination of diagnostic provision, self-assessment enablement, action guidance, and complete framework creates diagnostically improved audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when indicator you describe doesn't reliably reveal situation or when interpretation you provide is oversimplified. If you constantly interpret indicators, formula becomes repetitive. The hook also fails if you create anxiety through indicator-monitoring rather than helpful awareness. If diagnostic interpretation is wrong, you've encouraged misunderstanding situation. Too many indicator-says frameworks can make audiences obsessively self-monitor rather than act.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who constantly analyze indicators rather than addressing situations directly. You may attract people who prefer diagnostic frameworks over practical action. The constant indicator focus can reduce direct situation assessment through obviou observation. If your indicator interpretation is inaccurate, you've created false diagnostics. This approach can also foster hypervigilance where audiences seek revealing indicators everywhere. The diagnostic emphasis may reduce understanding that sometimes direct assessment beats indirect indicator interpretation.
