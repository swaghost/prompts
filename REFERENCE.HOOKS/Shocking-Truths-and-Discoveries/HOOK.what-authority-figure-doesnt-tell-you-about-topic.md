# What [authority figure] doesn't tell you about [topic]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who identifies information gaps in mainstream authority narratives. They see you as willing to supplement or challenge incomplete official information. You appear as someone who provides complete picture including what authorities omit.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers distrust or suspicion about what authorities withhold. Intellectually activates critical thinking about institutional information control. Psychologically creates perception that authorities have incentives to omit certain truths. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because information authorities "don't tell you" feels like protected insider knowledge.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because suggesting authorities withhold information triggers both curiosity and protective suspicion. This hook leverages mistrust of institutions where audiences assume authorities have motivations beyond full disclosure. The "doesn't tell you" framing implies intentional omission rather than oversight, suggesting either concealment or paternalistic information control. By positioning yourself as filling gaps, you become supplementary authority providing complete information. This creates us-versus-them dynamic where you're on audience's side against institutional information control.

## Example

What financial advisors don't tell you about index funds: they're paid to manage active investments, so they rarely mention passive index funds consistently outperform 80%+ of actively managed funds over 20 years.

## Why it works

This hook works because it positions you as exposing conflicts of interest or incentive structures that explain information gaps. The authority-challenger stance appeals to audiences who distrust institutions or want complete information before deciding. By filling gaps authorities leave, you provide genuine supplementary value. The hook validates suspicion that full truth isn't being shared through official channels. This combination of conflict exposure, institutional skepticism appeal, supplementary value, and suspicion validation creates protective engaged attention.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you attribute to malice what's actually constraint, oversight, or legitimate scope boundaries - authorities don't tell everything about everything. If you constantly frame authorities as withholding information, you seem paranoid or anti-institutional rather than thoughtfully critical. The hook also fails if what you claim they "don't tell" is actually readily available information. If you misrepresent authority positions to create false gaps, you damage your credibility. Too much institutional mistrust can alienate audiences who respect expertise and professionalism.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who distrust all authority and expertise, making them vulnerable to misinformation from unqualified sources. You may attract conspiracy-minded audiences who assume authorities always lie or conceal. The constant authority-challenging can position you as unreliable if you're wrong about what they "don't tell." If you benefit from alternatives to what authorities recommend (competing products, services), audiences may see you as creating mistrust for profit. This approach can also prevent productive partnerships with institutions you position as incomplete or misleading. The us-versus-them framing may reduce nuance recognition where authorities have legitimate reasons for scoping information.
