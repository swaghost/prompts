# Why I changed my mind about [topic] after [time/experience]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as intellectually honest enough to change views when evidence warrants. They see you as valuing truth over consistency. You appear as someone whose mind-change demonstrates growth through experience.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers respect for intellectual honesty. Intellectually activates interest in what evidence changed perspective. Psychologically creates permission to revise own views. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "changed my mind after" promises authentic evolution through experience.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because mind-changes signal new information or understanding - audiences want to know what evidence shifted perspective. This hook leverages credibility through flexibility where changing views demonstrates prioritizing truth over ego. The after-framing establishes learning journey. By acknowledging previous wrongness, you model intellectual honesty. This satisfies desire for authentic growth stories including perspective evolution.

## Example

Why I changed my mind about daily routines after 10 years trying to optimize them: they're useful for automation but harmful when rigid adherence creates guilt about necessary flexibility. Life isn't optimizable like code; variability is feature not bug. Better: loose structure with permission to adapt than rigid system generating shame when reality interferes.

## Why it works

This hook works because it demonstrates intellectual honesty and models healthy perspective revision. The mind-change framing shows growth. By explaining what shifted view, you share learning. The hook satisfies desire for authentic evolution stories. This combination of honesty demonstration, growth modeling, learning sharing, and evolution authenticity creates flexibly learning audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when mind-change reveals you held extreme position without justification originally or when flip-flopping appears unprincipled. If you constantly change your mind, you seem inconsistent rather than growth-oriented. The hook also fails if you strawman previous position to make current one look better. If mind-change is just reframing same view differently, evolution is false. Too many mind-changes can erode trust in your current positions.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who constantly revise views rather than developing stable frameworks. You may attract people who enjoy feeling growth through position-changing over building coherent perspectives. The constant mind-changing can reduce trust in your recommendations since they'll likely change again. If your original position was poorly thought through, mind-changes suggest initial advice was reckless. This approach can also encourage unprincipled flexibility where changing views becomes identity. The honesty emphasis may reduce understanding that some positions should be held firmly despite social pressure to reconsider.
