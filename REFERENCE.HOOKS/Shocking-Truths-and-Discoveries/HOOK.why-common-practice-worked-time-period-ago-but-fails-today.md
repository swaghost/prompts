# Why [common practice] worked [time period] ago but fails today

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone understanding temporal context shifts making old practices obsolete. They see you as analytical about why historical success doesn't transfer. You appear as someone whose context-awareness prevents outdated approach persistence.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers realization about own potentially outdated practices. Intellectually activates temporal context thinking. Psychologically creates motivation to update approaches for current environment. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "worked then but fails now" promises explanation of practice expiration enabling updated strategy.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because if successful historical practices now fail, context has shifted - understanding changes enables adaptation. This hook leverages appeal of context-awareness where recognizing environmental shifts prevents outdated persistence. The worked-but-fails framing creates temporal insight. By explaining context change, you justify practice evolution. This satisfies desire for understanding why old advice no longer applies.

## Example

Why email opt-in pop-ups worked 10 years ago but fail today: novelty created compliance when rare; now universal pop-ups create banner blindness and resentment - what worked through uniqueness fails through ubiquity. Context shifted from "interesting new thing" to "annoying standard practice." Same tactic, different environment, opposite results. Adapt to context not replicate historical tactics.

## Why it works

This hook works because it explains practice obsolescence through context evolution enabling timely adaptation. The worked-but-fails framing creates temporal clarity. By explaining environmental shifts, you prevent outdated replication. The hook satisfies desire for understanding time-sensitive effectiveness. This combination of obsolescence explanation, context awareness, adaptation motivation, and temporal understanding creates appropriately updated audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when practice you dismiss still works in proper contexts or when you claim recency to dismiss proven approaches. If you constantly declare practices obsolete, you seem to chase novelty over fundamentals. The hook also fails if you overstate context changes to position your approaches as only current valid ones. If practice "failure" is your poor implementation not temporal invalidity, claim misleads. Too many worked-then-fails messages can create constant second-guessing of working approaches.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences obsessed with latest tactics rather than timeless principles. You may attract people who prefer feeling current over building strong foundations. The constant obsolescence focus can reduce appreciation for enduring practices that work across contexts. If your obsolescence claim is wrong and practice still works, you've prevented effective approaches. This approach can also foster recency bias where only newest is considered valid. The temporal framing may reduce understanding that many fundamentals transcend specific time periods.
