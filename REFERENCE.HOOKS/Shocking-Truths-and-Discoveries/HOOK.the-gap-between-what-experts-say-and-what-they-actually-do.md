# The gap between what [experts] say and what they actually do

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone revealing expert say-do gaps providing insider truth. They see you as observant about advice-practice discrepancies. You appear as someone whose gap-revelation enables learning from expert behavior not just expert words.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers skepticism about expert advice without behavior observation. Intellectually activates distinction between stated and revealed preferences. Psychologically creates focus on actions over words. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "gap between say and do" promises revelation of expert actual practices versus public advice.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because if expert behavior differs from advice, true lessons come from observing actions - stated versus revealed preferences matter. This hook leverages appeal of insider knowledge where behind-scenes truth provides advantage. The "gap" framing creates revealed hypocrisy or hidden wisdom. By distinguishing say from do, you redirect learning. This satisfies desire for understanding actual expert practices.

## Example

The gap between what successful writers say and what they actually do: publicly claim "write every day" but actually write in intensive bursts with breaks - idealized advice versus messy reality. What works gets cleaned into inspirational prescription. Learn from actual sustainable patterns not aspirational stated rules. Observe behavior; don't just listen to advice shaped for public consumption.

## Why it works

This hook works because it reveals expert behavior-advice gaps enabling learning from actual practices. The gap framing creates insider revelation. By distinguishing say from do, you provide real truth. The hook satisfies desire for behind-scenes understanding. This combination of gap revelation, behavioral focus, insider knowledge, and actual-practice learning creates observantly learning audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you misinterpret expert practices or when gap is expert evolution where current practice differs from past advice. If you constantly reveal say-do gaps, you foster cynical distrust of all expert guidance. The hook also fails if you use gap to discredit experts rather than learn from complete picture. If gap you describe is unrepresentative, claim misleads. Too many hypocrisy revelations can reduce trust in valuable expert knowledge.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who dismiss all expert advice seeking only behavioral observation. You may attract people who prefer feeling savvy through gap-spotting versus learning from stated guidance. The constant gap-focus can reduce appreciation for valuable advice even when imperfectly followed. If you encourage cynical expert-distrust, you've reduced learning from legitimate expertise. This approach can also foster belief hypocrisy invalidates all guidance. The behavior-focus emphasis may reduce understanding that expert advice often represents learned ideal even if imperfectly practiced, and both advice and behavior offer learning.
