# The [time period] experiment that changed everything I thought about [topic]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who tests beliefs empirically rather than accepting assumptions. They see you as willing to have fundamental beliefs challenged by evidence. You appear as someone whose comprehensive belief revision demonstrates experiment's transformative power.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about what results so dramatically contradicted expectations. Intellectually activates interest in empirical challenge to established beliefs. Psychologically creates openness to having own beliefs similarly challenged. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "changed everything" from experiment promises paradigm-shifting findings.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because experiments provide evidence-based credibility beyond opinion - you discovered through testing not theorizing. This hook leverages transformation testimony where comprehensive belief revision signals profound insights worth understanding. The time period quantifies your commitment making results feel earned. By emphasizing beliefs changed versus just confirming them, you demonstrate intellectual honesty and openness. This creates trust through willingness to be wrong.

## Example

The 6-month sleep experiment that changed everything I thought about productivity: tracking deep work output against sleep hours showed my peak performance came at 8.5 hours sleep not 6 - I was chronically underperforming for years believing "sleep is for the weak" hustle culture.

## Why it works

This hook works because it demonstrates that even informed people hold wrong beliefs until empirically tested. The comprehensive revision ("everything") validates experiment's significance. By sharing what you learned wrong, you model intellectual humility and growth. The hook satisfies desire for evidence-based insights over opinions. This combination of empirical grounding, significance validation, humility modeling, and evidence appeal creates trusting engaged audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when experiment methodology is flawed making "discoveries" unreliable. If you constantly have experiments that change everything, audiences question your prior judgment. The hook also fails if your changed beliefs are actually specific to you not generalizable insights. If you overstate belief change for drama versus accurate revision, credibility suffers. Too many transformation experiments can make you seem like you hold beliefs loosely rather than developing stable understanding.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences in perpetual belief flux who never develop stable frameworks. You may attract people who enjoy disruption for its own sake rather than truth-seeking. The constant belief revision can reduce your authority - why trust current beliefs if they keep changing? If your experiments aren't rigorous, you spread experiential noise as insight. This approach can also promote excessive self-experimentation when some questions have established answers. The transformation emphasis may devalue careful theoretical thinking that prevents costly experiments.
