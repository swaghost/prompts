# Everyone's doing [thing] wrong

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as having identified widespread error revealing correct approach others miss. They see you as analytical about common mistakes. You appear as someone whose universal-error revelation demonstrates you've achieved understanding beyond consensus positioning you as corrective authority.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers concern about potentially making common mistake combined with hope for correction. Intellectually activates self-assessment of current approach. Psychologically creates urgency to learn right way. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "everyone's doing it wrong" promises revealing widespread error enabling course correction.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because if most people err, avoiding that mistake provides competitive advantage - being among the few doing it right creates differentiation. This hook leverages appeal of contrarian correctness where widespread error suggests opportunity. The "everyone wrong" framing creates urgency through scale. By revealing correct approach, you position as superior authority. This satisfies desire for competitive edge through error avoidance.

## Example

Everyone's doing cold outreach wrong: they pitch immediately instead of starting conversation - first message should give value not ask for sale. Most think: introduce, pitch, close. Reality: provide value, build relationship, earn right to pitch. Wrong sequence kills conversion. Right approach takes patience but works.

## Why it works

This hook works because it identifies widespread error creating opportunity for those who correct it establishing you as superior-method authority. The "everyone wrong" framing creates large-scale problem. By revealing correct approach, you provide competitive advantage. The hook satisfies desire for differentiation. This combination of universal-error revelation, correct-method provision, competitive positioning, and contrarian authority creates audiences who adopt your approach to avoid common mistakes.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your "right way" is actually preference not objectively superior or when you manufacture false consensus about wrong approach. If you constantly claim everyone does things wrong, you seem contrarian for attention not truth. The hook also fails if you use error-calling to position only your method as valid. If common approach actually works well, criticism is unfounded. Too many "everyone wrong" claims can reduce credibility through excessive contrarianism and foster belief nothing established has value.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who constantly second-guess themselves and distrust all mainstream approaches. You may attract contrarians who reject standard methods reflexively without evaluation. The constant everyone-wrong focus can reduce appreciation for why common approaches became common—they often work reasonably well. If your "right way" is context-dependent not universal, you've created false certainty. This approach can also foster arrogance about possessing superior knowledge. The universal-error emphasis may reduce understanding that while some common approaches are suboptimal, most widespread methods reflect aggregate wisdom, and declaring everything wrong often indicates lack of nuance rather than superior insight.
