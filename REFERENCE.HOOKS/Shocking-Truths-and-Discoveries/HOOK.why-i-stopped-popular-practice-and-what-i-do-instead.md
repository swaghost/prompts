# Why I stopped [popular practice] and what I do instead

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who critically evaluates even popular practices and makes informed changes. They see you as independent-minded and results-focused rather than trend-following. You appear as someone sharing both critique and alternative solution.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about what prompted your abandonment of popular practice. Intellectually activates interest in both critique and alternative approach. Psychologically creates permission to question practices they may have doubts about. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because personal testimony about change provides credible before/after comparison.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because personal departure from popular practice suggests you discovered significant flaws worth understanding. This hook leverages the power of conversion stories where someone who tried popular approach and abandoned it provides credible critique. The "what I do instead" framing prevents pure negativity - you're offering alternative not just complaining. By sharing your transition, you model permission to evolve away from mainstream practices when they don't serve. This creates both validation (popular practice has issues) and hope (better alternatives exist).

## Example

Why I stopped doing daily stand-ups and what I do instead: they became performative status updates optimizing for looking busy rather than genuine blockers discussion - switched to async written updates with synchronous problem-solving only when specific blockers need real-time collaboration.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides both critique grounded in experience and tested alternative audiences can consider. The personal testimony creates credibility - you actually tried both approaches. By explaining WHY you stopped, you help audiences evaluate if same reasons apply to them. The alternative provision prevents leaving audiences without direction. This combination of experienced critique, credibility through testing, evaluation framework, and alternative solution creates engaged consideration from audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you stopped popular practice for reasons unique to your context that won't generalize to audiences. If you constantly stop and replace practices, you seem like you're in perpetual experimentation rather than settled expertise. The hook also fails if your alternative isn't clearly better, just different. If you stopped because you were doing the practice wrong rather than the practice being flawed, critique misleads. Too many stopped-doing hooks can position you as oppositional to mainstream rather than thoughtfully selective.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who constantly abandon practices at first difficulty rather than learning to execute them well. You may attract serial practice-switchers who never develop mastery of anything. The constant evolution can reduce credibility - if you keep changing, why trust current approach? If your alternative requires resources or contexts audiences lack, you've critiqued their option without providing viable replacement. This approach can also antagonize people who teach or benefit from practices you abandoned, limiting partnerships. The conversion framing may oversimplify where both approaches have value in different contexts.
