# You're being lied to about [topic]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone actively protecting them from ongoing deception. They see you as vigilant and principled, willing to call out current misinformation even if controversial. You appear as a present-tense defender who spots active lies rather than just historical ones.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers immediate alarm - the lying is happening NOW, requiring urgent attention. Intellectually activates real-time threat assessment about current information sources. Psychologically creates present-tense betrayal where they're actively being deceived as they watch. Physically manifests as visceral reaction, stopping scroll immediately to identify who's lying and about what.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because present-tense framing ("you're being" versus "you've been") creates ongoing threat urgency - the deception continues right now, making response more critical. This hook triggers active betrayal detection where audiences immediately question what they currently believe or do. The personal address ("you're") makes the deception feel targeted at them specifically rather than abstract misinformation. This activates defensive attention where people intensely focus on threats that are current and personal versus historical and general.

## Example

You're being lied to about the "4-hour workweek": most people promoting it work 60+ hours behind the scenes.

## Why it works

This hook works because present-tense urgency creates immediate action motivation - if you're currently being lied to, you need to stop it now. By calling out active deception, you position yourself as someone monitoring information environment in real-time to protect audience. The personal targeting ("you're being") makes each viewer feel individually addressed and at risk, increasing emotional investment. The hook also creates immediate distrust of whatever sources are doing the lying, positioning you as the trustworthy alternative by contrast. This combination of urgency, protective positioning, personal relevance, and source distrust creates powerful immediate engagement.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you can't prove intentional active lying versus honest disagreement or outdated information - acc using people of lying is serious and requires evidence. If you constantly claim audiences are "being lied to" about everything, the urgency numbs and you seem paranoid. The hook also fails if the lying isn't actually current or widespread - claiming active deception that isn't happening destroys credibility. If you benefit from the panic or confusion your lie-accusations create, audiences may see you as fear-mongering for engagement. Too aggressive tone can make you seem angry or unbalanced rather than protective.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating hyper-vigilant audiences who see active deception everywhere, fostering paranoia rather than discernment. You may attract conspiracy theorists who expect constant lie-exposure, requiring escalating accusations to maintain interest. The present-tense accusation can invite legal action if you accuse specific entities of actively lying without sufficient evidence. If you're wrong about active lying, you suffer severe credibility damage because you made a moral accusation about ongoing behavior. This approach can also burn bridges with anyone you accuse, isolating you professionally. The aggressive framing may create toxic community culture where members constantly accuse each other and others of lying.
