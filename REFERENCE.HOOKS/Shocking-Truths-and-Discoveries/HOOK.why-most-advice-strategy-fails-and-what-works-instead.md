# Why most [advice/strategy] fails (and what works instead)

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as failure-analyst identifying why common approaches don't work while providing working alternative. They see you as diagnostic about ineffective strategies. You appear as someone whose failure-analysis-plus-solution demonstrates you understand both problem and answer enabling effective substitution.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers validation for strategy failures combined with hope for working alternative. Intellectually activates failure-analysis thinking. Psychologically creates urgency to switch approaches. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "why advice fails and what works" promises explaining failure while providing working replacement.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because understanding why approaches fail prevents wasted effort while learning working alternative enables immediate improvement - diagnosis plus solution creates complete value. This hook leverages appeal of failure prevention where identifying non-working strategies saves resources. The "what works instead" addition provides actionable replacement. By explaining failure plus solution, you enable strategic substitution. This satisfies desire for avoiding ineffective approaches while accessing effective ones.

## Example

Why most productivity advice fails: it ignores energy management - "time management" assumes constant energy; reality fluctuates. Scheduling deep work during low-energy hours guarantees poor output. What works instead: energy-based scheduling—match task complexity to current energy level. Failed approach: time optimization. Working approach: energy optimization. Understanding failure mechanism enables effective substitution.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides complete value through both failure diagnosis and working alternative creating immediate actionable improvement. The "why fails" portion validates struggles; "what works" provides solution. By explaining both problem and answer, you enable strategic replacement. The hook satisfies desire for understanding failure while accessing success. This combination of failure diagnosis, struggle validation, solution provision, and strategic substitution creates audiences who abandon failing approaches adopting your working alternative.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you oversimplify why strategies fail or when you claim universal failure for approaches that work in many contexts. If you constantly explain why advice fails, you foster excessive skepticism of all guidance. The hook also fails if you use failure-calling to position only your alternative as valid. If identified failure isn't actually common or alternative isn't superior, analysis misleads. Too many failure-explanation shares can reduce appreciation for how most advice works when properly applied and create belief all conventional guidance is flawed.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who dismiss all standard advice seeking alternatives reflexively without evaluating conventional approaches fairly. You may attract people who use failure-awareness to excuse not trying established methods. The constant failure-analysis focus can reduce appreciation for how execution matters more than strategy selection. If your failure diagnosis is incomplete or alternative is context-dependent, followers may abandon workable approaches. This approach can also foster belief most advice doesn't work. The replacement emphasis may reduce understanding that while some strategies do fail commonly, most standard advice reflects aggregate wisdom, and assuming conventional approaches fail often reflects misunderstanding proper application rather than strategy inadequacy.
