## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you claim multiplication for relationships that are actually additive or when you overstate synergistic effects. If you constantly reveal multiplicative relationships, you seem to see exponential synergies everywhere. The hook also fails if you use multiply-framing to sound sophisticated about normal combinations. If factors don't actually multiply, claim creates false expectations. Too many multiplicative claims can reduce accurate understanding of simple additive relationships.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who only pursue factors promising multiplicative synergies neglecting valuable additive improvements. You may attract people seeking exponential shortcuts versus steady development. The constant multiplication focus can reduce appreciation for simple additive progress providing real value. If your multiplicative claim is wrong, you've created false expectations about combination effects. This approach can also foster belief nothing matters unless it multiplies. The exponential emphasis may reduce understanding that while some factors multiply, most combine additively, and additive progress often more reliable than hoped-for multiplicative breakthroughs.
