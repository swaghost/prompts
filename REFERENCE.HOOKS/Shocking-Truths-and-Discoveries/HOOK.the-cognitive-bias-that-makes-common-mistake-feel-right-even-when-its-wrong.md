# The cognitive bias that makes [common mistake] feel right even when it's wrong

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone understanding psychological mechanisms behind systematic errors. They see you as analytical about cognitive biases creating mistakes. You appear as someone whose bias-awareness enables conscious error correction.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers recognition of own bias-driven mistakes. Intellectually activates metacognitive awareness. Psychologically creates motivation to counteract cognitive biases. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "cognitive bias makes mistake feel right" promises explanation of why wrong feels right, enabling conscious correction.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because understanding why mistakes feel right enables correction - cognitive bias awareness creates metacognitive defense. This hook leverages appeal of psychology where understanding mental mechanisms provides control. The "feel right when wrong" framing creates insight. By revealing bias, you enable conscious compensation. This satisfies desire for understanding systematic thinking errors.

## Example

The cognitive bias that makes sunk cost persistence feel right even when it's wrong: loss aversion makes cutting losses feel like admitting waste, so we throw good money/time after bad protecting ego - brain's "don't waste investment" instinct prevents rational "stop losing" decision. Feeling protects past ego; logic serves future outcomes. Bias makes escalating commitment feel responsible when it's irrational.

## Why it works

This hook works because it explains why systematic mistakes persist despite logic through cognitive bias understanding. The feel-right-when-wrong framing creates insight. By revealing bias mechanism, you enable conscious correction. The hook satisfies desire for error-pattern understanding. This combination of bias revelation, metacognitive awareness, correction enablement, and mechanism explanation creates bias-aware audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you misattribute mistakes to cognitive biases when other factors drive them or when you use bias-labeling to dismiss legitimate intuitions. If you constantly cite cognitive biases, you seem to think all intuition is flawed. The hook also fails if you oversimplify complex decisions into pure bias. If bias explanation is pop-psychology not validated mechanism, claim lacks credibility. Too many bias revelations can create distrust of all gut feelings.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who overthink every decision seeking biases rather than acting on reasonable judgment. You may attract people who prefer feeling rational through bias-spotting over practical wisdom. The constant bias-focus can reduce confidence in valuable intuitions that work well. If your bias attribution is wrong, you've created false self-doubt. This approach can also foster paralysis where audiences second-guess all natural thinking. The cognitive-bias emphasis may reduce understanding that many intuitions are well-calibrated, not systematically biased.
