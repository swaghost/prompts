# I tried every [thing] so you don't have to

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewers should perceive the creator as a trusted guide who has done exhaustive research and testing on their behalf, saving them time, money, and frustration. The creator is positioned as someone who makes significant personal investment in exploration to provide valuable shortcuts and insights, demonstrating both dedication and expertise while being relatable through their willingness to experience failures and disappointments for the audience's benefit.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Intellectually: Viewers should feel they're gaining valuable condensed knowledge from extensive research they don't have to conduct themselves. Emotionally: Relief and gratitude for having someone else do the difficult comparative work, combined with curiosity about findings and conclusions. Psychologically: Activation of shortcut bias and desire for efficiency, reducing decision anxiety through expert curation. Trust building through vicarious experience. Physically: Leaning in to absorb recommendations, potential note-taking or saving behavior to reference later.

## Psychology

Curiosity + shortcut bias (people love avoiding trial & error). This hook leverages cognitive economics—the brain's preference for conserving mental energy by accepting trusted shortcuts rather than conducting independent research. The comprehensive testing claim triggers curiosity about surprising discoveries while the "so you don't have to" promise provides value through saved effort, creating obligation-free value exchange that builds goodwill and positions creator as benevolent expert.

## Example

I tried every productivity app so you don't have to.

## Why it works

This hook works because it addresses universal desire to make optimal choices without investing time in exhaustive research. The comprehensive nature ("every") signals thoroughness that most individuals couldn't realistically achieve, making the creator's effort feel genuinely valuable. The explicit benefit statement ("so you don't have to") creates clear value proposition while building reciprocity—viewer receives benefit of extensive testing without the cost. The format also creates anticipation for rankings, surprises, and strong opinions formed through direct experience rather than theory. It positions creator as both relatable (willing to waste time/money) and authoritative (has comprehensive experience), creating trust through demonstrated commitment. The promise of discovering which options are worth attention versus which to avoid provides immediate practical value that justifies engagement.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails if the testing appears superficial or if the creator hasn't actually tried "every" option, leading to credibility damage when viewers notice missing alternatives. If the scope is too broad ("every business strategy"), it becomes unbelievable, whereas too narrow ("every blue pen") lacks sufficient interest. The hook also fails when conclusions are presented without sufficient supporting evidence from the testing process, or when the creator's criteria for evaluation don't align with audience priorities. If the final recommendations are predictable or obvious choices everyone already knows about, the extensive testing feels pointless. Additionally, if the format becomes too list-heavy without story or personality, it devolves into boring comparison chart rather than engaging content.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can create unrealistic expectations that the creator has literally tested every single option, leading to defensive corrections in comments when viewers point out missed alternatives. It may attract viewers primarily interested in the specific recommendation rather than the creator's broader content, creating subscriber churn after they get their answer. The comprehensive testing claim can also paint creator into corner requiring ongoing exhaustive testing for credibility maintenance, creating unsustainable content production demands. If creators accept sponsorships or have affiliations that bias recommendations, the "tried everything" claim amplifies perceptions of dishonesty when bias is revealed. The hook may also attract negative engagement from fans of products the creator criticizes, creating conflict rather than community. Finally, viewers may become dependent on creator's curation rather than developing their own evaluation skills, creating pressure for constant new comprehensive reviews.
