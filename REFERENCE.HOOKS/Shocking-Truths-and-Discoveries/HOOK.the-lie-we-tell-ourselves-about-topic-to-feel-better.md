# The lie we tell ourselves about [topic] to feel better

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone exposing comforting self-deceptions. They see you as honest about protective lies. You appear as someone whose self-deception revelation enables facing uncomfortable truths for authentic growth.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers uncomfortable recognition of own self-deception. Intellectually activates awareness of protective narratives. Psychologically creates motivation for honest self-examination. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "lie we tell ourselves to feel better" promises revelation of common self-deceptions enabling authentic growth.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because recognizing self-deception enables authentic growth - comfortable lies prevent real progress. This hook leverages appeal of truth-seeking where exposing self-deception demonstrates courage. The "lie we tell ourselves" framing creates collective admission. By revealing deception, you enable honest examination. This satisfies desire for understanding that comfortable narratives may obstruct growth.

## Example

The lie we tell ourselves about "not having time" to feel better: time exists; priorities don't align - "no time" protects ego from admitting we don't value activity enough to sacrifice something else. Easier to blame time than acknowledge preference. Self-deception maintains self-image while avoiding uncomfortable truth: we make time for what we actually value. Protective lie prevents honest priority examination.

## Why it works

This hook works because it exposes self-deception enabling honest self-examination for authentic growth. The lie-to-feel-better framing creates recognition. By revealing protective narrative, you enable truth-facing. The hook satisfies desire for cutting through comfortable illusions. This combination of deception exposure, honest examination, authentic growth, and truth-facing creates self-aware audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you judgment others' legitimate circumstances as self-deception or when you reveal "lies" that are actually helpful framings. If you constantly expose self-deceptions, you seem cynical about all explanations. The hook also fails if you use lie-revelation to feel superior or harsh. If deception you expose is your projection not common pattern, claim lacks validity. Too many lie-exposures can reduce compassionate understanding of human coping.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who harshly self-judge or develop unhelpful cynicism about all their explanations. You may attract people who prefer feeling honest through self-criticism versus balanced self-awareness. The constant lie-exposure can reduce appreciation for protective narratives that serve psychological health. If your lie-claim dismisses legitimate constraints, you've created false guilt. This approach can also foster belief all self-explanations are deceptions. The truth emphasis may reduce understanding that some "lies" are adaptive framings, brutal honesty isn't always productive, and compassionate self-understanding enables growth better than harsh exposure.
