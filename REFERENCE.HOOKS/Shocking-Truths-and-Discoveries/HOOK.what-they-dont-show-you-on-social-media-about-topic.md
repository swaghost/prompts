# What they don't show you on social media about [topic]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who reveals reality behind curated social media highlights. They see you as transparent about unshown difficulties and complexities. You appear as someone who counters unrealistic expectations created by selective sharing.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers relief about hidden struggles they also experience but don't see shared. Intellectually activates critical thinking about social media curation and selection bias. Psychologically validates their suspicion that social media presents incomplete reality. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because behind-the-scenes truth feels more real and relatable than curated highlights.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because social media curation creates systematic misrepresentation - what's hidden often matters more than what's shown. This hook leverages validation of suspicion where audiences sense social media isn't complete picture but need confirmation. The "don't show" framing highlights intentional omission from selective sharing rather than complete lying. By revealing what's systematically hidden, you provide context that makes audiences' experiences feel more normal. This counters harmful comparison where people judge their behind-the-scenes against others' highlight reels.

## Example

What they don't show you on social media about freelancing: the months of financial uncertainty, the clients who ghost after you deliver work, the constant feast-or-famine cycle, and the self-doubt between projects.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides honest counterbalance to unrealistic expectations created by curated content, building trust through transparency. The behind-the-scenes revelation satisfies desire to understand full reality before making decisions based on highlights alone. By showing what's systematically hidden, you normalize struggles audiences face but rarely see discussed. The hook positions you as honest guide versus selective sharers optimizing for appearances. This combination of transparency trust, reality counterbalance, struggle normalization, and honest positioning creates grateful relieved audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you use it to overly focus on negatives, creating unrealistically pessimistic picture as corrective to unrealistic optimism. If you constantly reveal what's "not shown," audiences may wonder why you're so focused on negatives. The hook also fails if what you claim isn't shown is actually widely discussed - you're not revealing hidden truth just restating known struggles. If you benefit from discouraging people from trying things (your alternative product/service), transparency seems self-serving. Too much behind-the-scenes negativity can make content feel discouraging rather than realistic.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating overly cautious audiences who focus on difficulties rather than pursuing opportunities despite challenges. You may attract people who want validation for not trying rather than realistic preparation for trying, limiting action-orientation. The constant curation-challenging can position you as someone who rains on parades rather than providing balanced reality. If you only show what's not shown (struggles) without also acknowledging real highlights, you're equally selective just inverting the bias. This approach can also create resentment from people whose work you position as unrealistically curated. The negativity focus may reduce inspirational quality audiences need alongside realism.
