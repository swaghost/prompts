# The dirty truth about [industry].

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as an insider or investigator exposing unethical, exploitative, or problematic practices within industries, offering protective knowledge through whistleblowing or fearless critique.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Concern about industries they interact with or work in
- Validation if they've suspected unethical practices
- Appreciation for exposure of hidden wrongdoing
- Protective awareness against exploitation
- Anger or disappointment about revealed practices
- Gratitude for insider truth-telling

## Psychology

"Dirty truth" creates strong visceral response—suggesting corruption, exploitation, or unethical behavior rather than just problems. Industries often hide problematic practices behind professional veneer and PR, making exposure both shocking and valuable. This taps into our distrust of corporate interests, our desire to be informed consumers/participants, and our need to protect ourselves from exploitation. It positions the creator as whistleblower or investigative critic willing to antagonize powerful interests for public benefit. The word "dirty" also creates moral framing—not just different business practices but wrong ones, justifying exposure and critique.

## Example

"The dirty truth about the supplement industry: it's almost entirely unregulated—companies can put anything in bottles and call it a supplement without proving it works or is even safe. Your protein powder could literally be sawdust."

## Why it works

This hook creates engagement through moral exposure—revealing wrongdoing, exploitation, or unethical practices hidden from public view. "Dirty truth" is emotionally charged language that frames issues as moral failures rather than business decisions, creating stronger reaction. It positions the creator as protective advocate willing to expose powerful interests, building trust through adversarial honesty. The industry-focus ensures relevance to people who interact with, work in, or care about those spaces. The format provides consumer protection value—helping people avoid exploitation through informed knowledge. It also validates suspicions people may have had about industries but couldn't confirm.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails when the "dirty truth" is exaggerated, one-sided, or ignores context and complexity of industry challenges. It loses credibility if the practices exposed are widely known, being addressed, or not actually unethical. If the exposure relies on isolated incidents presented as industry-wide norms, it misleads through unrepresentative examples. The hook also underperforms when the creator seems more interested in outrage and clicks than accurate understanding or constructive solutions. When moral language ("dirty") is applied to ordinary business practices or necessary trade-offs, it appears naive or manipulative.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can invite legal or professional consequences if industries feel defamed or unfairly characterized without adequate evidence. It may position the creator as perpetually antagonistic, repelling potential collaborators and making them seem untrustworthy to work with. The format can encourage sensationalizing ordinary problems as "dirty" to maintain the pattern, gradually eroding accuracy and nuance. If the exposed truth is later shown to be exaggerated, context-free, or wrong, it severely damages credibility. The hook can also contribute to cynical worldview where all industries are corrupt, preventing viewers from engaging with legitimate opportunities or appreciating positive developments. The moral framing can make complex issues seem black-and-white when nuanced understanding would be more helpful.
