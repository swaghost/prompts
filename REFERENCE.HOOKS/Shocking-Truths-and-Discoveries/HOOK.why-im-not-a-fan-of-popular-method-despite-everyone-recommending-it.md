# Why I'm not a fan of [popular method] despite everyone recommending it

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as independent thinker evaluating methods by personal experience not popularity. They see you as willing to dissent from consensus when evidence warrants. You appear as someone whose contrarian position offers alternative perspective for those similarly skeptical.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about what creates skepticism despite popularity. Intellectually activates interest in critical evaluation versus bandwagon adoption. Psychologically creates permission to question popular methods. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "not a fan despite" promises independent assessment contradicting consensus.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because dissenting from popular consensus requires reasoning - audiences want justification for contrarian position. This hook leverages appeal of independent thinking where resisting social proof demonstrates evaluation rigor. The "despite everyone" framing acknowledges popularity while maintaining position. By expressing skepticism, you provide permission for audiences feeling similar doubts. This satisfies desire for alternatives to consensus when personal experience doesn't align.

## Example

Why I'm not a fan of morning routines despite everyone recommending them: they work great for morning people but create shame and failure cycles for night-focused people forcing unnatural schedules - better to design routine around your natural energy patterns than fight biology to match popular advice. Circadian rhythms vary; one-size advice doesn't.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides reasoned alternative to popular consensus, validating audiences whose experience doesn't match majority. The not-a-fan framing creates permission for skepticism. By explaining reasoning, you justify contrarian position. The hook satisfies desire for perspective diversity when consensus doesn't serve everyone. This combination of alternative provision, skepticism permission, reasoning justification, and diversity recognition creates confidently independent audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your skepticism is baseless contrarianism not reasoned evaluation or when method works well for most despite not serving you. If you're constantly not a fan of popular things, you seem reflexively contrarian. The hook also fails if you strawman popular method to justify skepticism. If you present personal preference as universal critique, you overgeneralize. Too many I'm-not-a-fan positions can make you seem perpetually dissenting rather than selectively critical.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who feel superior through dissenting from popular methods regardless of merit. You may attract people who prefer feeling special through contrarianism over finding what works. The constant skepticism can reduce appreciation for popular methods that serve majority well. If your contrarian position is wrong, you've encouraged audiences to avoid beneficial methods. This approach can also alienate majority for whom popular method works effectively. The dissent emphasis may reduce willingness to try popular approaches that might actually help.
