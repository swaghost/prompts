# The truth about [trending topic] is not what you think

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries
SHOCKING TRUTHS & DISCOVERIES (continued)

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who looks beneath surface of trending topics to find actual reality. They see you as intellectually independent, not swayed by hype or popular narratives. You appear as a reliable source who separates truth from trend-driven distortion.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about what reality contradicts popular understanding. Intellectually activates skepticism about trending narratives they may have accepted. Psychologically creates framing curiosity gap - what IS the truth if it's not what they think? Physically manifests as stopping scroll, sustained attention to discover what surprising truth contradicts trending narrative.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because of curiosity gap framing - by saying the truth "is not what you think," you create tension between their current belief and promised reality that must be resolved. This hook leverages trending topics' existing attention while promising to correct or complicate understanding, making your content feel both timely and substantive. The phrase "not what you think" activates pattern break thinking - audiences expect content about trending topics to reinforce narratives, so contradiction creates surprise. This also positions you as contrarian authority who thinks independently rather than amplifying trending viewpoints.

## Example

The truth about AI tools is not what you think: they're only as smart as the person using them - garbage in, garbage out.

## Why it works

This hook works because it combines the attention-grabbing power of trending topics with the credibility-building power of contrarian insight. By addressing what's already on people's minds (the trending topic), you ensure immediate relevance. By promising correction ("not what you think"), you position yourself as having deeper understanding than surface-level trend participants. The hook flatters audiences who engage - by learning the real truth, they become more informed than the masses riding the trend blindly. This combination of timely relevance, contrarian authority, and audience flattery creates strong engagement.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your "truth" is actually just a different spin on common understanding rather than genuine correction, making you seem clickbait-y. If you're consistently contrarian about every trending topic, audiences may see you as someone who reflexively opposes popularity rather than thinking independently. The hook also fails if you can't substantiate why your version is "the truth" versus another perspective - without evidence, you just have a hot take. If the trending topic isn't actually widely misunderstood, your correction seems unnecessary or pedantic. Overuse of "not what you think" makes the phrase predictable and meaningless.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by positioning you as perpetually against trends, making you seem contrarian or out of touch with current developments. You may attract audiences who only engage to have their cynicism about trends validated, creating a negative community. The constant correction of trending topics can make you seem like you never approve of anything popular, which is exhausting. If your "truth" is later shown to be wrong or incomplete, you lose credibility on all contrarian claims. This approach can also make you seem reactive - always responding to trends rather than setting them, which limits thought leadership perception. The pattern may train audiences to expect you'll always contradict trends, making supportive coverage of genuinely good trends feel inconsistent.
