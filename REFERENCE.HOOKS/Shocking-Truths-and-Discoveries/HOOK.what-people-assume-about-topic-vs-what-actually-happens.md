# What people assume about [topic] vs. what actually happens

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone correcting false assumptions with reality. They see you as experientially informed about topic. You appear as someone whose assumption-versus-reality reveals what to actually expect.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about gap between assumption and reality. Intellectually activates interest in accurate expectations. Psychologically creates motivation to adjust mental models to reality. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "assume vs actually" promises correction of false expectations.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because false assumptions create disappointment and poor strategy - accurate expectations enable effective approach. This hook leverages appeal of insider truth where experience-based reality corrects naive assumptions. The versus-format creates clear contrast. By revealing actual reality, you enable realistic preparation. This satisfies desire for accurate understanding preventing assumption-based mistakes.

## Example

What people assume about quitting corporate for entrepreneurship vs what actually happens: Assume - instant freedom, flexible schedule, doing what you love all day. Actually - trading boss for customers who demand more, working longer hours especially starting out, spending most time on marketing/admin/finance not core craft. Freedom is real but comes after building systems, not immediately.

## Why it works

This hook works because it corrects false assumptions enabling realistic expectations and better preparation. The contrast format creates clear correction. By sharing actual experience, you prevent disappointment. The hook satisfies desire for reality-based understanding. This combination of assumption correction, realistic preparation, disappointment prevention, and truth provision creates accurately expecting audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when assumptions you attribute are strawman exaggerations nobody actually holds or when reality you describe is your unique experience not typical. If you constantly correct assumptions, you seem to think everyone is naive. The hook also fails if you present worst-case reality as universal experience. If your reality is negative due to poor execution, you've discouraged beneficial pursuit. Too many assumption-corrections can make audiences feel their expectations are perpetually wrong.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who distrust all positive expectations as naive assumptions. You may attract people who feel sophisticated through cynical "reality" awareness. The constant correction focus can reduce optimism necessary for attempting difficult things. If your reality assessment is wrong or outdated, you've created inaccurate expectations. This approach can also discourage beneficial pursuits by overemphasizing difficulties. The versus framing may reduce understanding that experiences vary and some people's reality matches positive assumptions.
