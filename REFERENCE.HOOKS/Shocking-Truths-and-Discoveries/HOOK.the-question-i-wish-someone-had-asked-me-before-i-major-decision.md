# The question I wish someone had asked me before I [major decision]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone identifying critical pre-decision consideration. They see you as reflective about decision-making gaps. You appear as someone whose question-sharing provides protective guidance preventing inadequately considered major decisions.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers concern about own upcoming major decisions. Intellectually activates comprehensive decision examination. Psychologically creates motivation to consider all relevant factors. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "question I wish someone asked" promises protective consideration preventing regretted decisions.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because critical overlooked questions prevent regretted decisions - audiences want comprehensive consideration. This hook leverages credibility through vulnerability where admitting decision gaps demonstrates honest reflection. The "wish someone asked" framing suggests important omitted consideration. By sharing question, you provide protective guidance. This satisfies desire for decision-making completeness preventing oversights.

## Example

The question I wish someone had asked me before I started business: "Are you willing to feel uncertain about income for 3-5 years while building?" - I optimistically assumed 6-12 month ramp when reality requires multi-year sustained effort through financial uncertainty. Financial psychology preparation matters more than business strategy for early years. Emotional readiness enables strategy execution; emotional unreadiness sabotages best plans.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides critical decision consideration enabling comprehensive evaluation. The wish-someone-asked framing creates protective value. By sharing overlooked factor, you prevent inadequate preparation. The hook satisfies desire for complete decision guidance. This combination of oversight prevention, comprehensive consideration, protective wisdom, and decision completeness creates thoughtfully deciding audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when question you share is obvious to thoughtful people or when you present your unique oversight as universal gap. If you constantly share wish-asked questions, you seem to make decisions without adequate thought. The hook also fails if you use question to second-guess past decision rather than guide future ones. If question is your regret not actually important consideration, value is limited. Too many wish-asked questions can create decision paralysis through endless consideration.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who endlessly consider rather than deciding with reasonable information. You may attract people using consideration as excuse to avoid committing to decisions. The constant wish-asked emphasis can reduce confidence in making decisions with incomplete information. If your question isn't actually critical, you've added unhelpful complexity. This approach can also foster decision regret focus rather than learning-and-moving-forward. The protective framing may reduce understanding that some learning only comes through deciding and experiencing, not through perfect pre-consideration.
