# Here's what the [industry/experts] aren't saying about [topic]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as insider or informed observer revealing what authorities omit. They see you as willing to expose inconvenient truths professionals avoid. You appear as someone whose outside/independent perspective enables honest revelation.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers suspicion about what authorities are hiding through omission. Intellectually activates critical examination of expert narratives. Psychologically creates validation of intuition that something isn't being said. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "aren't saying" promises insider revelation of deliberately omitted information.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because understanding what experts omit reveals complete picture beyond official narratives - audiences want full truth. This hook leverages distrust of authority where experts' silence on topics suggests ulterior motives or inconvenient facts. The "aren't saying" framing implies deliberate omission not ignorance. By positioning yourself outside or against establishment, you signal independence from interests that drive omission. This satisfies desire for complete unfiltered information versus sanitized expert commentary.

## Example

Here's what the productivity industry aren't saying about optimization: their business model depends on you feeling perpetually inadequate so you keep buying next solution - they profit from creating problems that need solving rather than helping you reach "good enough" and stop consuming productivity content.

## Why it works

This hook works because it reveals systemic incentives explaining why authorities omit information - audiences gain structural understanding. The omission-focus shifts attention to what's absent from narratives. By exposing deliberate silence, you position as truth-teller facing power. The hook satisfies desire for complete picture including information authorities prefer hidden. This combination of incentive revelation, attention to absence, truth-telling positioning, and information completeness creates critically engaged audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when experts aren't actually omitting what you claim - they discuss it openly but you're misrepresenting their position. If you constantly expose what experts "aren't saying," you develop reputation for conspiracy thinking. The hook also fails if you attribute deliberate omission to what's actually experts' blind spots or limitations. If experts have legitimate reasons for emphasizing certain aspects over others, omission accusation seems unfair. Too much expert-criticism can position you as outsider lacking credibility to assess what they should say.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who distrust all expert guidance, limiting access to valuable knowledge. You may attract people who prefer feeling informed-against-establishment over actually learning. The constant omission-revelation can reduce appreciation for what experts do say that's valuable. If your omission claims are wrong, you've created false suspicion about authorities. This approach can also trigger defensive responses from experts, limiting productive dialogue. The adversarial framing may reduce ability to integrate expert knowledge with critical perspective rather than dismissing wholesale.
