# After [time period] of [activity], here's what I'd do differently

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as reflective learner who can identify improvement opportunities from experience. They see you as generous with hindsight wisdom. You appear as someone whose retrospective analysis offers strategic shortcuts.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about what experience revealed about better approaches. Intellectually activates interest in refined strategy from lessons learned. Psychologically creates hope that understanding what to do differently enables better outcomes. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "here's what I'd do differently" promises refined approach from experience.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because retrospective analysis from experience reveals optimal strategies - audiences can adopt refined approach without years of trial-and-error. This hook leverages power of compressed learning where hindsight wisdom saves time. The time-period specification creates credibility through sustained experience. By sharing what you'd change, you demonstrate continuous improvement mindset. This satisfies desire for experience-refined strategy versus untested theory.

## Example

After 3 years of building in public, here's what I'd do differently: focus on one platform deeply before spreading across many - building substantial audience on single platform creates momentum and confidence, while spreading thin creates mediocrity everywhere and fragmented attention. Master one, then expand with leverage from established presence.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides battle-tested refinement from someone who's been through full cycle. The retrospective framing demonstrates learning humility. By sharing what you'd change, you enable audiences to start with refined approach. The hook satisfies desire for optimized strategy incorporating lessons learned. This combination of tested refinement, humble learning, optimized starting point, and lesson incorporation creates strategically accelerated audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your "do differently" is Monday-morning quarterbacking not legitimate learning. If you constantly share what you'd do differently, you seem full of regret not gratitude for learning journey. The hook also fails if optimal approach depends on context available then but not now. If you present reasonable choices as mistakes, you discourage healthy experimentation. Too many I'd-do-differently revelations can make audiences feel every choice will be regrettable.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who expect to avoid all suboptimal choices through others' hindsight. You may attract people using "learning from mistakes" as excuse to avoid taking action until perfect strategy emerges. The constant retrospective optimization can reduce appreciation for learning journey including "mistakes." If your refined approach wouldn't actually have worked earlier, you're projecting false optimization. This approach can also devalue the growth that came from "suboptimal" choices. The refinement emphasis may reduce understanding that optimal strategy only becomes clear through experience.
