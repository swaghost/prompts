# This is exactly why [popular thing] fails

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who can diagnose root causes of common failures. They see you as analytical enough to identify systematic problems. You appear as someone who helps audiences avoid wasted effort on fundamentally flawed approaches.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers validation if they've tried and failed with popular thing. Intellectually activates causal thinking about systematic rather than personal failure. Psychologically removes self-blame by attributing failure to approach rather than user. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "exactly why" promises definitive explanation versus speculation.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because "exactly why" signals you can provide precise causal explanation for failure audiences may have experienced personally. This hook leverages desire for root cause understanding where knowing WHY something fails feels empowering versus just knowing THAT it fails. The "popular thing" framing ensures broad relevance - many have tried this, making failure explanation valuable. By diagnosing failure precisely ("exactly"), you demonstrate analytical depth versus vague criticism. This provides protective value by helping audiences avoid repeating failed approaches.

## Example

This is exactly why New Year's resolutions fail: they rely on willpower and motivation (finite daily resources) rather than systems and environment design that make desired behaviors default rather than effortful.

## Why it works

This hook works because it removes shame from failure by showing systematic rather than personal causation. The precise diagnosis ("exactly") provides actionable understanding audiences can apply to alternative approaches. By analyzing popular failures, you address shared experiences rather than niche problems. The hook satisfies desire for expert analysis that explains confusing personal experiences. This combination of shame removal, actionable diagnosis, shared relevance, and explanatory satisfaction creates grateful relieved audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your "exactly why" is actually one factor among many or controversial opinion versus established understanding. If you constantly diagnose failures of popular things, you seem reflexively negative about anything mainstream. The hook also fails if popular thing actually works well in contexts you ignore - universal failure claims are rarely accurate. If you can't offer working alternatives, you're just explaining why things are hopeless. Too much failure focus can make your brand feel negative rather than solution-oriented.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who expect all popular things to be fundamentally flawed, fostering cynicism. You may attract people who enjoy explaining why things don't work rather than making things work. The constant failure diagnosis can position you as analyzer versus doer. If your diagnosis is wrong or oversimplified, audiences who trust it may abandon approaches that could work with modification. This approach can also alienate people who teach or use popular things successfully, limiting partnerships. The systematic failure framing may reduce growth mindset where people could adapt approaches rather than abandoning them entirely.
