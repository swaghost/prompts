# This is what actually happens when you [action]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who knows reality versus popular misconceptions about outcomes. They see you as experienced with the action and willing to share honest consequences. You appear as someone who provides realistic expectations rather than idealized narratives.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about gap between expectations and reality. Intellectually activates interest in accurate outcome prediction versus myths. Psychologically creates appreciation for honest assessment before they take action. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "actually happens" promises reality check on assumptions they may hold.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because "actually happens" implies popular understanding is wrong or incomplete - you're about to correct misconceptions. This hook leverages desire for realistic expectations where people want to know true consequences before acting. The word "actually" signals contrast between believed outcomes and real outcomes, creating correction framing. By focusing on specific action, you make revelation immediately applicable to decision-making. This provides protective value by preventing surprise or disappointment from false expectations.

## Example

This is what actually happens when you quit your job to start a business: 6-18 months of financial stress, identity crisis when structure disappears, relationship strain from resource constraints, and discovering you hate tasks you romanticized - freedom comes after surviving this gauntlet.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides decision-relevant reality check that helps audiences make informed choices. The contrast framing between expectations and reality creates both surprise and trust through honesty. By sharing unglamorous realities, you demonstrate credibility through transparency rather than selling dreams. The hook satisfies desire to avoid costly surprises by learning from others' experiences. This combination of decision utility, honest contrast, transparency credibility, and surprise avoidance creates grateful reality-seeking audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you paint unrealistically negative picture as correction to unrealistically positive one, swapping one bias for another. If you constantly reveal what "actually" happens, audiences may see you as pessimistic or focused on discouraging action. The hook also fails if your experience doesn't represent typical outcomes - personal reality isn't always "actual" reality. If you can't distinguish between inevitable realities and your specific context, you mislead about what will "actually" happen. Too much reality-checking can make audiences feel naive for having hopeful expectations.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating risk-averse audiences who overweight difficulties and underweight benefits, reducing healthy ambition. You may attract people seeking justification for not taking action rather than realistic preparation for taking it. The constant expectation-reality gaps can position you as someone who bursts bubbles rather than inspires with honest optimism. If your "actual" outcomes vary significantly from others' experiences, audiences who trusted your reality may feel misled. This approach can also discourage people from actions that would benefit them despite challenges. The negative emphasis may reduce your ability to inspire while maintaining realism.
