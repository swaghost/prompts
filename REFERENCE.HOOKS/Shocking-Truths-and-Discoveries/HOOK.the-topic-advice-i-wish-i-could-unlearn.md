# The [topic] advice I wish I could unlearn

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone recognizing that some advice causes more harm than good once internalized. They see you as honest about struggling to overcome unhelpful programming. You appear as someone warning against advice that's hard to undo once adopted.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers concern about internalizing harmful advice. Intellectually activates critical evaluation of advice before adoption. Psychologically creates awareness that some beliefs are harder to unlearn than never learning. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "wish I could unlearn" suggests advice caused lasting damage worth avoiding.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because "wish I could unlearn" signals advice has lasting negative impact beyond just being wrong - it created harmful patterns difficult to reverse. This hook leverages prevention motivation where audiences want to avoid internalizing advice they'll struggle to undo. The unlearning difficulty implies deep adoption where advice shaped automatic thinking or behavior. By sharing your struggle, you warn audiences before they embed similar harmful patterns. This creates protective value by preventing hard-to-reverse adoption.

## Example

The productivity advice I wish I could unlearn: "sleep is for the weak" hustle mentality that I internalized for years - even after understanding sleep science intellectually, I still feel guilty resting, my self-worth remains tied to output, and I have to actively fight autopilot that sees rest as weakness.

## Why it works

This hook works because it warns about advice that creates lasting harm even after recognizing it's wrong. The unlearning struggle validates how deeply advice can embed in identity and automatic patterns. By sharing specific advice to avoid, you prevent audiences from same difficult reversal. The hook satisfies desire to avoid mistakes that are hard to undo. This combination of lasting-harm warning, embedding validation, specific prevention, and difficulty-awareness creates cautiously grateful audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when advice you struggle with isn't actually harmful, just requires discipline you resist. If you constantly wish you could unlearn things, you seem like victim of advice rather than agent making choices. The hook also fails if your unlearning difficulty is unique to you not general pattern. If you don't explain why advice is wrong just that you regret it, audiences can't assess if avoiding makes sense. Too much unlearning-struggle can position you as damaged by advice rather than successfully evolved beyond it.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating overly cautious audiences who avoid all advice for fear of harmful adoption. You may attract people seeking justification for why they struggle to implement advice. The constant advice-damage emphasis can reduce trust in all guidance including yours. If audiences already internalized advice you warn against, you've made them feel damaged without solution. This approach can also position advice-givers as potentially harmful, discouraging helpful guidance sharing. The struggle emphasis may reduce credibility - successful people typically don't emphasize what they wish they didn't know.
