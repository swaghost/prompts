# You won't believe what I found about [topic]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone making surprising discoveries that challenge expectations. They see you as curious and persistent, someone who digs deeper than surface level. You appear as an enthusiastic researcher who finds insights others miss through thorough investigation.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about what was so unbelievable you had to share. Intellectually activates anticipation of paradigm-challenging information. Psychologically creates disbelief curiosity - if YOU won't believe it, they need to know what could be so extraordinary. Physically manifests as stopping scroll, leaning in to discover what finding exceeded your own belief threshold.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because the phrase "you won't believe" creates a challenge - audiences want to judge for themselves whether something is believable, making them engage to evaluate your claim. This hook leverages the curiosity gap where disbelief serves as intensity marker - if something exceeds belief thresholds, it must be extreme or surprising. The personal testimony ("I found") adds authenticity through firsthand discovery rather than repeating others. The phrase also creates mild oppositional psychology - telling someone they "won't believe" makes them want to prove they can handle surprising information.

## Example

You won't believe what I found about airline tickets: prices change based on your browsing history and device type.

## Why it works

This hook works because the disbelief framing creates instant intrigue - what could be so extraordinary that belief itself is challenged? By positioning yourself as the discoverer who "found" this, you demonstrate research initiative and curiosity. The hook flatters audiences who continue - they're open-minded enough to consider unbelievable things, unlike rigid thinkers. The phrase creates social proof through your own reaction - if it exceeded your belief, it's validated as genuinely surprising. This combination of intrigue, research demonstration, audience flattery, and reaction validation creates strong engagement.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when what you "found" is actually quite believable or common knowledge, making the disbelief framing seem exaggerated and clickbait-y. If you constantly claim things are "unbelievable," audiences realize you just mean "interesting," diluting the phrase's impact. The hook also fails if you focus more on your reaction than the actual finding - audiences care about the information, not just your surprise. If your unbelievable claims are frequently wrong or misleading, you lose credibility. The hyperbolic framing can repel audiences who prefer measured content over sensational claims.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by training audiences to expect constant jaw-dropping revelations, making normal valuable information feel insufficient. You may attract audiences focused on shock value rather than substance, limiting community quality. The frequent use of "unbelievable" can make you seem easily impressed or lacking critical thinking skills. If audiences consistently find your claims quite believable, they question your judgment and credibility standards. This approach can also pressure you to find increasingly extreme information to match the "unbelievable" promise, leading to sensationalism. The hyperbolic language may reduce your authority in professional contexts where measured communication is valued.
