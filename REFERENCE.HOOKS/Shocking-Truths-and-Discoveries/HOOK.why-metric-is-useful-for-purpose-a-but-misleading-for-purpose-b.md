# Why [metric] is useful for [purpose A] but misleading for [purpose B]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone understanding context-dependent metric validity. They see you as analytical about measurement applicability limits. You appear as someone whose metric-context awareness prevents misapplication of measurements to inappropriate purposes.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers caution about metric misapplication. Intellectually activates conditional measurement thinking. Psychologically creates discernment about appropriate metric use. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "metric useful for one misleading for other" promises understanding when measurements help versus mislead.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because if metrics are conditionally valid, purpose determines appropriateness - understanding context prevents measurement misuse. This hook leverages appeal of sophisticated measurement where recognizing limits demonstrates wisdom. The "useful-but-misleading" framing creates conditional awareness. By revealing purpose-dependence, you enable appropriate metric selection. This satisfies desire for understanding that measurements aren't universally valid but context-dependent.

## Example

Why revenue growth is useful for scaling assessment but misleading for business health evaluation: growth shows momentum; profitability shows sustainability - rapidly growing unprofitable is impressive but unsustainable. Purpose determines which metric matters. Revenue alone misleads about viability. Metric validity depends on question asked. Same number, different meanings in different contexts.

## Why it works

This hook works because it reveals conditional metric validity enabling appropriate measurement selection. The useful-misleading framing creates context awareness. By explaining purpose-dependence, you prevent misapplication. The hook satisfies desire for sophisticated measurement thinking. This combination of conditional validity, context awareness, appropriate selection, and purpose-dependent thinking creates wisely measuring audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you dismiss valuable metrics as misleading in contexts where they remain useful or when you suggest no metrics work broadly. If you constantly reveal metric limitations, you foster measurement paralysis. The hook also fails if you use context-dependence to justify ignoring uncomfortable metrics. If misleading claim is your interpretation not actual limitation, assessment misleads. Too many metric-criticism messages can reduce appropriate quantification use.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who distrust all metrics claiming context makes everything unreliable. You may attract people using metric-skepticism to avoid accountability to measurements. The constant limitation focus can reduce appreciation for metrics providing valuable if imperfect information. If you encourage excessive metric-dismissal, audiences lose quantification benefits. This approach can also foster belief measurement always misleads. The context emphasis may reduce understanding that while metrics have limits, most provide value when interpreted appropriately, and metric-skepticism can become excuse for avoiding data.
