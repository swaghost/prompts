## Example

The critical distinction between accountability and responsibility nobody explains: responsibility is task ownership (you're responsible for outcome), accountability is answerability for decisions (you're accountable to stakeholders). You can be responsible without being accountable (doing job with no oversight) or accountable without being responsible (manager answering for team's work). Conflating them creates unclear expectations.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when distinction you draw isn't actually critical or concepts are used interchangeably by domain experts. If you constantly reveal distinctions nobody explains, you seem pedantic. The hook also fails if your differentiation is overly technical without practical value. If you create false distinctions where concepts genuinely overlap, you add confusion. Too many distinction-drawing can make audiences feel they're constantly confusing things.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who obsess over technical precision rather than practical understanding. You may attract people who use precise distinctions to feel superior in discussions. The constant distinction-emphasis can reduce appreciation for pragmatic concept use where perfect precision isn't necessary. If your distinctions don't align with how field uses terms, you've created alternative definitions causing more confusion. This approach can also encourage pedantry where audiences correct others on distinctions without adding value. The precision focus may reduce tolerance for productive ambiguity that enables communication.
