# This is why [popular advice] is failing you

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who diagnoses why their efforts aren't working, providing clarity about advice failure. They see you as analytical and empathetic, understanding both the advice and why it doesn't deliver. You appear as a problem-solver who identifies root causes rather than blaming them.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers relief that failure isn't their fault mixed with hope for better guidance. Intellectually activates critical evaluation of advice they've been following. Psychologically removes self-blame by externalizing failure to faulty advice rather than personal inadequacy. Physically manifests as stopping scroll, forward lean, and sustained attention to discover why their efforts haven't worked and what to do instead.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because advice-contradiction effect challenges popular wisdom while validating audience frustration. When people follow advice that doesn't work, they often blame themselves rather than the advice - this hook reverses that, making them feel seen and validated. By stating advice is "failing you" (present tense), you create urgency - the failure continues until they correct course. This leverages external locus of control which is psychologically liberating - they're not failing, the advice is failing them. The promise to explain "why" satisfies need for understanding root causes rather than just symptoms.

## Example

This is why "just post daily" is failing your Instagram growth: algorithms reward engagement quality, not posting frequency.

## Why it works

This hook works because it validates struggle and redirects blame from person to system, which builds immediate trust and rapport. By explaining "why" advice fails, you demonstrate deeper understanding than those who just repeat popular wisdom. The hook positions you as someone who thinks critically about common recommendations rather than accepting them blindly. This creates perception of superior knowledge and analytical capability. By acknowledging the advice is "popular," you validate that following it was reasonable, removing shame. This combination of validation, blame-redirection, deeper understanding, and shame-removal creates powerful engagement and loyalty.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when the advice you attack actually works well for many people, making you seem contrarian or out of touch. If you can't provide clear explanation of why the advice fails, your contradiction feels baseless. Overuse makes you appear relentlessly critical of all advice, which exhausts audiences and positions you as perpetually negative. The hook also fails if you don't offer better alternatives - identifying failing advice without solutions creates frustration. If your explanation oversimplifies why advice fails, informed audiences see through the strawman argument and dismiss you.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating distrust of all advice, making audiences paralyzed by skepticism even toward your own recommendations. You may attract audiences who only engage with contrarian content, requiring you to constantly oppose popular wisdom even when it's sound. The constant criticism of popular advice can position you antagonistically toward other educators, burning professional bridges. If the advice you claim is failing actually works with proper execution, audiences may realize they're not implementing correctly rather than following bad advice, questioning your diagnosis. This approach can also create victim mindsets where audiences feel all guidance is designed to fail them rather than taking responsibility for implementation.
