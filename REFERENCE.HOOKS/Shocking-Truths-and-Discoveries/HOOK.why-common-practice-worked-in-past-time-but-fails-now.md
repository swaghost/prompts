# Why [common practice] worked in [past time] but fails now

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who understands how changing contexts require strategy evolution. They see you as historically informed and contemporary aware. You appear as someone helping audiences avoid outdated strategies that no longer work.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers concern about using obsolete strategies. Intellectually activates interest in contextual changes that invalidated old approaches. Psychologically creates urgency to update strategies matching current reality. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because continuing ineffective historical approaches wastes effort understanding what changed prevents.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because using outdated strategies explains confusing failures - what you're trying worked before just not now. This hook leverages relief through explanation where audiences understand their struggle isn't personal failure but contextual mismatch. The historical contrast format teaches both what worked and WHY it no longer does, providing complete understanding. By identifying changed conditions, you help audiences diagnose when advice is obsolete. This prevents wasted effort on once-valid but now-ineffective approaches.

## Example

Why cold emailing worked in 2010 but fails now: inboxes received 50 emails daily then versus 300+ now, no filtering algorithms, people were flattered by personalized outreach - now it's spam-filtered, legally regulated (GDPR), and recipients are numb to "personalized" templates they see daily.

## Why it works

This hook works because it explains mysterious failures through contextual understanding rather than personal inadequacy. The historical format teaches evolution of strategy landscape. By identifying what changed, you help audiences adapt rather than abandon good core ideas executed wrong. The hook satisfies desire to understand why previously-successful approaches stop working. This combination of failure explanation, evolutionary teaching, adaptation guidance, and inadequacy-removal creates relieved strategic audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when practice still works but requires modification, or your diagnosis of why it fails is wrong. If you constantly claim things no longer work, you seem dismissive of proven approaches. The hook also fails if changed conditions you identify are temporary not permanent shifts. If you sell alternatives to what you claim no longer works, motivation seems self-serving. Too much obsolescence-calling can make strategies feel fragile rather than adaptable.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who constantly seek newest tactics rather than mastering timeless principles. You may attract people who blame context for failures rather than examining execution. The constant obsolescence framing can reduce appreciation for adapted classic strategies still effective. If your change diagnosis is wrong, audiences abandon working approaches prematurely. This approach can also create ageism where anything historical is dismissed as obsolete regardless of continued validity. The failure emphasis may reduce confidence that any strategy will remain effective over time.
