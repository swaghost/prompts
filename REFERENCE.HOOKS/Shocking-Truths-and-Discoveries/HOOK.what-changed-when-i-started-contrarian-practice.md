# What changed when I started [contrarian practice]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone willing to experiment with contrarian approaches and share results. They see you as independent thinker testing assumptions through personal experimentation. You appear as someone whose behavior change stories provide evidence for alternative approaches.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about results from unconventional approach. Intellectually activates interest in contrarian practice outcomes. Psychologically creates permission to experiment with alternative methods. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because personal transformation story promises real-world validation of contrarian approach.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because personal transformation stories from contrarian practices provide social proof for alternative approaches - audiences want permission to try unconventional methods. This hook leverages power of demonstrated change where your results validate practice more than theory. The contrarian framing positions practice as deliberately different from mainstream. By sharing what changed, you provide outcome evidence. This satisfies desire for validation of alternatives through demonstrated results.

## Example

What changed when I started blocking 4-hour focus blocks (instead of staying "available"): halved project completion time because eliminating context-switching reduced cognitive reload time, improved work quality from sustained deep thinking, and paradoxically improved responsiveness because I could give full attention to communications in designated times rather than fractured attention all day.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides real-world outcome evidence for contrarian practice audiences might hesitate to try. The personal-change framing makes results concrete and credible. By demonstrating benefits, you reduce risk perception of trying alternative approach. The hook satisfies desire for validation through demonstrated results not just theory. This combination of outcome evidence, concrete credibility, risk reduction, and demonstrated validation creates experimentally-motivated audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your positive outcomes from contrarian practice are coincidental or due to other factors. If you constantly adopt contrarian practices, you seem to chase novelty over effectiveness. The hook also fails if benefits you describe are placebo effects not actual practice results. If your contrarian practice works for your specific context but doesn't transfer to audiences, you've overgeneralized. Too many contrarian-practice stories can make you seem unable to commit to any approach long-term.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who adopt contrarian practices without understanding when they're appropriate versus mainstream approaches. You may attract people who try alternatives to feel special rather than solve problems. The constant contrarian emphasis can reduce appreciation for conventional practices that work well. If your practice results don't replicate for audiences, they've invested in ineffective approach. This approach can also encourage contrarianism as identity rather than practical experimentation. The personal-story focus may reduce understanding that your context differs from audiences' situations.
