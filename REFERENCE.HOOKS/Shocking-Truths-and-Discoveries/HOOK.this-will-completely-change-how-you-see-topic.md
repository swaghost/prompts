# This will completely change how you see [topic].

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as offering truly transformative information that will fundamentally and permanently alter their perception, understanding, or relationship with a familiar topic—not just learning but transformation.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Excitement about perceptual transformation
- Openness to having views fundamentally altered
- Anticipation for paradigm-shifting insight
- Curiosity about what could be so transformative
- Readiness for cognitive reorganization
- Recognition that current understanding is incomplete

## Psychology

The promise of "completely change how you see" something taps into our desire for breakthrough moments and transformative insights rather than incremental learning. "Completely" is absolute language that creates high expectations for fundamental shift rather than minor adjustment. This leverages our attraction to paradigm shifts—moments when entire frameworks of understanding reorganize around new insights. The format also creates curiosity through promised transformation—viewers want to experience that shift happening in real-time. It positions the creator as having access to perspective-altering insights, establishing authority through transformative knowledge.

## Example

"This will completely change how you see small talk: it's not wasting time before real conversation—it's the trust-building required before real conversation becomes possible."

## Why it works

This hook creates maximum anticipation by promising comprehensive perceptual transformation. "Completely change" sets extremely high bar that makes viewers eager to experience such fundamental shift. The personal application ("how you see") makes it about the viewer's experience rather than just information transfer. It positions content as transformative rather than educational, elevating value. The format creates before/after dynamic where viewers anticipate being different people (in their understanding) after consuming the content. It also validates that topics have hidden depth worth exploring and that transformative insights exist, creating optimism about continuous learning and growth.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails dramatically when the content doesn't actually change viewers' perspectives in any meaningful way, creating massive gap between promise and delivery. It loses credibility through overuse—if everything "completely changes" how you see things, the language becomes meaningless. If the insight is obvious, already known, or not actually paradigm-shifting, viewers feel manipulated by hyperbolic framing. The hook also underperforms when the topic isn't important enough to viewers for perspective change to matter. When the creator has to force-fit transformative framing onto incremental insights, it damages trust in their judgment about what's truly significant.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook sets extremely high expectations that are nearly impossible to meet consistently, potentially leading to viewer disappointment regardless of content quality. It may train audiences to expect constant transformation and breakthroughs, devaluing steady incremental learning and creating unsustainable content pressure. The hyperbolic language can make the creator seem prone to exaggeration, damaging credibility even when they're sharing genuinely valuable insights. If perspectives don't actually change for most viewers, repeated use of this hook makes it feel like empty marketing language. The format can also create exhaustion—constant perspective-shifting is cognitively demanding, and viewers may eventually resist having views challenged so frequently.
