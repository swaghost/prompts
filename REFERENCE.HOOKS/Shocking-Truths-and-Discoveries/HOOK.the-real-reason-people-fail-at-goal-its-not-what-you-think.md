# The real reason people fail at [goal] (it's not what you think)

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who understands true failure causes beyond obvious explanations. They see you as analytical about what actually predicts failure versus surface attributions. You appear as someone whose insight into real causes enables effective prevention.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers relief that failure may not be due to assumed inadequacy. Intellectually activates reexamination of failure attributions. Psychologically creates hope that addressing real cause enables success previously thought impossible. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "not what you think" promises revelation of misunderstood failure mechanism.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because if people fail for different reasons than believed, correcting misunderstanding enables success - audiences want accurate causal models. This hook leverages appeal of hidden explanations where surface reasons mask deeper causes. The "not what you think" framing creates expectation of counterintuitive revelation. By focusing on failure cause, you address pain point for struggling audiences. This satisfies desire for accurate diagnosis that enables effective intervention.

## Example

The real reason people fail at building habits (it's not what you think): they focus on motivation to start instead of designing systems that make habits easy when motivation is low - initial enthusiasm launches habit but friction determines whether it survives routine periods when excitement fades.

## Why it works

This hook works because it corrects misdiagnosis of failure causes, enabling better prevention strategies. The real-reason framing positions you as someone who sees beyond surface explanations. By contradicting expected causes, you provide novel understanding. The hook satisfies desire for accurate causal models that predict success and failure. This combination of misdiagnosis correction, deeper insight, novel understanding, and predictive accuracy creates strategically engaged audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your "real reason" is just alternative explanation not objectively more true than common one. If you constantly reveal real reasons, audiences question why you're the only one who sees truth. The hook also fails if you dismiss legitimate failure factors to promote pet theory. If real reason you identify is obvious to experienced practitioners, you're claiming novelty that doesn't exist. Too many failure-explanation revisions can make audiences confused about what actually predicts failure.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who distrust all failure explanations including accurate ones. You may attract people looking for external explanations that absolve personal responsibility. The constant explanation-revision can reduce appreciation for straightforward causes that don't require reframing. If your real-reason diagnosis is wrong, you've misdirected preventive efforts away from actual causes. This approach can also create cynicism about any failure explanation including yours. The counterintuitive emphasis may reduce trust in straightforward analysis that doesn't promise surprise.
