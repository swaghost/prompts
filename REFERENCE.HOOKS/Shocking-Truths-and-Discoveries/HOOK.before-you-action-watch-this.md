# Before you [action], watch this

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone providing crucial information that should precede specific action. They see you as protective enough to intervene before potentially costly mistakes. You appear as someone with essential knowledge that changes action outcomes.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers urgency to pause and gather information before proceeding. Intellectually activates recognition that action requires preparation or awareness. Psychologically creates FOMO about proceeding without your crucial input. Physically manifests as stopping scroll immediately because "before you" makes content time-sensitive prerequisite.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because "before you" creates temporal urgency - this information matters NOW if they're considering the action. This hook leverages preventative intervention where stopping mistakes before they happen feels more valuable than fixing them after. The directive "watch this" establishes authority - you're confident enough to command their attention. By positioning content as prerequisite to action, you make it feel necessary rather than optional. This creates protective urgency where proceeding without your information feels risky.

## Example

Before you quit your job to pursue your passion, watch this: understand your bare-minimum monthly expenses, have 12 months savings minimum, test passion as side project first to separate romance from reality - I've seen 30+ people return to corporate burned and desperate after 6 months.

## Why it works

This hook works because it positions content as essential input to decision-making rather than general information. The action-specific framing ensures relevance for audiences considering that exact step. By intervening before action, you provide decision-relevant value at perfect timing. The hook satisfies desire for complete information before commitments. This combination of decision-relevance, perfect timing, protective intervention, and completion assurance creates grateful attentive audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your information isn't actually crucial enough to warrant "must watch before acting" framing. If you constantly create before-you-do urgency around many actions, audiences realize it's manufactured pressure. The hook also fails if content doesn't deliver genuinely action-changing information. If you use fear-based messaging that overstates risks to boost engagement, you seem manipulative. Too many intervention hooks can make you seem like perpetual buzzkill warning against all actions.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating analysis-paralysis where audiences feel they need permission or complete information before any action. You may attract excessively cautious people who use research as procrastination. The constant before-warnings can position you as obstacle rather than guide. If audiences watch, take your advice, and still have bad outcomes, they blame you for failed intervention. This approach can also create dependency where audiences check with you before decisions rather than developing judgment. The protective stance may reduce empowerment if audiences feel they can't safely act without your input.
