# Here's why everyone's getting [topic] completely wrong

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who identifies widespread misconceptions that even majority opinion misses. They see you as independent thinker capable of seeing what masses miss. You appear as someone with clarity to recognize fundamental errors in popular understanding.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about what massive error everyone's making. Intellectually activates contrarian thinking to reconsider consensus understanding. Psychologically creates openness to fundamental correction of popular belief. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "everyone's wrong" suggests they might be too, requiring immediate correction.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because "everyone's getting it wrong" creates urgency - if masses are mistaken, avoiding same error requires immediate attention. This hook leverages contrarian appeal where going against mainstream feels intellectually sophisticated. The totality framing ("everyone," "completely") amplifies significance of misconception and correction. By positioning yourself as lone voice identifying widespread error, you create authority through unique perspective. The definitive "here's why" promises explanation not just claim, satisfying need for understanding.

## Example

Here's why everyone's getting productivity completely wrong: they optimize for busyness and task completion rather than impact and value creation - doing more things faster won't matter if they're the wrong things.

## Why it works

This hook works because it positions audiences who engage as joining you in enlightened minority versus mistaken masses. The widespread error framing makes your correction feel more valuable - you're fixing fundamental not minor misunderstanding. By explaining WHY everyone's wrong, you demonstrate superior grasp of topic fundamentals. The hook appeals to desire to avoid majority mistakes and be ahead of curve. This combination of enlightened positioning, high-value correction, superior understanding demonstration, and avoidance motivation creates engaged grateful audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you strawman popular understanding rather than engaging with its actual reasoning and context. If you constantly claim everyone's wrong about everything, audiences realize you're reflexively contrarian. The hook also fails if "everyone" isn't actually getting it wrong - many people may understand correctly. If your alternative understanding is actually the mistaken one, you lead audiences into error while claiming correction. Too much majority-correction can make you seem arrogant or disconnected from legitimate reasons for popular understanding.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who distrust all mainstream understanding, making them vulnerable to fringe ideas. You may attract perpetual contrarians who value dissent over accuracy, limiting community quality. The constant everyone's-wrong framing can isolate you from mainstream communities and collaborations. If you're proven wrong about claims that "everyone" is mistaken, credibility damage is severe because you claimed superior understanding. This approach can also alienate people who are part of "everyone" you claim is wrong, reducing reach unnecessarily. The superiority implied in calling out universal error may create resentment rather than receptiveness.
