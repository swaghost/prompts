# If you knew this, you'd never [common action].

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as someone revealing critical information that, if known, would immediately change behavior—offering protective knowledge that prevents harmful or wasteful actions.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Urgency to learn what they don't know
- Concern about continuing harmful actions
- Curiosity about behavior-changing knowledge
- Gratitude for protective information
- Immediate desire to stop the mentioned action
- Validation if they've been hesitant about the action

## Psychology

This hook creates powerful urgency through the implication that current behavior is based on incomplete information—if you had full knowledge, you'd act differently. It leverages the information gap principle by suggesting crucial missing knowledge that affects decision-making. This taps into our fear of unknowingly harming ourselves and our desire to make informed decisions. The definitive language ("you'd never") creates strong certainty that this knowledge changes everything, not just might influence. It positions the creator as protective educator preventing continued harm through knowledge sharing, establishing value through danger mitigation.

## Example

"If you knew this, you'd never post content without testing headlines: the headline determines 80% of reach, but 95% of creators post the first idea they think of."

## Why it works

This hook creates immediate relevance by addressing common actions viewers likely perform, making the content directly applicable and urgent. The conditional framing ("if you knew") implies viewers are acting without full information, creating both curiosity and concern. "You'd never" is definitive language that suggests behavior-changing information, not just interesting facts. It positions the knowledge as protective—preventing harm, waste, or inefficiency—which creates strong value. The format also creates slight anxiety about current behavior while promising relief through knowledge, driving engagement. It establishes the creator as someone who sees consequences others miss, building credibility through protective insight.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails when the information revealed doesn't actually justify "never" doing the action—if it just makes it less optimal rather than clearly harmful, the dramatic framing feels manipulative. It loses credibility if the common action is actually fine for many contexts or people, making the absolute language incorrect. If the reasoning is just opinion or preference rather than factual consequence, it undermines the definitive framing. The hook also underperforms when the "this" knowledge is actually widely known, making viewers feel patronized. When the action can't realistically be stopped or when stopping it is impractical, the hook creates frustration rather than empowerment.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can create excessive caution, analysis paralysis, or fear of action if overused—viewers may feel they can't do anything without first knowing hidden consequences. It may position the creator as someone who always finds problems with common practices, appearing negative rather than helpful. The definitive "never" language can feel paternalistic or controlling, telling viewers what they should do rather than informing their decisions. If viewers continue the action anyway (because stopping isn't practical or they disagree with the reasoning), it damages the creator's credibility. The hook can also make viewers feel foolish for not already knowing this, creating shame rather than gratitude.
