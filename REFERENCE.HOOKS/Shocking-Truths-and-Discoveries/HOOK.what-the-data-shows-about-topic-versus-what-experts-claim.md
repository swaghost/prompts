# What the data shows about [topic] versus what experts claim

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone distinguishing evidence from authority claims. They see you as analytical about data-expert discrepancies. You appear as someone whose data-versus-claims revelation prevents accepting expert assertions without evidence verification.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers skepticism about unsupported expert claims. Intellectually activates evidence-based thinking. Psychologically creates demand for data backing assertions. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "data versus expert claims" promises revelation of evidence-authority gaps.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because if data contradicts expert claims, evidence matters more than authority - understanding discrepancies enables accurate beliefs. This hook leverages appeal of critical thinking where prioritizing evidence demonstrates rationality. The "versus" framing creates conflict revelation. By showing data-claim gap, you enable evidence-based updating. This satisfies desire for beliefs grounded in evidence not just authority.

## Example

What the data shows about multitasking versus what experts claim: experts say multitasking is always bad; data shows context matters - simple repetitive tasks can benefit from interleaving while complex creative work suffers. Absolute expert claims miss nuance data reveals. Evidence shows conditional effectiveness experts oversimplify. Data enables nuanced understanding versus blanket rules.

## Why it works

This hook works because it reveals data-expert gaps enabling evidence-based belief formation. The data-versus-claims framing creates critical evaluation. By showing discrepancy, you model evidence prioritization. The hook satisfies desire for accurate understanding. This combination of discrepancy revelation, evidence emphasis, critical thinking, and accurate belief formation creates evidence-based audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you cherry-pick data supporting your view while dismissing expert synthesis or when you misunderstand nuanced expert positions. If you constantly pit data against experts, you foster anti-expertise. The hook also fails if you present conflicting interpretations as absolute contradictions. If data you cite is limited or misinterpreted, claim misleads. Too many data-versus-expert revelations can reduce appropriate respect for informed expert synthesis.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who dismiss expert knowledge preferring raw data they may misinterpret. You may attract people using data-claims to feel superior to experts. The constant expert-questioning can reduce appreciation for expert synthesis integrating multiple data sources. If you encourage naive empiricism, audiences miss expert interpretive value. This approach can also foster belief anyone with data equals experts. The evidence emphasis may reduce understanding that good experts base claims on data and discrepancies often reflect communication simplification not actual contradiction.
