# The research on [topic] will surprise you

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who stays current with research and shares findings that contradict common assumptions. They see you as evidence-oriented and willing to update beliefs based on data. You appear as someone who translates academic research into accessible insights.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about what research reveals that contradicts expectations. Intellectually activates interest in evidence-based understanding. Psychologically creates openness to belief updating based on scientific findings. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "will surprise" promises unexpected research-backed insights.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because research provides authoritative credibility beyond opinion - if findings surprise, they contradict common assumptions worth examining. This hook leverages appeal of scientific authority where research trumps intuition or conventional wisdom. The "will surprise" framing creates expectation of counterintuitive findings. By citing research, you position as informed intermediary bringing academic insights to broader audience. This satisfies desire for evidence-based understanding versus folk wisdom.

## Example

The research on multitasking will surprise you: attempting multiple cognitively demanding tasks simultaneously isn't just less efficient, it causes measurable IQ drops equivalent to sleep deprivation - the brain task-switches creating lag time and error increases that compound with each switch.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides research-backed credibility for counterintuitive claims audiences might otherwise dismiss. The surprise framing prepares audiences for expectation violation. By translating research, you make academic findings accessible and actionable. The hook satisfies desire for scientific grounding of practical decisions. This combination of research authority, expectation preparation, accessibility, and evidence-based action creates trusting engaged audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you misrepresent or oversimplify research to create surprise that doesn't exist in actual findings. If you constantly claim research will surprise, audiences realize you're using it as emphasis not accuracy. The hook also fails if you cherry-pick studies supporting your view while ignoring contradicting research. If research you cite is low-quality or not replicated, authority claim is undermined. Too much surprise-promising can reduce trust when research confirms rather than contradicts expectations.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who only trust research-backed claims, dismissing valuable experiential knowledge. You may attract people who use research as weapon in arguments rather than learning tool. The constant research citation can position you as dependent on external authority rather than independent thinking. If research consensus later shifts, your surprise-based claims become outdated. This approach can also create scientism where complex human experiences are reduced to measurable phenomena. The surprise emphasis may reduce appreciation for research that confirms existing understanding but adds nuance.
