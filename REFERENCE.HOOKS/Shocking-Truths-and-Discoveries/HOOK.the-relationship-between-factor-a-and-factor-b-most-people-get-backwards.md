Motivation follows action more than preceding it. "I'll do it when I feel motivated" keeps you stuck; "I'll act and motivation will follow" creates movement.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when relationship you describe is actually bidirectional not simply reversed or when correct direction varies by context. If your reversal claim is wrong, you've directed toward opposite but equally ineffective strategy. Too many backwards revelations can make audiences constantly question their causal understanding.

The correction framing may reduce appreciation for bidirectional influences where both directions operate.
