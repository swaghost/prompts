# What I wish someone told me before I started [journey]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as experienced guide who wants to spare audiences painful learning you endured. They see you as generous with hard-won wisdom. You appear as someone whose hindsight offers foresight for those beginning similar journeys.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers gratitude for wisdom-sharing that prevents painful mistakes. Intellectually activates interest in lessons from someone ahead on journey. Psychologically creates opportunity to learn from others' expensive education. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because hindsight wisdom promises shortcut past beginner mistakes.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because experienced people's wish-I'd-known insights compress learning timelines - audiences can avoid costly mistakes through wisdom inheritance. This hook leverages authority through experience where time-served credibility makes advice trustworthy. The "wish someone told me" framing demonstrates humility and generosity. By focusing on before starting, you ensure relevance to audiences early in journey or contemplating it. This satisfies desire for mentorship that prevents avoidable pain.

## Example

What I wish someone told me before I started freelancing: your first clients will almost certainly be difficult ones because you haven't learned to qualify leads yet - don't use difficult early experiences as evidence you're not cut out for it; they're training wheels teaching you to recognize and filter for good-fit clients.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides preventive wisdom that spares audiences from painful learning through experience. The wish-framing demonstrates both humility and generosity. By sharing what you lacked, you position as protective guide. The hook satisfies desire for accelerated learning through others' experiences. This combination of pain prevention, humble generosity, protective guidance, and timeline compression creates grateful beginning audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your wish-I'd-known advice wouldn't actually have helped earlier-you understand or believe it - wisdom requires readiness. If you constantly wish someone had told you things, you seem to regret rather than value your learning journey. The hook also fails if advice you wish you'd had is actually readily available - you just didn't seek it. If your specific journey isn't representative and advice doesn't transfer, you've overgeneralized from personal experience. Too much hindsight wisdom-sharing can make you seem to live in past rather than present.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who expect to skip necessary learning rather than compress it. You may attract people looking for shortcuts past all difficulties not just avoidable mistakes. The constant wish-framing can reduce appreciation for organic learning journeys with natural trial-and-error. If audiences try to implement advice without readiness wisdom requires, you've pushed them past appropriate learning pace. This approach can also devalue your actual learning journey by positioning it as series of mistakes to avoid. The protective emphasis may reduce resilience audiences need when they make mistakes despite your warnings.
