# This mistake is going to cost you [big consequence]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who can foresee costly consequences and wants to prevent them. They see you as protective and experienced, able to identify expensive mistakes before they manifest. You appear as someone who has witnessed or made the mistake yourself and wants to spare others.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers fear of preventable costly error. Intellectually activates risk assessment about current behaviors or beliefs. Psychologically creates loss aversion where avoiding mistake feels more urgent than gaining benefits. Physically manifests as immediate attention to identify what mistake threatens their resources or outcomes.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because loss aversion makes avoiding costs more motivating than pursuing gains - we're hardwired to prevent losses. This hook leverages future consequence framing where present actions have predictable but delayed costs, creating urgency to course-correct now. The word "going to" creates inevitability - this will happen unless you intervene, raising stakes. By focusing on "big" consequences rather than minor ones, you justify the warning's urgency and audience's attention investment. The personal relevance ("you") makes threat feel immediate rather than abstract.

## Example

This mistake is going to cost you 6 figures: treating your business like a job (trading time for money) instead of building systems that generate revenue without your constant presence.

## Why it works

This hook works because it positions you as protecting audiences from expensive preventable mistakes, creating gratitude and loyalty. The future tense allows current correction - they can still avoid the cost if they act now. By quantifying consequences as "big," you justify dramatic framing and signal this is priority-level information. The hook satisfies desire for experienced guidance that helps avoid learning everything the hard way. This combination of protective positioning, preventable loss framing, priority justification, and experiential guidance creates intensely engaged audiences motivated by loss prevention.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when consequences aren't actually as severe as framed, making you seem like fear-mongerer exaggerating risks. If you constantly warn about costly mistakes, audiences become numb or paranoid rather than thoughtfully cautious. The hook also fails if you can't clearly explain the causal connection between mistake and consequence. If your "solution" is just avoiding the mistake without viable alternatives, you haven't actually helped. Too much focus on costs creates negative fearful brand rather than positive empowering one.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating anxious audiences who see potential costly mistakes everywhere, leading to decision paralysis rather than wise action. You may attract audiences motivated primarily by fear rather than growth, limiting community positivity. The fear-based framing can make you seem like you profit from audience anxiety about making mistakes. If your cost predictions don't materialize or are proven exaggerated, you lose credibility on risk assessment. This approach can also make you seem focused on problems rather than opportunities, reducing inspirational quality. The dramatic framing may alienate audiences who prefer balanced perspective over alarming warnings.
