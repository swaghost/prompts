# The warning signs that [approach] isn't working (and what to do instead)

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who can identify when approaches fail and provide alternatives. They see you as diagnostic about warning signs. You appear as someone whose early-warning system prevents wasted persistence on failing approaches.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers concern about missing failure signals in own approach. Intellectually activates diagnostic thinking about approach effectiveness. Psychologically creates motivation to recognize and pivot from failing approaches. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "warning signs and what to do" promises both detection and solution.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because recognizing approach failure early prevents wasted persistence - audiences want diagnostic criteria for pivoting. This hook leverages appeal of early warning where identifying signals enables timely adjustment. The warning-signs format provides clear diagnostic criteria. By offering alternative, you provide complete pivot guidance. This satisfies desire for both detection and direction when approaches aren't working.

## Example

The warning signs that hustle culture isn't working (and what to do instead): physical symptoms (constant fatigue, stress illness), diminishing returns (more hours producing less output), life sacrifice (relationships, health eroding for work), resentment building toward work itself. Instead: sustainable pace with recovery, boundaries protecting energy, quality over quantity focus.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides diagnostic criteria for recognizing failing approaches plus alternative direction. The warning-signs format enables self-assessment. By offering instead-option, you prevent diagnostic paralysis. The hook satisfies desire for complete guidance including detection and solution. This combination of early warning, self-assessment, alternative provision, and complete guidance creates wisely pivoting audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your warning signs could indicate multiple issues not just stated approach failure or when alternative you suggest isn't actually better. If you constantly provide warning signs for approaches, you seem to see failure everywhere. The hook also fails if you encourage premature abandonment when approach needs time to work. If warning signs you identify are obvious severe problems anyone would recognize, diagnostic value is low. Too many warning-signs lists can make audiences constantly second-guessing effective approaches.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who abandon approaches at first difficulty rather than pushing through necessary adaptation periods. You may attract people looking for permission to quit when things get challenging. The constant warning-sign focus can reduce appreciation for persistence through temporary difficulties. If your diagnostic criteria are wrong, you've encouraged abandoning effective approaches. This approach can also foster approach-hopping where audiences constantly seek warning signs rather than committing long-term. The alternative emphasis may reduce understanding that sometimes improving current approach beats switching.
