# The [industry] lie you've been believing

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewers should perceive the creator as an industry insider or expert with sufficient knowledge to identify and expose deliberate misinformation or misleading marketing narratives that serve industry interests rather than consumer welfare. The creator appears protective of their audience, acting as whistleblower or advocate willing to sacrifice industry relationships to reveal truth, positioning them as trustworthy ally against manipulative commercial forces.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Intellectually: Viewers should prepare to learn how they've been systematically misled by commercial interests, requiring mental revision of previous understanding shaped by marketing. Emotionally: Initial skepticism or defensive resistance followed by betrayal or anger at being deceived, ultimately leading to gratitude toward creator for revelation and protection from continued exploitation. Psychologically: Activation of distrust toward industry claims, desire to share the revelation with others being similarly misled, and increased trust in creator as honest broker. Physically: Alert, possibly defensive posture initially, potentially shifting to note-taking and information-gathering mode once receptive.

## Psychology

Conspiracy and deception detection → positions creator as protector revealing deliberate misinformation. The word "lie" is stronger than "myth" or "misconception," explicitly framing the misinformation as intentional deception rather than innocent error, triggering moral outrage and protective responses. Identifying the source as "industry" rather than individuals creates clear villain (faceless commercial interests) versus hero (truth-telling creator), simplifying moral narrative. The phrase "you've been believing" personalizes the deception, making viewers feel directly victimized and creating urgency to stop being manipulated.

## Example

The skincare lie you've been believing.

## Why it works

This hook works because it transforms viewers from passive consumers into potential victims of deception, creating strong motivation to learn protective information. The accusation of deliberate lying triggers moral judgment and anger that drives engagement far more powerfully than neutral correction of misconceptions. By naming "industry" as culprit, it allows viewers to externalize blame rather than feeling foolish for being wrong, preserving ego while still motivating behavior change. The hook also works through creating us-versus-them framing where creator and audience unite against manipulative commercial forces, building strong parasocial bond through shared opposition. It particularly resonates in domains where consumers already harbor suspicion of marketing claims (supplements, beauty, finance), validating existing skepticism while providing specific examples. The format creates highly shareable content as viewers want to warn friends and family who are "also being lied to," creating missionary zeal that extends reach. Additionally, the exposure of deliberate deception makes the creator appear courageous for breaking industry ranks, even if they're not actually industry insiders.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails if the "lie" is actually oversimplification of legitimate science or marketing rather than deliberate deception, making the accusation unfair and the creator appear conspiratorial or anti-business. If evidence doesn't support intentional deception versus honest differences in interpretation or emphasis, the strong "lie" framing backfires. The hook also fails when it descends into general industry-bashing without specific, verifiable claims, appearing as empty provocation. If the creator has commercial interests that bias their "revelation" (promoting alternatives they profit from), the hook's moral authority collapses entirely. The dramatic framing can also trigger legal concerns if specific companies feel defamed, creating risk beyond typical criticism. Additionally, if viewers already knew about the claimed "lie," the hook feels condescending rather than revelatory. When multiple creators expose the "same" lies, the space becomes crowded and the revelation loses impact.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can create overly cynical audience that distrusts all industry information indiscriminately, making them vulnerable to alternative misinformation or preventing them from benefiting from legitimate products/services. It may attract conspiracy-minded viewers who extend the distrust beyond warranted skepticism, shifting community culture toward paranoia. The adversarial framing can limit future partnership opportunities and industry access that would benefit content quality. If the "lie" claim is wrong or exaggerated, it can result in legal threats, public corrections, or severe credibility damage that affects creator's entire body of work. The format may pressure creator to continually find new "lies" to expose, pushing toward increasingly questionable claims to maintain the brand. It can also create hostile relationships with industry professionals who might otherwise collaborate or provide valuable information. Regular use positions creator as perpetual opponent rather than constructive critic, limiting influence on actual industry practices. Finally, viewers who work in the criticized industry may feel attacked or alienated, shrinking potential audience unnecessarily.
