# This [topic] myth is killing your results

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as myth-buster identifying false beliefs that damage outcomes. They see you as protective of audience results. You appear as someone whose myth-correction revelation demonstrates you prioritize audience success over comfortable but false conventional wisdom.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers concern about believing damaging myth combined with relief at correction. Intellectually activates belief-examination thinking. Psychologically creates urgency to correct false assumptions. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "myth killing results" promises revealing harmful false belief enabling performance improvement through correction.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because false beliefs that damage results create urgent correction need - if myth is harming outcomes, correction offers immediate improvement. This hook leverages appeal of performance-blocking removal where identifying damaging beliefs enables results liberation. The "killing your results" framing creates maximum urgency. By correcting myths, you remove invisible performance barriers. This satisfies desire for obstacle identification enabling breakthrough.

## Example

This productivity myth is killing your results: multitasking increases output - reality: switching between tasks reduces efficiency 40% per cognitive science. Brain doesn't actually multitask; it context-switches creating cognitive overhead. Myth promises more; delivers less. Believing it sabotages productivity. Truth: single-tasking with focus beats divided attention always. Myth correction liberates performance.

## Why it works

This hook works because it identifies specific false belief damaging outcomes creating urgent correction opportunity. The "killing results" framing establishes high stakes. By exposing myth's harm, you provide performance-recovery path. The hook satisfies desire for obstacle removal. This combination of myth-correction, harm identification, urgency creation, and performance-recovery creates audiences who abandon false beliefs and adopt your corrected understanding improving their results.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you label nuanced advice as myth or when you create false dichotomies ignoring contexts where "myth" actually works. If you constantly expose result-killing myths, you foster excessive belief-questioning and analysis paralysis. The hook also fails if you use myth-busting to position only your perspective as valid. If identified myth is actually reasonable heuristic not false belief, correction misleads. Too many myth-killing claims can reduce appreciation for how simplified rules often work well enough despite technical inaccuracy.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who constantly question all guidance seeking hidden myths becoming unable to act on any advice. You may attract people who use myth-identification to excuse poor results blaming beliefs rather than execution. The constant myth-busting focus can reduce appreciation for how useful simplifications enable action despite imperfection. If your myth correction is context-specific not universal, followers may abandon approaches that work for them. This approach can also foster belief all conventional wisdom is myth. The correction emphasis may reduce understanding that while some beliefs do damage results, most simplified guidance reflects practical tradeoffs, and obsessing over myth-correction often reflects perfectionism rather than performance optimization.
