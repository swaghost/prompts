# Why [outcome] has less to do with [assumed factor] and more to do with [actual factor]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone correcting causal misattributions. They see you as analytical about actual versus assumed outcome drivers. You appear as someone whose causal-correction redirects effort toward real leverage points away from misleading assumed factors.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers realization about potentially misplaced effort. Intellectually activates accurate causal thinking. Psychologically creates motivation to focus on actual drivers. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "less assumed more actual factor" promises revelation of real outcome drivers enabling strategic focus correction.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because if outcomes depend on different factors than assumed, effort allocation needs correction - understanding actual causation enables effective focus. This hook leverages appeal of accurate understanding where correcting misattribution improves results. The "less-more" framing creates causal reallocation. By revealing actual factor, you redirect strategic effort. This satisfies desire for understanding what actually drives outcomes versus intuitive but wrong assumptions.

## Example

Why business success has less to do with perfect product and more to do with distribution capability: great products without reach stay unknown; mediocre products with excellent distribution succeed - "build it and they will come" is myth. Product matters but distribution determines who experiences it. Obsessing over perfection while neglecting reach misallocates effort. Actual factor (distribution) beats assumed factor (perfection).

## Why it works

This hook works because it corrects causal misattributions enabling effort redirection toward actual leverage. The less-assumed-more-actual framing creates correction. By revealing real driver, you prevent wasted effort. The hook satisfies desire for accurate causation. This combination of misattribution correction, effort redirection, accurate causation, and leverage focus creates strategically focused audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when both factors actually matter or when you dismiss important factors to emphasize your preferred one. If you constantly reveal actual-versus-assumed factors, you seem contrarian about all intuitive causation. The hook also fails if you oversimplify multicausal outcomes into simple reattributions. If actual factor you emphasize isn't more important, claim misleads. Too many causal corrections can reduce confidence in intuitive understanding.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who neglect obvious important factors pursuing non-obvious ones you emphasize. You may attract people who prefer feeling sophisticated through non-intuitive causation beliefs. The constant misattribution focus can reduce appreciation for intuitive understanding often being correct. If your causal claim is wrong, you've misdirected strategic effort significantly. This approach can also encourage dismissing multiple contributing factors for single "actual" causes. The correction emphasis may reduce understanding that most outcomes are multicausal and both assumed and "actual" factors usually contribute together.
