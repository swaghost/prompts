# I tested [thing] for [time period] and here's what happened

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who commits to experiments and documents results transparently. They see you as empirical and patient, willing to invest time in thorough testing. You appear as someone who learns through direct experience rather than just theory or opinion.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about real-world results from sustained experiment. Intellectually activates interest in longitudinal findings versus one-time observations. Psychologically creates vicarious learning where they benefit from your time investment. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because specific time frame signals rigorous commitment rather than superficial trial.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because specific time periods signal methodological rigor - you didn't just try something once but committed to sustained testing. This hook leverages social proof through personal experiment where your lived experience provides evidence more compelling than theory. The "here's what happened" framing creates story arc with setup and resolution, activating narrative engagement. Time investment demonstrates you sacrificed something (time, effort) to generate insights, making findings feel more valuable.

## Example

I tested the 5am morning routine for 90 days straight and here's what happened: productivity increased 40% but I was miserable until I shifted to 6am, proving optimal wake time is personal not universal.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides longitudinal evidence most audiences can't generate themselves due to time/commitment required. The specific time frame quantifies your investment, making results feel earned and credible. By sharing both positive and negative findings, you demonstrate honest reporting rather than confirmation bias. The hook satisfies desire for real-world validation of popular advice before audiences invest their own time. This combination of surrogate experimentation, quantified commitment, honest reporting, and validation service creates grateful engaged audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when time period is too short to produce meaningful results or too long to seem credible you maintained consistency. If you don't share methodology clearly, audiences can't assess if your test was rigorous or representative. The hook also fails if you cherry-pick positive results while hiding negative ones - selective reporting destroys trust. If your personal results won't generalize to audiences due to unique circumstances, test seems irrelevant. Too many personal experiments can make you seem like perpetual self-experimenter rather than settled expert.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who wait for you to test everything rather than learning to experiment themselves. You may attract people seeking vicarious experience rather than direct learning, limiting their growth. The personal nature means your results may not transfer to others, creating disappointment when they replicate experiment. If you benefit from products or methods you tested (affiliate relationships), audiences may question experimental objectivity. This approach can also create pressure to constantly conduct newsworthy experiments, potentially leading to testing things for content rather than genuine curiosity. The testimonial format may reduce your authority versus expertise from systematic research or training.
