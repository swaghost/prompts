# Everything you know about [topic] is wrong

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewers should perceive the creator as someone with access to superior or contrarian knowledge that challenges mainstream understanding, positioning them as an enlightened insider willing to expose widespread misconceptions. The creator appears brave for contradicting popular belief while being knowledgeable enough to back up bold claims, creating perception of thought leadership and intellectual courage that distinguishes them from creators who merely repeat conventional wisdom.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Intellectually: Viewers experience cognitive dissonance requiring resolution through continued watching to understand how their knowledge is incorrect. Emotionally: Initial defensiveness or skepticism followed by curiosity about what they've misunderstood, potentially leading to revelation satisfaction if claims are validated. Psychologically: Activation of learning motivation through recognition of knowledge gaps, desire to avoid being wrong or misinformed, and intrigue about accessing "real" information. Physically: Alert posture, focused attention as they mentally inventory their current beliefs to compare against coming revelations.

## Psychology

Pattern break → makes audience question their beliefs. This hook exploits the mind's natural response to unexpected contradiction of established patterns or beliefs, creating cognitive dissonance that demands resolution through continued engagement. The absolute framing ("everything") creates maximum disruption to existing mental models, forcing complete attention to understand the scope and nature of the claimed error. The implication that viewers have been systematically misinformed creates urgency to correct understanding and avoid continued mistakes based on false information.

## Example

Everything you know about dieting is wrong.

## Why it works

This hook works because it simultaneously challenges ego (you thought you knew something) and offers ego repair (you'll know the truth after watching), creating powerful motivation to resolve the tension. The absolutist language ("everything") creates strong pattern interrupt that cuts through scroll fatigue by contradicting assumptions viewers hold with confidence. It positions the creator as possessing superior knowledge while implicitly criticizing other sources that led viewers astray, creating opportunity for the creator to become new trusted authority. The hook also triggers the psychological phenomenon where people pay disproportionate attention to information suggesting they've been wrong, as correcting errors feels more urgent than learning new information. Additionally, it creates shareable content opportunity as viewers want to share corrections with others who are "also wrong," extending reach through missionary zeal of the newly enlightened.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails catastrophically if the "correction" being offered is itself incorrect, misleading, or merely semantic wordplay rather than substantive revelation, as this destroys creator credibility permanently. If the actual content reveals that only minor or obvious aspects were "wrong" rather than fundamental understanding, viewers feel manipulated by the overblown hook. The hook also fails when it triggers defensive resistance rather than curious openness, especially if viewers have strong emotional investment in existing beliefs or if the topic relates to identity. When the creator cannot back up the strong claim with compelling evidence, the hook becomes empty provocation. Additionally, if the format becomes repetitive (creator claims everything is wrong about many topics), it loses impact and creator appears contrarian for attention rather than truth-seeking.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can create hostile comment sections where viewers with expertise or different perspectives challenge the creator's claims, especially when "wrong" is subjective or contextual rather than objectively verifiable. It may attract audience members predisposed to conspiracy thinking or distrust of established knowledge, shifting community culture toward cynicism rather than learning. The absolutist framing can be easily disproven by viewers who find exceptions, making the creator appear sloppy or dishonest even if core message has merit. Regular use of this hook can position the creator as perpetual contrarian, limiting collaboration opportunities and making them appear arrogant or needlessly provocative. It may also create pressure to continually escalate claims to maintain shock value, pushing creator toward increasingly extreme or poorly supported positions. Finally, viewers who trust the creator may uncritically accept claims and spread misinformation to others, creating reputational risk if corrections prove necessary.
