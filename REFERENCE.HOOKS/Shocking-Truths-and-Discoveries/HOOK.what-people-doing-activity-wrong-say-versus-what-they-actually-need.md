# What people doing [activity] wrong say versus what they actually need

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who understands gap between stated problems and actual needs. They see you as analytical about what people ask for versus what would actually help. You appear as someone whose diagnostic skill reveals true needs beneath surface requests.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about whether they're misidentifying own needs. Intellectually activates interest in diagnostic thinking. Psychologically creates openness to reframing stated problems. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "say versus actually need" promises revelation of misdiagnosis everyone makes.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because if people misidentify needs, solving stated problem won't help - accurate diagnosis enables effective solutions. This hook leverages appeal of deeper understanding where surface requests mask real needs. The say-versus-need framing creates diagnostic framework. By revealing true needs, you prevent wasted effort on wrong solutions. This satisfies desire for accurate problem identification versus treating symptoms.

## Example

What people struggling with consistency say versus what they actually need: they say "I need more motivation" when they actually need friction reduction - environment changes that make desired behavior easier than undesired behavior, not inspirational content. Motivation gets them started; low friction keeps them going when motivation fades.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides diagnostic insight revealing true needs beneath misidentified requests. The say-versus-need framework creates clarity. By correcting misdiagnosis, you enable effective solutions targeting real problems. The hook satisfies desire for accurate problem identification. This combination of diagnostic revelation, clarity creation, solution effectiveness, and accurate targeting creates properly directed audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you dismiss people's stated needs as wrong without understanding their context. If you constantly tell people they're misidentifying needs, you seem condescending. The hook also fails if you create false dichotomy where stated and actual needs aren't mutually exclusive. If your diagnosis of "actual need" is wrong, you've misdirected to different ineffective solution. Too much need-reframing can make audiences distrust their own problem identification.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who don't trust their own needs assessment, always seeking external diagnosis. You may attract people who prefer being told what they need over developing self-awareness. The constant reframing can reduce confidence in own problem-solving capacity. If your actual-need diagnosis is wrong, you've replaced one misdiagnosis with another. This approach can also create dependency on your diagnostic framework rather than developing own analytical skills. The expert-positioning may reduce respect for people's own understanding of their situations.
