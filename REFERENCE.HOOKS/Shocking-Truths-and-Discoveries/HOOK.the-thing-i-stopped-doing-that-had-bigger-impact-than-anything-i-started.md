# The thing I stopped doing that had bigger impact than anything I started

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone discovering subtraction's power over addition through experience. They see you as reflective about elimination impact. You appear as someone whose stopped-doing impact validates strategic removal over constant adding.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers interest in what to stop rather than start. Intellectually activates subtraction impact thinking. Psychologically creates permission for strategic elimination. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "stopped doing had bigger impact than started" promises revelation that removing beats adding for results.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because if stopping creates bigger impact than starting, subtraction deserves strategic focus - elimination often more powerful than addition. This hook leverages appeal of discovered wisdom where experience validates counterintuitive approaches. The "bigger impact than anything started" framing creates strong elimination value. By sharing stopped-doing impact, you validate subtraction strategy. This satisfies desire for understanding that strategic removal often beats strategic addition.

## Example

The thing I stopped doing that had bigger impact than anything I started: stopped checking email first thing morning - elimination of immediate reactivity created space for proactive priority work. Started many productivity systems; stopping one interruption habit transformed entire day structure. Subtraction created space addition never achieved. Removing interference beat adding techniques. What you stop determines what you can do.

## Why it works

This hook works because it validates subtraction through personal impact story enabling strategic elimination thinking. The stopped-bigger-than-started framing creates removal emphasis. By sharing elimination success, you enable subtraction strategy. The hook satisfies desire for understanding removal power. This combination of personal validation, subtraction emphasis, elimination success, and removal impact creates strategically eliminating audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you romanticize doing less or when stopped-doing story is unique benefit not transferable insight. If you constantly emphasize stopping over starting, you seem to think elimination is always answer. The hook also fails if you use subtraction story to avoid acknowledging necessary additions. If stopped thing wasn't actually impactful, story lacks credibility. Too many stopped-doing stories can reduce appreciation for necessary additions and positive actions.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences focused only on elimination without building positive elements. You may attract people using subtraction philosophy to avoid committing to building things. The constant stopping emphasis can reduce appreciation for positive action necessity. If your stopped-doing wasn't actually high-impact, you've modeled suboptimal choice. This approach can also foster belief doing less is always better strategy. The elimination emphasis may reduce understanding that high performance usually requires both strategic subtraction of distractions and intentional addition of value-creating activities.
