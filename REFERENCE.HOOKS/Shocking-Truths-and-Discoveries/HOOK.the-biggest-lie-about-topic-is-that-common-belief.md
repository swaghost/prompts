# The biggest lie about [topic] is that [common belief]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who identifies and corrects widespread misinformation. They see you as analytical enough to recognize systematic falsehoods. You appear as someone protecting audiences from believing and acting on lies.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers alarm about believing falsehood mixed with gratitude for correction. Intellectually activates critical examination of accepted beliefs. Psychologically creates openness to belief revision when framed as lie-correction. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "biggest lie" signals important widespread misinformation requiring immediate correction.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because labeling something "the biggest lie" elevates it beyond mere misconception to deliberate or systematic falsehood. This hook leverages betrayal detection where discovering we've been lied to activates intense attention. The superlative "biggest" prioritizes this correction above other misinformation. By naming common belief, you ensure audience recognition and potential subscription to lie. This creates urgency to correct before continued belief causes harm. The definitive framing demonstrates confidence in your correction.

## Example

The biggest lie about entrepreneurship is that successful founders are fearless risk-takers - actually, successful entrepreneurs are calculated risk-managers who reduce downside systematically while maintaining upside exposure, making them look bold only because they've made risks safer through preparation.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides protective correction that prevents audiences from continuing to believe and act on falsehood. The lie-framing creates moral urgency beyond intellectual curiosity. By explaining what's true instead, you provide replacement belief not just negation. The hook satisfies desire to understand reality accurately rather than operating on misinformation. This combination of protective intervention, moral urgency, replacement provision, and accuracy pursuit creates grateful attentive audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you label honest disagreement or oversimplification as "lies" - not all inaccuracy is deception. If you constantly expose "biggest lies," audiences realize hyperbolic framing. The hook also fails if common belief is actually accurate or context-dependent rather than false. If you present your controversial opinion as lie-correction, you mislead about epistemic status. Too much lie-calling can make you seem paranoid or unable to engage good-faith disagreement.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who see lies everywhere, fostering paranoia and distrust. You may attract conspiracy-minded people who assume systematic deception. The constant lie-calling can position you as someone who assumes bad faith rather than honest error. If your lie-corrections are wrong, you've spread misinformation while claiming correction. This approach can also invite legal action if you accuse specific entities of lying without evidence. The moral framing may reduce nuanced understanding where beliefs are oversimplified rather than deliberately false.
