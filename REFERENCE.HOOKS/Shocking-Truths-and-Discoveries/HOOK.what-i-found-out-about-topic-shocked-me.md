# What I found out about [topic] shocked me

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as honest and relatable - someone who shares genuine discoveries even when they're surprising. They see you as a fellow learner rather than a know-it-all, which builds trust. You appear humble yet informative, making discoveries accessible through personal experience.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers vicarious surprise - if it shocked you, it might shock them too. Intellectually activates curiosity about what specific discovery caused such a strong reaction. Psychologically creates credibility through personal testimony rather than abstract claims. Physically manifests as stopping scroll to discover what was shocking enough to move you emotionally.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because personal discovery builds credibility - you're sharing lived experience, not repeating information. The admission of being shocked suggests humility and honesty; you didn't know everything either, making you relatable. Emotional contagion plays a key role - your shock transfers to the audience, making them curious to experience the same revelation. This hook leverages testimony power where firsthand accounts feel more trustworthy than general statements because they're rooted in specific experience.

## Example

What I found out about bank fees shocked me: they make more from overdrafts than from loans.

## Why it works

This hook works because it positions you as a guide who's walked the path before them, discovering important truths along the way. The personal framing ("I found out") makes abstract topics feel concrete and real - you experienced this, so it's not theoretical. Your emotional reaction (shock) serves as social proof that the information is significant, helping audiences prioritize attention. The hook also creates a journey narrative where you're inviting them to share in your discovery process, which feels collaborative rather than preachy. By admitting surprise, you signal that this information isn't common knowledge, increasing its perceived value.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when what shocked you is actually common knowledge, making you seem uninformed or naive rather than insightful. If you overuse the "shocked" reaction, audiences stop trusting your judgment - nothing can legitimately shock you that often without seeming performative. The hook also fails if you can't explain why the discovery was personally significant; without context, your shock seems arbitrary. If your discovery is trivial or obvious, the emotional investment (shock) appears disproportionate, reducing credibility. Too much focus on your reaction rather than the actual insight can make content feel self-centered.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by positioning you as someone who's continuously surprised by things others already knew, undermining your authority over time. If audiences are not shocked by your discovery, they may question your expertise or feel the content doesn't match the emotional promise. The personal focus can attract audiences more interested in your reactions than the information itself, creating unsustainable personality-dependent content. If your "shocking" discoveries are later proven false or exaggerated, you lose credibility because you attached personal testimony to inaccurate claims. This approach may also make you seem emotionally volatile if you're constantly "shocked" by new information.
