# Why the opposite of [common advice] might be better for you

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who recognizes common advice doesn't work universally and opposite may serve some people better. They see you as thoughtful about individual differences versus one-size-fits-all guidance. You appear as someone validating alternative approaches for those poorly served by mainstream advice.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers relief that struggling with common advice doesn't mean personal failing. Intellectually activates interest in why opposite approach might work better. Psychologically creates permission to do opposite of what everyone recommends. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "opposite might be better" promises validation for those struggling with mainstream guidance.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because if common advice hasn't worked, opposite approach offers hopeful alternative - audiences want permission to try contrarian path. This hook leverages appeal of personalized guidance where one-size-fits-all fails some people. The "might be better for you" framing acknowledges individual variation not universal truth. By suggesting opposite, you validate audiences questioning mainstream without demanding full rejection. This satisfies desire for guidance that accounts for individual circumstances versus generic prescription.

## Example

Why the opposite of "morning person" productivity advice might be better for you: if you're naturally night-focused, forcing morning routine creates chronic stress from fighting biology - better to optimize schedule around natural energy peaks even if unconventional, because working with rather than against circadian rhythm yields better output and sustainability.

## Why it works

This hook works because it validates audiences for whom mainstream advice doesn't work, reducing self-blame. The opposite-framing provides clear alternative. By acknowledging individual variation, you demonstrate sophisticated understanding. The hook satisfies desire for personalized guidance that respects differences. This combination of validation provision, alternative clarity, sophisticated acknowledgment, and personalization creates relieved engaged audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you dismiss tested advice as wrong rather than acknowledging it works for many. If you constantly suggest opposites, you seem reflexively contrarian. The hook also fails if you don't explain when/why opposite works better - just contradicting without reasoning. If opposite you suggest is harmful for audiences who try it, you've given bad permission. Too much opposite-advice can make audiences confused about what approaches to follow.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who try opposites without considering whether mainstream advice actually suits them. You may attract people using "individual differences" to avoid challenging but beneficial approaches. The constant opposite-suggesting can reduce appreciation for advice that works for majority. If audiences adopt opposite approach when mainstream would serve them better, you've misdirected. This approach can also encourage premature abandonment of advice before giving it fair trial. The permission-granting may reduce perseverance needed for approaches that work but require adaptation period.
