# I used to believe [thing] until I discovered [contradiction]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone whose beliefs evolved through discovery rather than ideology. They see you as intellectually honest about changing views when evidence contradicts. You appear as someone modeling belief revision audiences can emulate.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about what discovery changed your belief. Intellectually activates interest in evidence that contradicts common beliefs. Psychologically creates permission to revise own beliefs when encountering contradictions. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because belief-contradiction creates narrative tension worth resolving.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because belief change stories demonstrate intellectual humility and growth - audiences respect those who update beliefs rather than defending them dogmatically. This hook leverages transformation testimony where personal journey provides both instruction and permission. The "until I discovered" framing emphasizes discovery-driven change versus persuasion or opinion shift. By admitting prior belief, you create relatability with audiences who currently hold it. This reduces defensiveness while modeling how to change minds gracefully.

## Example

I used to believe working longer hours meant more output until I discovered cognitive research showing focused work quality degrades after 4-5 hours - I was producing more time but less value, mistaking presence for productivity.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides both credibility through lived experience and model for belief revision. The contradiction format creates clear before/after understanding. By sharing discovery that changed you, you provide same catalyst for audiences. The hook satisfies desire to learn from others' intellectual journeys. This combination of experiential credibility, revision modeling, catalyst provision, and learning opportunity creates open engaged audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your prior belief is strawman you never actually held or extreme position few share. If you constantly used-to-believe different things, you seem unstable or lacking judgment. The hook also fails if your "discovery" is actually controversial opinion not established fact. If contradiction isn't actually contradictory just different emphasis, framing misleads. Too many belief-changes can reduce authority - why trust current beliefs if they keep reversing?

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences in perpetual belief revision who never develop stable frameworks. You may attract people who value belief flexibility over truth-seeking. The constant change emphasis can reduce your authority as expert with established expertise. If audiences adopt your new belief and it's later shown wrong, you've led them through multiple incorrect positions. This approach can also make you seem like you're easily swayed versus thoughtfully updating. The relatability through prior wrong belief may reduce respect if audiences see pattern of initially wrong beliefs.
