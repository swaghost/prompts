# The crazy truth about [thing you use daily]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who reveals surprising facts about familiar things, making the ordinary feel extraordinary. They see you as observant, knowledgeable, and capable of noticing what others overlook. You appear as an educator who makes everyday life more interesting through unexpected insights.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers surprise mixed with curiosity about something they interact with regularly. Intellectually activates reconsideration of familiar objects or habits they've never questioned. Psychologically creates everyday disruption - challenging assumptions about routine things makes them lean in because it directly affects their life. Physically manifests as immediate attention grab, stopping scroll, and heightened focus because the topic is personally relevant.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because of unexpected everyday disruption - when something familiar is revealed to be different than assumed, it creates cognitive dissonance that demands resolution. Humans pay more attention to information about things we use daily because it has immediate practical relevance to our lives. The word "crazy" signals that the truth significantly contradicts common understanding, intensifying the surprise factor. This hook leverages the mere-exposure effect in reverse - we think we know familiar things well, so discovering we don't triggers powerful curiosity.

## Example

The crazy truth about bottled water: most brands are just filtered tap water marked up 1000%.

## Why it works

This hook works because it takes something people interact with constantly and reveals hidden reality, making the revelation feel immediately actionable and relevant. The everyday focus ensures universal relatability - almost everyone uses the daily thing mentioned, creating broad audience appeal. By disrupting assumptions about routine choices, you make people reconsider automatic behaviors, which feels empowering and smart. The hook also creates shareability because telling others "did you know?" facts about common items makes for interesting conversation. This positions you as someone who helps audiences make more informed decisions about everyday life.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when the "crazy truth" is actually well-known among informed people, making your revelation seem obvious or behind-the-times. If you sensationalize minor details about familiar things, audiences feel manipulated by clickbait rather than genuinely informed. Overuse trains people to question everything in their life, which can create decision paralysis rather than empowerment. The hook also fails if your revelation lacks practical implications - knowing a "crazy truth" that doesn't change anything feels like trivia rather than valuable insight. If your tone is too alarmist about everyday things, you risk making audiences anxious about normal activities.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by making your audience paranoid about routine aspects of life, creating anxiety rather than empowerment. You may develop a reputation as someone who just shares negative facts about everyday things, making your content feel exhausting rather than enlightening. If your "crazy truths" are later debunked or shown to be misleading, you lose credibility on all claims. The constant disruption of familiar assumptions can erode audience's sense of stability and trust in their own judgment. This approach may also alienate sponsors or partners in industries you regularly critique, limiting monetization options.
