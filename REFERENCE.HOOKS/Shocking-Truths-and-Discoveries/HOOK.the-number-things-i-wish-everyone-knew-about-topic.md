# The [number] things I wish everyone knew about [topic]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who has distilled essential knowledge into prioritized list. They see you as experienced enough to identify what truly matters. You appear as someone who wants to elevate collective understanding by sharing crucial insights.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers anticipation of curated essential knowledge. Intellectually activates list-based information processing with clear structure. Psychologically creates expectation of comprehensive but manageable insight collection. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because numbered list promises complete yet digestible wisdom package.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because numbered lists create structure promise - specific count means complete package, not overwhelming infinite information. This hook leverages the effectiveness of finite goals where audiences commit knowing exactly what investment is required (number items). The "I wish everyone knew" framing suggests these insights would improve collective outcomes if widely shared, elevating your motivation from selfish to altruistic. By curating to specific number, you demonstrate synthesis ability - identifying truly essential insights versus exhaustive listing. This satisfies desire for curated over raw information.

## Example

The 5 things I wish everyone knew about personal finance: (1) compound interest works both for and against you, (2) time in market beats timing market, (3) lifestyle inflation prevents wealth, (4) insurance protects wealth, (5) financial literacy is learned not inherited.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides clear scope reducing overwhelm while promising comprehensive essential coverage. The wish-everyone-knew framing positions insights as universally valuable rather than niche. By using specific number, you help audiences commit to consuming complete content knowing the endpoint. The hook satisfies desire for curated expert distillation rather than overwhelming comprehensive resources. This combination of clear scope, universal value, commitment enablement, and expert curation creates engaged grateful audiences who complete content.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your numbered items aren't actually essential or contain obvious widely-known information. If you constantly wish everyone knew different numbered things, your curation seems arbitrary rather than thoughtful. The hook also fails if number is too high (overwhelm) or too low (insufficient depth) for topic's actual essential insights. If your items are really sub-points of one thing or unrelated random facts, list structure seems forced. Too many numbered lists can make your content feel formulaic rather than organic.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by training audiences to expect packaged numbered insights rather than engaging with complex longform exploration. You may attract people who want bite-sized content over depth, limiting community sophistication. The constant wish-everyone-knew framing can seem preachy or assuming ignorance rather than sharing among equals. If your essential items are actually debatable or contextual, you present opinions as universal truths. This approach can also pressure you to artificially package insights into arbitrary numbers that fit hook rather than natural synthesis. The list format may reduce nuanced understanding where items interrelate in complex ways.
