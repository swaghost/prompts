# If you believe [myth], prepare to be shocked.

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as someone about to challenge and debunk a widely held belief with shocking evidence or reasoning, offering corrective knowledge that liberates from false understanding.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Curiosity about what shocking truth contradicts the myth
- Defensive engagement if they hold the belief
- Anticipation for belief-challenging information
- Openness to having misconceptions corrected
- Excitement about learning surprising truth
- Validation if they've been skeptical of the myth

## Psychology

Directly addressing a specific belief creates immediate relevance for those who hold it and curiosity for everyone else. "Prepare to be shocked" sets expectation for dramatic revelation that contradicts the belief, creating tension between current understanding and promised new information. This leverages cognitive dissonance—the discomfort when beliefs clash with new evidence—which motivates resolution through attention. It positions the creator as myth-buster providing corrective education, building authority through willingness to challenge popular beliefs. The format also creates slight challenge dynamic that engages ego—viewers want to see if they'll actually be shocked or if they already knew this.

## Example

"If you believe hustle culture leads to success, prepare to be shocked: research shows working over 55 hours per week decreases productivity so much you'd accomplish more in 40—you're grinding yourself backward."

## Why it works

This hook creates powerful engagement by directly challenging specific beliefs viewers may hold. The "prepare to be shocked" warning pre-frames content as belief-challenging while creating anticipation for how shocking the contradiction will be. It addresses both believers (who need to hear this) and skeptics (who want validation), creating broad relevance. The format positions myth-busting as protective service—correcting beliefs that may be limiting or misleading viewers. It also creates memorable before/after dynamic where viewers' understanding changes through the content. The direct challenge makes content feel important and substantive rather than entertainment.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails when the myth isn't actually widely believed, making the challenge feel like attacking a strawman. It loses credibility if the debunking isn't actually shocking, well-evidenced, or convincing, disappointing the dramatic buildup. If the "myth" is more nuanced than the binary framing allows, it oversimplifies complex beliefs into easily-attacked caricatures. The hook also underperforms when the shocking truth is just contrarian opinion without solid evidence, or when the myth has legitimate contexts where it's true. When the framing seems more interested in proving people wrong than helping them learn, it alienates rather than educates.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can make viewers feel attacked or stupid for holding the belief, creating defensiveness rather than openness to correction. It may attract audiences who enjoy seeing beliefs debunked more than learning truth, building community around intellectual combat rather than growth. The format can encourage finding myths to bust even when commonly held beliefs are actually accurate, promoting contrarianism for its own sake. If the debunking is later shown to be wrong or incomplete, it damages credibility as a myth-buster and truth-teller. The hook can also create unnecessary conflict with people who hold the challenged beliefs, making the creator's brand about antagonism rather than education.
