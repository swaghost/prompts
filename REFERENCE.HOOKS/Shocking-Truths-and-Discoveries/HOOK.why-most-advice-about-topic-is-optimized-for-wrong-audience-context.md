# Why most advice about [topic] is optimized for [wrong audience/context]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone identifying why mainstream advice doesn't serve them. They see you as analytical about advice mismatch. You appear as someone whose context-awareness prevents following wrong-fit guidance.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers relief about why advice hasn't worked plus validation of struggle. Intellectually activates critical evaluation of advice applicability. Psychologically creates permission to reject ill-fitting guidance. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "optimized for wrong audience/context" promises explanation for advice failure plus context-appropriate alternative.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because if advice targets wrong context, following it produces failure - understanding mismatch prevents wasted effort. This hook leverages frustration with inapplicable advice where following "best practices" doesn't work. The "optimized for wrong" framing explains systematic advice failure. By identifying intended audience/context, you enable fit-assessment. This satisfies desire for understanding why mainstream guidance doesn't serve individual situations.

## Example

Why most productivity advice is optimized for employees not entrepreneurs: assumes fixed schedule, predictable workload, and clear role boundaries - entrepreneurs face variable demands, self-directed priorities, and role-juggling. "Leave work at work" doesn't apply when you're always on; time-blocking fails when fires demand immediate attention. Different contexts need different systems.

## Why it works

This hook works because it explains why mainstream advice fails for specific audiences, preventing further misdirected effort. The wrong-audience framing validates struggle. By identifying intended context, you enable relevance assessment. The hook satisfies desire for context-appropriate guidance. This combination of failure explanation, struggle validation, fit-assessment, and context matching creates appropriately guided audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when advice you critique is actually broadly applicable and you're creating false niche-need or when your context distinction doesn't meaningfully change guidance. If you constantly critique advice as wrong-optimized, you seem perpetually misunderstood. The hook also fails if you use context-difference to dismiss valuable advice requiring adaptation. If wrong-audience claim is excuse for not implementing difficult but applicable advice, positioning is defensive. Too many advice-mismatch critiques can foster belief that no general guidance applies.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who reject all mainstream advice as not-for-them rather than thoughtfully adapting. You may attract people using context-difference as excuse to avoid challenging but applicable guidance. The constant wrong-optimization focus can reduce appreciation for adaptable principles underneath specific tactics. If your context distinction is false, you've encouraged avoiding helpful advice. This approach can also foster special-snowflake identity where people believe common guidance never applies to them. The critique emphasis may reduce understanding that most good advice requires contextual adaptation, not wholesale rejection.
