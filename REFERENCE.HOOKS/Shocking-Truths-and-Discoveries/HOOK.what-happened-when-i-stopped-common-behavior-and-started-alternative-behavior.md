# What happened when I stopped [common behavior] and started [alternative behavior]

## Hook Group

Shocking Truths & Discoveries

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who discovered transformation through behavior substitution. They see you as experimental about alternative approaches. You appear as someone whose before-after comparison validates behavior change.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about transformation from behavior swap. Intellectually activates interest in specific alternative approaches. Psychologically creates motivation to try behavior substitution. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "stopped and started" promises transformation evidence from concrete behavior change.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because before-after behavior comparisons show transformation mechanisms - understanding what changed enables replication. This hook leverages appeal of transformation stories where specific behavior substitution provides clear action path. The stopped-started framing creates clear contrast. By sharing outcomes, you validate alternative approach. This satisfies desire for concrete behavior-change evidence.

## Example

What happened when I stopped consuming content first thing in morning and started creating content first thing: creative energy got channeled into original work instead of being drained by inputs, developed consistent output habit, and produced better work because morning brain was fresh not influenced by others' ideas. Consumption-first made me reactive; creation-first made me generative.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides transformation evidence from specific behavior substitution enabling replication. The stopped-started framing creates actionable comparison. By sharing outcomes, you validate approach. The hook satisfies desire for concrete change examples. This combination of transformation evidence, behavior clarity, outcome validation, and action specificity creates behaviorally shifted audiences.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when transformation you describe is modest but exaggerated or when alternative behavior isn't actually superior for most people. If you constantly share stopped-started transformations, you seem to change behaviors frequently. The hook also fails if you strawman common behavior to elevate alternative. If transformation is your unique result and won't transfer, example lacks applicability. Too many behavior-swap stories can make audiences feel they should constantly experiment rather than commit to approaches.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who constantly swap behaviors seeking transformation instead of sustained practice. You may attract people who prefer novelty of change over depth of consistency. The constant behavior-swapping can reduce appreciation for refining existing approaches. If your alternative behavior doesn't work for audiences, you've created frustration. This approach can also encourage dismissing common behaviors that work well for many people. The transformation focus may reduce understanding that consistency with decent approach often beats constantly seeking optimal behavior.
