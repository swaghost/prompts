# Nine Rules I Follow Before Posting Anything

## Hook Group

List Hooks, Process Hooks, Quality-Control Hooks, System Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

Viewers should perceive this as quality-control checklist from someone who thinks strategically about posting. The creator should appear as disciplined practitioner with standards.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

**Emotional:** Interest in systematic approach, respect for discipline, motivation for standards

**Intellectual:** Curiosity about quality criteria, analysis of own standards, strategic thinking about posting

**Psychological:** Desire for own checklist, belief in systematic quality, motivation to raise standards

**Physical:** Attention for rules, mental comparison to own process, engagement with systematic thinking

## Psychology

This hook exploits interest in behind-the-scenes systems of successful creators. The "rules I follow" framing suggests discipline and intentionality rather than random posting, which is aspirational. The "before posting anything" specificity creates universal quality-control framework—not selective rules but consistent standards. This hook works because it addresses creator desire for systematic improvement; having checklist removes uncertainty about when content is ready. The nine-rule quantity is psychologically substantial—enough to feel comprehensive, demonstrating thorough thinking. The hook also addresses imposter syndrome or quality anxiety; if you follow clear rules consistently, you can trust your content. By sharing your rules, you're offering insight into decision-making process that typically remains invisible. The implicit promise is that following systematic rules produces consistently better content than gut-instinct posting.

## Example

"Nine rules I follow before posting anything: 1) Hook promises value. 2) First 3 seconds prove value. 3) One clear idea only. 4) Speaks to specific person. 5) Delivers on promise. 6) Ends with CTA. 7) Zero filler. 8) Could I say it in conversation? 9) Would I save this?"

"Nine rules I follow before posting anything: 1) Clear positioning. 2) Serves my niche. 3) Authentic voice. 4) Actionable takeaway. 5) Visual clarity. 6) Grammar check. 7) Aligns with brand. 8) Not reactive anger. 9) Proud to claim it."

"Nine rules I follow before posting anything: 1) Solves real problem. 2) Title creates curiosity. 3) Thumbnail stops scroll. 4) Pacing maintains attention. 5) Personality shows through. 6) Educational + entertaining. 7) Clear next step. 8) Optimized for platform. 9) Worth someone's time."

## Why it works

This hook succeeds by offering systematic quality-control framework that creates both inspiration (systematic approach works) and practical value (specific rules to implement). The "I follow" framing makes rules feel demonstrated rather than theoretical. The hook also works because it addresses creator uncertainty about posting standards; having clear checklist removes ambiguity about quality thresholds. The nine-rule comprehensiveness suggests thorough thinking about quality factors rather than cherry-picked advice. The hook appeals to creators who want to raise content quality but lack clear criteria for evaluation. It also positions you as disciplined practitioner with standards, not just prolific poster. The systematic framing creates aspirational positioning—following rules consistently separates strategic creators from random posters. The implicit message is that quality isn't subjective but can be systematized through clear criteria.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails if rules are generic, obvious, or don't actually distinguish quality content from poor content—if everyone already follows these rules, value disappears. It also fails if rules are so rigid they remove creativity, authenticity, or spontaneity that often makes content resonate. The hook doesn't work if you can't explain why each rule matters or what happens when violated—without reasoning, rules feel arbitrary. Another failure mode: if rules are too specific to your niche, format, or context to apply broadly, making checklist feel interesting but not actionable. The hook also falls flat if your own content doesn't consistently follow stated rules, revealing rules as aspirational rather than practiced. Finally, if nine rules are too many to remember or implement practically, checklist becomes overwhelming rather than useful.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can accidentally create rule-bound rigidity where creators evaluate every post through checklist, removing flexibility, experimentation, and authentic spontaneity. It might also encourage mechanical content creation where following rules becomes more important than genuine value delivery or audience connection. The systematic emphasis could also pressure creators to develop complex quality-control processes when actually consistent, simple execution often matters more. By presenting nine pre-posting rules, you might also create analysis paralysis where creators overthink every piece rather than publishing, learning from feedback, and iterating. The hook can also attract audiences seeking formulas and checklists rather than developing content instincts and editorial judgment through practice. Additionally, if creators adopt rules without understanding principles, they might apply them mechanically in inappropriate contexts. The quality-control framing might also create perfectionism where creators never feel content meets all nine criteria, drastically reducing output while pursuing unattainable standards. The rule-following approach might also devalue the legitimate role of intuition, creative risk-taking, and breaking rules that often produces breakthrough content.
