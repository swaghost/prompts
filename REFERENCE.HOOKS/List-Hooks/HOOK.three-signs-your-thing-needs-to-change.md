# Three Signs Your [Thing] Needs To Change

## Hook Group

List Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should see you as a pattern-recognition expert who can identify early warning signals before complete failure. You're positioned as preventative rather than reactive, helping people course-correct before it's too late.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

- **Intellectual:** Self-diagnostic assessment, warning signal recognition, preventative thinking
- **Emotional:** Concern if signs are present, relief if caught early, urgency to address
- **Psychological:** Early detection empowerment, problem awareness, course-correction agency
- **Physical:** Immediate self-audit, mental sign-checking, strategy reassessment

## Psychology

The word "signs" frames problems as detectable early-warning signals rather than failures, reducing defensiveness. Three signs feels comprehensive yet manageable for quick self-assessment. This hook leverages prevention motivation: people prefer catching problems early over waiting for crisis. By focusing on signs rather than problems, you create diagnostic distance - viewers can objectively assess without admitting failure. The implication that change is needed creates urgency while offering hope that intervention is still possible.

## Example

"Three signs your content strategy needs to change: 1) You're posting consistently but growth has plateaued, 2) Engagement rate is declining even as reach increases, 3) You feel disconnected from what you're creating."

## Why it works

This hook works because it offers a quick diagnostic tool that viewers can immediately apply to their situation. Signs are easier to acknowledge than failures - they're neutral indicators rather than judgments. The three-item structure allows rapid self-assessment: viewers can quickly determine if one, some, or all signs apply. By framing the need to change as observable rather than opinion-based, you reduce resistance and defensiveness. The hook positions change as proactive optimization rather than reactive damage control. It also demonstrates your ability to recognize patterns, establishing analytical credibility.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails if the signs you identify are too vague or apply to almost everyone, losing diagnostic specificity. It can backfire if the signs are actually normal fluctuations rather than meaningful warnings, creating unnecessary panic. If you don't follow up with what changes to make, viewers are left anxious without direction. The hook loses power if the signs require advanced analytics or data viewers don't have access to. It also fails if the recommended changes aren't proportional to the severity of the signs - minor signals shouldn't require complete overhauls.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can create hypervigilance where viewers constantly monitor for warning signs instead of focusing on creation and growth. It may attract an audience that's overly reactive, changing strategy at every small fluctuation. The diagnostic framing can make viewers feel like patients being diagnosed with problems rather than strategists making informed decisions. If signs are presented without nuance, viewers might make premature changes before giving approaches adequate time to work. The hook might also create comparison anxiety if viewers see these signs in their work while others' seem fine.
