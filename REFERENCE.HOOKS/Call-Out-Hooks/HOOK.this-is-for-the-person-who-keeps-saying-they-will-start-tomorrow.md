# This Is For The Person Who Keeps Saying They Will Start Tomorrow

## Hook Group

Call-Out Hooks, Procrastination Hooks, Accountability Hooks, Action-Motivation Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

Viewers should perceive this as firm but compassionate confrontation of procrastination pattern. The creator should appear as accountability partner who calls out delay tactics.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

**Emotional:** Slight discomfort at call-out, determination to break pattern, motivation to start today

**Intellectual:** Recognition of procrastination pattern, understanding of delay costs, strategic thinking about starting

**Psychological:** Awareness of avoidance behavior, belief in starting capability, readiness to take first action

**Physical:** Attention for direct message, engagement with accountability, readiness to begin immediately

## Psychology

This hook exploits the universal procrastination pattern of perpetual postponement—the "start tomorrow" promise that becomes chronic delay mechanism. The "who keeps saying" repetition emphasis highlights this as recurring pattern rather than one-time delay. This hook works because it confronts comfortable procrastination narrative many creators use to avoid uncertainty and failure risk of actually starting. The "they will start" future tense captures the perpetual deferral while "tomorrow" represents the ever-receding starting point. Many creators plan, research, and promise themselves they'll begin while never actually starting, using "tomorrow" as indefinite delay. The "this is for" targeting creates self-recognition moment for chronic procrastinators. The implicit promise is confrontation that breaks the delay cycle and provides push or framework for starting today rather than continuing postponement pattern.

## Example

"This is for the person who keeps saying they'll start tomorrow: Tomorrow becomes next week becomes next month becomes never. Waiting for perfect doesn't exist. Start messy today. Post one thing. Imperfect action beats perfect planning. Today is tomorrow."

"This is for the person who keeps saying they'll start tomorrow: You're not waiting for readiness. You're avoiding discomfort. You'll never feel 100% ready. 80% ready + action > 100% ready + delay. Confidence comes from doing, not preparing. Start."

"This is for the person who keeps saying they'll start tomorrow: Every tomorrow that passes is content you didn't create, audience you didn't build, progress you didn't make. Delay compounds negatively. Action compounds positively. Choose compounding direction. Start today."

## Why it works

This hook succeeds by directly confronting comfortable procrastination pattern with firm accountability message. The keeps-saying emphasis highlights recurring pattern needing interruption. The hook also works because it exposes "tomorrow" as indefinite delay tactic rather than legitimate timeline. The targeting creates self-recognition for procrastinators who need external accountability. The hook appeals to chronic starters who've delayed repeatedly and need firm push to actually begin. It also positions you as accountability coach who confronts avoidance patterns compassionately but directly. The implicit message is that perpetual "tomorrow" becomes never—starting imperfectly today matters more than waiting for perfect conditions.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails if confrontation feels judgmental rather than supportive—if creators feel attacked rather than motivated. It also fails if you can't provide starting framework that makes first action feel achievable beyond "just start." The hook doesn't work if you dismiss legitimate preparation needs when actually some groundwork does help. Another failure mode: if call-out creates shame that further paralyzes rather than motivates action. The hook also falls flat if you can't address underlying fears (failure, judgment, inadequacy) driving procrastination. Finally, if you suggest starting is simple when actually beginning requires overcoming real psychological and practical barriers.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can accidentally shame procrastinators when actually compassionate encouragement might be more effective than confrontation. It might also oversimplify when actually some creators genuinely need preparation before starting. The call-out could also create defensive response where creators justify delays rather than examining patterns honestly. By emphasizing immediate action, you might also encourage hasty starting without sufficient planning when actually thoughtful preparation improves outcomes. The hook can also make chronic procrastinators feel hopeless about pattern rather than empowered to change. Additionally, if you don't address root causes (perfectionism, fear, overwhelm), creators may start impulsively then quit quickly. The tomorrow-pattern focus might also make creators hyper-aware of delays in unhelpful way, creating anxiety rather than constructive action.
