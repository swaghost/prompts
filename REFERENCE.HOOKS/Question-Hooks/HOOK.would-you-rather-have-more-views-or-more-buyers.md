# Would You Rather Have More Views Or More Buyers?

## Hook Group

Question Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should see you as someone who forces clarity about true goals and trade-offs. You're positioned as business-focused and results-oriented, helping creators distinguish vanity from value.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

- **Intellectual:** Goal clarification, trade-off consideration, priority assessment
- **Emotional:** Discomfort (forced choice), clarity (what actually matters), strategic realignment
- **Psychological:** Value vs. vanity distinction, authentic goal identification, metric reframing
- **Physical:** Decision-making tension, strategic pause, goal reconsideration

## Psychology

This hook forces a binary choice that reveals priorities. Most creators say they want buyers but optimize for views, creating misalignment. By making the trade-off explicit, you create cognitive dissonance that demands resolution. The question exploits the difference between what people say they want (revenue) and what they actually pursue (attention). It challenges the assumption that more views automatically lead to more buyers. This psychological pressure creates clarity about actual goals versus stated goals.

## Example

"Would you rather have more views or more buyers? If you said buyers but your content has no clear offer, no trust-building, and no call-to-action - you're optimizing for the wrong metric. Views feel good. Buyers build businesses. Your content strategy should reflect which one you actually want."

## Why it works

This hook works because it forces strategic clarity through an uncomfortable choice. The binary frame eliminates hedging: you can't optimize for both simultaneously without strategy. By juxtaposing views (vanity metric) with buyers (business metric), you highlight common misalignment. The question format demands mental engagement - viewers must choose and justify their choice. It positions you as someone who understands that attention and conversion require different strategies. The hook also challenges the assumption that views lead to buyers, suggesting a more direct path might exist.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails if you present it as truly binary when most strategies need some of both. It can alienate creators who are legitimately in awareness-building phase where views matter more now. If you don't acknowledge that views can lead to buyers (just not automatically), the question seems to create false dichotomy. The business focus might not resonate with creators pursuing non-monetization goals. It also fails if you don't help viewers understand how to optimize for buyers, making the question rhetorical rather than actionable.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can create excessive sales focus that makes content feel transactional, actually harming conversion. It may lead creators to abandon awareness-building prematurely, limiting long-term growth. The binary framing might cause unnecessary guilt about caring about views when attention is part of a valid strategy. If viewers feel judged for wanting views, they may disengage rather than reconsider strategy. Over-emphasis on buyers can also create short-term thinking that undervalues brand-building and relationship cultivation that enable sustainable sales.
