# I charge $X/hour to share this advice — you're getting it free.

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive this as premium, paid-level content being generously shared for free, creating strong perception of value and exclusivity while establishing the creator's professional rates and expertise level.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Gratitude for receiving paid-level content without charge
- Excitement about the exceptional value proposition
- Trust in advice worth significant payment
- Urgency to pay attention since it's normally expensive
- Appreciation for the creator's generosity
- Validation that they're getting insider professional content

## Psychology

Price anchoring is a powerful psychological tool—when we hear something costs $X, we automatically value it at or near that price point. By establishing the advice as something they charge high rates for, the creator anchors its perceived value at that level, making the free delivery feel like an exceptional gift. This triggers strong reciprocity—viewers feel they've received something valuable and subconsciously want to reciprocate through engagement, follows, or future purchases. The specific rate also establishes professional credibility and market validation of the creator's expertise.

## Example

"I charge $500/hour to share this advice — you're getting it free: most businesses fail because they solve problems nobody has."

## Why it works

This hook combines price anchoring with generosity to create powerful perceived value. The specific hourly rate establishes professional credibility—people pay that much for this person's time, validating their expertise. The dramatic contrast between "$X/hour" and "free" creates perception of extraordinary value transfer and generosity. It triggers reciprocity instincts—viewers feel they owe the creator attention at minimum, possibly more. The format also differentiates paid consultancy content from free generic content, positioning what follows as premium-level insight. It establishes the creator's professional market position while simultaneously making expertise accessible.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails when the hourly rate mentioned seems inflated, unbelievable, or when viewers doubt anyone actually pays that much for the creator's advice. It loses credibility if the advice shared is generic, obvious, or clearly not worth the stated hourly rate—the disconnect damages trust. If the creator doesn't actually offer paid consultancy at that rate or can't demonstrate who pays it, the claim feels fabricated. The hook also underperforms when the content immediately following is clearly a sales pitch or lead magnet rather than genuinely free value, creating resentment. When the advice is too brief or superficial relative to the claimed hourly rate, it feels disappointing.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can make viewers question the creator's business model—if they're giving away hourly-rate advice for free, why would anyone pay? It may devalue the creator's actual paid services or create expectation that all their best content should be free. The specific rate can invite criticism or ridicule if viewers think it's too high, potentially creating negative discussions about pricing rather than content value. If the advice doesn't lead to results for free viewers who lack the support structure paying clients receive, it can damage reputation. The hook can also attract extractors who only want free content and will never convert to paying customers, building an audience with wrong expectations.
