# This is what I teach my private clients who pay me $10K/month

## Hook Group

AUTHORITY & EXPERTISE HOOKS (continued)

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as premium expert whose services command high prices and deliver proportional value. They see you as someone with exclusive client relationships and insider access to your best work. You appear generous for sharing private client material publicly.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers curiosity about premium content normally behind paywall and gratitude for free access to expensive knowledge. Intellectually activates interest in what justifies such high monthly investment. Psychologically creates scarcity perception around your expertise and authority through premium positioning. Physically manifests as stopping scroll to access otherwise expensive insider knowledge.

## Example

This is what my $10K/month clients pay me for: permission to focus on just one thing until it's perfect.

## Why it works

This hook works because premium positioning creates powerful authority through market validation — clients paying $10K monthly proves your value. By sharing private client material publicly, you demonstrate generosity that builds trust and goodwill. The scarcity framing (normally only for private clients) makes the content feel more valuable even when freely shared. The monetary anchor ($10K/month) creates context that whatever you're sharing is worth significant investment. This combination of validated expertise, generous sharing, scarcity perception, and premium positioning creates irresistible engagement.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when the shared content doesn't feel worth $10K/month, creating disconnect between claimed value and perceived value. If you don't actually have private clients at this rate, the claim is fraudulent and damages all credibility when discovered. The hook also fails if you frequently share "private client" material, diluting the exclusivity that made it valuable. If the content is basic or widely known, audiences question whether your private clients are getting fair value. Overuse of price disclosure can seem like humble-bragging rather than value demonstration.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by commodifying your private client relationship, making current clients feel their exclusive access is being shared publicly. You may attract audiences who want $10K value for free, creating unrealistic expectations and entitlement. The premium positioning can alienate audiences who feel priced out or judged for not being able to afford your services. If you later reduce rates or struggle to fill private client slots, the $10K claim becomes awkward. This approach can also make you seem focused primarily on high-end clients, limiting relatability and reach. The constant reference to premium pricing may make you seem more interested in positioning than helping.
