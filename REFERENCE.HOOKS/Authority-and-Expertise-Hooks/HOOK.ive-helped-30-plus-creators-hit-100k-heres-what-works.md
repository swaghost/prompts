# I've helped 30+ creators hit 100K — here's what works

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

The creator is a proven guide who has successfully helped multiple others achieve a specific, significant milestone. They're not just sharing what worked for them personally but a pattern that's been validated across different people and situations.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel confident that the advice is battle-tested across multiple cases, not just one person's lucky experience. Intellectually, they expect insights about universal principles that work across different niches and personalities. Psychologically, there's strong social proof—if 30+ people succeeded with this approach, it must be legitimate and replicable. The specific milestone (100K followers) creates clear aspiration.

## Psychology

Social proof + "tested on others" trust.

## Example

I've helped creators hit 100K followers — this is the pattern I see.

## Why it works

This hook combines coaching authority with multiplication of proof—instead of one success story, there are 30+. The specificity of both the number of people helped (30+) and the milestone achieved (100K) adds tangibility and credibility. The phrase "here's what works" promises universal principles rather than personalized tactics, making the advice feel more broadly applicable. The hook positions the creator as a guide and pattern-spotter rather than just a personal achiever, which can be more trustworthy for viewers who think "that worked for you but won't work for me." It leverages vicarious social proof—if many others succeeded following this guidance, viewers can too.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

If the milestone (100K followers) isn't impressive or relevant to the target audience, the hook lacks pull. The number of people helped (30+) must be verifiable or at least believable given the creator's visibility and time in the field. If the "what works" turns out to be generic advice that any coach might give, viewers will question whether the creator really helped those people or if they would have succeeded anyway. The help provided must be substantial and direct—if the creator only gave minimal input or advice to those 30+ people, the claim feels inflated. The pattern must be clear and actionable, not vague observations.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

The hook may attract people who want the creator to personally guide them to 100K, creating service expectations the creator cannot fulfill at scale. If any of the 30+ people publicly dispute the creator's role in their success or if their growth wasn't sustained, it damages credibility. The emphasis on helping others might make the creator's own achievements seem less impressive, or raise questions about why they're coaching if they're so successful themselves. If viewers try to replicate the pattern and don't achieve similar results, they may publicly complain or challenge the creator's methods. The hook can also date quickly—what worked to hit 100K in one platform era may not work in the current algorithm environment, requiring constant updates.
