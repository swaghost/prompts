# I've tested this with 50+ businesses. It works every time.

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

The creator is a rigorous experimenter who has validated their approach across multiple real-world scenarios. They're offering empirically tested advice, not theory or one-off success, and the strategy has proven universal applicability.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel confident that what they're about to learn is battle-tested and reliable. Intellectually, they expect evidence-based recommendations with high replication success. Psychologically, the phrase "works every time" reduces perceived risk—this isn't a gamble but a proven formula. The specificity of "50+ businesses" suggests methodical testing and professional-level validation.

## Psychology

Evidence + replication → certainty bias.

## Example

I've tested this sales script with 50+ startups — it converts like crazy.

## Why it works

This hook leverages multiple psychological principles to create maximum credibility. The claim of testing across "50+ businesses" provides both volume and diversity of evidence, suggesting the approach works across different contexts. The phrase "works every time" triggers certainty bias—people strongly prefer options they perceive as reliable and low-risk. The combination of empirical testing and consistent results positions the creator as both scientific and results-oriented. The hook implicitly addresses the common objection "but will it work for me?" by demonstrating broad applicability. The specific number (50+) is more credible than vague claims like "many" or "lots," and the "+" suggests ongoing validation.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The claim "works every time" sets an impossibly high bar—if viewers try it and it doesn't work immediately, they'll feel deceived and the creator loses all credibility. The hook creates expectations of a plug-and-play solution that requires minimal adaptation, which is rarely realistic. If the creator cannot provide specific examples, case studies, or data from those 50+ businesses, the claim seems fabricated. The strategy must be genuinely universal and not dependent on specific industry, timing, or market conditions. If the hook is used too frequently for different strategies, audiences will become skeptical of the "works every time" pattern.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

The absolute language ("every time") can attract nitpickers and skeptics who want to find the exception that proves the rule wrong. It may create analysis paralysis as viewers over-think implementation, afraid they'll be the one case where it doesn't work. The hook can backfire spectacularly if even one viewer publicly tries the strategy and it fails, as they'll challenge the "every time" claim. It may also attract unrealistic expectations from desperate or struggling businesses looking for a silver bullet. If results vary significantly based on implementation quality or context, viewers may blame the creator rather than examining their own execution. The hook can also make the creator seem arrogant or overselling, especially to sophisticated audiences who understand that nothing works "every time."
