# I ignored this advice for years — it cost me everything.

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as someone who learned a devastating lesson by dismissing crucial advice, and is now sharing that hard-won wisdom with urgency and humility to help others avoid the same catastrophic mistake.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Concern about potentially ignoring the same advice
- Curiosity about what advice was so important
- Urgency to listen because the stakes are clearly high
- Trust in lessons learned through severe consequences
- Relief at being warned before facing similar losses
- Validation if they've sensed this advice was important but weren't sure

## Psychology

This hook leverages regret aversion—we desperately want to avoid mistakes that lead to devastating consequences. The phrase "it cost me everything" creates maximum stakes and fear, suggesting total loss rather than just setbacks. The admission of ignoring advice humanizes the expert while creating urgency—if even someone knowledgeable made this mistake, anyone can. It also validates the advice through extreme negative proof: its absence caused complete failure, so its presence must be crucial. The time element ("years") shows this wasn't a quick mistake but sustained ignorance, amplifying the lesson's importance.

## Example

"I ignored this advice for years — it cost me everything: diversify your income streams before you need to."

## Why it works

This hook creates emotional resonance through vulnerability and high stakes. The admission of ignoring advice for years shows the creator is human and fallible, making them relatable rather than superior. "Cost me everything" is dramatic enough to capture attention and create genuine concern in viewers about replicating the mistake. The format implies the advice being shared next isn't optional or nice-to-know but essential for survival. It also suggests the creator now knows from painful experience that this advice is worth following, giving their current recommendation enormous weight. The regret narrative makes viewers pay attention in ways positive success stories might not.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails when "cost me everything" is revealed to be hyperbolic—if the consequences were recoverable or minor, it feels manipulative. It loses impact if the advice shared is something obvious that anyone would know to follow, making viewers question the creator's judgment. If the creator hasn't actually recovered or rebuilt after this loss, it undermines their authority to give advice. The hook also underperforms when the specific consequences aren't explained or when the advice doesn't clearly connect to preventing those consequences. When the loss story lacks concrete details, it can feel fabricated or exaggerated.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can make the creator appear incompetent or stubborn—if they ignored obvious advice for years, why should viewers trust them now? It may overshadow any positive expertise with this negative story, defining the creator by their failure rather than recovery. The dramatic "cost me everything" framing can attract sympathy-seekers or be perceived as playing victim rather than teaching. If viewers later discover the creator didn't actually lose everything or has since recovered easily, it damages credibility. The hook can also create excessive fear or paralysis in viewers, making them overly cautious or risk-averse when calculated risks would be appropriate.
