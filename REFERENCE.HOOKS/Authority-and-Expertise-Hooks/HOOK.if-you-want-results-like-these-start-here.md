# If you want results like these, start here

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

The creator has achieved impressive results and is offering the optimal starting point for others who want to follow a similar path. They're providing clarity on where to begin, cutting through complexity and analysis paralysis.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel both inspired by the results and relieved to have a clear starting point. Intellectually, they appreciate having the overwhelm of "where do I begin" resolved. Psychologically, the hook leverages the authority of proven results while addressing the common barrier of not knowing how to start. The directive "start here" creates decisiveness and removes ambiguity.

## Psychology

Shortcut psychology — audience wants "easy starts."

## Example

If you want 10k followers in 30 days, start here.

## Why it works

This hook works because it addresses one of the most common pain points for any goal: knowing where to begin. By combining aspirational results with a clear starting directive, it creates both motivation and a path forward. The conditional "if you want" creates self-selection—viewers who want those results will lean in. The phrase "start here" simplifies complexity and suggests the creator has mapped out an optimal sequence, saving viewers from trial-and-error. The hook implies that the creator has reverse-engineered their success and can show others the most efficient path. It works particularly well for audiences feeling overwhelmed by too many options or strategies.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

If the results shown aren't impressive or relevant to the target audience, the hook has no pull. The starting point must be genuinely foundational and not require prerequisites that viewers don't have. If "start here" leads to something overly complex or vague, it contradicts the promise of clarity. The hook can fail if the results timeline or scale seems unrealistic, triggering skepticism rather than aspiration. If the starting point is already well-known or obvious to the audience, the hook feels patronizing or like common knowledge dressed up as exclusive insight. The directive must lead to immediate action, not another layer of planning or theory.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can attract people looking for shortcuts who may not be willing to put in sustained effort, leading to quick dropout and potential negative feedback. It may create false expectations that starting in the "right" place guarantees results, when execution quality and persistence matter more. If the starting point works for some personality types or situations but not others, viewers who don't succeed may feel the advice was bad. The hook can inadvertently suggest there's only one right way to start, discouraging creative or alternative approaches that might work better for some individuals. It may also attract viewers who are perpetual "starters" but don't follow through, diluting the creator's community with uncommitted people.
