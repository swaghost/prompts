# If you ignore this, you'll regret it in 5 years.

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as someone with foresight who can see the long-term consequences of current actions (or inaction), offering crucial advice that might not seem urgent now but will prove essential over time.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Concern about future regret and missed opportunities
- Urgency to act now despite not seeing immediate benefits
- Trust in the creator's ability to predict long-term outcomes
- Anxiety about ignoring advice they'll wish they'd followed
- Motivation to invest in future self
- Recognition that some important actions aren't immediately urgent

## Psychology

Regret aversion is one of the most powerful motivators—we desperately want to avoid future self-blame and the pain of "if only I had..." thinking. The specific timeframe (5 years) makes the consequence feel real and imminent enough to act on, while being distant enough to explain why the urgency isn't obvious now. This taps into our difficulty with delayed gratification and long-term thinking—the creator is helping viewers overcome present bias by making future consequences emotionally salient. It also leverages the creator's experience—they've likely seen this pattern play out and can predict the regret trajectory.

## Example

"If you ignore this, you'll regret it in 5 years: start building your personal brand now, before you need it for leverage."

## Why it works

This hook creates urgency through future consequences rather than immediate crisis, addressing important-but-not-urgent decisions people tend to postpone. The specific timeframe makes the prediction concrete and testable, adding credibility through precision. It positions the creator as having valuable foresight from experience—they've either experienced this regret themselves or seen others face it. The emotional weight of "regret" is powerful and personal, making viewers imagine their future selves wishing they'd listened. It also validates that the advice might not seem urgent now, addressing why people haven't already acted on it while creating pressure to override that instinct.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails when the advice given doesn't actually lead to significant regret if ignored—if the consequences are minor or recoverable, the dramatic framing feels manipulative. It loses credibility if the 5-year timeframe is arbitrary or if it's unclear why this specific period matters. If viewers can't act on the advice due to circumstances, it creates anxiety without empowerment. The hook also underperforms when it's not clear why ignoring this leads to regret while ignoring countless other pieces of advice doesn't. When the advice is too obvious (save for retirement) the hook feels patronizing rather than insightful.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can create excessive anxiety and future-focused worry, making viewers feel paralyzed by all the things they should be doing to avoid future regret. It may come across as fear-mongering or manipulation, using emotional pressure rather than logical persuasion. The specific prediction ("5 years") creates accountability—if viewers follow the advice and don't see the claimed benefits, or ignore it without regret, it damages the creator's credibility as a futurist. The hook can also contribute to FOMO and hustle culture, making people feel they're never doing enough to secure their future. When overused, it becomes the boy who cried wolf—everything can't be regret-inducing if ignored.
