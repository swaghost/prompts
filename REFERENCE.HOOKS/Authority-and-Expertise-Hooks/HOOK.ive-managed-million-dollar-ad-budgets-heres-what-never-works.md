# I've managed million-dollar ad budgets — here's what never works

## Hook Group

AUTHORITY & EXPERTISE HOOKS (continued)

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone entrusted with significant financial responsibility and proven track record. They see you as a steward of major investments who learned expensive lessons. You appear as financially sophisticated expert who operates at scale.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers respect for high-stakes experience and fear of costly mistakes. Intellectually activates curiosity about what consistently fails despite large budgets. Psychologically creates trust through big-money credibility and tension through mistake revelation. Physically manifests as stopping scroll to avoid repeating expensive errors.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because big numbers create trust anchor — managing millions signals trusted expertise and proven capability. By focusing on "what never works" rather than success, you demonstrate wisdom earned through experience including failures. This creates credible authority because anyone managing that scale has seen enough iterations to identify consistent patterns. The negative framing (what never works) is more valuable than positive advice because mistakes create tension and urgency — audiences want to avoid expensive failures more than replicate uncertain successes.

## Example

I've managed $3M in ad spend — and this mistake always kills results: targeting everyone instead of a specific segment.

## Why it works

This hook works because the monetary scale immediately establishes high-stakes credibility that commands attention. By revealing what never works, you save audiences from expensive mistakes while demonstrating pattern recognition across many campaigns. The willingness to discuss failures shows confident expertise — secure experts share what doesn't work, insecure ones only brag about wins. The scale makes even small insights more valuable because they're proven at significant investment levels. This combination of financial authority, mistake prevention, pattern wisdom, and confident vulnerability creates powerful trust.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when the budget number is inflated or unverifiable, destroying trust if discovered. If the "never works" advice is too obvious or already common knowledge, you seem out of touch with current understanding. The hook also fails if you can't provide specific examples or context for why this never works. If you only focus on one budget scale, audiences at different levels may feel the advice doesn't apply to them. Overuse of "never" absolute language can seem dogmatic rather than experienced.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by attracting only audiences interested in large-scale advertising, limiting your reach. You may face skepticism from successful practitioners who have made your "never works" approach succeed in specific contexts. The financial emphasis might alienate smaller businesses who feel excluded from your expertise. If the marketing landscape changes and your "never works" advice becomes outdated, your authority suffers. This approach can also make you seem failure-focused rather than success-oriented, or positioned primarily through spending other people's money rather than generating returns. The absolute "never" framing removes nuance and can be proven wrong with single counterexample.
