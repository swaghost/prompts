# I wish someone told me this before I started

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as reflective mentor sharing hard-won wisdom to spare others from your mistakes. They see you as generous with experience-based insights. You appear as someone whose hindsight revelation demonstrates you've progressed beyond initial struggles and now help others avoid similar pain.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers gratitude for shortcut to wisdom usually gained through painful experience. Intellectually activates anticipatory learning. Psychologically creates trust through vulnerable admission of past ignorance. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "wish someone told me" promises valuable lesson enabling avoidance of common costly mistakes.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because learning from others' mistakes is far less costly than personal failure - hindsight wisdom offers shortcut to experience. This hook leverages appeal of vicarious learning where mentor's regret becomes student's advantage. The "wish someone told me" framing builds intimacy through vulnerability. By sharing what you now know, you provide fast-track past common pitfalls. This satisfies desire for wisdom without pain.

## Example

I wish someone told me this before I started freelancing: client work always expands to fill available time - without boundaries, you'll work 80 hours for 40-hour pay. I learned through burnout; you can learn through warning. Set boundaries from day one. Protect your time like revenue. Wish I'd known: time boundaries aren't optional; they're survival.

## Why it works

This hook works because it positions you as experienced guide preventing others from repeating your mistakes creating protective value. The "wish someone told me" framing builds connection through vulnerable admission. By sharing hard-won wisdom, you accelerate audience learning curve. The hook satisfies desire for vicarious learning. This combination of mentor positioning, vulnerability connection, mistake prevention, and wisdom-sharing creates audiences who appreciate your guidance and avoid your past errors.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your "wish I knew" insight is actually common knowledge or when you manufacture regret for dramatic effect. If you constantly share what you "wish you knew," you seem to have made excessive mistakes. The hook also fails if you use regret-sharing to position yourself as only valid guide. If lesson isn't actually important, dramatic framing is manipulative. Too many hindsight revelations can reduce confidence in your judgment since you apparently missed many obvious things.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who accumulate warnings without taking action, paralyzed by others' mistakes. You may attract people who use vicarious learning to avoid their own experimentation. The constant mistake-sharing focus can reduce appreciation for productive failure and learning through doing. If your regret is context-specific, you've created false universal warning. This approach can also foster belief all mistakes are avoidable. The hindsight emphasis may reduce understanding that while some mistakes are preventable through warning, most wisdom comes from personal experience, and excessive caution based on others' regrets often prevents necessary experimentation that drives unique learning.
