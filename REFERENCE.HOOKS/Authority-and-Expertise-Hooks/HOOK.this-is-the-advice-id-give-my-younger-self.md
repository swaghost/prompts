# This is the advice I'd give my younger self.

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as offering deeply considered, hindsight-refined wisdom—the most important lesson extracted from their journey, chosen specifically because it would have maximally accelerated or improved their path.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Trust in carefully selected, high-priority advice
- Curiosity about what the creator wishes they'd known sooner
- Connection through the relatable "younger self" framing
- Appreciation for distilled, essential wisdom
- Motivation to avoid the creator's mistakes or delays
- Validation that shortcuts and better paths exist

## Psychology

The "advice to younger self" framing creates powerful authenticity because it implies the creator has reflected deeply on their journey and identified what truly mattered versus what was wasted time or effort. It suggests this isn't generic advice but the specific insight that would have been most valuable to them personally. This taps into our desire to learn from others' experience without repeating their mistakes. The hindsight element adds credibility—they now know what they didn't know then, can see the journey's arc, and have isolated the highest-leverage insight. It's also universally relatable—everyone wishes they'd known certain things earlier.

## Example

"This is the advice I'd give my younger self: start building in public from day one, don't wait until you're 'ready.'"

## Why it works

This hook creates intimacy and authenticity through the personal, reflective framing. The hypothetical conversation with one's younger self feels genuine rather than performative—it's advice the creator genuinely wishes they'd received. It positions the wisdom as the single most important insight from their entire journey, creating prioritization and focus. The format also makes the advice feel specific and situational rather than generic—this particular insight for this particular journey. It validates viewers' desire for shortcuts and optimized paths, suggesting the creator has identified how to compress their timeline. The vulnerability of admitting what they didn't know creates trust and relatability.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails when the advice given is too obvious, too generic, or something their younger self probably already knew but didn't implement. It loses impact if the advice is highly specific to the creator's unusual circumstances and not broadly applicable to viewers. If the creator's journey or outcome isn't impressive enough to justify the "wish I'd known" framing, it lacks weight. The hook also underperforms when it's unclear why this particular advice would have been so impactful, or when the creator's younger self couldn't have acted on it due to circumstances. When the advice contradicts other things the creator teaches or their actual behavior, it creates confusion.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can inadvertently make the creator's actual journey seem inefficient or regrettable, potentially undermining credibility—if they did it wrong, why should viewers follow them? It may create unrealistic expectations that following this advice guarantees similar or better outcomes, ignoring the role of experience, timing, and context. The hindsight framing can feel dismissive of the learning value in the creator's actual path, suggesting struggle was wasted time rather than necessary education. If younger viewers actually do what the creator wishes they had, but face different contexts or challenges, the advice may not translate. The hook can also make the creator seem focused on the past rather than current wisdom and forward thinking.
