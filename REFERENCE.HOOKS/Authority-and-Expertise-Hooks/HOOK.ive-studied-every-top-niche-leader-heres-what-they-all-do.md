# I've studied every top [niche] leader — here's what they all do

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as someone who has invested significant research into understanding success patterns across leaders. They see you as a meta-analyst who extracts commonalities from multiple exemplars. You appear as someone whose pattern synthesis provides shortcut to success principles enabling replication of elite behaviors.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers excitement about discovering the "secret" shared by all successful people. Intellectually activates comparative analysis thinking. Psychologically creates confidence that success follows patterns not luck. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "every top leader does this" promises validated universal success behavior worth emulating.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because patterns across multiple successful examples suggest replicable factors rather than unique circumstances - if everyone at the top does something, it must matter. This hook leverages appeal of synthesized research where studying many leaders demonstrates thorough analysis. The "they all do" framing creates certainty through consensus. By revealing shared behavior, you provide actionable commonality. This satisfies desire for understanding that success isn't mysterious but follows identifiable patterns.

## Example

I've studied every top marketer — they all master email: Gary Vee has it, Russell Brunson built on it, Ryan Deiss teaches it - regardless of platform strategy, owned audience always matters. Top marketers diversify platforms but consolidate to email. Pattern is universal across successful marketers. Email ownership trumps platform dependence. Common thread reveals priority.

## Why it works

This hook works because it demonstrates authority through comprehensive research while revealing actionable commonality enabling replication. The "they all do" framing creates strong social proof through consensus of elite performers. By synthesizing multiple examples into single pattern, you provide clarity amid complexity. The hook satisfies desire for understanding universal success factors. This combination of research demonstration, elite consensus, pattern synthesis, and replicable insight creates audiences who trust your analysis and adopt the shared behavior.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you cherry-pick examples ignoring successful people who don't fit your pattern or when you mistake correlation for causation. If you constantly reveal "what they all do," you seem to find universal patterns where nuance actually matters more. The hook also fails if you use pattern-claiming to sell oversimplified formulas. If commonality is superficial not causal, advice misleads. Too many pattern revelations can reduce appreciation for context-specific factors and individual adaptation that also drive success.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who copy behaviors mechanically without understanding context or underlying principles. You may attract people who seek formulas to follow rather than developing strategic thinking. The constant pattern focus can reduce appreciation for unique approaches and innovative strategies that deviate from common practices. If your identified pattern is correlation not causation, you've encouraged adopting non-essential behaviors. This approach can also foster belief success requires only copying elite behaviors. The consensus emphasis may reduce understanding that while some patterns do predict success, most achievement requires both common fundamentals AND unique differentiation, and mechanical copying often fails without strategic adaptation.
