# I've been in [industry] for [years] — this advice never fails.

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as a seasoned veteran whose extensive experience has revealed timeless, reliable principles that transcend trends and consistently deliver results across different contexts and timeframes.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Confidence in receiving battle-tested wisdom
- Trust in the speaker's longevity and sustained success
- Relief at finding universal principles rather than temporary tactics
- Respect for the accumulated experience
- Anticipation for foundational advice
- Security in learning something proven and reliable

## Psychology

Time-tested authority carries special weight because sustained presence in a field suggests both competence (they've survived and thrived) and pattern recognition (they've seen enough to identify what consistently works). The phrase "never fails" taps into our desire for certainty and reliability in an uncertain world. Longevity also implies the speaker has witnessed multiple cycles, trends, and contexts, making their advice more universally applicable rather than tied to specific circumstances or timing.

## Example

"I've been in real estate for 23 years — this advice never fails: location beats renovation every single time."

## Why it works

This hook establishes authority through temporal depth rather than just achievement. Years of experience signal pattern recognition, resilience, and the ability to distinguish fundamental principles from passing trends. The "never fails" claim is bold and creates strong anticipation while filtering for viewers seeking reliable, proven advice rather than experimental strategies. The specificity of stating both the industry and timeframe creates credibility. The format also implies the speaker has tried many approaches and this one has proven universally effective, suggesting they're sharing refined wisdom rather than untested theory.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook loses credibility if the advice delivered is obvious, oversimplified, or something anyone would know without years of experience. It fails when the "never fails" claim is too absolute and viewers immediately think of exceptions or contexts where it wouldn't apply. If the speaker appears out of touch with current industry changes or dismissive of innovation, it can make them seem outdated rather than wise. The hook also underwhelms if the years mentioned aren't substantial enough to justify claims of timeless wisdom, or if the advice is too vague to be actionable.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can make the creator appear rigid, resistant to change, or stuck in old ways of thinking, especially in fast-evolving industries. Younger or newer professionals may feel excluded or dismissed, viewing the message as gatekeeping. The absolute claim of "never fails" invites counterexamples and can lead to debates that undermine credibility if the advice doesn't account for changing contexts or new challenges. If the industry or timeframe mentioned doesn't align with viewer needs, it can create disconnect rather than trust. The hook may also attract an older, more conservative audience while alienating those seeking innovative or disruptive approaches.
