# I tested [number] different approaches — here's what worked

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as methodical experimenter who invested effort testing multiple approaches empirically. They see you as scientific about validation. You appear as someone whose testing-based revelation demonstrates you've done comprehensive comparison work enabling data-driven recommendations shortcutting their trial-and-error process.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers relief at accessing tested conclusions without personal experimentation cost. Intellectually activates empirical-thinking appreciation. Psychologically creates trust through methodical validation. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "tested X approaches" promises data-validated recommendation saving them extensive testing time and resources.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because empirical testing provides more reliable guidance than theory - someone who actually tested multiple options offers validated insight. This hook leverages appeal of vicarious experimentation where your testing becomes their shortcut. The number quantification establishes comprehensiveness. By revealing what worked, you provide tested recommendation. This satisfies desire for evidence-based guidance over untested claims.

## Example

I tested 12 different email subject line types—here's what worked: questions had highest open rate (34% vs 23% average), urgency words increased clicks but lowered trust scores, personalization tokens boosted opens 8%. Tested systematically over 10,000 sends. Data revealed: curiosity beats urgency. Numbers don't lie; testing validates. Empirical results beat guru opinions.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides empirically validated recommendations shortcutting audience's experimentation needs creating immediate value. The specific number establishes testing comprehensiveness. By sharing results, you enable data-driven decisions. The hook satisfies desire for tested guidance. This combination of empirical validation, comprehensive testing, vicarious experimentation, and data-driven recommendations creates audiences who trust your findings over untested theories.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your sample size is too small for valid conclusions or when you claim testing without actual rigorous methodology. If you constantly share test results, you may seem to test everything excessively or manufacture fake testing claims. The hook also fails if you generalize context-specific results as universal. If testing methodology was flawed, conclusions mislead. Too many tested-approaches claims can reduce appreciation for theoretical understanding and principles that explain why some approaches work better than others.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who trust data uncritically without evaluating methodology or considering context differences. You may attract people who seek formulas to copy rather than understanding principles. The constant testing-focus can reduce appreciation for strategic thinking and first-principles reasoning. If your results are context-dependent, followers applying conclusions directly may fail. This approach can also foster belief only tested approaches work. The empirical emphasis may reduce understanding that while testing provides valuable evidence, most progress requires combining data with theoretical understanding, and data without context often misleads more than vague principles.
