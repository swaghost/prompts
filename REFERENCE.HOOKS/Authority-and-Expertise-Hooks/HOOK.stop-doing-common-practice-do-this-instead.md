# Stop doing [common practice] — do this instead

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as contrarian expert identifying better alternatives to standard practices. They see you as improvement-focused innovator. You appear as someone whose practice-replacement revelation demonstrates you've found superior methods enabling audience to upgrade from suboptimal common approaches.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers relief at discovering superior alternative combined with concern about continuing inferior practice. Intellectually activates comparative-evaluation thinking. Psychologically creates urgency to switch methods. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "stop doing X do Y instead" promises revealing better alternative enabling immediate practice upgrade.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because discovering better alternatives to current practices offers immediate improvement opportunity - replacement recommendations provide actionable upgrade. This hook leverages appeal of optimization where superior methods create value. The "stop-start" structure provides clear action direction. By contrasting approaches, you demonstrate analytical thinking. This satisfies desire for continuous improvement and method optimization.

## Example

Stop doing daily posts—do weekly deep dives instead: posting every day creates shallow content treadmill exhausting you while training audience to expect less. Weekly allows depth, research, quality. Daily habit serves algorithm not audience. Better approach: one excellent piece weekly beats seven mediocre daily posts. Quality concentration outperforms quantity distribution.

## Why it works

This hook works because it provides direct replacement recommendation enabling immediate improvement rather than abstract optimization advice. The stop-start structure creates clear behavioral directive. By contrasting approaches, you demonstrate analytical evaluation. The hook satisfies desire for actionable upgrades. This combination of replacement clarity, immediate improvement enabling, comparative analysis, and upgrade-path provision creates audiences who adopt your superior alternative abandoning suboptimal common practice.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when your "better" alternative is actually preference not objectively superior or when you dismiss effective practices because they don't fit your approach. If you constantly tell people to stop common practices, you seem excessively contrarian. The hook also fails if you oversimplify by ignoring contexts where common practice works well. If replacement isn't actually superior, advice damages results. Too many stop-start recommendations can reduce appreciation for why common practices became common and foster reflexive rejection of standard methods.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who constantly change methods seeking optimal approach never building mastery through consistency. You may attract people who use method-switching to avoid executing any approach long enough to see results. The constant practice-replacement focus can reduce appreciation for consistent execution and iterative refinement. If your alternative works for your context but not universally, followers switching may fail. This approach can also foster belief common practices are always suboptimal. The optimization emphasis may reduce understanding that while some alternatives are superior, most progress comes from executing methods consistently, and constant method-switching often reflects shiny-object syndrome rather than strategic improvement.
