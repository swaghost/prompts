# This is the strategy top performers use daily

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

The creator has insider access to or knowledge of what elite performers do consistently. They can reveal the "secret habits" or strategies that separate the best from the rest, offering a glimpse into high-performance routines.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel they're gaining access to exclusive information about how the "winners" operate. Intellectually, they're curious about the specific tactics and routines that drive success. Psychologically, there's strong aspirational pull—people want to emulate top performers and believe adopting their strategies will elevate their own performance. The word "daily" suggests consistency and discipline, reinforcing that success comes from repeated actions.

## Psychology

Social proof from "elite" performers.

## Example

This is the morning routine 7-figure entrepreneurs swear by.

## Why it works

This hook combines several powerful psychological triggers. First, it leverages social proof by association with top performers, suggesting that if elite individuals use this strategy, it must be effective. Second, it taps into aspiration and modeling behavior—people naturally want to emulate those they admire. Third, the word "daily" emphasizes consistency and habit formation, which aligns with widely accepted principles of success. Fourth, it creates implicit exclusivity by suggesting this is insider knowledge about what "top performers" do differently. The hook works because it promises a specific, actionable strategy rather than vague advice, and frames it as proven through elite usage.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

If the strategy revealed is too generic or already widely known, it undermines the "top performer" claim and feels like clickbait. The hook must deliver something that genuinely distinguishes high performers from average ones. If the creator cannot substantiate their claim with examples, names, or data about top performers actually using this strategy, credibility suffers. The strategy must be practical and accessible enough for the average viewer to implement, or it becomes aspirational without being actionable. Additionally, if the hook is overused across multiple pieces of content, audiences become desensitized to the "top performer" framing.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can inadvertently reinforce survivorship bias by suggesting that one strategy is responsible for success when multiple factors may be at play. It may discourage viewers if the strategy seems too demanding or if they've tried similar approaches without seeing results. The emphasis on "top performers" can create a status hierarchy that makes viewers feel inadequate or excluded if they're not at that level. If the strategy is highly specific to certain contexts or requires significant resources, it may alienate most of the audience. Additionally, the hook can backfire if viewers later discover that many successful people don't actually use this strategy, making the creator seem out of touch or deceptive.
