# What I'm about to share built me [result] from scratch.

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as someone who started with nothing and built something significant through the specific knowledge they're about to share. This establishes both relatability (started from zero) and aspiration (achieved major results).

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Hope that they can achieve similar results starting from their current position
- Excitement about learning a foundation-building strategy
- Trust in the creator's authentic journey
- Anticipation for transformative information
- Motivation from the rags-to-riches narrative
- Validation that starting small can lead to big outcomes

## Psychology

Origin stories that begin "from scratch" create powerful relatability because most people feel they're starting from zero in some area. This addresses the common objection "it's easy for them because they already had X." The phrase "what I'm about to share" creates immediate anticipation and implies the knowledge being shared is the actual cause of success, not luck or circumstances. It suggests that replicating the knowledge can replicate the results, making the audience feel empowered rather than envious.

## Example

"What I'm about to share built me a $2M business from scratch: master one traffic source before trying to be everywhere."

## Why it works

This hook combines humble origins with impressive outcomes, making success feel accessible rather than distant. Starting "from scratch" removes excuses and addresses impostor syndrome—if they did it with nothing, viewers can too. The directness of "what I'm about to share" creates urgency and implies the following advice is causally linked to the result, not peripheral information. The format also positions the content as the actual blueprint used, not theoretical advice. It transforms the speaker's success into a transferable asset rather than a personal achievement, making viewers feel they're getting access to a proven formula.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook loses credibility if "from scratch" is later revealed to be misleading (they actually had significant advantages, capital, or connections). It fails when the result mentioned isn't impressive enough to justify the buildup, or when it's in an area the audience doesn't care about. If the advice given is generic, obvious, or already widely known, viewers feel deceived by the dramatic setup. The hook also underperforms when the "scratch" starting point isn't clearly defined or when the timeline isn't mentioned, making the achievement impossible to evaluate properly.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can inadvertently create false expectations that results will be quick or easy, especially if viewers don't see the years of work that were involved. It may attract get-rich-quick seekers rather than those willing to put in genuine effort. If the "from scratch" claim is exaggerated or leaves out important context (like prior experience in related fields, education, or soft starting advantages), it can lead to accusations of dishonesty. The hook may also create unnecessary pressure to prove the "scratch" credentials, inviting skeptics to dig into the creator's background looking for advantages they didn't mention.
