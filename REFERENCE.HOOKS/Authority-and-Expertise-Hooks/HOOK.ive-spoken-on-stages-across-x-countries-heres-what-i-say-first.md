# I've spoken on stages across [X] countries — here's what I say first

## Hook Group

AUTHORITY & EXPERTISE HOOKS (continued)

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as a globally recognized speaker with international credibility and stage presence. They see you as someone trusted by diverse audiences across cultures. You appear as a seasoned presenter who has refined their craft through extensive real-world experience.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers respect for international speaking credentials and curiosity about universal opener. Intellectually activates interest in what works across cultures and contexts. Psychologically creates authority through geographic scope and speaking volume. Physically manifests as stopping scroll to learn the proven opening technique from experienced speaker.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because authority bias combined with global credibility creates powerful trust anchor. Speaking internationally suggests your message transcends cultural boundaries, indicating universal value. The numeric specificity ("[X] countries") provides concrete proof rather than vague claims. By revealing your opening line/technique, you're sharing insider professional knowledge that demonstrates confidence in your methods. The phrase "what I say first" creates curiosity about battle-tested opening that has worked consistently across diverse audiences.

## Example

I've spoken in 5 countries — and I always open my talks with this line: "Raise your hand if you've ever felt like an imposter."

## Why it works

This hook works because international speaking credentials establish authority that transcends local expertise. By quantifying countries, you provide verifiable scale that builds trust. Sharing your consistent opening demonstrates pattern success rather than one-time luck. The hook positions you as someone who has pressure-tested their approach across diverse audiences and refined it to universal effectiveness. The willingness to share your opening technique signals confidence and generosity, making you seem secure in your expertise rather than protective of secrets.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when the country count is too low to feel impressive (2-3 countries may not establish global credibility). If your opening line is generic or uninspiring, it undermines the authority you built. The hook also fails if you can't follow through with genuine speaking experience stories or if your claimed countries are easily verified as false. If you've only spoken in similar cultural contexts (e.g., all English-speaking Western countries), the "international" claim feels inflated. Overuse of this hook across content can make it seem like your main credential rather than one of many.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating expectations you're a polished professional speaker, making any presentation missteps more damaging. You may attract audiences only interested in speaking/presentation tips rather than your core expertise. The geographic emphasis might alienate local audiences who feel their context is being overshadowed by international scope. If your opening line fails when audiences try it, they question all your advice. This approach can also make you seem focused on credentials over substance, or like you're name-dropping countries rather than sharing genuine insight. The "I always" claim removes flexibility, making any variation seem like abandonment of proven method.
