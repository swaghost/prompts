# I've reviewed [X] campaigns — this is the pattern I see.

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as someone with extensive analysis experience who has identified consistent patterns across large sample sizes, offering data-backed insights rather than individual anecdotes or opinions.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Confidence in pattern-based, data-informed insights
- Trust in conclusions drawn from large sample sizes
- Curiosity about what pattern emerges consistently
- Appreciation for systematic analysis versus guesswork
- Validation that success follows identifiable patterns
- Relief at accessing condensed wisdom from extensive research

## Psychology

Large numbers create credibility through scale and systematic observation. When someone has reviewed dozens or hundreds of campaigns, their conclusions feel more reliable than single anecdotes. Pattern recognition across volume suggests the insight is universal rather than situational. This taps into our trust in data and empirical observation—the creator isn't guessing but has seen the same thing repeatedly. The word "pattern" implies predictability and replicability, making success feel more achievable. It also positions the creator as an analyst and researcher, not just a practitioner, adding another dimension of authority.

## Example

"I've reviewed 347 high-performing sales campaigns — this is the pattern I see: they all lead with the problem, not the solution."

## Why it works

This hook leverages both volume (many campaigns) and analytical rigor (identified patterns) to establish credibility. The specific number makes the claim concrete and believable while suggesting extensive experience. "Pattern I see" implies systematic observation and critical thinking, not cherry-picking examples that fit a preferred narrative. It positions the creator as having unique perspective through volume analysis that individual practitioners lack. The format promises actionable insight distilled from extensive research, saving viewers from analyzing campaigns themselves. It also suggests the pattern is causal not correlational—this factor appears consistently in successful campaigns, making it worth replicating.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails when the number seems inflated, unverifiable, or when viewers doubt the creator has actually reviewed that many campaigns systematically. It loses impact if the pattern identified is too obvious, too general, or something anyone would notice without extensive analysis. If the pattern doesn't actually correlate with the campaigns' success or if successful counter-examples exist, it undermines credibility. The hook also underperforms when the insight isn't actionable or doesn't clearly explain what to do differently. When the creator can't provide specific examples or demonstrate how the pattern was identified, it feels fabricated.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can create pressure to constantly provide new pattern-based insights, requiring ongoing extensive analysis to maintain credibility. It may attract skeptics who want to audit the methodology or see proof of the campaign reviews, potentially exposing weak analysis or overstated expertise. The emphasis on patterns can make viewers believe success is purely formulaic, ignoring creativity, adaptation, or context. If the pattern identified later proves to be survivor bias or correlation rather than causation, it damages the creator's analytical credibility. The hook can also make individual campaign creators feel their work is being reduced to patterns, creating potential conflict with collaborators or subjects of the analysis.
