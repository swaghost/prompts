# Most "gurus" won't tell you this… but I will.

## Hook Group

Authority & Expertise Hooks

## Desired Viewer Perception

The viewer should perceive the creator as an insider who's breaking ranks to share uncomfortable truths that other experts hide for self-serving reasons. They should feel they're getting privileged access to information usually kept behind closed doors.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Viewers should feel:

- Appreciation for the creator's honesty and courage
- Suspicion toward other experts who hide this information
- Excitement about learning insider secrets
- Trust in someone willing to break industry silence
- Gratitude for transparency
- Validation of their suspicions about mainstream advice

## Psychology

This hook leverages in-group/out-group dynamics by positioning the creator as different from "most gurus" while inviting the viewer into an exclusive circle of those who know the truth. It taps into conspiracy thinking and distrust of authority figures, suggesting other experts are withholding information. The ellipsis and contrast ("won't... but I will") creates dramatic tension and positions the creator as a truth-teller willing to sacrifice peer approval for viewer benefit. It also activates reactance—our resistance to feeling manipulated or kept in the dark.

## Example

"Most 'gurus' won't tell you this… but I will: they make more money selling courses about success than from the success itself."

## Why it works

This hook creates immediate differentiation by contrasting the speaker with "other gurus," positioning them as more honest and trustworthy. The quotes around "gurus" add subtle contempt, suggesting these others are fake or self-proclaimed. The dramatic pause ("…") builds suspense and makes the revelation feel more significant. By suggesting others are hiding this information, it makes the truth feel more valuable and exclusive. The format also creates an us-versus-them dynamic where the viewer joins the creator in seeing through industry BS, building parasocial loyalty and trust through shared knowledge and perspective.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

The hook fails when the "secret" revealed is common knowledge, too obvious, or not actually hidden by anyone, making the dramatic setup feel manipulative. It loses credibility if the creator can't explain why others would hide this information or if the conspiracy logic doesn't hold up. Using this hook too frequently makes the creator seem focused on tearing others down rather than building value. The hook also underperforms when the audience doesn't share the creator's skepticism about "gurus" or when viewers see the creator themselves as just another guru using this as a marketing tactic.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This hook can make the creator appear bitter, jealous, or overly focused on competitors rather than helping their audience. It may invite retaliation from other experts in the field or create unnecessary industry drama. The contrarian positioning can attract a cynical, difficult-to-please audience that will eventually turn the same skepticism on the creator. If the "secret" isn't actually that significant or if other experts do talk about it openly, the creator looks dishonest or out of touch. The hook can also contribute to broader distrust in expertise and information, making audiences more susceptible to misinformation while thinking they're being discerning.
