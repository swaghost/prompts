# Good creators copy. Great creators remix

## Hook Group

Content Creation & Creativity Hooks

## Desired Viewer Preception

Viewer perceives you as adaptation-advocate teaching ethical borrowing. They see you as remix-methodology expert. You appear as someone whose remix-emphasis demonstrates you understand transformation creates originality enabling audience to build on existing work ethically.

## Desired Viewer Emotional/Intellectual/Psychological/Physical Elicitations or Response

Emotionally triggers permission to adapt. Intellectually activates remix-thinking. Psychologically creates belief borrowing is acceptable. Physically manifests as stopping scroll because "great creators remix" promises legitimizing adaptation as creative strategy.

## Psychology

The mind interrupts to pay attention because gives permission to adapt - if remixing defines great creators, borrowing becomes legitimate. This hook leverages appeal of permission-giving where ethical adaptation enables creation. The good-versus-great hierarchy creates aspiration. By emphasizing remix over copy, you teach transformation. This satisfies desire for ethical borrowing permission.

## Example

I took a trending sound, added my spin → 500K views. Didn't create original audio; transformed it through unique application. Remix created value. Adaptation generated reach. Transformation beat invention. Borrowing with twist outperformed creating from scratch. Permission to adapt unlocked success.

## Why it works

This hook works because it legitimizes borrowing while teaching transformation creating ethical adaptation framework. The good-versus-great framing creates aspirational hierarchy. By showing remix success, you validate approach. The hook satisfies desire for borrowing permission. This combination of legitimization, transformation teaching, validation provision, and permission-giving creates audiences who remix existing work adding value through adaptation rather than paralyzed by originality pressure.

## Pitfalls - How it can fail

This hook fails when you encourage copying without transformation or when you blur ethical borrowing boundaries. If you constantly advocate remixing, you may enable plagiarism disguised as adaptation. The hook also fails if you use remix-claiming to excuse stealing without credit. If transformation is minimal, it's copying not remixing. Too much remix emphasis can reduce appreciation for how original creation matters and create belief all content is remixed justifying lazy copying.

## Unintended Consequences - How it can backfire

This can backfire by creating audiences who copy minimally calling it remix avoiding original creative work. You may attract people who use remix-justification for plagiarism. The constant adaptation focus can reduce appreciation for how true innovation advances fields. If remix becomes copying without transformation, you've enabled theft. This approach can also foster belief everything is remix dismissing original creation. The borrowing emphasis may reduce understanding that while building on existing work is valid, most valuable remixing requires substantial transformation, and minimal adaptation often constitutes plagiarism rather than creative remixing.
