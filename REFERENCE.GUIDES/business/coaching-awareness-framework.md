# How To Coach Someone Who Doesn't See The Problem

## A Cheat Sheet for Leaders

**The Challenge:** You can't just tell someone they have a problem. When people don't see the issue, direct confrontation triggers defense mechanisms and closes them off.

**The Solution:** Create space for self-discovery through strategic frameworks that build awareness without resistance.

---

## The Core Frameworks

### 1. The OARS Approach

**(h/t William Miller & Stephen Rollnick)**

**Guide the conversation without pushing**

```
O - Open questions that invite exploration
A - Affirmations that recognize effort
R - Reflections that mirror what you hear
S - Summaries that connect the dots
```

**Use For:** Creating space for self-discovery

---

### 2. Stages of Change Model

**(h/t Prochaska & DiClemente)**

**Meet them where they are, not where you want them to be**

```
Precontemplation → Contemplation → Preparation → Action → Maintenance
"I don't see        "Maybe          "I'm ready    "I'm working  "I'm staying
a problem"          something's     to try"       on it"        the course"
                    off"
```

**Critical Insight:** If they're in precontemplation, your job isn't to convince. It's to **plant seeds**.

## 1. The OARS Approach - Detailed Breakdown

### O - Open Questions That Invite Exploration

**The Principle:** Open-ended questions encourage thinking without triggering defense.

#### Question Transformations

| ❌ Instead of This         | ✅ Try This                          |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| "Why did you do that?"     | "What were you hoping would happen?" |
| "Don't you see the issue?" | "How do you think that landed?"      |
| "You need to fix this"     | "What would you do differently?"     |

#### Effective Question Templates

**For Building Awareness:**

- "What do you notice about [situation]?"
- "How do you think [person] experienced that?"
- "What patterns are you seeing?"
- "What's your take on how that played out?"

**For Exploring Impact:**

- "How did that feel for you?"
- "What was the result you were going for?"
- "What happened after that?"

**For Inviting Alternative Perspectives:**

- "What's another way to look at this?"
- "What might you be missing?"
- "How else could someone interpret that?"

**For Moving Toward Action:**

- "What would you do differently next time?"
- "What's one small step you could take?"
- "How could you test that idea?"

---

### A - Affirmations That Recognize Effort

**The Principle:** Acknowledge what's working, even when addressing what's not.

#### Why Affirmations Matter

People need to feel valued before they can hear critique. Affirmations build psychological safety.

#### Effective Affirmations

**Effort Recognition:**

- "You've handled tougher situations before"
- "I can see you care about getting this right"
- "I notice you're really thinking this through"

**Strengths Acknowledgment:**

- "I can see you care about [value]"
- "It takes courage to talk about this"
- "You're willing to have this difficult conversation"

**Progress Celebration:**

- "I notice you're [positive behavior]"
- "That's a shift from where you started"

**Intention Validation:**

- "I know you want the best for [stakeholder]"
- "Your intention was good, even if the impact wasn't what you hoped"

---

### R - Reflections That Mirror What You Hear

**The Principle:** Show you're listening deeply and help them hear their own thinking.

#### Types of Reflections

**1. Simple Reflection:**

- Them: "I just don't have time for this."
- You: "You're feeling stretched thin."

**2. Amplified Reflection:**

- Them: "It's not a big deal."
- You: "So this isn't something worth addressing?"

**3. Double-Sided Reflection:**

- You: "On one hand, you want to keep moving fast. On the other hand, you're noticing some things aren't landing well."

**Why Reflections Work:**  
Hearing their words reflected back creates distance and allows them to see their thinking from outside perspective.

---

### S - Summaries That Connect the Dots

**The Principle:** Help them see patterns they might be missing.

#### Summarizing Techniques

**Pattern Summary:**

- "You've mentioned [pattern] a few times now..."
- "I'm noticing a theme in what you're sharing..."

**Sequence Summary:**

- "Let me make sure I understand: you tried X, then Y happened, and now you're thinking Z?"

**Comprehensive Summary:**

- "So what I'm hearing is... [summarize their points]"
- "If I put this together, it sounds like..."

**Why Summaries Work:**  
They reveal patterns and connections the person may not have consciously recognized.

## 2. Stages of Change Model - Detailed Breakdown

### Understanding the Stages

The journey from unawareness to sustained change follows predictable stages. Your coaching must match where they are.

---

### Stage 1: Precontemplation - "I don't see a problem"

#### What's Happening Internally

- ❌ Unaware of the issue
- 🛡️ Defensive when challenged
- 🚫 Not considering change
- 👉 May see problem as external ("they're the issue, not me")

#### Your Goal: Plant Seeds, Don't Convince

#### ❌ What NOT to Do

- "You need to fix this"
- "Don't you see the problem?"
- Presenting evidence and arguing
- Pushing for immediate action

**Why this fails:** Creates resistance and hardens their position.

#### ✅ What TO Do

**Ask Open Questions:**

- "How do you think that landed?"
- "What do you make of [outcome]?"
- "Help me understand your thinking..."

**Reflect Without Judgment:**

- "You feel good about how that went"
- "You see it differently than they did"

**Share Observations Gently:**

- "I noticed [behavior]. What was your intention?"
- "I'm curious about [specific moment]..."

**Create Curiosity:**

- "What would it look like if...?"
- "Have you ever considered...?"

#### Key Principle

**If they're in precontemplation, your job isn't to convince. It's to plant seeds.**

Wait for the right moment. Check for signs they're moving to contemplation.

---

### Stage 2: Contemplation - "Maybe something's off"

#### What's Happening Internally

- 🤔 Starting to question
- ⚖️ Ambivalent (seeing pros and cons)
- ⏸️ Not ready to commit to change
- 🔍 May acknowledge issue but minimize it

#### Your Goal: Explore Ambivalence Without Forcing Resolution

#### Questions That Work

**Explore Concerns:**

- "What concerns you about this?"
- "What's making you hesitate?"

**Explore Possibilities:**

- "What would be different if you addressed this?"
- "What might change look like for you?"

**Explore Barriers:**

- "What's holding you back from making a shift?"
- "What would need to be true for you to try something different?"

#### Techniques

✅ Explore both sides of ambivalence  
✅ Don't rush to action  
✅ Let them voice both concerns and possibilities  
✅ Affirm the difficulty of change  
✅ Normalize the internal conflict

#### Key Principle

Contemplation is uncomfortable but necessary. Don't try to resolve their ambivalence for them.

---

### Stage 3: Preparation - "I'm ready to try"

#### What's Happening Internally

- ✅ Committed to taking action
- 📝 Planning next steps
- 🤝 Ready for support and guidance
- 🛠️ May need help with "how," not "why"

#### Your Goal: Support Planning and Build Confidence

#### What Helps

**Brainstorm Specific Actions:**

- "What's one small step you could take this week?"
- "How would you start?"

**Identify Potential Obstacles:**

- "What might get in the way?"
- "What would you do if [obstacle] happens?"

**Create Accountability Structure:**

- "How will you track progress?"
- "Who can support you?"

**Affirm Readiness:**

- "You're ready for this"
- "I can see you've thought this through"

---

### Stage 4: Action - "I'm working on it"

#### What's Happening Internally

- 💪 Actively making changes
- 📊 Needs encouragement and troubleshooting
- 🔄 May face setbacks or challenges
- 🌱 Building new habits and skills

#### Your Goal: Support, Encourage, Course-Correct as Needed

#### What Helps

**Celebrate Progress:**

- "Look how far you've come"
- "You're doing it"

**Troubleshoot Challenges:**

- "What's working? What's not?"
- "What adjustment might help?"

**Normalize Setbacks:**

- "Setbacks are part of learning"
- "What did you learn from that?"

---

### Stage 5: Maintenance - "I'm staying the course"

#### What's Happening Internally

- 🔄 Change is becoming habit
- 👀 Vigilance against old patterns
- 🧬 Integration into identity
- ⚠️ May face triggers or relapse

#### Your Goal: Reinforce Progress, Plan for Challenges

#### What Helps

**Reinforce Identity:**

- "This is who you are now"
- "You've become someone who..."

**Plan for Challenges:**

- "What situations might test you?"
- "What will you do if [trigger] happens?"

---

## 3. Open Questions That Work

### The Golden Rule

**The right question invites reflection. The wrong one triggers defense.**

### Question Transformation Guide

#### Building Awareness

❌ "Why are you doing it that way?"  
✅ "What's your thinking behind that approach?"

❌ "Can't you see the problem?"  
✅ "What are you noticing about the results?"

❌ "That's wrong."  
✅ "What other ways might we look at this?"

#### Exploring Impact

❌ "Do you realize what you did?"  
✅ "How do you think that landed?"

❌ "You hurt their feelings."  
✅ "What do you imagine they were feeling?"

❌ "That was a mistake."  
✅ "What was the outcome compared to what you hoped?"

#### Inviting Change

❌ "You need to change."  
✅ "What might you want to do differently?"

❌ "Fix this problem."  
✅ "What would a good solution look like to you?"

❌ "Stop doing that."  
✅ "What approach might work better?"

---

## 4. Affirmations That Build Trust

### The Trust Formula

**Trust = Safety + Being Valued**

Affirmations create both. Without trust, no awareness can develop.

### Affirmation Formula

```
"I notice/see/recognize [specific positive behavior/quality/effort]"
```

### Categories of Affirmations

**1. Effort & Intention:**

- "You've handled tougher situations before"
- "I can see you care about getting this right"
- "I notice you're really thinking this through"
- "It takes courage to talk about this"

**2. Strengths & Capabilities:**

- "You're good at [specific skill]"
- "I've seen you navigate [similar challenge] well"
- "You have strong [quality]"

**3. Progress & Growth:**

- "I notice you're [positive change]"
- "That's different from [before]"
- "You've made real progress on [area]"

**4. Values & Priorities:**

- "I can see you care about [value]"
- "I know this matters to you"
- "Your commitment to [value] is clear"

## 5. Signs They're Starting to See

### Awareness Often Shows Up Before Words Do

Watch for these behavioral signals that indicate growing awareness:

### 🔦 They pause longer before responding

**What it means:**

- Internal processing happening
- Moving from reaction to reflection
- Taking your question seriously

**What to do:**

- Don't fill the silence
- Let them think
- Show patience

---

### ❓ They ask clarifying questions

**What it means:**

- Curiosity replacing certainty
- Openness to understanding
- Moving from defending to exploring

**Examples:**

- "What do you mean by that?"
- "Can you help me understand?"
- "How do you see it?"

**What to do:**

- Answer thoughtfully
- Encourage more questions
- Affirm their curiosity

---

### 💬 They request past conversations differently

**What it means:**

- Reframing events with new perspective
- Connecting dots previously missed
- Reassessing previous certainty

**Examples:**

- "Now that I think about it..."
- "Looking back, I wonder if..."
- "Maybe I didn't see..."

**What to do:**

- Reflect back their insight
- Don't say "I told you so"
- Encourage deeper exploration

---

### ⚙️ They stop defending and start wondering

**What it means:**

- Moving from Stage 1 (Precontemplation) to Stage 2 (Contemplation)
- Openness to possibility
- Willingness to question assumptions

**Examples:**

- "I wonder if..." (replacing "But I...")
- "Maybe there's..." (replacing "No, because...")
- "What if..." (replacing "That won't work")

**What to do:**

- Follow their wondering
- Ask more "what if" questions
- Don't rush to solutions

## Remember: Core Coaching Principles

### ✅ Coaching someone into awareness takes time

- One conversation rarely creates breakthrough
- Hold space without forcing the moment
- The breakthrough often comes after the conversation
- Sometimes the person who shifts the most is you

### ✅ Hold space without forcing the moment

- Silence is your friend—let them think
- Resist the urge to fill pauses
- Trust the process of self-discovery
- Don't "rescue" them from discomfort

### ✅ The breakthrough often comes after the conversation

- Insight happens in reflection, not just in dialogue
- Give them time to process between sessions
- Suggest they journal or reflect
- Follow up: "What have you been thinking about since we talked?"

### ✅ Sometimes the person who shifts the most is you

- You may realize you misunderstood the situation
- Your assumptions get tested
- Genuine curiosity leads to mutual understanding
- The best coaches stay learners

## Quick Reference Checklist

### Before the Conversation

- [ ] Check your mindset: curious, not certain
- [ ] Identify their likely stage of change
- [ ] Prepare open questions (not solutions)
- [ ] Plan affirmations (what's working?)

### During the Conversation

- [ ] Start with open questions
- [ ] Use affirmations early and often
- [ ] Reflect what you hear
- [ ] Summarize to connect dots
- [ ] Notice signs of growing awareness
- [ ] Resist solving their problem
- [ ] Hold space for silence

### After the Conversation

- [ ] Let them process—don't push
- [ ] Follow up after time has passed
- [ ] Notice behavioral shifts
- [ ] Adjust approach based on their stage

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### ❌ Telling them they have a problem

**Fix:** Ask questions that let them discover it

### ❌ Using "Why" questions

**Fix:** Use "What" or "How" questions instead

### ❌ Skipping affirmations

**Fix:** Start with what's working before addressing what's not

### ❌ Pushing for action too soon

**Fix:** Match your approach to their stage of change

### ❌ Solving the problem for them

**Fix:** Guide them to their own insights

### ❌ Filling every silence

**Fix:** Get comfortable with pauses—that's where thinking happens

### ❌ Being certain you're right

**Fix:** Stay curious—you might be missing something

---

## The Bottom Line

**You can't force someone to see a problem they're not ready to see.**

But you can:

- ✅ Create safety through OARS
- ✅ Meet them at their stage of change
- ✅ Ask questions that invite reflection
- ✅ Build trust through affirmations
- ✅ Hold space for self-discovery
- ✅ Be patient—awareness takes time

**The goal isn't to win an argument. It's to create the conditions for someone to see clearly for themselves.**
