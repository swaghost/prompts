# Smartphone Montage Video Guide – Authentic Memory-Style Content

## Overview

Guide for creating authentic smartphone home-video montages with rapid jump-cut editing that feels like casually scrolling through personal phone memories. This aesthetic works for college life, travel moments, friend groups, lifestyle content, and nostalgia-driven storytelling. The key is maintaining raw, unpolished authenticity while preserving strict character consistency throughout rapid scene changes.

## Core Principle

**Authentic Imperfection**: The video should feel personally recorded by a friend on their phone, not professionally produced. Embrace natural shake, imperfect framing, realistic autofocus, and spontaneous moments. The editing is fast-paced (1-2 second cuts) like flipping through photo memories.

---

## Video Style Characteristics

### Smartphone Aesthetic

- **Camera Quality**: Mobile phone camera (not professional cinema camera)
- **Movement**: Genuine handheld shake (not stabilized)
- **Framing**: Imperfect, spontaneous composition
- **Focus**: Realistic autofocus changes (sometimes hunting, sometimes soft)
- **Exposure**: Automatic adjustments (visible when moving between light/shadow)
- **Motion**: Normal frame rate (24-30fps), no slow motion

### Editing Style

- **Cut Frequency**: Every 1-2 seconds (rapid jump cuts)
- **Transitions**: Abrupt cuts only (no fades, dissolves, wipes, or effects)
- **Pacing**: Fast, energetic, memory-scroll feeling
- **Flow**: Scenes don't need to connect perfectly (like casual memories)
- **Authenticity**: Slightly messy, unplanned feeling throughout

### What This Is NOT

- ❌ Cinematic production value
- ❌ Professional stabilization
- ❌ Dramatic lighting setups
- ❌ Commercial composition
- ❌ Beauty filters or enhancement
- ❌ Stylized color grading
- ❌ Perfectly choreographed movement
- ❌ Influencer-style staged content

## Character Consistency System

### Identity Lock (@reference)

For main subject, use **@reference** system for strict identity preservation:

**Preserve Exactly:**

- Facial structure and features
- Hairstyle and hair color
- Skin tone and texture
- Age and body proportions
- Clothing and accessories
- Overall appearance

**Never:**

- Replace or reinterpret identity
- Beautify or morph features
- Swap faces between shots
- Allow identity drift
- Change outfit mid-sequence
- Alter hairstyle or color

### Supporting Characters

- **Visual Distinction**: Each person must remain unique (no duplicated faces)
- **Consistency**: Same clothing and appearance throughout
- **Recognition**: All characters recognizable in every appearance
- **No Morphing**: Characters don't merge, swap, or change
- **Outfit Lock**: Clothing remains consistent throughout montage

### Anatomy Requirements

- Realistic human proportions
- Natural hand movements (no extra fingers, no malformed hands)
- Believable eye direction
- Correct body contact (when interacting)
- Physically accurate object interaction
- No missing or extra limbs

## Shot Sequence Structure

### Typical 15-Second Montage Format

Break into 2.5-second segments (6 segments total):

**Segment 1 (0-2.5s)**: Arrival/Introduction

- Establish location and main subject
- Quick cuts showing movement and energy
- Flash details (feet walking, props, environment)

**Segment 2 (2.5-5s)**: First Activity

- Introduction of supporting characters
- Group interaction (selfie, talking, walking)
- Imperfect framing, natural movement

**Segment 3 (5-7.5s)**: Second Activity

- Change of sub-location or activity
- Props and environmental details
- Natural lighting variations visible

**Segment 4 (7.5-10s)**: Peak Energy

- Playful moments, laughter, spontaneous actions
- Close-ups mixed with wider shots
- Handheld imperfection emphasized

**Segment 5 (10-12.5s)**: Group Interaction

- Shared activity (eating, toasting, passing objects)
- Natural overlapping conversation
- Realistic object handling

**Segment 6 (12.5-15s)**: Resolution/Ending

- Calmer moment or final memory
- Subject acknowledgment of camera
- Natural ending (camera sways away, stops filming)

## Camera Movement and Framing

### Authentic Handheld Movement

- **Shake**: Slight continuous shake (like holding phone while recording)
- **Sway**: Natural weight shift and body movement
- **Imperfect Leveling**: Occasionally slightly tilted horizon
- **Focus Hunting**: Autofocus sometimes searches briefly
- **Reframing**: Occasional adjustment of composition mid-shot

### Framing Techniques

- **Vertical Format**: 9:16 (smartphone orientation)
- **Subject Placement**: Sometimes centered, sometimes off-center
- **Head Room**: Variable (not perfectly composed)
- **Cut-Off Elements**: Occasionally crops people at awkward points (authentic)
- **Background**: Not always perfectly clean (real environment visible)

### What to AVOID

- ❌ No drone shots
- ❌ No gimbal-stabilized movement
- ❌ No cinematic camera moves (dolly, crane, slider)
- ❌ No slow motion or speed ramps
- ❌ No artificial motion blur
- ❌ No professional camera movements

## Lighting Guidelines

### Natural Lighting

- **Primary Source**: Available light (sunlight, indoor lighting, mixed sources)
- **Auto-Exposure**: Visible smartphone auto-adjustment
- **Transitions**: Light changes visible when moving locations
- **Shadows**: Natural, not controlled
- **Skin Texture**: Real, no smoothing or beauty enhancement

### Lighting Scenarios

- **Outdoor Daylight**: Warm sunlight, realistic shadows, occasional lens reflections
- **Indoor Mixed**: Natural window light + overhead lights + lamps
- **Shade to Sun**: Visible exposure adjustment when moving
- **Evening/Sunset**: Natural warm tones (not stylized)

### What to AVOID

- ❌ No dramatic cinematic lighting
- ❌ No perfect three-point lighting
- ❌ No professional light diffusion
- ❌ No beauty lighting techniques
- ❌ No artificial fill light (unless naturally present)

- **Laughter**: Genuine, spontaneous

- **Environment**: Ambient noise appropriate to location

## Tips for Best Results

### Identity Consistency

- Upload clear @reference image showing face, hair, clothing
- Reference multiple angles if available
- Specify clothing details in prompt
- Reinforce "zero deviation" language
- Use negative prompt to prevent beautification

### Natural Movement

- Emphasize "genuine handheld shake" not "smooth cinematic"
- Allow imperfect framing and composition
- Permit occasional focus hunting
- Accept natural exposure adjustments

### Authentic Editing

- Rapid cuts feel more authentic than long takes
- Abrupt transitions (no effects) maintain phone aesthetic
- Varying shot lengths within 1-2 second range adds realism
- Occasional awkward cut-off adds authenticity

### Environmental Realism

- Include scattered props (backpacks, drinks, phones)
- Allow natural clutter and lived-in spaces
- Background activity acceptable and realistic
- Don't over-clean or stage the environment
