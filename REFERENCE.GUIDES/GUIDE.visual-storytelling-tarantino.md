# Visual Storytelling Guide: Quentin Tarantino Quick Reference

## Signature Techniques Summary

### 1. The Trunk Shot ★ SIGNATURE

- **Setup:** Camera inside car trunk, 45° upward angle, total darkness
- **Action:** Trunk violently opened after 1s silence
- **Effect:** Backlit silhouettes, power dynamic, viewer as victim/participant
- **Lighting:** High-contrast rim lighting, dramatic light burst

### 2. Extreme Low Angles

- **Purpose:** Make characters larger than life, establish dominance
- **Use:** Feet-first entrances, gun barrels at camera, towering presence
- **Effect:** Mythic quality, viewer positioned as inferior

### 3. Long Takes & Dialogue

- **Style:** Extended scenes without cuts, characters breathe naturally
- **Application:** Restaurant conversations, negotiations, character introductions
- **Purpose:** Build tension through real-time interaction

### 4. Crash Zoom

- **Technique:** Rapid zoom into face/object during realization
- **Effect:** Punctuates drama, mimics 1970s exploitation films
- **Timing:** Sudden emphasis at peak moment

### 5. High-Contrast Noir Lighting

- **Style:** Deep blacks, blown highlights, minimal fill light
- **Effect:** Graphic comic-book quality, moral ambiguity visualized
- **Sources:** Practical lights, harsh directional, strong shadows

## Visual Style Elements

### Color Palettes

**Crime/Noir:**

- Cool greenish-white fluorescents
- Low color saturation
- Gritty surveillance texture
- Desaturated with selective color pops

**Korean Noir:**

- High color saturation
- Deep rich shadows
- Cool blue overall palette
- Crisp rim highlights

**Warm Interiors:**

- Amber/orange practical lights
- Vintage 1970s film stock emulation
- Warm skin tones

**Violence:**

- Hyper-saturated bright red blood
- High contrast against environment
- Stylized, almost cartoonish intensity

### Texture Requirements

- Noticeable film grain throughout
- CCTV surveillance texture (specific scenes)
- 35mm film aesthetic
- Vintage print quality
- Deliberate imperfections

## Character Direction

### Body Language

- **Power:** Confident stance, direct eye contact, centered framing
- **Weakness:** Slouching, shifty eyes, off-center positioning
- **Contrast:** One manic/one calm, synchronized yet opposed movements

### Performance Style

- Long monologues with pop culture references
- Naturalistic yet stylized delivery
- Profanity as punctuation
- Slow build-up to explosive moments

## Action & Violence

### Approach

- Sudden explosions of brutality after slow burn
- Graphic but purposeful
- Hyper-realistic blood effects
- Mixed with dark humor

### Camera During Violence

- Static framing during chaos (shows choreography)
- Extreme close-ups on impacts
- POV shots from victim perspective
- Wide shots to capture full action

## Quick Scene Types

### 1. Trunk Discovery

- Characters open trunk, reveal contents (weapon, body, money)
- 45° trunk shot, backlit silhouettes
- High contrast rim lighting
- Character reactions and interactions

### 2. Tense Negotiation

- Two characters in booth/confined space
- Static wide shot, slow push-in
- Warm practical lights
- Long dialogue, sudden violence

### 3. Mexican Standoff

- Multiple characters, guns drawn
- Rotating camera or rapid face cuts
- High contrast shadows
- Tense dialogue, explosive climax

### 4. Calm Before Storm

- Mundane setting, everyday talk
- Long take, relaxed framing
- Natural lighting
- Violence erupts without warning

### 5. Revenge Confrontation

- Protagonist vs antagonist
- Low angles for villain
- Stark high contrast
- Dialogue build, stylized violence

## Tropes Checklist

**Visual Must-Haves:**

- [ ] Film grain and texture
- [ ] High contrast lighting
- [ ] Low or extreme angles
- [ ] Backlit silhouettes
- [ ] Practical lights visible in frame

**Narrative Elements:**

- [ ] Strong character personalities
- [ ] Power dynamics visualized
- [ ] Tension through dialogue or silence
- [ ] Sudden violence or unexpected moment
- [ ] Dark humor potential

**Style Markers:**

- [ ] Gritty, lived-in locations
- [ ] Vintage or period details
- [ ] Pop culture references (if dialogue)
- [ ] Genre homage evident
- [ ] Bold, confident choices

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Clean, sterile digital look → ✅ Gritty film grain  
❌ Flat, even lighting → ✅ High contrast shadows  
❌ Generic angles → ✅ Bold low/extreme angles  
❌ Rapid cuts during dialogue → ✅ Long takes, let it breathe  
❌ CGI effects → ✅ Practical effects  
❌ Shaky cam chaos → ✅ Deliberate camera placement  
❌ Generic action → ✅ Character-driven violence  
❌ Soft focus beauty → ✅ Sharp, detailed, imperfect
