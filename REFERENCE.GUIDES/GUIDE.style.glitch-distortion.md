# GLITCH DISTORTION: Prompt Guide

## Overview

GLITCH DISTORTION is a controlled corruption style: RGB channel separation, scanline breakup, compression artifacts, frame tearing, and digital signal noise layered over a locked subject. The strongest outputs keep identity and composition intact while distorting texture and signal behavior.

---

## What You Need

- 1 clear base image
- Model supporting strong image-edit controls
- Optional mask (to glitch background more than face, or vice versa)

---

## Base Prompt Template

Use image edit mode.

```
Transform this image into a high-end GLITCH DISTORTION editorial frame while preserving the original subject identity and composition.

Apply controlled digital corruption:
- RGB channel splitting (subtle red/cyan fringes)
- horizontal scanline tearing
- intermittent pixel-sorting streaks
- VHS-style noise and compression blocks
- mild frame displacement and datamosh-like edge smearing
- chromatic aberration around high-contrast contours

Keep the subject recognizable with intact facial structure and body proportions.
Preserve the original camera angle, perspective, framing, and scene layout.

Lighting stays consistent with the source image.
Output must feel like a premium cyber-editorial still, not random low-quality artifacting.

No extra text, no watermark, no anatomy distortion, no duplicated limbs.
Keep the pose, silhouette, framing and composition exactly as they are.
```

---

## Tuning Controls

### Lighter Glitch (clean editorial)
- low RGB split
- minimal tearing
- soft grain only

### Medium Glitch (social/cover style)
- moderate channel shift
- visible scanline + compression mix
- controlled edge breakup

### Heavy Glitch (poster/experimental)
- stronger frame offsets
- denser digital breakup
- limited legibility zones preserved on eyes/face

---

## Quality Checklist

- Identity remains recognizable
- Distortion appears intentional, not accidental low quality
- No malformed anatomy
- Composition lock preserved
- Glitch density matches intended style tier
