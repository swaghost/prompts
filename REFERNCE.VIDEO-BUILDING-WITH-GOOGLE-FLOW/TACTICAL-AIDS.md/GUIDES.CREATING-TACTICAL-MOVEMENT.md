# Templates for 11v11 Team Spacing & Tactical Blocks in Motion

These templates generate videos that act like a digital tactics board. Success relies on high angles, deep focus (to keep the distant lines clear), and minimal, steady camera movement.

**Hierarchy used:** `[Style] -> [High/Wide Angle] -> [Deep Focus] -> [Steady Motion] -> [Specific 11v11 Tactics]`

## Template 1: The High-Press (Aggressive Shifting)

This template simulates a "bird's-eye view" drone shot. It is best used to show the vertical and horizontal shift of a defensive block that forces the opponent into a mistake.

```text
/cinematic /overhead /deepfocus /steadicam Tactical master shot, bird's-eye view, 11v11 visualization. A compact defensive block in a 4-4-2 formation shifts aggressively, closing down all passing lanes. Ten outfield players move vertically and horizontally in synchronized harmony, tracking the ball as it moves. Dynamic color coding (red team block vs. blue team attacker) highlights the closing spatial geometry.

```

## Template 2: The Low-Block "Park the Bus"

Use this to demonstrate a defense that prioritizes compactness inside their own penalty box, showing the congestion and strict discipline of a 5-man backline.

```text
/scifi /wide /deepfocus /craneup High-angle overview looking down on a defensive unit inside their own penalty area. A team in a disciplined 5-4-1 low-block maintains strict geometry, moving only laterally to cover attacks. The camera remains mostly static, illustrating the dense congestion and lack of space created by the defensive structure against an 11v11 attacking onslaught. Subtle grid overlays enhance the tactical analysis.

```

## Template 3: Negative Transition (Recovering Shape)

This is a critical tactical sequence—showing the speed at which an attacking shape collapses back into a organized defensive structure after losing possession.

```text
/documentary /establishing /panleft /tilt-down Tactical drone view, tracking a fast counter-attack. A team (white jerseys) just lost possession high up the pitch (4-3-3 shape) and is retreating into a defensive block. We see individual players sprinting back to recover their defensive grid positions, transitioning rapidly from an open attacking shape to a compact 4-4-2 block in motion. The high angle captures all 22 players clearly.

```
