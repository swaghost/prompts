# Diagnose Why Content Gets Views But No Saves

## Purpose

Audit content performance through save-and-share psychology to diagnose why content attracts views but fails to generate saves, identifying missing value-density, reference-ability, shareability factors, framing issues, and resource positioning gaps.

## Description of Target Content or Subject

Social media content that achieved high view counts or impressions but demonstrated disproportionately low save rates, indicating the content sparked initial curiosity but failed to deliver lasting value worth returning to or referencing later.

## Discovery - Description of Resulting Output

A diagnostic analysis examining Value Density (whether takeaway is immediately clear or buried), Reference-ability (whether someone would realistically need to look at this again or if it's one-time read), Shareability (whether it makes the reader look smart, helpful, or in-the-know by sending it to someone else), Framing issues (whether it reads as saveable or sounds like momentary opinion/not saveable), and specific ending or framing rewrites that would increase save/share likelihood. Explains the psychological reason each rewrite would work better.

## Usage

Perfect for content performance auditors specializing in save-and-share psychology, social media strategists diagnosing engagement gaps, and creators seeking to transform scroll-past content into reference resources. Ideal for analyzing Instagram posts with view/save disparities, LinkedIn articles generating impressions without bookmarks, Twitter/X threads getting reads without retweets, and any content demonstrating attention without retention. This prompt excels at identifying the subtle framing and structural differences between consumable content and saveable resources.

## Prerequisites

**MANDATORY:**

- Full text of content that got views but low saves/shares
- Performance metrics (views, saves, shares) if available
- Platform context and audience type

## Prompt

You are a content performance auditor specializing in save-and-share psychology. I will paste a piece of content that got views but low saves/shares. Diagnose it across these dimensions: (1) Value Density — is the takeaway immediately clear, or is something worth returning to later? (2) Reference-ability — would someone realistically need to look at this again, or was it a one-time read? (3) Shareability — does it make the reader look smart, helpful, or in-the-know by sending it to someone else? (4) Framing — is it framed as saveable (e.g., "keep this for later") or does it read like an opinion/rant? (5) Give me 3 specific rewrites of the ending or framing that would increase save/share likelihood, and explain the psychological reason each rewrite works. Content to diagnose: [PASTE YOUR CONTENT HERE]
