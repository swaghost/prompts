# Low-Ticket Digital Product Development Accelerator

## Purpose

Use this prompt to identify and design a profitable low-ticket digital product that solves an urgent problem, delivers a quick win, and naturally leads buyers toward a higher-ticket offer.

## Prompt

```
Act as my direct-response digital product strategist specializing in low-ticket offers, buyer psychology, and funnels. Help me create a profitable $27-or-less product that solves one urgent problem, delivers a fast win, is easy for beginners, and naturally leads to my higher-ticket offer.

My audience is [DESCRIBE NICHE]. My higher-ticket offer is [DESCRIBE OFFER, OR SAY "NONE YET"].

First identify their pain, buying intent, and quick win.

Give me:

- 3 product ideas with differentiated angles
- A one-line offer statement for each
- The problem, outcome, and reason someone would impulse-buy
- Recommended price, deliverables, lead magnet, order bump, and upsell
- A funnel flow
- A 7-day creation plan

Score each idea for urgency, value, ease, impulse-buy potential, and upsell potential. Rank them, choose the winner, and explain why.
```

## Required Output Structure

### 1. Audience Pain, Buying Intent, and Quick Win

- Identify the audience's most urgent relevant problem.
- Separate stated pain from likely purchasing motivation.
- Define the smallest useful result the buyer can achieve quickly.
- Note the evidence or assumptions behind the diagnosis.

### 2. Three Product Ideas

For each idea, provide:

- Product name
- Differentiated angle
- One-line offer statement
- Urgent problem solved
- Specific promised outcome
- Beginner-friendly quick win
- Why the buyer may impulse-buy at a low price
- What the product does not attempt to solve

### 3. Offer Architecture

For each idea, specify:

- Recommended price at or below $27
- Core deliverables
- Format and delivery method
- Free lead magnet that logically precedes the offer
- Order bump that increases immediate usefulness
- Upsell that deepens or accelerates the result
- Natural bridge to the higher-ticket offer, or a recommended higher-ticket offer if none exists

### 4. Funnel Flow

Map the buyer journey:

```
[Traffic or content] -> [Lead magnet] -> [Low-ticket offer] -> [Order bump] -> [Upsell] -> [Higher-ticket offer]
```

Explain the job of each step, the belief it should create, the main objection it addresses, and the conversion action required. Keep the funnel proportionate to a low-ticket product; do not introduce unnecessary complexity.

### 5. Seven-Day Creation Plan

Provide a practical day-by-day plan:

- Day 1: Validate the pain, buyer intent, promise, and scope.
- Day 2: Outline the minimum useful product and quick-win path.
- Day 3: Create the core deliverable.
- Day 4: Create the lead magnet, order bump, and upsell assets.
- Day 5: Write the sales page, checkout copy, delivery instructions, and FAQs.
- Day 6: Build and test the funnel, links, files, and onboarding.
- Day 7: Launch a small test, collect objections, and make the first revision.

Adapt the plan to the product and available time. Identify what can be removed without weakening the promised outcome.

### 6. Scoring and Recommendation

Score each product from 1-10 on:

| Criterion             | Question                                                    |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Urgency               | Does the buyer want this problem solved now?                |
| Value                 | Is the outcome clearly worth more than the price?           |
| Ease                  | Can a beginner use it and get a result quickly?             |
| Impulse-buy potential | Is the promise specific, accessible, and low-friction?      |
| Upsell potential      | Does the result naturally create demand for the next offer? |

Show the score for every idea, calculate the total out of 50, rank the ideas, choose one winner, and explain the decision. Name the largest risk and the cheapest test that could disprove the recommendation.

## Quality Rules

- Optimize for one urgent problem and one measurable quick win.
- Avoid broad information products, vague transformation claims, and unnecessary modules.
- Make the low-ticket offer useful on its own; do not make it a disguised sales pitch.
- Use impulse-buy psychology ethically: clarity, specificity, low friction, immediate usefulness, and credible value.
- State assumptions and distinguish evidence from speculation.
- Keep the product beginner-friendly without making the promise trivial.
- Ensure the order bump and upsell are relevant extensions, not unrelated add-ons.
- If no higher-ticket offer exists, propose one that is a credible next step rather than forcing an artificial upgrade.

## Optional Follow-Up Prompts

### Validate the Winner

```
Stress-test the winning low-ticket product idea. Identify the strongest reason someone would not buy it, the evidence needed to validate demand, the fastest ethical pre-sale or message test, and the smallest version I can create before investing more time.
```

### Build the Product

```
Turn the winning low-ticket product into a complete production brief. Define the promise, learning or action path, modules, worksheets, examples, completion criteria, delivery format, and the exact quick win the buyer should achieve within the first session.
```

### Write the Funnel

```
Write the landing page, checkout page, order-bump copy, upsell page, delivery email, and three follow-up emails for the winning low-ticket product. Keep every claim specific, credible, and aligned with the promised quick win.
```
