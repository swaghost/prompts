# Stretch Marks Realism for Human Image Prompts

## Purpose

Use this guide when stretch marks are visible, requested, or important to a subject's body realism. Treat them as natural skin features with biological variation, not as damage, decoration, or a repeated graphic texture.

## Core Rule

Name the location, maturity, direction, width, color, contrast, and relationship to surrounding skin. Stretch marks should follow the body's form and remain integrated with pores, skin folds, vellus hair, pigmentation, and realistic light response.

## Biological Detail

- Describe placement anatomically: hips, thighs, abdomen, breasts, lower back, shoulders, upper arms, or other requested areas.
- Vary line length, width, spacing, branching, opacity, and direction; do not repeat identical parallel marks.
- Distinguish newer striae with subtle pink, red, violet, or warm tones from mature striae with pale silver, ivory, or softly hypopigmented tones.
- Let marks follow contours and tension lines of the body rather than floating across the surface.
- Preserve natural interruption from body folds, pressure, movement, hair, fabric, and changing light.
- Keep the surrounding skin alive: visible pores, fine vellus hair, natural tonal variation, slight redness, and realistic elasticity.
- Match visibility to camera distance. Use broad distribution in full-body images, clear line variation in portraits, and fine edges and skin relief in macro shots.

## Identity and Dignity

- If a reference is supplied, preserve the exact location, pattern, density, color, and maturity of visible stretch marks as part of the subject's appearance.
- Do not remove, minimize, sexualize, stigmatize, or exaggerate stretch marks unless explicitly requested.
- Do not infer weight, health, pregnancy, age, or ethnicity from stretch marks.
- Keep the subject's body proportions, skin tone, age, expression, and identity unchanged.
- Avoid medical or injury language unless the prompt specifically requires clinical documentation.

## Prompt Formula

```text
[subject] with natural stretch marks on [specific body areas], [newer/mature/mixed] striae with varied length, width, spacing, direction, opacity, and [pink/red/violet/silver] tones following the body's anatomical contours, marks embedded in realistic skin with visible pores, fine vellus hair, natural pigmentation, elasticity, and light response, unretouched photographic texture, no airbrushing
```

## Close-Up and Macro Formula

```text
close-up of natural stretch marks across [body area], individual striae with irregular edges, varied width and spacing, subtle changes in color and translucency, realistic skin ridges and pores continuing across and around the marks, fine vellus hair, natural creasing and light falloff, authentic unretouched skin, no repeated pattern, no painted lines, no smoothing
```

## Negative Prompt

```text
painted lines, tattoo-like marks, repeated parallel pattern, identical stripes, perfectly symmetrical marks, floating texture, sticker-like skin, artificial overlay, exaggerated scarring, wound, gore, diseased skin, airbrushed skin, plastic skin, poreless skin, beauty filter, erased stretch marks, altered body proportions, identity drift
```

## Builder Integration

When stretch marks are specified or visible in a reference, builders must:

1. Treat them as part of the subject's natural appearance and identity.
2. State the anatomical location, maturity, color, direction, density, and variation.
3. Scale the detail to the framing: distribution for full-body, clusters for portraits, line edges and relief for macro.
4. Preserve surrounding pores, vellus hair, pigmentation, folds, and realistic elasticity.
5. Avoid assumptions about weight, health, pregnancy, age, or cause.
6. Add the negative prompt constraints unless the user explicitly requests a clinical, artistic, tattoo, or transformation treatment.

## Specificity Beats Resolution

An `8K` or high-resolution request does not create realistic stretch marks by itself. Name the imperfections and their physical behavior: where they occur, how they vary, how they follow the body, and how light reveals them.
