## French Film Noir Characteristics

- **1950s Paris Detective Films**: References classic French noir cinema
- **Quiet Tension**: Subject holds emotional restraint
- **Elegant Danger**: Sophistication mixed with threat
- **Long Shadows**: Classic noir visual signature
- **Glistening Surfaces**: Wet or reflective for mood
- **Misty Air**: Adds mystery and cinematic atmosphere
- **Angular Pose**: Sharp, not soft - enhances drama

## Noir vs. Romance

This version emphasizes:

- **Tension** over relaxation
- **Mystery** over intimacy
- **Danger** over serenity
- **Sharp angles** over soft curves
- **Intrigue** over contemplation

## Tips

- "Quiet tension" suggests restrained danger, not overt action
- Sharp angular pose creates visual unease
- Long shadows are signature noir element
- Glistening cobblestones suggest dampness, mystery
- Misty air diffuses light cinematically
- 1950s reference means classic noir, not neo-noir
- "Elegant danger" combines sophistication with threat

## Period Reference

**1950s Paris Detective Films**: Rififi (1955), Bob le flambeur (1956), classic French polar (detective) cinema with atmospheric tension and urban poetry.
