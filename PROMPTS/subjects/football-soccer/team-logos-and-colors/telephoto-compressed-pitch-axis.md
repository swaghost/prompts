## Best Practices

### Camera Positioning Strategy

- **Axis Alignment**: Precisely align with pitch longitudinal centerline
- **Building Access**: Secure access to appropriate rooftop or elevated position
- **Altitude Optimization**: Height providing view down axis without excessive angle
- **Stability**: Tripod mandatory for extreme telephoto sharpness
- **Safety**: Appropriate rooftop safety measures and permissions

### Extreme Telephoto Technique

- **Lens Selection**: 400mm minimum, 600mm+ for maximum compression
- **Image Stabilization**: Critical for handheld or even tripod-mounted shooting
- **Mirror Lock-Up**: Reduce internal camera vibration for sharpness
- **Remote Shutter**: Eliminate any camera shake from pressing button
- **Weather Stability**: Shoot only in calm conditions to minimize vibration

### Focus and Sharpness Strategy

- **Hyperfocal Distance**: Calculate and use for maximum depth sharpness
- **Small Aperture**: f/11-f/16 for deep depth of field across layers
- **Manual Focus**: Lock focus at calculated hyperfocal point
- **Live View Magnification**: Use camera live view to verify critical sharpness
- **Focus Stacking**: Optional technique for guaranteed sharpness throughout

### Timing and Conditions

- **Empty Field**: Schedule when facilities are vacant for clean documentation
- **Fresh Maintenance**: Recent mowing and field lining for optimal appearance
- **Clear Atmosphere**: Minimal haze or pollution for sharpness at distance
- **Weather**: Calm conditions with no wind affecting camera stability
- **Time of Day**: Midday for even lighting across all compressed elements

## Technical Notes

### Extreme Telephoto Compression Physics

- **Focal Length Impact**: 400mm+ creates dramatic spatial collapse
- **Distance Magnification**: Distant objects magnified significantly
- **Perspective Flattening**: Near and far appear similar size
- **Atmospheric Effects**: Haze and diffraction more pronounced
- **Detail Magnification**: Brings distant detail into close view
- **Depth Compression**: 100+ meters appears as a few meters

### Atmospheric Challenges

- **Haze Impact**: Atmospheric moisture reduces sharpness at distance
- **Heat Shimmer**: Thermal distortion especially in warm conditions
- **Pollution**: Urban air quality affects clarity across compression
- **Optimal Conditions**: Cool, clear, dry air with high visibility
- **Seasonal Considerations**: Fall and winter often have clearest air
- **Time of Day**: Early morning usually has clearest atmosphere

### Camera Stability Requirements

- **Tripod Quality**: Heavy-duty tripod necessary for extreme telephoto
- **Vibration Reduction**: Every movement magnified at 400mm
- **Wind Protection**: Even breeze causes significant camera movement
- **Mirror Lock-Up**: Internal camera movements cause blur
- **Remote Shutter**: Cable release or timer to eliminate button press
- **Exposure Delay**: Allow camera to settle after movement

### Depth of Field Calculation

- **Hyperfocal Distance**: Critical for maximizing sharpness across layers
- **Aperture Selection**: f/11-f/16 optimal for telephoto depth
- **Focus Point**: Position approximately 1/3 into compressed scene
- **Diffraction Limit**: Avoid f/22+ which softens extreme telephoto
- **Sensor Size**: Full-frame provides better DOF control at telephoto
- **Calculator Use**: Employ hyperfocal calculator for precise focus point
