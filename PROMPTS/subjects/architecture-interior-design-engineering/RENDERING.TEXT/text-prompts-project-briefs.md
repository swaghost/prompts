# Project Briefs & Programming - Architectural Text Prompts

## Overview

AI text prompts for transforming client meeting notes, requirements lists, or rough ideas into comprehensive technical project briefs with clear programs of requirements, priorities, and constraints. Use ChatGPT or other AI text tools to organize and structure project information, capture stakeholder requirements, and create actionable project documentation.

**Part of:** [Architectural Professional Text Tools Collection](architectural-professional-text.md)

---

## Use Cases

- **Project Initiation Documents** - Foundation for project start
- **Client Requirement Capture** - Organize and document needs
- **Design Phase Kickoff** - Clear direction for design team
- **Stakeholder Alignment** - Unified understanding of goals
- **Scope Definition** - Clear project boundaries
- **Program of Requirements** - Detailed space and functional needs
- **Constraint Documentation** - Budget, schedule, site, regulatory
- **Priority Setting** - Must-haves vs. nice-to-haves
- **Success Criteria Definition** - Measurable project goals

## Basic Prompt Templates

### Meeting Notes to Brief

**Prompt:**

```
I have these notes from a meeting with my client: [paste notes here].

Turn them into a complete technical project brief, including:
- Project overview and objectives
- Program of requirements (spaces, sizes, relationships)
- Priorities and must-haves vs. nice-to-haves
- Key constraints (budget, timeline, site, regulatory)
- Important project considerations (sustainability, style preferences, special needs)
- Success criteria
- Next steps and deliverables
```

**Example Input:**

```
I have these notes from a meeting with my client:

- Family of 4 (2 kids, ages 8 and 11)
- Current house too small, want 4 bedrooms
- Love to cook, need big kitchen
- Work from home 2 days/week, need office
- Budget around $600K
- Timeline: start construction next spring
- Lot is 800m², flat, faces south
- Contemporary style, lots of natural light
- Want sustainable/energy efficient
- Pool maybe, depends on cost
- Need 2-car garage

Turn them into a complete technical project brief...
```

**When to Use:**

- After initial client meeting
- Converting informal notes to formal document
- Organizing scattered information
- Creating project foundation document
- Team briefing preparation

## Detailed Prompt Templates

### Comprehensive Requirements Organization

**Prompt:**

```
Organize these client requirements into a structured architectural program:

[List or paste requirements - can be bullet points, paragraphs, or scattered notes]

Deliver:
1. Space program (room list with approximate sizes and specific requirements)
2. Functional relationships diagram (which spaces need adjacency or separation)
3. Technical requirements by space (lighting, climate, acoustics, power, data)
4. Special equipment or system needs
5. Accessibility requirements
6. Sustainability goals
7. Budget and schedule parameters
8. Regulatory/code considerations (zoning, building code, special permits)
9. Design priorities and preferences
10. Project success criteria
```

**When to Use:**

- Complex projects with many requirements
- Multiple stakeholder input
- Technical project documentation
- Design team briefing
- Comprehensive scope definition

### Stakeholder Requirement Synthesis

**Prompt:**

```
I have requirements from multiple stakeholders for [project type]:

Stakeholder A (Owner/Developer): [requirements and priorities]
Stakeholder B (End Users): [requirements and priorities]
Stakeholder C (Facility Manager): [requirements and priorities]
Stakeholder D (Other): [requirements and priorities]

Synthesize into unified project brief:
- Reconcile conflicting requirements with recommendations
- Prioritize requirements by stakeholder impact and project success
- Identify gaps, ambiguities, or unclear requirements
- Create comprehensive program addressing all needs
- Suggest design strategies that satisfy multiple stakeholders
- Flag requirements needing clarification or decision
- Organize into coherent project framework
```

**Example:**

```
I have requirements from multiple stakeholders for new elementary school:

School Board (Owner):
- 500 student capacity
- Cost under $15M
- LEED Silver minimum
- Must open for 2025 school year
- Standard classroom prototype

Teachers (Users):
- Natural daylight in classrooms
- Flexible furniture arrangements
- Adjacent outdoor learning spaces
- Adequate storage in classrooms
- Staff collaboration spaces

Principal (Facility Manager):
- Secure entry with visibility to office
- Minimal maintenance finishes
- Easy supervision of common areas
- Separate bus and parent drop-off
- Community use after hours (isolated gym/cafeteria)

Parents (Community):
- Safe pick-up/drop-off
- Neighborhood-friendly design
- Connection to walking trails
- State-of-art technology
- Focus on STEM education

Synthesize into unified project brief...
```

**When to Use:**

- Multiple decision-makers
- Conflicting requirements
- Complex stakeholder landscape
- Public/institutional projects
- Partnership developments
- User group input synthesis

### Concept to Detailed Brief

**Prompt:**

```
Expand this project concept into a detailed architectural brief:

Concept: [brief project description or vision statement]

Develop:
- Detailed program of requirements (spaces with sizes and characteristics)
- Site analysis requirements (what needs to be studied)
- Design aspirations and principles (what should guide design)
- Material and sustainability direction
- Budget framework (total and breakdown)
- Timeline framework (design phases and construction)
- Regulatory research needed (codes, permits, approvals)
- Precedent studies to review (example projects)
- Design phase scope and deliverables
- Key decisions needed before design begins
- Project team requirements (consultants, specialists)
```

**Example:**

```
Expand this project concept into a detailed architectural brief:

Concept: A modern wellness retreat center in the mountains combining spa facilities, yoga studios, and overnight accommodations in a sustainable design that connects guests to nature.

Develop detailed brief including: [all elements above]
```

**When to Use:**

- Early concept development
- Grant applications
- Investor presentations
- Project fundraising
- Vision to reality planning
- Competition briefings

## Space Programming Prompts

### Detailed Space Program

**Prompt:**

```
Create detailed space program for [building type] serving [population/use]:

For each space include:
- Space name and quantity
- Area (m² or SF)
- Occupancy and use description
- Environmental requirements (light, temperature, ventilation, acoustics)
- Adjacency requirements (must be adjacent to/near/separated from)
- Special requirements (ceiling height, equipment, power, data, water, finishes)
- Accessibility requirements
- Furniture and equipment (major items)
- Security/privacy needs

Organize by:
- Functional zones
- Public vs. private
- Circulation (provide efficiency factor)
- Support spaces
- Total net area and gross area (with efficiency factor)
```

---

### Adjacency Matrix

**Prompt:**

```
Create adjacency matrix for [project type] with following spaces:
[List all spaces]

For each space pair, indicate relationship:
- Critical (must be adjacent)
- Important (should be near)
- Neutral (no requirement)
- Separate (should not be adjacent)

Provide:
- Matrix diagram (visual or table)
- Functional zones grouping spaces by relationship
- Circulation strategy recommendations
- Rationale for key adjacencies
```

---

### Phased Program Development

**Prompt:**

```
Develop phased program for [project] with limited initial budget:

Total program desired: [full scope]
Initial budget: [Phase 1 amount]
Future phases: [when additional funding expected]

Provide:
- Phase 1 scope (immediate needs, foundation for future)
- Phase 2 scope (medium-term expansion)
- Phase 3 scope (complete vision)
- Cost allocation by phase
- Design strategies for phasing (infrastructure, expansion zones)
- What must be built in Phase 1 to enable future phases
- Standalone functionality of Phase 1
```

## Project Definition Prompts

### Project Vision and Goals

**Prompt:**

```
Develop comprehensive project vision and goals statement for [project]:

Input:
- Owner/client vision: [aspirations]
- User needs: [functional requirements]
- Context: [site, community, market]
- Constraints: [budget, time, regulations]

Create:
- Overarching project vision (inspirational statement)
- Measurable project goals (what defines success)
- Design principles (what should guide decisions)
- Priorities hierarchy (what matters most)
- Project values (sustainability, community, innovation, etc.)
```

---

### Success Criteria Definition

**Prompt:**

```
Define measurable success criteria for [project]:

Categories:
- Functional success (does it work well for intended use)
- Financial success (budget, value, operating costs)
- Schedule success (milestones and completion)
- Quality success (materials, craftsmanship, durability)
- User satisfaction (occupant experience)
- Sustainability success (energy, water, materials, certifications)
- Community impact (neighborhood, social value)
- Design excellence (awards, recognition, innovation)

For each provide:
- Specific measurable criteria
- Target metrics or standards
- How it will be measured/verified
- Priority level (essential/important/desirable)
```

## Site Analysis Integration

### Site Requirements Brief

**Prompt:**

```
Create site analysis brief for [project] on [site description]:

What needs to be studied:
- Site boundary, dimensions, area
- Topography and existing grades
- Existing vegetation and natural features
- Views and vistas (to preserve or capitalize on)
- Solar orientation and shading
- Wind patterns and microclimate
- Existing structures or features
- Utilities (location, capacity, connections)
- Access points and circulation
- Neighboring properties and context
- Soil conditions (if known or testing needed)
- Drainage and water issues
- Environmental considerations (wetlands, protected species, etc.)
- Zoning and regulatory constraints
- Easements and restrictions
- Opportunities and challenges summary
```

---

### Site-Program Integration

**Prompt:**

```
Analyze fit between program requirements and site constraints for:

Program: [size, components, special requirements]
Site: [size, zoning, topography, access, constraints]

Evaluate:
- Does program fit within zoning envelope (FAR, height, coverage, setbacks)
- Parking requirements and feasibility
- Site access adequacy for program
- Topography challenges or opportunities for program
- View and orientation optimization for program
- Phasing implications for occupied site
- Environmental constraints impact on program
- Utilities capacity for program needs
- Recommendations for program or approach adjustments
```

## Budget and Schedule Framework

### Budget Framework Development

**Prompt:**

```
Create budget framework for [project]:

Total budget available: [amount]

Break down:
- Land/site acquisition (if applicable): [amount or %]
- Construction cost: [amount or %]
  - Site work
  - Building
  - Parking and access
  - Landscaping
- FF&E (furniture, fixtures, equipment): [amount or %]
- Soft costs:
  - Design fees (architect, engineers, consultants)
  - Permits and fees
  - Testing and inspections
  - Legal and financing
  - Owner's project management
- Contingency: [% of construction cost]
- Escalation: [% or method]

Provide:
- Detailed breakdown
- Assumptions stated clearly
- What's included/excluded
- Risk factors affecting budget
- Value engineering opportunities if over budget
```

---

### Project Schedule Framework

**Prompt:**

```
Develop project schedule framework for [project]:

Key dates:
- Project start: [date]
- Desired occupancy: [date]
- Other critical dates: [list any]

Create schedule including:
- Pre-design phase (programming, site analysis)
- Schematic design
- Design development
- Construction documents
- Permitting and approvals
- Bidding and contractor selection
- Construction
- Closeout and occupancy

For each phase provide:
- Duration (weeks or months)
- Key deliverables
- Decision points
- Critical path items
- Typical contingency time
- Dependencies and prerequisites

Include:
- Overall timeline from start to occupancy
- Key milestones dates
- Schedule risks
- Fast-track opportunities if needed
- Float and contingency recommendations
```

## Regulatory and Code Considerations

### Code Research Brief

**Prompt:**

```
Identify applicable codes, regulations, and permits for [project] in [jurisdiction]:

Research needs:
- Zoning requirements (use, FAR, height, setbacks, parking, coverage)
- Building code (which code and amendments)
- Energy code requirements
- Accessibility requirements
- Fire and life safety requirements
- Environmental review needs (NEPA, SEPA, CEQA, etc.)
- Historic preservation (if applicable)
- Special permits or approvals needed
- Approval authority and process
- Typical timeline for approvals
- Public hearing requirements
- Agency coordination needs
- Critical code issues that impact program or design
```

---

### Permitting Strategy

**Prompt:**

```
Develop permitting strategy for [project]:

Required permits:
- Zoning approval/variance/conditional use
- Environmental review
- Building permit
- Specialized permits [list: fire, health, alcohol, etc.]

For each detail:
- Approval authority
- Submission requirements
- Review process and timeline
- Fees
- Public involvement requirements
- Appeal period
- Prerequisites and dependencies
- Critical path impact

Provide:
- Permitting roadmap and sequence
- Total timeline estimate
- Key risks and contingencies
```

## Specialty Project Briefs

### Sustainable Design Brief

**Prompt:**

```
Create sustainable design brief for [project] targeting [certification/goal]:

Define:
- Sustainability goals and priorities
- Certification target (LEED level, Passive House, Net Zero, LBC, etc.)
- Energy performance targets (EUI, % reduction from baseline)
- Water conservation goals
- Material selection criteria (recycled content, regional, low-VOC, etc.)
- Site sustainability (stormwater, landscaping, habitat)
- Indoor environmental quality priorities
- Renewable energy goals
- Waste reduction (construction and operational)
- Measurement and verification approach
- Budget implications
- Team and consultant needs (energy modeler, commissioning agent, etc.)
```

---

### Renovation/Adaptive Reuse Brief

**Prompt:**

```
Create project brief for renovation of existing [building type] built in [year]:

Existing conditions:
- Current size and program
- Structural system
- Condition assessment (if available)
- Historic status (if applicable)
- Known issues

New program:
- Proposed use and program
- Retention vs. demolition strategy
- Code upgrade requirements
- Accessibility improvements needed
- Systems replacement/upgrade needs

Address:
- What must be preserved (historic, structural, character)
- What can be demolished
- Code compliance strategy (existing building code provisions)
- Phasing if occupied during construction
- Abatement needs (asbestos, lead, etc.)
- Budget implications of renovation vs. new
- Unknown conditions contingency
```

## Output Format Specifications

### Standard Project Brief Format

**Request this structure:**

```
Format the project brief with following sections:

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
   - Project overview (one paragraph)
   - Key goals and objectives
   - Major challenges and opportunities

2. PROJECT DESCRIPTION
   - Project type and use
   - Location and site description
   - Program overview (brief)

3. PROGRAM OF REQUIREMENTS
   - Detailed space list with areas
   - Functional relationships
   - Special requirements by space
   - Net and gross area totals

4. PROJECT GOALS AND OBJECTIVES
   - Vision statement
   - Measurable success criteria
   - Design principles

5. CONSTRAINTS AND PARAMETERS
   - Budget framework
   - Schedule framework
   - Site constraints
   - Regulatory requirements
   - Other constraints

6. DESIGN DIRECTION
   - Style and character guidance
   - Material preferences
   - Sustainability goals
   - Special considerations

7. PROJECT TEAM AND STAKEHOLDERS
   - Owner/client contact
   - Key decision makers
   - User groups
   - Required consultants

8. NEXT STEPS
   - Immediate action items
   - Decisions needed
   - Information to gather
   - Project milestones
```

---

## Quality Control Checklist

Before using AI-generated brief:

- [ ] All client requirements captured
- [ ] Space list complete with areas
- [ ] Budget clearly defined and allocated
- [ ] Schedule framework established
- [ ] Site constraints identified
- [ ] Code and regulatory issues noted
- [ ] Priorities clearly established
- [ ] Success criteria defined
- [ ] Stakeholders identified
- [ ] Gaps or questions flagged

After receiving brief:

- [ ] Review for completeness
- [ ] Verify areas and totals are reasonable
- [ ] Check that adjacencies make sense
- [ ] Confirm budget is realistic for program
- [ ] Validate schedule is achievable
- [ ] Ensure no major requirements missed
- [ ] Verify technical requirements are accurate
- [ ] Check that priorities are clear
- [ ] Confirm brief reflects client intent
- [ ] Share with client for confirmation

## Professional Use Guidelines

### Appropriate Applications

✅ **Good for:**

- Organizing meeting notes
- Structuring scattered requirements
- Creating first draft briefs
- Educational project briefs
- Internal project planning
- Requirement brainstorming

✅ **With Client Review:**

- Formal project briefs (confirm with client)
- Funding applications (verify accuracy)
- Stakeholder communications (review messaging)
- Programming documents (validate requirements)

---

### Best Practices

**Always:**

- Confirm AI output with client before proceeding
- Verify space areas and totals for reasonableness
- Check that nothing critical was missed
- Clarify ambiguous or assumed requirements
- Document where assumptions were made
- Update brief as project evolves
- Use as living document through design
- Keep stakeholders informed of changes

**Never:**

- Proceed with design based solely on AI brief
- Assume AI captured all requirements
- Trust areas or numbers without verification
- Skip client review and approval
- Treat brief as final without iterations

---

### Risk Management

1. **Client Confirmation** - Always review and confirm brief with client
2. **Document Everything** - Keep meeting notes and correspondence
3. **Clarify Assumptions** - State what was assumed vs. confirmed
4. **Identify Gaps** - Flag missing information clearly
5. **Iterate** - Brief should evolve through discussions
6. **Stakeholder Buy-in** - Ensure alignment among decision makers
7. **Change Management** - Track scope changes from original brief
8. **Budget Reality** - Verify program fits budget early
