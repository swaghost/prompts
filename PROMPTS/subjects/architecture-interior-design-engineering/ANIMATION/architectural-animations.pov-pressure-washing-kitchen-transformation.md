### Hand Positioning

- **Position**: Lower on wand handle, supports weight
- **Visibility**: Visible in bottom-left of frame throughout
- **Movement**: Stabilizes wand, absorbs recoil

#### Right Hand (Dominant):

- **Position**: Upper on wand handle, controls direction and trigger
- **Visibility**: Visible in bottom-right of frame throughout
- **Movement**: Guides wand direction, finger on trigger
- **Details**: Realistic skin tone, work gloves (optional), thumb position visible

### Wand & Nozzle

**Pressure-Washer Wand**:

- **Length Visible**: 30–50cm of wand visible in frame
- **Material**: Metal (chrome, aluminum) or industrial plastic
- **Nozzle**: Visible at far end, angled spray tip
- **Hose**: Attached to wand, curves out of frame bottom

**Water Jet Visual**:

- **Stream**: Visible white/clear high-pressure stream
- **Spray Cone**: Widens slightly at impact point
- **Mist Halo**: Mist surrounds main jet

#### Recoil Dynamics:

- **Initial Activation** (1.0–1.5s): Noticeable kickback when trigger pulled, camera jerks slightly back and down

- **Direction Changes**: Slight lag as weight shifts when changing spray direction

#### Handheld Shake Patterns:

- **Breathing Motion**: Subtle up-down cycle (1–2cm amplitude, ~3 seconds period)
- **Weight Shift**: Side-to-side sway when repositioning feet
- **Fast Sweeps**: Increased shake during rapid wand movements
- **Stabilization**: Gradually smooths out in Scene 3 as worker relaxes

#### Movement Types:

## Grime & Dirt Design

### Grime Coverage (Scene 1)

**Visual Characteristics**:

- **Color**: Deep black to dark brown/grey
- **Texture**: Thick, caked-on appearance (not just surface dust)
- **Coverage**: 90–100% of all visible surfaces
- **Variation**: Slightly thicker in corners, edges, under cabinets (natural accumulation patterns)

### Grime Types by Surface

| Surface                    | Grime Type                 | Visual Description                                    |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cabinets**               | Soot, grease, smoke damage | Black oily residue, streaks from top to bottom        |
| **Island/Countertops**     | Mud, dried spills, soot    | Thick black/brown layer, uneven texture               |
| **Appliances (Stainless)** | Grease, fingerprints, soot | Black film covering reflective surfaces               |
| **Backsplash Tile**        | Cooking grease, soot       | Dark brown/black coating, tile grout filled with dirt |
| **Pendant Lights**         | Dust, smoke residue        | Darkened glass or metal, less glow visible            |
| **Stools**                 | Dirt, grime, possible mold | Dark spots on upholstery, grimy legs                  |
| **Floor**                  | Mud, tracked dirt, grime   | Almost entirely black, wet-looking in some areas      |

### Grime Removal Physics

**How Grime Peels Away**:

✅ **Correct**:

- Grime **peels** in strips following water jet direction
- Clean surface **immediately visible** underneath
- Sharp contrast line between dirty and clean
- Grime particles fly off into air (dark mist)
- Dirty water runs down due to gravity

❌ **Incorrect**:

- Grime **fades** or dissolves gradually (too magical)
- Clean area appears through blur or soft transition
- Grime color lightens instead of removing
- Dirt disappears without water interaction

### Realistic Grime Removal Stages

**Stage 1: First Pass**

- Pressure-washer cuts initial clean line through black grime
- ~80% of surface dirt removed in one pass
- Underlying surface color revealed
- Edges still have residual grime

**Stage 2: Second Pass (if needed)**

- Removes remaining 20% in edges and corners
- Surface now fully clean
- Slight wetness remains (specular reflections)

**Stage 3: Drying**

- Water droplets evaporate quickly (Scene 3)
- Surface dries to match **@Image1** exactly

## Water Physics & Effects

### Pressure-Washer Water Jet

**Main Jet**:

- **Appearance**: White/clear concentrated stream, ~2–5cm diameter at nozzle
- **Spray Cone**: Widens to ~10–15cm at impact (1–2 meters from nozzle)
- **Velocity**: Visibly fast, slight motion blur in jet itself
- **Sound**: High-pressure roar (if audio added)

**Impact Behavior**:

- **On Flat Surfaces**: Splashes outward in circular pattern, mist rises
- **On Vertical Surfaces**: Water runs downward immediately after impact
- **On Corners/Edges**: Spray redirects, creates more mist
- **Impact Force**: Visible pressure—grime peels back from impact point

### Water on Camera Lens

**Progressive Droplet Accumulation**:

- **0–2s**: Few droplets appear on lens edges
- **2–5s**: More droplets accumulate, some blur portions of frame
- **5–7s**: Maximum droplet density, lens partially obscured (adds realism)
- **7–10s**: Droplets fade/dry as mist settles and cleaning finishes

**Droplet Characteristics**:

- Realistic refraction (world visible through droplets)
- Some droplets streak downward (gravity)
- Variable sizes (tiny specks to large drops)
- Positioned across frame (not uniformly distributed)

### Mist & Airborne Particles

**Mist Cloud**:

- **Source**: Pressure-washer spray creates constant mist
- **Color**: White/grey from water, darkens with grime particles
- **Volume**: Builds up in Scene 2 (2–7s), settles in Scene 3 (7–10s)
- **Light Interaction**: Diffuses light sources, creates volumetric rays if strong lighting

**Airborne Grime Particles**:

- **Appearance**: Dark specks/particles flying through air during spraying
- **Motion**: Follows water spray direction, then falls due to gravity
- **Density**: Highest during active cleaning (Scene 2), dissipates in Scene 3

### Dirty Water on Floor

**Flow Pattern**:

- **Origin**: Water hits surfaces, runs down to floor carrying black grime
- **Color**: Dark brown to black (dirty water)
- **Spread**: Flows outward from cleaned areas, pools in low spots
- **Behavior**: Gravity-accurate, realistic fluid dynamics
- **Progression**: More dirty water accumulates throughout Scene 2, begins to clear in Scene 3

**Floor Wetness**:

- Wet floor surfaces are darker, more reflective
- Specular highlights from pendant lights reflected in puddles
- Wetness dries by end of Scene 3 (10s) to match **@Image1**

### Splashes & Splatter

**Close-Range Splashes**:

- Small water droplets splash toward camera during close-up spray passes
- Some hit lens (become lens droplets)
- Creates dynamic, immersive feeling

**Surface Splatter**:

- Water and grime splatter onto adjacent surfaces during cleaning
- Example: Spraying island causes splatter on nearby cabinet doors
- These secondary splatters get cleaned in subsequent passes

## Audio Design Guide

### Essential Sound Layers

#### 1. Pressure-Washer Motor

- **Sound**: Continuous high-pitched roar, gasoline engine or electric motor hum
- **Volume**: Medium-high, constant throughout Scenes 1-2
- **Variation**: Slightly louder when trigger engaged, quieter when released (Scene 3)

#### 2. Water Jet

- **Sound**: Hissing, whooshing, high-pressure spray
- **Volume**: Loud, sharp, cutting sound
- **Variation**: Changes pitch/tone when hitting different surfaces (metal vs. wood vs. tile)

#### 3. Water Impact on Surfaces

- **Sound**: Splashing, splattering, varies by material
  - **Hard surfaces (tile, metal)**: Sharp, high-pitched splatter
  - **Soft surfaces (wood, upholstery)**: Duller, absorbed sound
  - **Floor**: Continuous splashing and runoff

#### 4. Grime Removal

- **Sound**: Scraping, peeling, squelching as thick grime loosens
- **Volume**: Medium, most prominent during satisfying strip reveals
- **Variation**: Wet, sloppy sounds as dirty water flows

#### 5. Mist & Droplets

- **Sound**: Ambient hiss, light spray atmosphere
- **Volume**: Low, background layer
- **Effect**: Creates immersive environment

#### 6. Movement & Handling

- **Sound**: Wand movement through air (whoosh), footsteps, body movement
- **Volume**: Low-medium, adds realism
- **Variation**: Faster whooshes during whip pans and speed ramps

#### 7. Environment Ambience

- **Sound**: Room reverb, echo in kitchen space
- **Volume**: Very low, subtle
- **Effect**: Grounds scene in physical space

---

### Audio Progression

#### Scene 1 (0–2s):

- Motor starts (revving up)
- Footsteps approach
- Trigger engagement sound (click)
- Water jet activates (sudden loud hiss)
- First impact on island (sharp splash)

#### Scene 2 (2–7s):

- Dirty water flowing sounds

- Final spray bursts
- Trigger release (click, water stops)
- Motor idles down or shuts off
- Mist settling (hiss fades)
- Quiet ambience (clean, calm)

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

### Issue 1: Grime Fades Instead of Peeling

**Problem**: Grime gradually fades/dissolves instead of physically peeling away in strips

**Solution**:

- Add to prompt: "Grime must PEEL AWAY in strips—not fade or dissolve. Physical removal only."
- Specify: "Sharp contrast line between dirty and clean. Water pressure pushes grime away."
- Reference: "Satisfying strip reveal like real pressure-washing videos"
- Regenerate Scene 2 with emphasis on physical grime removal

---

### Issue 2: Clean Kitchen Doesn't Match @Image1

**Problem**: Final clean kitchen has wrong colors, layout, or design

**Solution**:

- Ensure **@Image1** is actually uploaded as reference image
- Add to prompt: "Final kitchen MUST match @Image1 exactly: same cabinets, countertops, appliances, colors, materials"
- Verify platform supports reference image conditioning
- If platform doesn't support reference well, describe kitchen in extreme detail in prompt

---

### Issue 3: Hands Disappear or Morph

**Problem**: POV hands vanish, multiply, or change appearance mid-video

**Solution**:

- Emphasize: "Two realistic hands remain VISIBLE THROUGHOUT entire video. Never disappear."
- Add: "Consistent hand position on pressure-washer wand handle. Same skin tone and gloves across all frames."
- Use platform with better character consistency (Runway Gen-3, Kling)
- Consider generating in shorter segments and stitching

---

### Issue 4: Water Physics Look Unrealistic

**Problem**: Water jet appears magical, mist is flat, no dirty water runoff

**Solution**:

- Add: "Ultra-realistic water physics. High-pressure water jet visible. Dirty water flows downward due to gravity."
- Specify: "Volumetric mist with light diffusion. Water droplets accumulate on camera lens."
- Reference: "Realistic pressure-washer footage, documentary realism"
- Use platform known for physics (Runway Gen-3, Luma)

---

### Issue 5: Camera Too Shaky or Too Stable

**Problem**: Camera shake is nauseating or video looks tripod-locked

**Solution**:

- For too shaky: "Medium intensity handheld shake, not extreme. Realistic worker movement, not nauseating."
- For too stable: "Energetic handheld movement throughout. Realistic recoil from water pressure. NOT locked-off."
- Specify: "Breathing motion, weight shift, subtle shake during spray, increased shake during fast sweeps"

---

### Issue 6: Lighting Doesn't Progress

**Problem**: Kitchen stays dark throughout or lighting is inconsistent

**Solution**:

- Add: "Lighting progression: Scene 1 dark and grimy → Scene 2 progressively brightens as clean areas expand → Scene 3 bright and illuminated matching @Image1"
- Specify: "Clean surfaces reflect more light. Pendant lights become visible and glowing in Scene 3."
- Describe final lighting: "Natural daylight from windows, pendant lights on, bright and airy"

---

### Issue 7: Scene 3 Pullback Not Smooth

**Problem**: Final cinematic pullback is jerky or doesn't happen

**Solution**:

- Emphasize: "Smooth cinematic pullback in Scene 3 (9-10s). Camera dollies backward steadily showing entire clean kitchen."
- Add: "Handheld shake reduces as worker relaxes. Final frame is wide view, stable and composed."
- If platform struggles, consider generating Scene 3 separately and stitching

---

### Issue 8: Duration Wrong

**Problem**: Video is shorter or longer than 10 seconds

**Solution**:

- Explicitly state: "Total duration: EXACTLY 10 seconds"
- Break down: "Scene 1: 0-2s (2 seconds), Scene 2: 2-7s (5 seconds), Scene 3: 7-10s (3 seconds)"
- If platform limits duration, generate in segments and stitch in post

## Pro Tips & Best Practices

### 1. Reference Image is Critical

Always generate a high-quality **@Image1** first. The cleaner and more detailed your reference, the better the final result.

### 2. Grime Must Peel, Not Fade

Emphasize "physical removal" and "strips" in prompt. Fading looks magical, peeling looks real.

### 3. Test Different Platforms

Each AI video platform handles water physics differently. Test Runway, Pika, and Luma to find best results.

### 4. Dirty Water Adds Realism

Specify "dirty water flows downward" and "brown/black runoff." This grounds the transformation in reality.

### 5. Hands = Believability

Visible hands throughout make POV feel authentic. If hands disappear, regenerate.

### 6. Speed Ramps Create Energy

Use 40% slow-motion for satisfying grime peels, 200% fast for transitions. Dynamic pacing = viral potential.

### 7. Audio is 50% of the Experience

Even if video quality is good, weak audio kills immersion. Layer pressure-washer, water, splashes, and movement sounds.

### 8. Scene 1 Sets Expectations

If Scene 1 isn't dirty enough, transformation isn't impressive. Go heavy on grime for maximum impact.

### 9. Scene 3 Payoff Must Match @Image1 Exactly

Viewers expect perfection. Any remaining dirt or wrong colors break the illusion.

### 10. Vertical Format = Social Media Success

9:16 vertical is non-negotiable for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. Landscape versions get lower engagement.

### 11. Lighting Progression Tells the Story

Dark → Bright transformation reinforces clean = good. Don't skip lighting progression.

### 12. Mist Adds Atmosphere

Volumetric mist makes water spray feel 3D and immersive. Flat mist looks cheap.

### 13. Motion Blur During Fast Sweeps

Natural motion blur adds realism. Don't over-sharpen—embrace slight blur during fast movements.

### 14. Lens Droplets = Immersion

Water droplets on camera lens make viewer feel like they're holding the wand. Don't skip this detail.

### 15. Test Multiple Grime Levels

Heavy grime is most dramatic, but light grime may convert better for real cleaning services (more believable).

### 16. Use Real Kitchen Layouts

Generic kitchens work, but real client kitchens (with permission) make better testimonial content.

### 17. End with Pullback

Smooth cinematic pullback gives viewer satisfaction of seeing the full transformation. Don't end on closeup.

### 18. Consistent Hand Position

If hands shift unnaturally on wand, it breaks POV immersion. Regenerate if hands morph.

### 19. Dirty Kitchen Should Feel Hopeless

Maximum contrast = maximum satisfaction. Make Scene 1 look impossible to clean.

### 20. Clean Kitchen Should Spark Joy

Scene 3 should make viewer think "Wow, I want to cook in that kitchen." Lighting, cleanliness, and warmth matter.

### 21. Avoid Text Overlays During Video

Save text for end card. Overlays during transformation distract from satisfying visual.

### 22. Trending Audio Boosts Reach

On TikTok/Reels, pairing with trending audio increases discoverability. Balance music with water sounds.

### 23. Post Consistently

Pressure-washing content performs well with daily/frequent posting. Create templates for faster production.

### 24. Engage with Comments

Respond to "Do my kitchen next!" comments. Builds community and engagement signals for algorithm.

### 25. Cross-Post Everywhere

Same video works on TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, and Facebook. Maximize reach across platforms.

### 26. Save Failed Generations

Even "failed" videos with interesting glitches can go viral as "AI fails" or behind-the-scenes content.

### 27. Iterate on Successful Videos

If one kitchen transformation goes viral, create variations: bathroom, garage, patio, etc.

### 28. Use Hashtags Strategically

#satisfying #pressurewashing #oddlysatisfying #cleaning #transformation #beforeandafter #powerwashing

### 29. Consider Series Format

"Pressure-Washing Different Rooms" series keeps audience coming back. Tease next room in end card.

### 30. Monitor Performance Metrics

Track which grime levels, rooms, and audio choices get highest engagement. Double down on what works.
