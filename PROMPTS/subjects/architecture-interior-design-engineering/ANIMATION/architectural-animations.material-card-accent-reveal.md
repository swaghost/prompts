# Architectural Accent Reveal - Material Card to Villa

## Description

An ultra-premium 10-second continuous architectural technology commercial in which a miniature modern villa is completed by three material samples: natural oak, glass, and brick. A realistic adult hand selects the oak card, pulls it toward the villa, and the card disintegrates into real wood-derived particles that travel along the architecture before becoming the villa's wood facade, followed by glass and brick accents. The sequence ends with a warm, fully finished interior-exterior hero reveal.

## Usage

Use this sequence for architecture studios, interior-design firms, facade-material manufacturers, real-estate developers, and premium building-material campaigns. It is designed to communicate how a material sample becomes a finished architectural system through a refined, physically grounded transformation.

## Engines/Models

Suitable for Seedance, Kling, Runway, Veo, Sora, or comparable image-to-video tools supporting a storyboard reference and a continuous 16:9 sequence. Use a high-quality miniature villa image or finished architectural render as the visual reference.

## Prerequisites / Dependencies

- **Dependency 1 - Required storyboard reference:** One 8-panel storyboard showing the same miniature villa, hand, three material cards, camera path, and reveal order.
- **Dependency 2 - Required architecture reference:** Finished or near-finished modern villa reference defining the exact proportions, graphite concrete base, glazing, rooflines, and facade geometry.
- **Dependency 3 - Required material reference:** Three clearly separated cards representing natural oak, glass, and brick.
- References must preserve the same villa, materials, hand scale, black studio void, reflective ground, and 16:9 composition.

## Storyboard Requirement

The storyboard is required to coordinate the hand gesture, material-card selection, particle effect, architectural mapping, and final camera move. Create one 8-panel storyboard image with numbered frames in chronological order. Every panel must show the same miniature villa and material cards, with no changes to the architecture or prop inventory. The storyboard is a visual timing and composition map, not a set of separate shots or edit points.

### Required Storyboard Description

**Panel 1 (0:00-0:01):** MATERIAL OBJECTS. A miniature modern villa floats centre-left over a subtly reflective black surface in an infinite pure-black studio void. Three cards stand upright on the right: glass above, natural oak in the middle, brick below. The hand is not yet visible. Cold controlled studio rim lighting outlines the graphite concrete, black metal, and floor-to-ceiling glass.

**Panel 2 (0:01-0:02):** SELECT WOOD. A realistic adult male hand enters slowly from the right. Thumb and index finger approach the middle natural-oak card while the glass and brick cards remain stationary behind it. The camera begins a restrained push toward the materials.

**Panel 3 (0:02-0:03):** GRAB AND DRAG. The hand pulls the oak card away from the other samples and carries it smoothly toward the villa. The camera follows the movement. Focus stays on the wood card and hand; the house remains visible behind in graphite concrete and glass.

**Panel 4 (0:03-0:04):** APPROACH. The oak card moves close to the villa facade. Microscopic wood fibers and tiny fragments begin lifting from the card and drifting toward the building, while the card remains almost completely solid. No explosion or random particle cloud.

**Panel 5 (0:04-0:05.4):** CONTACT / MATERIAL PORTAL. As the card physically touches the facade, it disintegrates into millions of real wood-derived particles: microscopic fibers, fine wood fragments, thin veneer strips, and miniature facade slats. The camera begins a subtle orbit toward the strongest three-quarter hero angle.

**Panel 8 (0:08.1-0:10):** LIFE. The index finger performs one small tap on the black reflective surface beside the villa. After a brief pause, interior illumination activates sequentially: living room, kitchen, upper floor, and exterior architectural lights. The hand slowly retreats into darkness as the camera gently pushes toward the finished villa and holds on the warm hero reveal.

## Effect

### Material Card to Architectural Surface

The core effect is a physical material-transfer portal. A selected material card remains solid until contact with the villa facade, then disintegrates into real material particles that travel along the building and become finished architectural components. The effect must read as material science and precise construction, not magic, smoke, energy, or a generic dissolve.

- **Source:** Natural-oak card held by the hand.
- **Trigger:** Physical contact between the card and the graphite facade.
- **Particles:** Wood fibers, fine fragments, veneer strips, and miniature slats with real wood colour and grain.
- **Path:** Particles follow the facade, roofline, openings, and architectural surfaces.
- **Resolution:** Particles assemble into individually aligned, mechanically plausible wood cladding.
- **Split-state:** Untransformed graphite concrete remains visible beside the new oak surface.
- **Completion:** The last particles become solid facade slats; no particles remain floating.
