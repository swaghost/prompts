# Exploded Isometric Views - Architectural Image Transformations

## Overview

Transform architectural facades into exploded isometric views showing internal organization, floor separation, and building components with realistic materials. Perfect for design presentations, client communication, educational diagrams, and portfolio pieces.

**Part of:** [Architectural Transformations Collection](architectural-transformations-image.md)

---

## Use Cases

- **Design Presentations** - Explain spatial organization visually
- **Client Communication** - Show how building is composed
- **Educational Diagrams** - Teaching building assembly concepts
- **Portfolio Pieces** - Striking architectural graphics
- **Construction Understanding** - Visualize building systems
- **Marketing Materials** - Eye-catching technical illustrations
- **Competition Boards** - Clear diagram communication
- **Publication Graphics** - Professional architectural illustrations

---

## Base Prompt Template

```
Transform this architectural façade into an exploded isometric view, preserving the original massing. Separate the floors and building components to reveal the internal organization, using realistic materials and a professional architectural presentation style.
```

**When to Use:**

- Quick transformation of facade image
- Standard exploded diagram needed
- General architectural presentation
- Time-constrained projects

**Customization:**

- Replace "façade" with your input type (elevation, rendering, photo)
- Adjust separation distance in follow-up prompts
- Add specific material requirements
- Specify background color/treatment

## Technical Specifications

### Projection Type

**Isometric (30°/30°):**

- True isometric angles
- All axes equally foreshortened
- Consistent scale in all directions
- Classic technical illustration look

**Dimetric:**

- Two axes equal, one different
- Slight perspective feel
- More dynamic appearance
- Architectural preference

**Trimetric:**

- All three axes different angles
- Most natural appearance
- Flexible composition
- Complex to construct

**Recommendation:** True isometric or dimetric for architectural work

---

### Separation Distance

**Tight (0.3-0.5m):**

- Subtle suggestion of separation
- Visual coherence maintained
- Elegant presentation
- Form reading priority

**Standard (0.5-1.0m):**

- Clear component separation
- Good balance of clarity and coherence
- Professional presentation standard
- Versatile for most uses

**Wide (1.0-2.0m+):**

- Maximum clarity of components
- Dramatic presentation
- Educational emphasis
- Construction detail visibility

**Recommendation:** 0.75-1.0m for most applications

---

### Material Rendering

**Realistic PBR:**

- Physically based rendering

**Stylized Technical:**

**Mixed Approach:**

## Common Issues and Solutions

### Issue: Original Form Lost

**Problem:** Transformation doesn't preserve building massing  
**Solution:** Emphasize "preserving the original massing" multiple times in prompt. Add "exact proportions maintained" and "original building form."

---

### Issue: Too Much Separation

**Problem:** Components too far apart, loses coherence  
**Solution:** Specify separation distance (e.g., "0.5m vertical offset"). Add "maintain visual coherence" to prompt.

---

### Issue: Wrong Projection

**Problem:** Perspective instead of isometric  
**Solution:** Explicitly state "isometric projection" or "axonometric view, not perspective." Mention 30° angles if needed.

---

### Issue: Materials Too Simplified

**Problem:** Materials look unrealistic or flat  
**Solution:** Add "realistic materials with PBR textures," "photorealistic material rendering," and specify exact materials wanted.

---

### Issue: Poor Background

**Problem:** Distracting or inappropriate background  
**Solution:** Explicitly specify "white background" or "light grey background" or "transparent background with no context."

---

### Issue: Missing Components

**Problem:** Not all building systems shown  
**Solution:** List all components explicitly in prompt: "show structural frame, floor slabs, walls, windows, roof, and mechanical systems."
