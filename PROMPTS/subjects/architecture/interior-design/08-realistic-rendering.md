# PROMPT 08: Realistic Rendering

**Purpose:** Render Realist  
**Workflow Position:** Phase 3 - Presentation & Finalization

---

## Goal

Convert SketchUp model or sketch into photorealistic interior render. Preserve geometry, proportions, furniture positions, and camera angle. Apply realistic materials, natural daylight, warm indirect lighting, and refined details. Architectural photography quality, high realism.

---

## Full Prompt

```
Convert this SketchUp model or sketch into a photorealistic interior render. Preserve
geometry, proportions, furniture positions and camera angle. Apply realistic materials,
natural daylight, warm indirect lighting and refined details. Architectural photography,
high realism.
```

---

## No Variables Required

This prompt works universally with any sketch, 3D model, or basic room visualization. It focuses on enhancing realism while preserving the original design intent.

---

## Preservation Requirements

### Maintain Exactly:

- Geometry and spatial relationships
- Proportions and scale
- Furniture positions
- Camera angle and perspective
- Room layout
- Architectural elements
- Window and door placement

**Critical:** The design itself should not change, only the rendering quality and realism.

---

## Material Application

### Realistic Materials:

- Accurate textures at proper scale
- Correct reflectivity for each material
- Appropriate specularity (shine/matte balance)
- Natural color representation
- Proper displacement/bump mapping
- Realistic wear and age (not perfect)

### Material Types:

- **Wood:** Grain direction follows construction logic, finish type (matte/satin/gloss)
- **Stone:** Veining patterns, surface texture (polished/honed/rough)
- **Fabric:** Weave pattern visible, natural drape and compression
- **Metal:** Finish appropriate (brushed/polished/aged)
- **Glass:** Correct transparency, subtle reflections
- **Paint:** Appropriate sheen level (flat/eggshell/satin/gloss)

---

## Lighting Approach

### Natural Daylight:

- Accurate sun position (time of day, season)
- Sky light contribution (blue bounce from sky)
- Window light directionality
- Natural color temperature (5000K-6500K for daylight)
- Time of day consistency
- Seasonal light quality (summer vs. winter angle)

### Warm Indirect Lighting:

- Artificial light sources (2700K-3000K)
- Light bounce and GI (global illumination)
- Soft shadows (no harsh black shadows)
- Ambient occlusion (subtle darkening in corners)
- Subtle highlights (not blown out)
- No overexposure (balanced exposure)

### Light Characteristics:

- Realistic falloff (light dims with distance)
- Color temperature accuracy
- Shadow softness appropriate to light source
- Caustics where appropriate (light through glass, water)
- Atmospheric effects (dust motes in sunbeams)
- Balanced exposure (no blown-out whites or crushed blacks)

---

## Refined Details

### Architectural Details:

- Baseboard and crown molding (proper scale)
- Door and window trim (reveals, profiles)
- Hardware and fixtures (realistic finish)
- Ceiling details (coffers, beams, texture)
- Floor patterns (wood grain direction, tile layout)
- Wall textures (paint texture, plaster imperfections)

### Furniture Details:

- Joinery and construction (visible joints)
- Cushion compression (weight-bearing sag)
- Fabric drape (natural folds)
- Hardware (knobs, handles, hinges)
- Wear patterns (use in high-touch areas)
- Proper scale (realistic dimensions)

### Styling Details:

- Books and objects (not perfectly aligned)
- Plants (realistic, not CG-perfect leaves)
- Textiles and pillows (natural wrinkles, compression)
- Art and décor (framed properly, realistic scale)
- Surface items (coffee cups, magazines, remotes)
- Natural arrangement (lived-in, not staged perfection)

---

## Quality Standards

### Architectural Photography:

- Professional composition
- Correct perspective (minimal distortion)
- Appropriate depth of field (some bokeh, not excessive)
- Natural color grading (not over-saturated)
- Realistic contrast (not HDR)
- Print-quality resolution

### High Realism:

- Passes as photograph (not obviously CG)
- No CG tells (perfect symmetry, unrealistic materials)
- Natural imperfections (slight asymmetry, wear)
- Believable materials (proper texture scale)
- Authentic lighting (natural shadows and highlights)
- Real-world accuracy (obeys physics)

## Pro Tips

### Input Quality Matters:

- Better input model = better output render
- Clear geometry helps AI understand intent
- Proper camera angle crucial (height, perspective)
- Realistic proportions in input essential

### Lighting is Everything:

- Natural light makes renders believable
- Combine natural and artificial light
- Soft shadows look more realistic
- Avoid pure white or pure black

### Material Realism:

- Imperfections make it believable (slight variations in wood grain, color)
- Not everything is perfectly clean (subtle dust, wear)
- Proper scale for textures (giant wood grain looks fake)
- Mix matte and shiny materials for visual interest

### Styling Adds Life:

- Empty rooms look like CG
- Add books, plants, personal items
- Not perfectly arranged (slight asymmetry)
- Lived-in feel vs. showroom perfection

### Common Mistakes to Avoid:

- **Over-saturation:** Keep colors natural
- **Too perfect:** Add subtle imperfections
- **Wrong scale:** Furniture and materials must be realistic size
- **Harsh lighting:** Soft, diffused light looks more natural
- **Empty scenes:** Add styling and life

---

## Technical Considerations

### Camera Settings:

- **Perspective:** 2-point perspective (not fisheye or extreme wide angle)
- **Height:** Eye level (5-5.5 feet) or slightly below
- **Depth of field:** Subtle bokeh, not excessive blur
- **Composition:** Rule of thirds, balanced frame

### Lighting Setup:

- **Key light:** Primary light source (window, main fixture)
- **Fill light:** Soften shadows (bounce, secondary sources)
- **Accent light:** Highlight features (spotlights, decorative fixtures)
- **Ambient:** Overall base illumination

### Post-Processing:

- Natural color grading (not Instagram filters)
- Slight contrast adjustment (not HDR)
- Subtle vignette if appropriate
- Sharpening for clarity (not over-sharpened)
