# PROMPT 03: Materials & Finishes Testing

**Purpose:** Finish Selector  
**Workflow Position:** Phase 2 - Design Development

---

## Goal

Test combinations with more coherence. Exchange finishes without losing architecture, scale, framing, or original lighting.

---

## Full Prompt

```
Replace only the visible finishes in this room with a sophisticated (STYLE) material
palette. Use (WOOD), (STONE), (FABRIC) and (METAL) in realistic proportions. Preserve
architecture, furniture positions, lighting and camera angle. Create physically accurate
textures, scale, reflections and material transitions. Photorealistic.
```

---

## Variables

- **STYLE:** modern, rustic, industrial, elegant, minimalist, traditional, coastal, etc.
- **WOOD:** oak, walnut, maple, teak, pine, cherry, mahogany, etc.
- **STONE:** marble, granite, limestone, travertine, concrete, slate, quartzite, etc.
- **FABRIC:** linen, cotton, wool, velvet, leather, silk, jute, etc.
- **METAL:** brass, chrome, black steel, copper, stainless steel, bronze, iron, etc.

---

## Key Elements

### Replace Only:

- Visible surface finishes
- Wall materials/colors
- Floor materials
- Ceiling finishes
- Accent materials

### Material Categories:

- **Wood:** Species, grain, finish (matte/gloss)
- **Stone:** Type, cut, surface treatment
- **Fabric:** Texture, weave, color
- **Metal:** Finish (brushed, polished, patina)

### Preserve Exactly:

- Architecture and structure
- Furniture positions
- Lighting (natural and artificial)
- Camera angle and perspective
- Room proportions
- Spatial relationships

### Quality Requirements:

- Physically accurate textures
- Realistic scale (material size appropriate)
- Accurate reflections (material-specific)
- Natural material transitions
- Proper material interaction with light
- Photorealistic rendering

---

## Material Selection Guidelines

### Wood Options:

- **Oak:** Light, medium, dark - versatile grain
- **Walnut:** Rich brown tones - elegant
- **Maple:** Light, subtle grain - clean
- **Teak:** Golden brown, oily - durable
- **Pine:** Knotty, rustic - casual
- **Cherry:** Warm reddish brown - traditional
- **Mahogany:** Deep red-brown - luxurious

### Stone Options:

- **Marble:** Veined, polished - elegant
- **Granite:** Speckled, durable - robust
- **Limestone:** Soft, textured - natural
- **Travertine:** Porous, warm - Mediterranean
- **Concrete:** Modern, industrial - contemporary
- **Slate:** Layered, dark - rustic
- **Quartzite:** Crystalline, strong - modern

### Fabric Options:

- **Linen:** Natural, textured - casual elegance
- **Cotton:** Soft, breathable - versatile
- **Wool:** Warm, substantial - cozy
- **Velvet:** Luxurious, reflective - elegant
- **Leather:** Smooth or distressed - sophisticated
- **Silk:** Shiny, smooth - luxurious
- **Jute:** Natural, rough - casual

### Metal Options:

- **Brushed Brass:** Warm, matte - contemporary
- **Polished Chrome:** Cool, reflective - modern
- **Black Steel:** Modern, matte - industrial
- **Copper:** Warm, develops patina - rustic
- **Stainless Steel:** Clean, modern - minimalist
- **Bronze:** Warm, aged look - traditional
- **Iron:** Dark, substantial - industrial

## When to Use

- Testing different material combinations before committing
- Exploring finish options for client presentations
- Comparing multiple material palettes side-by-side
- Refining the material selection after initial concept
- When materials need to match a specific style
- Before specifying materials for construction documents

---

## Pro Tips

### Material Proportions:

- Don't use all materials equally - vary the amounts
- One material should be dominant (60%)
- Supporting material (30%)
- Accent material (10%)

### Realistic Combinations:

- Consider how materials naturally pair (e.g., wood and leather, stone and metal)
- Avoid too many competing textures
- Balance rough with smooth, matte with shiny

### Testing Variations:

- Run this prompt multiple times with different material combinations
- Compare results side-by-side
- Consider lighting conditions for each material choice
