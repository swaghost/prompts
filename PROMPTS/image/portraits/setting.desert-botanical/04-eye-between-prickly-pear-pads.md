# IMAGE 4 — Eye Hidden Between Prickly Pear Pads

Hyper-realistic intimate botanical portrait showing the same woman's face almost completely hidden behind several enormous natural prickly pear cactus pads.

Composition is extremely tight and unconventional. Three to four dusty sage-green oval cactus pads overlap across the foreground and occupy nearly 80% of the frame. They form a narrow irregular opening through which only one hazel-green eye, eyebrow, part of her freckled cheek and a subtle fragment of her nose and lips can be seen.

The woman has messy short dark hair falling around her eye. Her visible eye looks intensely and directly toward the camera with quiet confidence. No exaggerated expression.

Foreground cactus pads must show authentic matte waxy surface, dusty bloom, scars, tiny dark areoles, scattered fine golden needles and natural discoloration. Needles close to the camera should create believable shallow-focus elements.

Focus priority on the woman's visible eye, catching a natural outdoor reflection in the iris. The nearest cactus surfaces are slightly softer but still texturally recognizable.

Soft directional outdoor daylight with moderate natural contrast; no dramatic artificial lighting. Subdued sage, olive, earth and warm skin palette.

Macro-style portrait photography using approximately 90–100mm lens, extremely shallow depth of field, organic foreground occlusion, documentary intimacy. Real iris detail, wet eye line, individual eyelashes, eyebrow hairs, pores, freckles and subtle skin creases.

Editorial botanical photography inspired by analog fashion magazines, quietly strange and poetic, real camera image rather than digital artwork. Fine natural grain, slight color variation, gentle film softness.

No surreal mutations, no artificial eye color, no smooth skin, no CGI cactus, no glamour makeup, no text, no watermark.
