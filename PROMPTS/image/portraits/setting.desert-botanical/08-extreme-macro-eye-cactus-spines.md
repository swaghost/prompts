# IMAGE 8 — Extreme Macro Eye + Cactus Spines

Extreme macro botanical portrait of the same adult woman's single hazel-green eye framed dramatically beside the edge of a prickly pear cactus.

Only a small section of her face is visible: one eye, eyebrow, forehead texture and freckled upper cheek. Messy dark bangs fall naturally from the upper edge of the frame.

A thick dusty blue-green cactus pad enters diagonally from frame-right and covers almost half the composition. Numerous long pale golden spines extend horizontally and diagonally across the image, some crossing close in front of the eye without making contact. Several foreground spines appear slightly out of focus because they sit extremely close to the lens.

The eye must be tack-sharp: intricate green-grey iris fibers, dark pupil, moist natural catchlight, visible sclera veins, individual upper and lower eyelashes, natural eyelid folds. Surrounding skin contains visible pores, freckles, fine lines, tiny hairs and absolutely no smoothing.

Macro lens approximately 100–120mm, very close focusing distance, razor-thin depth of field. Focus locked precisely on iris and eyelashes while near cactus needles transition naturally between sharp and soft planes.

Soft natural daylight, gentle highlights on the iris and cactus wax surface, balanced neutral exposure. No dramatic studio reflections.

The image should feel tactile, slightly unsettling but beautiful, like high-end experimental botanical fashion photography shot on medium-format macro film.

Natural olive skin, blue-green cactus, golden needles and dark brunette hair. Fine analog grain, subtle muted color response, no digital oversharpening.

No artificial contact lenses, no fake eye glow, no excessive sharpening halos, no skin smoothing, no CGI cactus, no text, no watermark.
