# Foreign Menu Translation Prompt

## Description

Translate restaurant menus in any language to English with detailed ingredient lists, spice level indicators, local specialty markers, and preparation descriptions. Eliminate confusion, identify dietary restrictions, and discover authentic regional dishes with confidence.

## Overview

Dining in foreign countries becomes stress-free when you can understand exactly what you're ordering. This prompt translates menu items and goes beyond simple translation—providing ingredient details, identifying allergens, highlighting local specialties, indicating spice levels, and offering cultural context for an informed and enjoyable dining experience.

## The Prompt

```
This is a menu in another language. Translate it into English, and for each dish tell me what's in it, if it's spicy, and if it's a local specialty.
```

## How to Use

### Step 1: Photograph the Menu

**What to Photograph:**

- Full menu pages with dish listings
- Specific sections (appetizers, mains, desserts)
- Daily specials boards
- Street food vendor signs
- Wine or drink lists
- Prix fixe or tasting menu descriptions

**Photography Tips:**

- Capture full menu page(s) clearly
- Ensure text is readable and in focus
- Include prices if you want cost context
- Photograph in good lighting (use phone flashlight if needed)
- Avoid shadows obscuring text
- Take multiple photos if menu is multi-page

### Step 2: Upload to ChatGPT

- Open ChatGPT mobile app
- Tap photo icon in chat
- Select or capture menu photo
- Verify all text is legible

### Step 3: Send the Prompt

- Copy the prompt exactly as shown
- Paste below your uploaded menu photo
- Hit send
- Wait for translation and analysis

### Step 4: Make Informed Choices

**You'll Receive for Each Dish:**

- English translation of dish name
- List of main ingredients
- Preparation method description
- Spice level indicator
- Local specialty designation
- Flavor profile description
- Common allergens noted

## What You'll Get

### Standard Translation Elements

**1. Dish Name Translation**

- English name with pronunciation hint if helpful
- Original name preserved for ordering
- Alternate names or regional variations
- Literal translation if interesting

**2. Ingredient List**

- Main ingredients clearly listed
- Protein type (meat, fish, vegetarian)
- Vegetables and accompaniments
- Sauces, seasonings, and spices
- Common allergens highlighted

**3. Spice Level**

- Not spicy / Mild / Medium / Hot / Very Hot
- Type of spice (chili, pepper, wasabi, etc.)
- Whether spice can be adjusted
- Regional spice expectations

**4. Local Specialty Marker**

- ⭐ Local specialty: Traditional regional dish
- Popular tourist favorite
- Chef's signature dish
- Seasonal or time-limited offering

**5. Preparation Description**

- Cooking method (grilled, fried, steamed, raw, etc.)
- Texture description (crispy, tender, chewy)
- Temperature (hot, cold, room temperature)
- Presentation style if notable

**6. Flavor Profile**

- Dominant tastes (sweet, savory, sour, umami, bitter)
- Flavor intensity (rich, light, delicate, bold)
- Complementary flavors
- Comparative descriptions ("like [familiar dish]")

## Example Applications

### Example 1: Japanese Menu (Izakaya)

**Original Menu Item:**

```
鶏の唐揚げ
とりのからあげ
¥680
```

**Translation & Analysis:**
**Tori no Karaage** (Chicken Karaage)

- **Ingredients**: Bite-sized chicken thigh pieces, soy sauce marinade, ginger, garlic, potato starch coating
- **Preparation**: Marinated, coated, and deep-fried until golden and crispy
- **Spice Level**: Not spicy (mild garlic and ginger flavor)
- **⭐ Local Specialty**: Popular Japanese pub food, crispy outside and juicy inside
- **Flavor**: Savory, umami-rich, garlicky with slight sweetness from soy
- **Best with**: Cold beer or highball

---

### Example 2: Italian Menu (Trattoria)

**Original Menu Item:**

```
Cacio e Pepe
€14
```

**Translation & Analysis:**
**Cacio e Pepe** (Cheese and Pepper Pasta)

- **Ingredients**: Tonnarelli or spaghetti pasta, Pecorino Romano cheese, black pepper, pasta cooking water
- **Preparation**: Pasta tossed with cheese and pepper to create creamy sauce using starch-rich pasta water
- **Spice Level**: Not spicy (black pepper provides warmth, not heat)
- **⭐ Local Specialty**: Classic Roman dish, deceptively simple but technique-intensive
- **Flavor**: Rich, creamy, peppery, sharp cheese flavor
- **Notes**: Only 4 ingredients but considered test of chef's skill

---

### Example 3: Thai Menu (Street Food)

**Original Menu Item:**

```
ส้มตำไทย
Som Tam Thai
฿60
```

**Translation & Analysis:**
**Som Tam Thai** (Thai Green Papaya Salad)

- **Ingredients**: Shredded unripe papaya, cherry tomatoes, long beans, peanuts, dried shrimp, palm sugar, fish sauce, lime juice, Thai chilies, garlic
- **Preparation**: Pounded in mortar and pestle to bruise ingredients and blend flavors
- **Spice Level**: 🌶️🌶️🌶️ Hot (contains fresh Thai chilies—can request mild)
- **⭐ Local Specialty**: Iconic Thai street food from Isaan region
- **Flavor**: Sweet, sour, salty, spicy all balanced; crunchy texture
- **Allergens**: Contains peanuts, shellfish (dried shrimp), fish sauce
- **Served**: Usually with sticky rice and grilled chicken

---

### Example 4: French Menu (Bistro)

**Original Menu Item:**

```
Confit de Canard
avec pommes sarladaises
€22
```

**Translation & Analysis:**
**Confit de Canard** (Duck Confit)

- **Ingredients**: Duck leg slow-cooked in its own fat, garlic, herbs de Provence, served with Sarladaise potatoes (potatoes cooked in duck fat with garlic and parsley)
- **Preparation**: Duck salt-cured, then slow-cooked in fat for 2-3 hours until tender, crisped before serving
- **Spice Level**: Not spicy (herbaceous and savory)
- **⭐ Local Specialty**: Traditional southwestern French preservation technique, now delicacy
- **Flavor**: Rich, deeply savory, tender meat with crispy skin
- **Texture**: Fork-tender duck, crispy potatoes
- **Best with**: Full-bodied red wine from Southwest France

## Advanced Techniques

### Comparative Menu Analysis

**Technique:** Compare similar dishes across menu
**Prompt:**

```
This menu has three pasta dishes. Translate all three, compare their ingredients and flavors, tell me which is spiciest, which is most authentic to this region, and which would appeal to someone who likes creamy sauces.
```

### Complete Meal Planning

**Technique:** Build full course progression
**Prompt:**

```
Translate this menu and help me plan a traditional 3-course meal. Suggest an appetizer, main, and dessert that represent authentic local cuisine, flow well together, and tell me what's in each dish.
```

### Dietary Navigation

**Technique:** Filter by requirements
**Prompt:**

```
Translate this menu and identify all dishes that are: 1) vegetarian-friendly or easily modifiable, 2) not spicy, 3) gluten-free or can be made without gluten. Provide alternatives where possible.
```

### Cultural Deep Dive

**Technique:** Learn food history and context
**Prompt:**

```
Translate this menu's local specialty section and for each dish explain: traditional eating occasion, historical origin, why it's significant to this region, and proper way to eat it.
```

### Budget-Conscious Ordering

**Technique:** Optimize value and portions
**Prompt:**

```
Translate this menu with prices and tell me which dishes: 1) offer best value, 2) are large enough to share, 3) are must-try local specialties regardless of price. Suggest a meal plan for two people on moderate budget.
```

## Tips for Best Results

### Photography Best Practices

- **Full pages**: Capture entire menu page rather than sections
- **Multiple shots**: Photograph all pages of multi-page menus
- **Specials boards**: Don't miss handwritten daily specials
- **Good lighting**: Use phone flashlight if restaurant is dim
- **Flat surface**: Lay menu flat to avoid text distortion
- **Include headers**: Capture section titles (appetizers, mains, etc.)

### Prompt Enhancement

**Add context:**

- "This is a restaurant in [city/region/country]"
- "This appears to be [type of cuisine]"
- "The waiter mentioned some dishes are family recipes"

**Specify needs:**

- "I'm vegetarian but eat fish"
- "I have a severe peanut allergy"
- "I don't eat pork for religious reasons"
- "I'm looking for authentic traditional dishes"
- "I want to try something unusual but not too adventurous"

**Request specific information:**

- "Tell me which dishes are meant for sharing"
- "Indicate which are typically lunch vs. dinner items"
- "Let me know which dishes take longest to prepare"
- "Suggest wine pairings for each main course"

### Follow-Up Questions

After receiving translation:

- "Tell me more about [specific dish that interests you]"
- "What's the difference between [dish A] and [dish B]?"
- "Which of these would be most impressive to serve guests?"
- "If I can only try one thing, what should it be?"
- "Are any of these dishes considered breakfast/lunch/dinner specific?"
- "How do locals typically eat this—with hands, fork, bread?"

## Technical Notes

### Translation Accuracy

- **Common languages**: Very high accuracy (French, Spanish, Italian, German, Japanese, Chinese, Thai, etc.)
- **Less common languages**: Good accuracy, may need menu context
- **Handwritten menus**: May require clearer photo or interpretation
- **Regional dialects**: Usually identifies regional variations
- **Specialty terms**: Provides both literal and culinary translations

### Response Time

- **Short menus (5-10 items)**: 20-40 seconds
- **Full menus (20-30 items)**: 1-2 minutes
- **Multi-page menus**: May need to process page by page
- **Follow-up questions**: 5-15 seconds

### Dietary Information Reliability

- **Ingredient lists**: Based on typical preparations—always confirm with restaurant for allergies
- **Allergen warnings**: Comprehensive but verify critical allergies directly
- **Spice levels**: Relative to cuisine type—"mild" Thai may be spicier than "medium" Italian
- **Local specialty designation**: Based on traditional regional cuisine knowledge

## Cultural Dining Etiquette Tips

### Ordering Considerations

- **Portion sizes**: Ask about typical portions (some cultures serve small plates, others large)
- **Sharing culture**: Many cuisines (Chinese, Spanish tapas, Middle Eastern mezze) expect communal eating
- **Course progression**: Some dishes meant as appetizers, others as mains
- **Traditional combinations**: Certain dishes pair with specific sides or accompaniments

### Restaurant Customs

- **Tipping practices**: Varies by country (already included in price, expected percentage, round up, etc.)
- **Pacing**: Some cultures take meals slowly over hours, others eat quickly
- **Bread/rice/sides**: Sometimes free, sometimes charged, sometimes unlimited
- **Water**: May need to specify tap vs. bottled in some countries

## Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Translation seems generic
**Solution:** Add location context

```
This is a menu from a restaurant in [specific city/region]. Translate and provide context about which dishes are specifically local to this area.
```

**Issue:** Uncertain about spice level
**Solution:** Request relative comparison

```
Translate and rank all dishes from mildest to spiciest. Compare the spice levels to familiar foods (like "milder than jalapeño pepper" or "hotter than black pepper").
```

**Issue:** Need more detail on preparation
**Solution:** Request cooking method focus

```
Translate this menu and emphasize cooking methods. Tell me which are fried, grilled, steamed, raw, etc., and describe textures I can expect.
```

**Issue:** Menu has unfamiliar ingredients
**Solution:** Request ingredient explanation

```
Translate this menu and explain any unusual or regional ingredients I might not recognize. Describe what they taste like or compare to familiar ingredients.
```

**Issue:** Prices seem high/low—need value assessment
**Solution:** Request value analysis

```
Translate with prices and tell me which dishes offer good value, which are premium items, and what the typical cost range means for this type of restaurant.
```

## Allergen Quick Reference

### Common Allergens to Specify

When requesting translations, mention your specific concerns:

- **Nuts**: Tree nuts (almonds, walnuts, cashews, etc.) and peanuts
- **Shellfish**: Shrimp, crab, lobster, mussels, clams, oysters
- **Fish**: Including fish sauce (common in Asian cuisine)
- **Dairy**: Milk, cheese, butter, cream, yogurt
- **Eggs**: Including in pasta, sauces, and batters
- **Gluten**: Wheat, barley, rye (in bread, pasta, sauces, beer)
- **Soy**: Soy sauce, tofu, edamame
- **Sesame**: Seeds and oil (common in Asian and Middle Eastern food)

### Hidden Allergen Sources

Be aware these may contain allergens:

- **Sauces and dressings**: May contain fish sauce, soy, nuts
- **Fried foods**: May share oil with shellfish
- **Pasta**: Often contains eggs
- **Broths and stocks**: May contain shellfish or fish
- **Pestos and spreads**: Often contain nuts and dairy
- **Breading and crusts**: Contains gluten and sometimes eggs

**Always confirm with restaurant staff for serious allergies—AI translation is helpful but not a substitute for direct verification.**
