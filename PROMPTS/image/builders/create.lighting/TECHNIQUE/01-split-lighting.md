# 01 — Split Lighting

**Purpose:** Apply dramatic side lighting that illuminates one half of the face while the other falls into shadow  
**Use Case:** Editorial portraits, character photography, dramatic mood, mystery  
**Platform:** GPT Image 2, Picsart Flow (image-to-image)

## What This Does

Transforms an existing portrait by placing a single directional key light approximately 90 degrees to one side of the subject at face level. One side of the face becomes illuminated while the opposite side falls into deep shadow, creating strong dramatic contrast.

This is one of the most dramatic lighting patterns in portrait photography, emphasizing facial structure and creating mystery or intensity.

## Full Relighting Prompt

```
Relight the uploaded portrait using authentic photographic split lighting while preserving the original subject, identity, facial features, expression, pose, hairstyle, clothing, jewelry, composition, background, and image proportions.

Place a single directional key light approximately 90 degrees to one side of the subject at face level. Illuminate one side of the face while allowing the opposite side to fall naturally into deep shadow.

The transition between light and shadow must follow the real three-dimensional contours of the forehead, brow ridge, bridge of the nose, lips, cheekbones, chin, neck, shoulders, and collarbones. Do not create a straight vertical division, graphic mask, artificial line, or symmetrical half-and-half effect.

Keep the illuminated eye clearly visible with a subtle catchlight. The shadow-side eye may remain darker but should retain faint natural detail. Use strong cinematic contrast, minimal ambient fill, realistic skin texture, controlled highlights, and smooth natural light falloff.

The result should look like the original photograph was captured with one professional side light positioned directly beside the model.

Do not alter the subject's face, anatomy, pose, wardrobe, jewelry, background, framing, or camera perspective. No vertical light strip, no hard digital masking, no artificial symmetry, no visible light source, no haze, no glossy skin, and no beauty retouching.
```

## Key Technical Elements

- **Light position:** 90° to one side at face level (true side lighting)
- **Shadow character:** Deep, controlled, following natural facial contours
- **Contrast:** Strong cinematic separation between light/shadow
- **Falloff:** Smooth and natural, not abrupt graphic division
- **Catchlight:** Visible in illuminated eye, minimal or absent in shadow eye
- **Fill:** Minimal ambient to retain drama while keeping shadow detail

## What Gets Preserved

✓ Subject identity and facial features  
✓ Expression and emotional quality  
✓ Pose and body position  
✓ Hairstyle and texture  
✓ Clothing and jewelry  
✓ Composition and framing  
✓ Background elements  
✓ Camera angle and perspective  
✓ Skin texture and natural detail

## What Changes

- Light direction (becomes side light)
- Shadow placement (one side dark)
- Contrast level (increases dramatically)
- Highlight areas (concentrated on lit side)
- Overall mood (becomes dramatic/mysterious)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **"Make it symmetrical"** — Split lighting is intentionally asymmetric; half-and-half graphic division is wrong  
❌ **Applying to profile shots** — Works best on front-facing or slight three-quarter; pure profile has no "other side"  
❌ **Over-filling shadows** — Drama comes from deep shadow; too much fill makes it broad lighting instead  
❌ **Adding color** — Prompt produces neutral split; don't add "orange glow" or similar unless intentional

## When to Use Split Lighting

✅ **Use split lighting when:**

- Creating dramatic editorial portraits
- Emphasizing facial structure and bone definition
- Evoking mystery, intensity, or duality
- Masculine or character-driven portraits
- Film noir or dramatic cinematic aesthetic

❌ **Avoid split lighting when:**

- Subject needs to look approachable or friendly (too dramatic)
- Commercial beauty work (unflattering on some face shapes)
- Group portraits (inconsistent on multiple faces)
- Client wants "safe" traditional headshot

## Variations to Try

After generating split-lit result, you can experiment with:

- **Reversed side:** Upload result, relight again to flip which side is illuminated
- **Warmer tone:** Add "warm tungsten key light" to prompt for golden drama
- **Harder edge:** Add "hard directional light" for crisper shadow transition
- **Softer edge:** Add "slightly diffused key" for gentler split

## Integration Notes

Split-lit portraits work well as:

- Character reference boards for film/video (@char_villain, @char_antihero)
- Thumbnail/poster images (high contrast reads well at small size)
- Before/after demonstrations of lighting technique
- Editorial portfolio pieces
