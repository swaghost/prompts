# 04 — Rim Lighting

**Purpose:** Apply backlight that traces the edges of the subject, separating them from the background  
**Use Case:** Music videos, fashion, cinematic depth, subject separation, dimensional edge highlights  
**Platform:** GPT Image 2, Picsart Flow (image-to-image)

## What This Does

Transforms an existing portrait by placing one or two controlled backlights slightly behind the subject and outside the frame, aimed toward the back and sides of the head and shoulders. This creates a narrow natural highlight tracing selected outer edges — typically hair, jawline, neck, and shoulders — clearly separating the subject from the background.

Rim lighting adds depth, dimension, and cinematic polish without fundamentally changing the frontal lighting on the face.

## Full Relighting Prompt

```
Relight the uploaded portrait using authentic photographic rim lighting while preserving the original subject, facial identity, expression, pose, hairstyle, clothing, jewelry, camera angle, composition, background, and image proportions.

Place one or two controlled backlights slightly behind the subject and outside the frame, aimed toward the back and sides of the head and shoulders. Create a narrow, natural highlight tracing selected outer edges of the hair, jawline, neck, shoulders, and clothing, clearly separating the subject from the background.

Keep the rim light brightest along the edges facing the rear light source, with realistic variation caused by hair density, facial structure, fabric, and body contours. The light should wrap subtly around the silhouette without illuminating the entire front of the subject. Maintain restrained frontal fill so facial features remain readable while the rim light stays visually dominant.

Use realistic light falloff, controlled highlights, natural skin and hair texture, cinematic contrast, and clean photographic depth. The rim should appear optically produced by real studio lights, not drawn around the subject.

Do not create a uniform glowing outline, neon border, halo, graphic stroke, artificial cutout effect, or light around every edge. Do not alter the subject's identity, anatomy, pose, wardrobe, jewelry, background, framing, or perspective. No visible light sources, excessive haze, coloured glow, glossy artificial skin, or beauty retouching.
```

## Key Technical Elements

- **Light position:** Behind subject, outside frame, aimed at rear edges
- **Highlight placement:** Hair, jawline, neck, shoulders, clothing edges
- **Character:** Narrow traced edge, not uniform glow
- **Variation:** Realistic intensity changes based on surface (hair vs. skin vs. fabric)
- **Wrap:** Subtle around silhouette, not flooding the front
- **Frontal fill:** Maintained so face stays readable
- **Realism:** Optical backlight effect, not graphic outline

✓ Camera angle and perspective

## What Changes

- Edge highlights added along silhouette
- Subject-background separation increased
- Depth and dimension enhanced
- Hair edges become more defined and luminous
- Overall mood becomes more cinematic/polished

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **"Make it glow all around"** — Rim light is selective, not uniform halo  
❌ **Expecting every edge lit** — Only edges facing backlight get highlighted  
❌ **Confusing with silhouette** — Rim adds edge accent while keeping face lit; silhouette makes entire subject dark  
❌ **Adding neon colors** — Prompt uses neutral photographic rim; color is separate choice

## When to Use Rim Lighting

✅ **Use rim lighting when:**

- Adding cinematic polish to portraits
- Separating subject from busy or dark backgrounds
- Creating depth and three-dimensionality
- Music video aesthetic or editorial fashion
- Enhancing hair texture and edge definition

❌ **Avoid rim lighting when:**

- Subject is against very bright background (rim won't read)
- Flat graphic look is intentional (rim adds dimension)
- Natural documentary style preferred (too stylized)
- Hair is very short or absent (less surface for rim to trace)

## Single vs. Double Rim

**Single rim (one backlight):**

- Highlights one side of subject (left or right)
- More naturalistic, less symmetrical
- Works well with existing directional key light

**Double rim (two backlights):**

- Highlights both sides of subject
- More stylized and cinematic
- Creates symmetrical edge separation
- Common in music videos and high-end fashion

The prompt allows either; specify "one backlight" or "two backlights" if you need control.

## Variations to Try

After generating rim-lit result, you can experiment with:

- **Colored rim:** Add "warm golden rim light" or "cool blue rim light" for genre/mood
- **Stronger rim:** Add "bright rim light" for more pronounced edge separation
- **Subtler rim:** Add "gentle rim light" for delicate edge accent
- **Hair-only rim:** Add "rim light concentrated on hair edges" to emphasize hair specifically

## Technical Background

Rim lighting (also called edge lighting, kicker, or hair light depending on context) became popular in Hollywood during the Golden Age of cinema to:

- Separate actors from painted backdrops
- Add glamour and dimension to black-and-white film
- Define hair and clothing edges in low-contrast scenes
- Create visual hierarchy (star gets rim, extras don't)

In modern photography/video, rim lighting serves the same purposes and signals "professional production value."
