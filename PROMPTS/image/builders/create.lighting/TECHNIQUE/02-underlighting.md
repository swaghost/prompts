# 02 — Underlighting

**Purpose:** Apply dramatic upward lighting from below the subject's face  
**Use Case:** Horror, villain shots, theatrical drama, unsettling mood  
**Platform:** GPT Image 2, Picsart Flow (image-to-image)

## What This Does

Transforms an existing portrait by placing a controlled directional key light below the subject's face, angled upward from beneath chin level. This creates the opposite of natural lighting (which typically comes from above), resulting in upward-cast shadows that appear theatrical, eerie, or cinematic.

This is one of the most unnatural lighting patterns, deliberately creating an unsettling or dramatic effect.

## Full Relighting Prompt

```
Relight the uploaded portrait using authentic photographic underlighting while preserving the original subject, facial identity, expression, pose, hairstyle, clothing, jewelry, camera angle, composition, background, and image proportions.

Position one controlled directional key light below the subject's face, angled upward from beneath chin level. Illuminate the underside of the chin, jawline, lips, nose, cheekbones, and brow ridge while allowing natural shadows to form upward across the face. Create a dramatic, cinematic result with clear upward light direction and realistic three-dimensional facial modelling.

The light must follow the subject's actual anatomy. Shadows should form naturally above the nose, beneath the brows, around the eye sockets, and along the upper cheek structure. Maintain visible facial detail, realistic skin texture, restrained highlights, controlled contrast, and subtle ambient fill so the face remains readable.

Do not create a glowing face, flat upward wash, artificial shadow mask, fantasy lighting, or exaggerated horror effect. Do not alter the subject's identity, facial structure, anatomy, pose, wardrobe, jewelry, background, framing, or lens perspective. No visible light source, no coloured glow, no haze, no glossy artificial skin, and no beauty retouching. The final result should look like the original photograph was professionally captured with a single light placed below the subject.
```

## Key Technical Elements

- **Light position:** Below chin level, angled upward at face
- **Shadow character:** Upward-casting, reversing natural light direction
- **Shadow placement:** Above nose, beneath brows, upper cheeks, eye sockets
- **Contrast:** Dramatic but controlled, face remains readable
- **Fill:** Subtle ambient to prevent complete blackout above
- **Realism:** Photographic uplighting, not fantasy glow or horror exaggeration

## What Gets Preserved

✓ Subject identity and facial structure  
✓ Expression and emotional state  
✓ Pose and head position  
✓ Hairstyle and hair texture  
✓ Clothing, jewelry, accessories  
✓ Camera angle and framing  
✓ Background and environment  
✓ Composition and proportions  
✓ Natural skin texture and detail

## What Changes

- Light direction (becomes upward from below)
- Shadow direction (reverses to upward-casting)
- Facial modeling (underside lit, top shadowed)
- Mood (becomes theatrical/unsettling)
- Highlight areas (chin, underside of nose, lower face)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **"Make it horror movie"** — Prompt produces controlled underlighting; extreme horror needs separate approach  
❌ **Expecting green/blue glow** — Prompt uses neutral photographic light; color comes from separate instruction  
❌ **Applying to downward-looking poses** — Works best on level or upward gaze; downcast eyes hide the effect  
❌ **Over-shadowing** — Face should remain readable, not lost in darkness

## When to Use Underlighting

✅ **Use underlighting when:**

- Creating villain or antagonist character portraits
- Evoking unsettling, mysterious, or theatrical mood
- Cinematic dramatic effect for storytelling
- Experimenting with non-traditional lighting
- Music video or editorial with edgy aesthetic

❌ **Avoid underlighting when:**

- Client wants flattering beauty portrait (extremely unflattering on most faces)
- Professional headshot or corporate use (too unconventional)
- Approachable or friendly branding (creates distance/unease)
- Natural documentary style (too stylized)

## Variations to Try

After generating underlit result, you can experiment with:

- **Colored glow:** Add "warm orange uplighting" or "cool blue uplighting" for genre effect
- **Harder light:** Add "hard directional uplight" for more dramatic shadow edges
- **Stronger fill:** Add "balanced ambient fill" to soften the dramatic effect
- **Campfire simulation:** Add "warm flickering uplight as if from firelight below"

## Integration Notes

Underlit portraits work well as:

- Villain/antagonist character references for film (@char_villain, @char_threat)
- Horror or thriller genre visual development
- Album covers or music video stills (rock, metal, dark genres)

## Cultural/Genre Context

Underlighting has strong associations:

- **Classic horror films:** Monster reveal, Frankenstein's monster, vampire
- **Film noir:** Interrogation scenes, shadowy figures
- **Theatre:** Footlight era, dramatic stage lighting
- **Contemporary:** Edgy fashion, music videos, villain archetype

Understanding these associations helps choose when this lighting serves the story.
