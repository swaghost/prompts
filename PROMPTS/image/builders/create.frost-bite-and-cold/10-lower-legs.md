# Lower Legs After Prolonged Cold Exposure

## Classification

- **Body area:** Both lower legs and fine leg hair
- **Severity/stage:** Prolonged cold exposure with pronounced visible skin changes
- **Visual mode:** Ultra-photorealistic RAW close-up

## Description of Resulting Image

A close view of lower legs showing uneven pale yellow-white, red, and purple coloration, rigid dry skin with micro-cracks, and fine hairs frosted white under clear cold daylight.

## Usage

For medical education, survival documentary work, and cold-exposure training or visual reference. Graphic-content warning: depicts pronounced cold-injury discoloration and damaged skin texture.

## Clinical Visual Indicators

- Pale yellow-white regions
- Surrounding red and purple inflamed-looking areas
- Rigid, dry surface with micro-cracks
- Fine leg hairs visibly frosted white

These depicted signs are visual references only and do not establish a diagnosis or blood-flow status.

## Technical Direction

Use a RAW close-up that includes both lower legs with enough context to read their anatomy. Keep discoloration, micro-cracks, and frosted hairs in sharp focus with zero blur. Use cold daylight, natural texture, restrained contrast, and a neutral documentary grade.

## Image Prompt

```text
Ultra-photorealistic RAW close-up of lower legs affected by prolonged cold exposure. Skin shows uneven coloration: pale yellow-white regions where blood flow is compromised, surrounded by red and purple inflamed areas. Texture appears rigid and dry, with micro-cracks. Fine hairs frosted white. Cold daylight, zero blur.
```

## Realism and Anatomy Lock

Show exactly two anatomically coherent lower legs with natural tibial, calf, ankle, and skin contours. Keep hair direction and density plausible. Ensure micro-cracks, discoloration, and frost follow the cylindrical surfaces and remain continuous with the skin rather than appearing as separate overlays.

## Negative Prompt

No beautification, airbrushed legs, CGI, illustration, extra or fused limbs, broken knees or ankles, duplicated texture, floating hair or frost, exposed anatomy, irrelevant gore, decorative ice, blur, warm glamour lighting, logos, captions, text, or watermark.

## Safety and Interpretation Note

This is a fictional image prompt for educational and documentary reference, not diagnosis or treatment guidance. Real frostbite is a medical emergency requiring prompt professional care. Do not present a generated image as clinical evidence.
