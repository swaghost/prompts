# Frost and Ice Crystals on Exposed Skin

## Classification

- **Body area:** Unspecified exposed skin with pores and fine hair
- **Severity/stage:** Visible surface frost on cracked cold-exposed skin
- **Visual mode:** Ultra-photorealistic RAW documentary macro

## Description of Resulting Image

An extreme surface study of elderly skin in which fine frost and ice structures visibly cling to pores, hair, and cracked skin, rendered with uncompromising texture in cold natural daylight.

## Usage

For medical education, survival documentary work, and cold-exposure training or visual reference. Graphic-content warning: depicts cracked cold-exposed skin at forensic macro detail.

## Clinical Visual Indicators

- Fine ice structures attached to the skin surface
- Ice collecting around pores and fine hairs
- Cracked skin texture
- Sharply resolved frost-to-skin contact

These are visual observations from the prompt, not diagnostic findings.

## Technical Direction

Use a RAW documentary macro with a narrow, deliberate plane of focus that still resolves the key pores, hairs, cracks, and ice structures. Employ cold natural daylight, true-to-scale texture, restrained neutral color, and no artistic effects or cosmetic finish.

## Image Prompt

```text
Ultra-photorealistic RAW macro documentary photograph of frost and ice crystals forming on the exposed skin of an elderly man. Fine ice structures cling to pores, hair, and cracked skin surfaces. Texture is brutally sharp and realistic. Lighting is cold, natural daylight, no artistic effects.
```

## Realism and Anatomy Lock

Keep the image recognizably human skin at macro scale, with plausible pore spacing, fine-hair emergence, wrinkle direction, and crack depth. Ice must rest on or bridge real surface features with coherent contact, translucency, scale, and cold-light reflection rather than appearing embedded without support.

## Negative Prompt

No beautification, pore removal, cosmetic smoothing, CGI, illustration, crystalline fantasy patterns, oversized snowflakes, floating ice, mineral or reptile texture, unrelated wounds or gore, excessive depth blur, warm studio grade, logos, captions, text, or watermark.

## Safety and Interpretation Note

This is a fictional image prompt for educational and documentary reference, not diagnosis or treatment guidance. Real frostbite is a medical emergency requiring prompt professional care. Do not present a generated image as clinical evidence.
