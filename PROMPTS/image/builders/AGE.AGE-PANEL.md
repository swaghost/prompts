# Age Progression Contact Sheet

## Description of Resulting Image

A photorealistic age-progression contact sheet showing the same person at twelve ages from 5 to 80 in a clean 3x4 studio grid. Each panel is labelled by age, with the same face identity, grey backdrop, t-shirt, lighting, framing, and neutral expression preserved while only the visible ageing changes.

## Usage

Perfect for lifespan lookbooks, identity-consistent portrait studies, age-progression concepts, character development sheets, social media transformations, and visual storytelling around time, memory, family, or personal history.

Ideal for image-to-image workflows using one clear front-facing reference photo, where the goal is to preserve facial architecture while generating child, adult, and elder versions of the same person across a single comparison sheet.

## Engines/Models

Originally intended for GPT Image 2 and Picsart Flow image-to-image workflows. Attach one clear, front-facing source portrait; the reference supplies facial architecture and colouring, while the panel label determines the age shown in each panel.

## Actor or Subject

One real person from a reference portrait, shown at twelve ages: 5, 8, 12, 16, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, and 80. Includes separate tuned versions for male and female subjects.

## Prerequisites

- Dependency #1 - One sharp, neutral, front-facing source portrait with an unobstructed face.
- Dependency #2 - Choose the men's version for a male subject with facial-hair ageing rules.
- Dependency #3 - Choose the women's version for a female subject with no facial hair, no makeup, and no cosmetic-surgery look.

## Image Prompt

AGE PROGRESSION - 5 TO 80

GPT Image 2 / Picsart Flow image-to-image prompt.

One face at twelve ages on a single sheet, with men's and women's versions.

Attach one clear, front-facing reference photo. Use it only for the underlying facial architecture and colouring. The age of each panel is set by the panel label, not by the apparent age of the photo.

The identity rule holds the bone: eye shape and spacing, nose bridge, ears, skull width, brow, natural facial asymmetry, eye colour, skin tone, and natural hair colour carry unchanged through all twelve panels. The ageing rule changes the soft tissue: skin, fat, hair, lines, volume, texture, and age-specific facial details. Every panel is locked to the same framing, same grey backdrop, same soft key light, same neutral expression, and same plain t-shirt so the only visible variable is ageing.

The 12 ages are 5, 8, 12, 16, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, and 80.

Age guide:

- Ages 5, 8, 12: round cheeks, soft nose, smooth skin, undeveloped jaw.
- Age 16: face lengthens, jaw begins to square, brow sets; men's version includes first patchy stubble.
- Ages 20, 25: adult jaw and nose, taut clear skin, visible pores.
- Ages 30, 40: fine eye and forehead lines, set nasolabial folds, first grey.
- Ages 50, 60: cheek volume drops, jaw softens, lids hood, grey spreads.
- Ages 70, 80: thin creased skin, age spots, hollow cheeks, white thinning hair.

MEN'S AGE PROGRESSION - PROMPT

Purpose: age-progression contact sheet - one person shown at twelve ages, for a lifespan lookbook. Output: 4:5 vertical, 1152x1440. Quality: high.

Image 1: source portrait - the identity reference. Take from it the subject's underlying facial architecture and colouring. Everything about his age is set by the panel, not by Image 1.

Scene: one flat uniform light grey seamless studio backdrop behind every panel - the same grey value across the whole sheet, evenly lit, smooth and empty.

Layout: a single sheet divided into a 3-column by 4-row grid of twelve equal panels that butt directly against each other with no gutters and no drawn borders. Row 1, left to right: ages 5, 8, 12. Row 2, left to right: ages 16, 20, 25. Row 3, left to right: ages 30, 40, 50. Row 4, left to right: ages 60, 70, 80.

Framing rule, absolute: identical framing in all twelve panels - head-and-shoulders portrait, the subject centred, same camera distance, same head height in frame, same head angle and same eyeline, so the twelve faces line up for direct comparison. Within that framing keep true proportions for each age: a young child's head sits larger relative to the shoulders, an adult's smaller.

Identity rule, absolute: the subject is one real man across his whole life. These traits carry unchanged through all twelve panels and are what make him recognisable: the shape and spacing of the eyes, the bridge and tip of the nose, the philtrum, the shape of the ears, the width of the skull, the set of the brow ridge, the natural asymmetry of his face, and his eye colour, skin tone and natural hair colour from Image 1. Same calm neutral expression, lips closed, looking straight into the lens, in every panel.

Ageing rule, absolute: age is expressed in the soft tissue, not in the bone. Render each age honestly and specifically:

- Ages 5 to 12: round full cheeks, a short soft nose, smooth fine skin, a high smooth forehead, features still unformed, the jaw narrow and undeveloped, clean-shaven with fine downy skin.
- Age 16: the face lengthens, the jaw begins to square, the brow ridge sets, sparse patchy adolescent stubble along the upper lip and chin only.
- Ages 20 to 25: adult jaw and nose, taut clear skin with visible pores, even short stubble across the beard area.
- Ages 30 to 40: fine lines at the outer eye corners and across the forehead, the nasolabial folds become permanent, the temples begin to recede slightly, stubble denser with the first grey hairs in it.
- Ages 50 to 60: cheek volume drops, the jawline softens, the upper eyelids begin to hood, marionette lines appear, skin texture coarsens, the hairline recedes further and grey spreads from the temples through the hair and the stubble.
- Ages 70 to 80: skin is thin, dry and finely creased all over, with age spots and broken capillaries, the cheeks hollow, the eyelids hood fully, the nose and ears are larger, the eyebrows coarse and unruly, the hair white, thin and receded, the stubble white.

Constant rule, applies to all twelve panels:

- The same plain mid-grey crew-neck cotton t-shirt, sized naturally to the body.
- No jewellery, no glasses, no watch, no accessories of any kind.
- The same haircut and parting as in Image 1 in the adult panels, and a natural child's version of it in the first three. Length and thickness follow the age; the hairline and colour follow the ageing rule above.
- Facial hair follows the ageing rule and nothing else - never a full grown beard, never a moustache, never a styled shape.

Lighting rule, absolute: one soft key with subtle fill, identical direction, identical exposure, identical white balance and identical colour grade in all twelve panels. Everything in frame is in sharp focus.

Labels: a small plain sans-serif label in the top-left corner of each panel, dark grey, reading exactly "AGE 5" "AGE 8" "AGE 12" "AGE 16" "AGE 20" "AGE 25" "AGE 30" "AGE 40" "AGE 50" "AGE 60" "AGE 70" "AGE 80" in the row order above.

Photorealistic studio portrait, 85mm look, natural skin rendering with visible pores, fine lines and individual hair strands.

Constraints: plain light grey backdrop only, no room, no furniture, no props, no gradient, no texture, no vignette; no retouching, no skin smoothing, no face slimming, no beautification; no de-ageing in the later panels, no adult features or facial hair in the child panels; no full beard, no moustache; no period costume, no era styling, no hats; no jewellery, no glasses; no panel borders, no dividing lines, no extra captions, no watermark, no logo; no illustration, no painterly effect, no CGI look.
