# Holding a Sleeve or Wrist

## Description

An image-to-image pose adjustment that creates a small self-adjusting action by letting one hand touch either the opposite wrist or its sleeve cuff. It preserves identity and wardrobe while coordinating grip choice, fingertip placement, wrist alignment, fabric response, and relaxed shoulders.

## Best For

Fashion portraits, quiet self-adjusting moments, modest body language, casual street imagery, and contemplative poses.

## Actor or Subject

One person with both forearms and at least one sleeve cuff or wrist clearly visible. Medium, three-quarter, and full-body references work best when the active hand and contact area are unobstructed.

## Prerequisites

- One uploaded identity and wardrobe reference image
- Both wrists visible or physically inferable
- Clear sleeve construction if using the sleeve-cuff variant
- Enough frame space to retain both elbows and the contact point

## Preservation Contract

- Preserve the exact face, apparent age, skin tone, hairstyle, body proportions, wardrobe, sleeve design, accessories, background, lighting, color treatment, camera angle, and aspect ratio.
- Choose one unambiguous action: touch the opposite wrist or hold the sleeve cuff.
- Change only the hands and forearms plus minimal shoulder and weight-shift adjustments.

## Anatomy Requirements

- Two anatomically correct hands and wrists with clear ownership
- Soft grip with natural thumb opposition and finger spacing
- Wrist bones align plausibly with each forearm
- Sleeve contact produces a small fold and localized compression
- Fingers never pass through fabric or encircle the wrist impossibly

## Prompt

```text
Use the uploaded image as the exact identity and wardrobe reference. Preserve the same face, age, hairstyle, body proportions, outfit, accessories, lighting, setting, and color treatment.

Create a natural self-adjusting gesture with one hand gently touching or loosely holding the opposite wrist or sleeve cuff. Keep the grip soft, fingers relaxed with realistic spacing, thumb placed naturally, and wrist alignment anatomically correct. Add slight fabric compression and a small fold where the sleeve is touched. Relax the shoulders and use a subtle body-weight shift so the action feels effortless and unposed.

Photorealistic fashion or lifestyle portrait, authentic candid body language, realistic hand anatomy and proportions, believable fabric response, soft comfortable posture.

Avoid pulling the sleeve aggressively, crushing the wrist, fingers clipping through fabric, fused fingers, extra fingers, missing fingers, duplicated arms, impossible cuff shape, broken wrist, floating contact, identity drift, or wardrobe changes.
```

## Gesture Lock

- One hand touches either wrist or sleeve cuff, not both ambiguously
- Grip remains loose
- Fabric compresses slightly beneath the fingers
- Thumb and fingertips remain anatomically visible
- Shoulders stay relaxed

## Camera and Framing Guidance

- Prefer medium, three-quarter, or full-body framing.
- Keep both wrists, the contact point, and both elbows visible.
- Preserve source camera, focal-length character, crop, depth of field, and perspective.
- Avoid severe foreshortening that obscures whether the hand touches skin or fabric.

## Technical Notes

- **Method:** Reference-guided image editing or image-to-image generation
- **Output:** Match source dimensions and aspect ratio
- **Priority:** Identity and sleeve fidelity first, hand anatomy second, candid adjustment third
- **Realism:** Natural cuff deformation, light grip pressure, contact shadows, visible nails, and correct wrist depth

## How to Use

- Apply this as the only new gesture and choose one target explicitly: the opposite wrist or the sleeve cuff.
- Specify which hand performs the action when left-right continuity matters.
- Use medium, three-quarter, or full-body framing so both wrists, elbows, and the contact point remain visible.
- Keep the grip light, with only a small fabric fold or skin contact response.
- Compare the result against the source for identity, sleeve design, wardrobe, background, lighting, and camera continuity.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] The same recognizable person, sleeve design, outfit, setting, and lighting are preserved.
- [ ] One hand clearly touches either the opposite wrist or sleeve cuff, not an ambiguous blend of both.
- [ ] Thumb, fingers, wrist alignment, and arm ownership are anatomically plausible.
- [ ] The grip creates only slight localized compression or a small fabric fold.
- [ ] Both elbows and the complete contact relationship remain readable.
- [ ] No extra, missing, fused, floating, duplicated, or fabric-clipping fingers appear.

## Negative Prompt

```text
aggressive sleeve pulling, crushed wrist, fingers clipping through fabric, fused fingers, extra fingers, missing fingers, duplicated hands, duplicated arms, impossible cuff shape, broken wrist, reversed hand, floating contact, ambiguous wrist or sleeve target, excessive fabric stretch, cropped wrists, identity drift, wardrobe drift, changed sleeve design, background change, lighting change
```
