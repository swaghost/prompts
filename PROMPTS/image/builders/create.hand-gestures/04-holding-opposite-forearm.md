# Holding the Opposite Forearm

## Description

An image-to-image pose adjustment that creates a quiet self-comforting gesture by loosely placing one hand around the opposite forearm. The edit preserves the source identity and styling while maintaining plausible arm overlap, grip direction, sleeve or skin compression, and relaxed posture.

## Best For

Calm, approachable, introspective, reserved, vulnerable, or self-comforting portraits.

## Actor or Subject

One person with both forearms, wrists, and hands visible or physically inferable. Medium, three-quarter, or full-body references are preferred so the arm crossing and lower-torso relationship remain clear.

## Prerequisites

- One uploaded identity and wardrobe reference image
- Both arms visible with room to cross naturally at the lower torso
- Clear sleeve boundaries or visible skin at the intended contact point
- Source framing that retains both elbows and wrists

## Preservation Contract

- Preserve the exact face, apparent age, skin tone, hairstyle, body proportions, wardrobe, accessories, background, lighting, color treatment, camera angle, and aspect ratio.
- Change only the arms and hands plus minimal shoulder and weight-shift adjustments needed for comfortable contact.
- Do not turn the gesture into a rigid crossed-arm pose or alter the outfit and scene.

## Anatomy Requirements

- Two anatomically correct hands and arms with plausible overlap
- Thumb and four fingers occupy opposite sides of the held forearm
- Grip circumference respects the actual forearm size
- Wrists remain aligned and elbows bend within a comfortable range
- Sleeve or skin shows only mild, physically plausible compression

## Prompt

```text
Use the uploaded image as the exact identity and wardrobe reference. Preserve the same face, age, hairstyle, body proportions, outfit, accessories, lighting, setting, and color treatment.

Create a relaxed natural pose with one hand gently holding the opposite forearm. Use a loose, comfortable grip: the thumb rests on one side while four naturally curved fingers wrap partially around the other side without squeezing. Keep the held arm relaxed across the lower torso, shoulders soft, elbows comfortably bent, and body weight shifted subtly to one leg. Show believable sleeve compression or skin contact where the fingers rest.

Photorealistic candid portrait, quiet approachable body language, realistic anatomy, natural finger spacing, plausible wrist alignment, gentle contact pressure, comfortable posture.

Avoid crushing grip, fingers wrapping impossibly around the arm, hand clipping through sleeve or skin, extra fingers, missing fingers, duplicated arms, crossed-arm stiffness, broken wrists, floating contact, identity drift, or wardrobe changes.
```

## Gesture Lock

- One hand loosely holds the opposite forearm
- Thumb and fingers occupy opposite sides naturally
- Grip produces only slight contact compression
- Shoulders and elbows remain relaxed
- Held arm crosses the lower torso comfortably

## Camera and Framing Guidance

- Prefer medium, three-quarter, or full-body framing.
- Keep both elbows, wrists, and the contact point visible.
- Preserve source camera, lens character, crop, depth of field, and perspective.
- Avoid severe foreshortening that makes the arm ownership ambiguous.

## Technical Notes

- **Method:** Reference-guided image editing or image-to-image generation
- **Output:** Match source dimensions and aspect ratio
- **Priority:** Identity fidelity first, correct arm ownership and anatomy second, relaxed emotion third
- **Realism:** Natural grip pressure, sleeve folds, contact shadows, and believable arm depth ordering

## How to Use

- Apply this as the only new gesture and specify which hand holds which forearm when continuity matters.
- Use medium, three-quarter, or full-body framing so both elbows, wrists, and the contact point remain visible.
- Keep the grip loose and the shoulders relaxed; this is a self-comforting gesture, not rigid crossed arms.
- Preserve clear arm ownership and realistic foreground-background ordering at the overlap.
- Compare the result against the source for identity, wardrobe, sleeves, background, lighting, and camera continuity.

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] The same recognizable person, outfit, setting, and lighting are preserved.
- [ ] Each hand and arm has clear ownership with anatomically plausible overlap.
- [ ] The thumb and fingers rest on opposite sides of the held forearm.
- [ ] Grip pressure creates only subtle sleeve or skin compression.
- [ ] Both wrists and elbows align naturally without merged limbs.
- [ ] No extra, missing, fused, duplicated, or clipping fingers or arms appear.

## Negative Prompt

```text
crushing grip, impossible wraparound fingers, hand clipping through forearm, fingers clipping through sleeve or skin, extra fingers, missing fingers, fused fingers, duplicated hands, duplicated arms, merged arms, crossed-arm stiffness, broken wrists, reversed hands, floating contact, excessive compression, ambiguous arm ownership, cropped wrists, identity drift, wardrobe drift, background change, lighting change
```
