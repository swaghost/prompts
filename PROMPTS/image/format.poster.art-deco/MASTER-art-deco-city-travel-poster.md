# Art Deco City Travel Poster

## Description

A dramatic landmark-led travel poster in the visual language of 1930s luxury ocean-liner and express-train advertising. The destination remains immediately recognizable while restrained Art Deco geometry, theatrical perspective, hard-edged lighting, and a cool limited palette give the image a monumental collectible-print character.

## Usage

Use for city, regional, or national travel prints, tourism campaigns, hospitality artwork, destination branding, and coordinated poster series. Replace each bracketed field with destination-specific landmarks, environmental elements, and colors.

## Placeholders

- `[PLACE / CITY / COUNTRY]`: Full destination used to guide architecture, scenery, and palette
- `[PRIMARY LANDMARK]`: Dominant recognizable structure
- `[SECONDARY LANDMARKS / DESTINATION ELEMENTS]`: Supporting architecture and directional environmental features
- `[PLACE NAME]`: Exact destination name displayed on the poster

## Image Prompt

```text
Create a dramatic vertical travel poster for [PLACE / CITY / COUNTRY] in the visual language of a 1930s luxury-line Art Deco travel poster, designed as a high-resolution 2:3 collectible travel print.

The image should feel bold, theatrical, confident, sophisticated and premium, like a printed poster from the golden age of ocean liners and express trains.

The core visual balance must be LANDMARK 70% + ART DECO STYLISATION 30%, meaning the destination must be immediately recognizable through its actual architecture and landmarks, with Art Deco geometry used only to enhance and dramatize the scene.

Make [PRIMARY LANDMARK] the dominant visual protagonist, shown from a dramatic low viewpoint looking upward so it feels monumental, towering and powerful, with strong vertical convergence toward the top of the frame.

Add [SECONDARY LANDMARKS / DESTINATION ELEMENTS] as supporting elements, including at least one strong directional element such as a bridge, river, canal, road, railway or shoreline entering from the lower left and creating a strong diagonal toward the upper right.

Preserve the real-world silhouette, proportions and defining architectural characteristics of the landmarks; simplify surface detail but never distort the structure.

Build the composition around a strong lower-left-to-upper-right diagonal, using streamlined monumental forms, simplified geometric volumes, perfect arcs, absolutely straight verticals, clean deliberate edges, stepped setbacks, fluted bands, repeated parallel speed lines and concentric geometric rays. The geometry should enhance the architecture rather than overwhelm it.

Use hard-edged theatrical directional lighting with bright flat illuminated planes against deep solid geometric shadows using a single dark shadow tone, with sharp straight shadow edges and no soft transitions.

Place a large pale cream geometric disc behind the primary landmark with strict radiating Art Deco rays; the disc should feel cool and elegant, never like a sunset.

Keep the upper third relatively open for the sky, disc, rays and destination name.

At the upper centre, place only [PLACE NAME] in uppercase geometric sans-serif typography with wide letter spacing and a slightly condensed architectural character, using a single solid colour. No slogan, subtitle or additional text.

The primary landmark must dominate the middle and lower portions of the composition, while the supporting landmarks and environmental elements create depth and guide the eye diagonally through the poster.

Use a destination-specific palette of 6-8 cool, slightly desaturated colours inspired by [PLACE / CITY / COUNTRY], with blues, teals and cool neutrals dominating the sky, water, architecture and shadows. Warm colours may appear only as small accents on illuminated architectural planes. Always use a deep desaturated navy as the single shadow tone.

Use solid flat colour planes with no gradients except within the geometric radiating rays. The overall image should have a cool colour cast and feel like a refined museum-quality art print.

Add flat printed-ink texture on paper with extremely subtle ink-density variation and faint, even printing grain, while keeping the surface clean, crisp and contemporary.

The visual language should combine 1930s Art Deco travel posters, luxury ocean-liner advertising, Streamline Moderne, monumental architecture, geometric stylisation, theatrical perspective and sophisticated collectible poster design.

The landmark must remain the hero and the Art Deco geometry must remain secondary. Within the first second, the viewer should immediately recognize [PLACE / CITY / COUNTRY] from its landmark silhouette, architecture and environmental cues, without relying on text.

The final mood should be monumental, theatrical, confident, streamlined, elegant, cool, sophisticated and collectible.

STRICT CONSTRAINTS: exact spelling of “[PLACE NAME]”, no text other than the destination name, no slogan, no subtitle, no yellow, no mustard, no ochre, no gold, no amber, no warm sunset palette, no orange sky, no overall warm colour cast, no soft gradients, no photorealism, no 3D rendering, no airbrush glow, no neon colours, no gold foil effects, no fake paper aging, no distressing, no tears, no stains, no travel sticker aesthetics, no cartoon style, no cute characters, no complex typography, no watermark and no logos.
```

## Technical Specifications

- **Format:** Vertical 2:3 high-resolution collectible print
- **Visual hierarchy:** 70% recognizable landmark, 30% Art Deco stylisation
- **Viewpoint:** Dramatic low angle with strong upward convergence
- **Composition:** Lower-left-to-upper-right diagonal with an open upper third
- **Geometry:** Streamlined volumes, arcs, straight verticals, setbacks, fluting, speed lines, and concentric rays
- **Lighting:** Hard-edged directional illumination with one deep navy shadow tone
- **Palette:** Six to eight cool, slightly desaturated destination-specific colors
- **Typography:** Exact destination name only, uppercase geometric sans-serif
- **Finish:** Flat printed ink, faint even grain, clean contemporary paper surface
