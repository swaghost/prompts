# Rubber Stamp Travel Field Note

## Description of Resulting Image

A standalone 4:3 landscape travel field-note poster that keeps the uploaded photograph faithfully intact on the left and pairs it with a small hand-carved, multi-color rubber stamp interpretation of the same place on the right. The result feels like a tactile page collected by an architect, travel writer, or naturalist.

## Usage

Run this prompt one photo at a time to create a consistent numbered series from a trip. Perfect for travel journals, architectural documentation, landscape archives, editorial postcards, and social posts. The prompt works with landscape or portrait source photos, but every output is a 4:3 landscape poster; do not batch multiple photos because the model may blend them into a collage.

## Engines/Models

ChatGPT with image generation, GPT Image, or another image-to-image model that can preserve a source photograph and generate restrained typography.

## Actor or Subject

The uploaded travel photograph and a simplified rubber-stamp version of its location-defining silhouette.

## Prerequisites

- One travel photograph per generation
- A visible location, landmark, landscape, or strong foreground silhouette
- Optional trip numbering convention

## Included Effects

**Lighting Effects:** Preserve the source photograph's natural light and shadow

**Photographic Effects:** Restrained art-publication grade, very fine film grain

**Atmospheric Effects:** Aged paper fibers, speckle, handling wear, matte surface

**Post-Processing:** Faithful photo preservation, hand-ink texture, slight registration drift

**Style Filters:** Quiet field notebook, carved rubber stamp, tactile travel archive

## Included Camera Frames

- **Camera Type & Lens:** Source photograph remains the visual authority
- **Framing & Composition:** 4:3 landscape poster, left photo zone and right paper zone
- **Camera Angle:** Preserve the source photograph's camera angle
- **Depth of Field:** Preserve the source photo's natural depth and texture
- **Aspect Ratio:** 4:3 landscape
- **Shot Distance:** Determined by the uploaded photograph

## Image Prompt

```
Turn the single uploaded travel photo into its own standalone "rubber stamp travel field note" poster. Output one image for this photo only, never a collage and never a multi-photo layout.

LAYOUT
Use a 4:3 landscape composition split into a left and a right zone with no visible dividing line.

LEFT ZONE - approximately 58 percent of the frame
Keep the original photograph faithfully intact. Preserve the subject's identity, terrain, architecture, vegetation, people, spatial relationships, natural light and shadow, real texture, and original color mood. Apply only a restrained art-publication-grade photographic grade plus very fine film grain. Crop naturally if needed to fit the layout, but never stretch, warp, move, replace, redraw, or reinterpret the subject or the photograph.

RIGHT ZONE - approximately 42 percent of the frame
Use warm off-white aged paper with fine fibers, natural speckle, light handling wear, and a matte surface. Leave large areas completely unprinted. The white space is essential and must not be filled with decoration.

THE STAMP
Study the original photo and extract only the most location-defining elements: the main silhouette, architectural structure, lay of the land, posture of vegetation, roads, shoreline, or the key visual relationship that makes the place recognizable. Compress those elements into one small multi-color rubber stamp image. Do not copy every detail. Remove crowds, vehicles, dense rows of windows, repeated buildings, fussy small vegetation, decorative trim, and irrelevant background.

Place the stamp in the lower-middle portion of the paper zone. It must occupy only about 30 to 38 percent of that zone's height, with generous white space around it. It is a small hand-carved stamp, never a complete landscape painting, regular illustration, circular seal, or brand logo.

Let the original composition decide the stamp structure. For a landmark building, keep the recognizable outline, roofline, dome, arches, tower, or primary structure. For a hillside settlement, compress buildings into a few stepped blocks following the terrain. For coastal scenery, keep the headland slope, settlement tiers, shoreline, and a few broken water lines. For a distant cityscape, keep the skyline, one landmark, and one or two hill layers. For natural landscape, keep the directional relationship between landform, trees, water edge, or road. Keep foreground objects as stamp silhouettes only when they matter to the story.

INK AND COLOR
Extract 2 to 4 spot inks from the original photo. Favor desaturated charcoal black, deep green, brick red, ochre, gray-blue, and gray-brown, but do not force a fixed palette. Keep the color character that makes the photo recognizable and allow only one small area of color as an accent.

Every color must look separately stamped by hand: carved-rubber texture, hand-cut marks, uneven line weight, gaps in outlines, broken edges, dry ink starvation, paper showing through, grainy ink, uneven pressure, slight ghosting, and approximately 1 to 2 mm of registration drift between color layers. Edges must never look digitally smooth. The stamp should read as genuinely pressed onto old paper, not as a vector illustration or smooth line-art logo.

TEXT
Generate restrained text from the location, subject, and visual motifs in the photo:

[Location name in English]
No. [number]
[three short English keywords]
[year]

Set it below the stamp or in adjacent white space in a small typewriter face with slight mechanical imprecision. It should look like a traveler's field record, not an advertisement. Spell everything correctly. Add no extra taglines, brands, or decorative copy.

OVERALL FEEL
Quiet, restrained, tactile, specific to its place, imperfect in the way of handwork, and collected rather than designed as a commercial poster. The photograph records what was there; the stamp keeps the part worth remembering.

AVOID
An obvious center divider, circular seals, red Chinese chops, stamp perforations, wax seals, sticker collages, souvenir-shop templates, smooth vector logos, generic city icons, copying every building, overly dense engraving, childlike craft styling, cartoon styling, 3D rendering, plastic texture, smooth digital gradients, oversaturation, excessive text, decorative clutter, and any redrawing or alteration of the original photo on the left.
```

## Technical Specifications

- **Format:** 4:3 landscape
- **Photo zone:** Approximately 58 percent of the frame, left
- **Paper zone:** Approximately 42 percent of the frame, right
- **Stamp scale:** Approximately 30 to 38 percent of paper-zone height
- **Stamp inks:** 2 to 4 extracted spot colors
- **Text:** Location, number, three keywords, year only
- **Output rule:** One uploaded photo becomes one standalone poster

## Negative Prompt (What to Avoid)

```
collage, multiple photos, blended photos, visible center divider, circular seal, red Chinese chop, stamp perforations, wax seal, sticker collage, souvenir template, vector logo, generic city icon, full detailed landscape illustration, dense engraving, childlike craft, cartoon, 3D render, plastic paper, smooth digital gradients, oversaturation, excessive text, extra tagline, brand name, decorative clutter, altered source photo, redrawn subject, moved subject, replaced subject, warped photograph, stretched photograph, fake location text, spelling errors, watermark
```
