# 🎥 **Cinematic Style + Blocking Map + Lighting Diagram Extraction Prompt**

**Prompt:**  
“Analyze this image/video as a fully constructed cinematic scene. Break down the visual, spatial, and psychological architecture with professional film‑language precision. Provide a prompt‑ready reconstruction of the cinematography style, blocking layout, lighting diagram, and emotional palette.

---

## 1. Cinematography Breakdown

Describe the camera’s technical and artistic choices:

- **Camera angle & height:** low, eye‑level, high, overhead, Dutch, etc.
- **Lens & focal length:** wide, normal, telephoto, macro, anamorphic, and how it shapes depth, distortion, intimacy, or tension.
- **Framing & composition:** rule of thirds, center framing, symmetry, negative space, depth layers, foreground/midground/background relationships.
- **Camera movement:** static, handheld, dolly, crane, pan, tilt, tracking, push‑in, pull‑out, orbit, and the emotional effect of each.
- **Shot type:** close‑up, medium, wide, extreme close‑up, establishing, insert.
- **Visual texture:** grain, softness, bloom, haze, diffusion, reflections, environmental particles.

---

## 2. Blocking Map (Character & Object Placement)

Create a spatial diagram in descriptive form:

- **Character positions:** where each character stands/sits in relation to camera, environment, and each other.
- **Distances & spatial tension:** closeness, separation, dominance, avoidance, intimacy, confrontation.
- **Body orientation:** facing camera, profile, back‑turned, angled, slouched, upright, guarded, open.
- **Movement arcs:** describe how characters move through the space (paths, gestures, pacing).
- **Environmental blocking:** placement of props, furniture, obstacles, architecture, and how they shape emotional or narrative dynamics.
- **Power geometry:** who occupies higher/lower ground, who is framed larger/smaller, who controls the space.

Write this as a **verbal blocking diagram** that could be used by a cinematographer or director.

---

## 3. Lighting Diagram Extraction

Break down the lighting setup as if reconstructing a professional lighting diagram:

- **Key light:** position, height, angle, intensity, softness/hardness, color temperature.
- **Fill light:** presence or absence, ratio, direction, softness.
- **Backlight / rim light:** placement, strength, purpose (separation, halo, tension).
- **Practical lights:** lamps, neon, headlights, screens, candles — describe their contribution to mood.
- **Ambient/environmental light:** skylight, bounce, diffusion, fog, haze, reflections.
- **Shadow behavior:** deep shadows, soft gradients, harsh contrast, silhouette, chiaroscuro.
- **Lighting style:** naturalistic, noir, high‑key, low‑key, Rembrandt, split lighting, motivated lighting.
- **Emotional impact:** how lighting shapes fear, intimacy, isolation, warmth, dread, nostalgia.

Write this as a **text‑based lighting diagram** describing positions relative to camera and subjects.

---

## 4. Psychological & Emotional Palette

Extract the emotional architecture of the scene:

- **Primary emotional tone** of the environment.
- **Secondary emotional tones** that subtly influence the mood.
- **Character‑level emotional states:** internal conflict, fear, longing, confidence, detachment, anticipation.
- **Micro‑expressions:** eyes, brows, mouth tension, posture, breathing.
- **Relational dynamics:** power imbalance, intimacy, avoidance, confrontation, dependency.
- **Environmental psychology:** how lighting, color, silence, clutter, emptiness, movement, or stillness shape emotion.
- **Temporal emotion:** how pacing or motion affects tension or release.

---

## 5. Sound Design & Emotional Reinforcement

If video or implied audio:

- **Music:** genre, tempo, instrumentation, emotional intention.
- **Sound effects:** diegetic vs. non‑diegetic, environmental noise, Foley.
- **Silence:** how absence of sound creates tension, intimacy, dread, or focus.
- **Rhythm:** how audio pacing shapes emotional flow.
- **Psychological effect:** how sound reinforces or contradicts the visual mood.

---

## 6. Final Prompt‑Ready Cinematic Blueprint

Combine all extracted elements into a **single, clean, generation‑ready prompt** that recreates:

- the cinematography style
- the blocking layout
- the lighting diagram
- the emotional palette
- the character psychology
- the atmospheric tone
- the movement and pacing
- the sound‑implied mood

Write it as a **fully usable cinematic prompt** that reproduces the scene’s technical and emotional essence.”
