# 🎬 **Cinematic Visualization + Psychological/Emotional Extraction Prompt**

**Prompt:**  
“Analyze this image/video as a cinematic scene. Break down the full visual and emotional construction with professional film‑language precision. Provide a prompt‑ready description of the scene’s cinematic style and the psychological/emotional state of the environment and each character.

## 1. Cinematic Visualization Style

Describe the scene’s visual grammar:

- **Camera language:** angle, height, lens length, framing, movement, stability, proximity, and how these choices shape emotion.
- **Lighting:** quality, direction, contrast, color temperature, shadows, practicals, and how lighting influences mood or tension.
- **Color palette:** dominant hues, accents, saturation, temperature, and how color contributes to emotional tone.
- **Composition:** symmetry/asymmetry, balance, negative space, depth, foreground/background relationships, leading lines, and visual hierarchy.
- **Texture & atmosphere:** grain, softness, haze, fog, smoke, dust, reflections, weather, or environmental effects.
- **Production design:** environment, props, wardrobe, and how these elements reinforce theme or psychology.
- **Cinematic influences:** identify any recognizable stylistic lineage (e.g., neo‑noir, arthouse minimalism, 90s thriller, surrealist, documentary realism).

## 2. Psychological & Emotional Palette of the Scene

Extract the emotional architecture:

- **Primary emotional tone** of the entire scene.
- **Secondary emotional tones** that subtly shape the atmosphere.
- **Underlying psychological tension** (fear, longing, dread, anticipation, detachment, intimacy, etc.).
- **Environmental emotional cues:** lighting, color, silence, clutter, emptiness, movement, or stillness.
- **Temporal emotion:** how pacing, rhythm, or motion (or lack of motion) affects the emotional palette.

## 3. Character‑Level Emotional States

For each character visible:

- **Primary emotion** (the strongest emotional signal).
- **Secondary emotions** (supporting or conflicting feelings).
- **Micro‑expressions:** eyes, brows, mouth tension, jaw set, breathing, posture.
- **Body language:** openness/closedness, weight distribution, movement quality, gestures.
- **Psychological context:** what the character seems to be thinking, fearing, wanting, hiding, or anticipating.
- **Relational dynamics:** emotional tension between characters, power imbalance, intimacy, avoidance, confrontation.

## 4. Sound Design & Emotional Impact

If video or implied audio:

- **Music:** genre, tempo, instrumentation, emotional intention.
- **Sound effects:** environmental noise, Foley, diegetic vs. non‑diegetic sound.
- **Silence:** describe how absence of sound creates tension, intimacy, dread, or focus.
- **Rhythm:** how audio pacing shapes emotional flow.
- **Psychological effect:** how sound reinforces or contradicts the visual mood.

## 5. Movement, Pacing & Temporal Emotion

For video or implied motion:

- **Camera movement:** slow, drifting, handheld, locked‑off, aggressive, floating, stalking.
- **Character movement:** speed, hesitation, confidence, rhythm, emotional intention.
- **Scene pacing:** fast, slow, meditative, chaotic, suspenseful.
- **Emotional trajectory:** how the mood evolves over time.

## 6. Prompt‑Ready Cinematic Style Summary

Create a **single, clean, prompt‑ready description** that reproduces the scene’s cinematic style and emotional psychology. Include:

- camera angle + lens
- lighting style
- color palette
- emotional tone
- character emotional states
- atmosphere
- movement
- sound‑implied mood
- stylistic influences

Write it as a **ready‑to‑use generation prompt** that recreates the scene’s cinematic and psychological essence.”
