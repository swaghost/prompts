# Post-Impressionist Impasto Oil Transformation

## Purpose

Transform a supplied image into a source-faithful, late-19th-century Post-Impressionist oil painting with expressive directional brushwork, tactile canvas texture, and rich chromatic modeling.

## Prerequisites

**MANDATORY:**

- Source image uploaded as the primary visual reference

## Image Transformation Prompt

```text
Use the uploaded image as the absolute structural and semantic reference. Transform it into an expressive late-19th-century Post-Impressionist oil painting with energetic, hand-painted brushwork and tactile impasto, while preserving the source image's subject matter, identity, composition, pose, proportions, perspective, spatial relationships, objects, architecture, landscape features, framing, and overall scene organization.

The source determines what is depicted; the painterly treatment determines only how it is rendered. Do not redesign, replace, add, or remove elements to make the scene look like a particular existing artwork.

Replace photographic smoothness with visible, directional oil strokes that follow the forms already present: curved contour strokes over faces and bodies; rhythmic marks on clothing and objects; structured linear marks on architecture; and fluid organic marks on vegetation, clouds, water, terrain, and ground. Create tangible oil paint on textured canvas through layered pigment, broken-color passages, subtle impasto ridges, short repeated strokes, and varied stroke direction, thickness, density, and length. Every stroke must describe form, volume, movement, depth, or emotion; avoid a generic digital-paint filter.

Translate the existing palette into vivid Post-Impressionist color relationships. Build intensity with complementary contrasts and adjacent strokes of distinct pigment instead of smooth gradients. Use ultramarine, cobalt, blue-green, ochre, golden yellow, amber, warm orange, earth green, muted olive, burnt sienna, reddish brown, vermilion, and restrained dark accents only when supported by the original scene's colors and illumination. Do not force colors onto objects where they contradict the source.

Preserve the direction and logic of the original light. Model highlights and shadow as layered painted color passages and directional brushwork rather than photorealistic shading. Preserve believable depth while allowing restrained expressive chromatic contrast.

Use organic, selectively strong outlines around important forms. Let contours vary in thickness, color, and intensity. Avoid perfectly clean vector edges. Shapes must remain recognizable and structurally faithful while carrying the slight irregularity of physical painted oil work.

When people appear, preserve exact facial identity, recognizable anatomy, expression, gaze direction, hairstyle, clothing, pose, hands, body proportions, and placement. Describe skin with layered ochres, warm earth pigments, muted reds, cool complementary shadows, and individually visible brushstrokes that follow facial planes. Do not distort facial features for style.

When landscapes appear, preserve exact geography and arrangement. Use rhythmic directional brushwork on existing vegetation, fields, clouds, water, and terrain. Introduce swirling or wave-like motion only when naturally compatible with the original forms; do not introduce arbitrary stars, sunflowers, cypress trees, or other absent motifs.

When architecture or interiors appear, preserve geometry and object placement. Render existing surfaces with hand-painted irregularity, pigment variation, expressive perspective accents, and directional marks appropriate to each material. For night scenes, preserve the existing composition and use deep blues, warm lights, and painterly halos only around real light sources.

Maintain the original crop, aspect ratio, viewpoint, focal hierarchy, negative space, foreground, middle ground, background, relative scale, and spatial organization. The final result must read as a physical oil painting on textured canvas, with layered pigments, visible brush marks, controlled impasto, handcrafted irregularity, and cohesive Post-Impressionist energy across the whole image.
```

## Negative Constraints

```text
Do not add famous-painting motifs absent from the source. No arbitrary stars, sunflowers, cypress trees, extra people, extra objects, altered identity, changed pose, anatomical deformation, missing or extra fingers, distorted architecture, changed perspective, displaced objects, excessive abstraction, smooth digital gradients, airbrushed surfaces, plastic texture, watercolor appearance, acrylic appearance, flat cartoon rendering, generic Photoshop filter effects, uniform brush patterns, repetitive procedural textures, text, signatures, frames, borders, or watermarks.
```
