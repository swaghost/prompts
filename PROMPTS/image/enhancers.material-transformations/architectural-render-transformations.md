## 1. Interior Style Transformations

### Kitchen Style Conversion — Minimalist to Contemporary Vibrant

**Transformation Goal:** Convert neutral minimalist kitchen into vibrant contemporary space with bold color choices and premium materials.

**Prompt:**

```
Transform the neutral minimalist kitchen into a contemporary space with vibrant colors, adding wine-colored base cabinets, a deep teal upper panel, and colorful chairs. Replace the backsplash with marbled stone featuring blue and green veins, while maintaining the same kitchen composition and soft lighting.
```

**Preservation Requirements:**

- Same kitchen layout and spatial organization
- Original appliance placement
- Existing composition and camera angle
- Soft lighting quality and direction

**Transformation Elements:**

- **Base Cabinets:** Wine-colored/burgundy finish
- **Upper Panel:** Deep teal accent wall or cabinet section
- **Seating:** Colorful chairs (mix of complementary hues)
- **Backsplash:** Marbled stone with blue and green veining
- **Overall Aesthetic:** Contemporary vibrant vs original minimalist neutral

**Use Cases:**

- Interior design presentations showing style options
- Before/after visualization for renovation proposals
- Client presentations demonstrating material and color alternatives
- Portfolio work showing design versatility

## 2. Aerial View & Perspective Transformations

### Tiny Planet Effect — Architectural Aerial View

**Transformation Goal:** Convert standard architectural aerial render into spherical "tiny planet" perspective showing entire site context.

**Prompt:**

```
Transform the architectural house render into a "tiny planet" aerial view, with a spherical effect showing the entire surrounding site. Keep the modern house at the center, with streets, landscaping, and the neighborhood forming a small urban planet around it.
```

**Technical Requirements:**

- **Center Focus:** Modern house remains at center of sphere
- **Spherical Warping:** 360-degree panoramic projection bent into sphere
- **Context Preservation:** Streets, landscaping, neighborhood visible
- **Perspective:** Small planet/world effect with house as focal point
- **Composition:** Circular frame with architecture at center

**Visual Characteristics:**

- Dramatic fisheye/spherical distortion
- Complete site context visible in single view
- Urban environment wrapped around central architecture
- Whimsical yet informative presentation style

**Use Cases:**

- Unique project presentation angles
- Social media marketing content
- Site context visualization in creative format
- Portfolio pieces showing innovative visualization approaches

## 3. Facade & Architectural Modifications

### Modern Facade Enhancement — Cantilevered Addition

**Transformation Goal:** Expand existing facade with modern architectural additions while maintaining core design language.

**Prompt:**

```
Transform the existing façade into a modern house with an upper cantilevered volume, expanding the second-floor block and creating a balcony with a glass railing. Add people on the balcony and at the ground level, with an outdoor deck area, landscaping, and natural lighting, while maintaining exposed concrete, large glass panels, and a contemporary architectural style.
```

**Preservation Requirements:**

- Existing facade character and materials
- Exposed concrete aesthetic
- Large glass panel design language
- Contemporary architectural style
- Natural lighting approach

**Transformation Elements:**

- **Upper Level:** Cantilevered volume extending beyond lower floor
- **Second Floor:** Expanded block with overhang
- **Balcony:** Glass railing for transparency and modern look
- **Human Scale:** People on balcony and ground level
- **Ground Level:** Outdoor deck area integration
- **Landscaping:** Enhanced vegetation and site treatment
- **Lighting:** Natural daylight with architectural shadows

**Design Principles:**

- Maintain contemporary minimalist aesthetic
- Preserve material palette (concrete, glass)
- Add functional outdoor spaces
- Introduce human scale and activity
- Enhance connection between interior and exterior

**Use Cases:**

- Renovation visualization
- Architectural expansion proposals
- Facade improvement studies
- Vertical addition feasibility presentations

## 4. Scale Model Conversions

### Architectural Scale Model — Worktable Presentation

**Transformation Goal:** Convert photographic or rendered architecture into detailed physical scale model aesthetic on architect's worktable.

**Prompt:**

```
Transform the photo of the rustic house into a detailed architectural scale model, placed on an architect's worktable. Add a wooden base, miniature vegetation, scale trees, and technical drawings around the table, while maintaining the same house design.
```

**Model Characteristics:**

- **Base Platform:** Wooden architectural model base with clean edges
- **Scale Accuracy:** Consistent scaling throughout model
- **Materiality:** Model-making materials (foam core, balsa wood, acrylic)
- **Vegetation:** Miniature scale trees and landscape elements
- **Context:** Architect's worktable setting with ambient lighting

**Supporting Elements:**

- **Technical Drawings:** Rolled or flat plans around table edges
- **Tools:** Optional drafting tools, scale rulers
- **Lighting:** Studio or task lighting creating realistic shadows
- **Surface:** Wood worktable texture and character
- **Atmosphere:** Professional architectural studio environment

**Preservation Requirements:**

- Same house design and proportions
- Architectural details at appropriate scale
- Original design intent and character

**Use Cases:**

- Physical model visualization without building actual model
- Presentation boards showing "model photography"
- Client presentations preferring tactile/physical aesthetics
- Portfolio work demonstrating analog presentation styles
- Academic project visualization

### Architectural Sketch to Photorealistic 3D — Strict Preservation

**Transformation Goal:** Convert hand-drawn architectural sketch into photorealistic three-dimensional representation with absolute fidelity to original design intent.

**Prompt:**

```
Transform this architectural sketch into a photorealistic three-dimensional representation, preserving the massing, proportions, perspective, and original design intent exactly as drawn. Interpret only the elements shown in the sketch, without adding or altering any architectural features. The final result should look like a real architectural photograph, with no CGI or rendered appearance.
```

**Critical Preservation Requirements:**

- **Massing:** Exact volumetric form and building envelope as sketched
- **Proportions:** All dimensional relationships maintained precisely
- **Perspective:** Same viewpoint, camera angle, and vanishing points
- **Design Intent:** Original architectural concept preserved faithfully
- **Elements Only:** Interpret only what is explicitly shown in sketch

**Strict Interpretation Rules:**

**What to Preserve Exactly:**

- Building footprint and overall form
- Window and door placement, size, and proportions
- Rooflines, eaves, and architectural features shown
- Material boundaries and surface divisions
- Spatial relationships between elements
- Sketched details (trim, columns, railings, etc.)
- Ground plane and immediate context

**What NOT to Add:**

- Architectural features not in original sketch
- Design embellishments or "improvements"
- Additional windows, doors, or openings
- Changed proportions or scales
- Redesigned elements or modern interpretations
- Landscape elements not sketched

**Photorealistic Conversion Requirements:**

- **Materials:** Interpret sketch lines as realistic architectural materials
  - Line quality suggests material type (rough vs smooth, heavy vs light)
  - Convert sketch textures to photorealistic equivalents
  - Maintain implied material boundaries
- **Lighting:** Natural architectural photography lighting
  - Soft natural daylight or appropriate time-of-day
  - Realistic shadows consistent with building form
  - No dramatic or stylized lighting unless implied in sketch
- **Atmosphere:** Professional architectural photography aesthetic
  - No CGI "look" or rendered appearance
  - Subtle environmental context (sky, ground plane)
  - Natural depth and atmospheric perspective
- **Detail Level:** Match sketch detail density
  - Don't over-detail beyond sketch information
  - Don't simplify beyond sketch information
  - Faithful interpretation of drawn elements

**Photography Quality Standards:**

- Professional architectural photography lens characteristics
- Appropriate depth of field for architectural documentation
- Natural color palette (no oversaturation or stylization)
- Subtle texture and material realism
- Clean, crisp focus on building form
- Environmental context that supports subject without dominating

**Common Mistakes to Avoid:**

❌ Adding architectural features not in sketch (extra windows, details, ornamentation)
❌ Changing proportions to "improve" design
❌ Altering perspective or viewpoint
❌ Over-rendering with CGI appearance
❌ Dramatic or stylized lighting not implied in original
❌ Adding landscape elements not sketched
❌ Modernizing or reinterpreting design intent
❌ Simplifying or over-complicating beyond sketch information

**Use Cases:**

- Concept design visualization from client sketches
- Historical architectural reconstruction from original drawings
- Design development documentation maintaining design intent
- Presentation materials showing sketch-to-reality progression
- Academic projects requiring faithful interpretation of hand-drawn work
- Architectural competition entries from conceptual sketches

**Quality Indicators:**

✓ Someone familiar with the sketch immediately recognizes the design
✓ Proportions feel identical to original drawing
✓ No "added" features that weren't in sketch
✓ Looks like professional architecture photography, not CGI render
✓ Materials are plausible interpretations of sketch lines
✓ Perspective and viewpoint match exactly
✓ Design intent is clearly preserved and honored

## General Transformation Principles

### Core Preservation Rules

**Always Preserve:**

1. **Geometry:** Original architectural proportions and dimensions
2. **Composition:** Camera angle, framing, spatial relationships
3. **Design Intent:** Core architectural concept and character
4. **Scale:** Realistic human scale and dimensional relationships
5. **Spatial Logic:** Room layouts, circulation, structural logic

**Never Alter:**

- Floor plans or spatial organization without explicit instruction
- Structural proportions or dimensional ratios
- Original camera perspective or viewpoint
- Aspect ratio unless specifically requested

### Enhancement Guidelines

**Appropriate Enhancements:**

- Material specifications and texture detail
- Lighting quality and atmospheric effects
- Landscape and environmental context
- Photographic realism and rendering quality
- Human scale elements and lifestyle context

**Quality Standards:**

- Professional architectural photography aesthetic
- Realistic material behavior and physics
- Accurate lighting and shadow behavior
- Natural environmental integration
- Editorial presentation quality

## Technical Specifications

### Photorealistic Rendering Standards

**Material Realism:**

- Accurate reflectance and specularity

- Natural color variation and imperfection

**Lighting Realism:**

- Physically accurate light behavior
- Natural shadow softness and direction
- Proper light bounce and indirect illumination
- Realistic sky and environmental lighting

**Atmospheric Depth:**

- Subtle atmospheric perspective
- Natural depth cues (focus, haze, detail falloff)
- Realistic sky and cloud rendering
- Environmental integration (landscape, context)

**Photography Aesthetic:**

- Appropriate lens characteristics
- Natural depth of field
- Professional color grading
- Editorial composition and framing

## Advanced Techniques

### Multi-Step Transformations

**Example: 3D Model → Sketch → Photorealistic**

1. Convert 3D render to architectural sketch style
2. Transform sketch to photorealistic with specified materials
3. Apply time-of-day lighting transformation

**Example: Single Model → Multiple Seasons**

1. Base photorealistic render with spring/summer landscaping
2. Transform to autumn colors and lighting
3. Transform to winter with snow coverage
4. Transform to rainy day atmosphere

### Batch Variation Strategy

From single 3D architectural render, create:

- Photorealistic contemporary version
- Traditional material palette version
- Scale model presentation
- Technical blueprint drawing
- Tiny planet aerial perspective
- Multiple time-of-day variations

This approach maximizes content output from single 3D investment.

---

**Remember:** The goal is transformation, not redesign. Preserve the architectural intent and spatial relationships while enhancing realism, changing materials, or altering presentation style. The core geometry and design concept should remain recognizable and true to the original.
