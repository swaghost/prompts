# Rubber Stamp Travel Field Notes Poster

## Concept

Create one standalone travel field-notes poster for each uploaded photo. Preserve the original photograph as the on-site record and pair it with a small, hand-carved, multi-color rubber stamp that captures the most recognizable visual memory of the location. The result should feel like a quiet, tactile page kept by an architect, travel writer, or natural observer.

## Prompt

```
Create a separate "Rubber Stamp Travel Field Notes Poster" for each uploaded photo. Output each photo individually: one photo becomes one poster. Never combine photos, create a collage, or blend multiple images.

COMPOSITION
Use an exact 4:5 landscape composition divided visually into a left photo region and a right paper region without drawing an obvious dividing line. The left region occupies approximately 58 percent of the frame and the right region approximately 42 percent.

LEFT PHOTO REGION
Faithfully preserve the original photograph. Maintain the main subject's identity, terrain, architecture, plants, people, spatial relationships, authentic textures, natural lighting, shadows, perspective, and original color atmosphere. Apply only restrained art-publication-level photo color grading and extremely subtle fine-grained film noise. Natural cropping is allowed to adapt the photo to the layout, but never stretch, distort, shift, replace, redraw, or reinterpret the main subject or scene.

RIGHT PAPER REGION
Use warm off-white aged paper with subtle fibers, natural grain, light usage marks, and a matte finish. Preserve large areas of unprinted paper whitespace; blank space is an essential part of the composition. Do not fill the paper with decoration.

RUBBER STAMP
Analyze the original photograph and extract only the minimal location-defining information needed to recognize the place and its visual relationships. Compress the most distinctive subject outlines, architectural structures, terrain contours, plant forms, roads, shorelines, or foreground silhouettes into one small multi-color rubber stamp image.

Do not replicate every element item by item. Remove crowds, vehicles, dense windows, repetitive buildings, fragmented vegetation, decorative details, and irrelevant background. The stamp must remain a small visual memory, never a full landscape painting, standard illustration, generic city icon, or brand logo.

Place the stamp in the lower-middle of the right paper region, occupying approximately 30 to 38 percent of that region's height. Keep generous whitespace around it.

Let the source photo determine the stamp structure. For iconic architecture, retain the distinctive outer contour, roof, dome, arch, tower, or primary structure. For mountain settlements, compress buildings into a few stepped color blocks aligned with the terrain. For coastal scenery, retain mountain contours, settlement layers, shoreline, and sparse broken water lines. For city panoramas, retain the main skyline, one iconic building, and one or two distant mountain layers. For natural landscapes, retain the primary landforms, trees, shorelines, and road orientations. Retain a foreground occlusion only when it is narratively important.

INK AND COLOR
Extract 2 to 4 spot inks from the original photo. Favor desaturated carbon black, deep green, brick red, ochre yellow, slate blue, or taupe brown, but do not force a fixed palette. Preserve the most distinctive color character of the photo and allow only a small area of color for emphasis.

Render every color as a separately hand-stamped layer with authentic rubber-stamp carving texture: hand-engraved marks, uneven line widths, contour notches, fractured edges, dry-ink shortages, paper show-through, granular ink, uneven pressure, partial ghosting, and approximately 1 to 2 mm of natural misregistration. Do not digitally smooth the edges. The print must resemble a real carved stamp pressed onto aged paper, not a filtered photo, smooth vector illustration, or line-art logo.

TEXT
Generate only restrained text based on the photo's location and subject:

[Location name in English]
No. [number]
[three short English keywords]
2026

Place the text below or beside the stamp in the whitespace. Use a small, slightly mechanically imperfect typewriter font. Make it feel like a traveler's field record, not an advertising headline. Spell every word and number accurately. Add no slogans, brands, or decorative copy.

OVERALL FEEL
Quiet, restrained, tactile, regionally specific, handmade, and collectible. The left photo records what was there; the small stamp preserves the most recognizable fragments of memory.

AVOID
Obvious central dividing lines, circular seals, red Chinese stamps, postage-stamp perforations, wax seals, sticker collages, tourist souvenir templates, smooth vector logos, generic city icons, full replication of all architecture, dense detailing, childlike craftiness, cartoon style, 3D rendering, plastic textures, glossy digital gradients, oversaturation, excessive text, decorative clutter, and any redrawing, alteration, relocation, or replacement of the original photograph or its main subject.
```

## Technical Specifications

- **Format:** Exact 4:5 landscape
- **Photo region:** Approximately 58 percent, left
- **Paper region:** Approximately 42 percent, right
- **Stamp scale:** Approximately 30 to 38 percent of paper-region height
- **Stamp inks:** 2 to 4 extracted spot colors
- **Text:** Location, number, three keywords, and year only
- **Output rule:** One uploaded photo becomes one standalone poster
