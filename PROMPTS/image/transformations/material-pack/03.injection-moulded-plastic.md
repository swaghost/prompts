# Injection-Moulded Plastic - Material Transformation

**Source:** The Material Pack by Waviboy | [waviboy.com/material-pack](https://www.waviboy.com/material-pack)

## Material Description

A moulded part can only have shapes that pull cleanly out of a steel tool. Ask for the parting line and the ejector marks and the whole object starts obeying that rule. This prompt creates authentic injection-moulded plastic with all manufacturing evidence.

## Key Characteristics

- Rigid polymer shot into steel tool under pressure
- Fine parting line around form
- Circular ejector pin marks
- Cut sprue scar
- Thin flash along seams
- Tool surface texture (grained or glossy)
- Faint flow lines from gate
- Shallow sink marks in thick sections

## Image Edit Prompt

Transform the subject of this photograph into an injection-moulded plastic figure. Abandon the original rendering entirely: no skin, no hair and no fabric survive anywhere, only rigid polymer that was shot molten into a steel tool under pressure and left to cool. The object carries its manufacture openly: a fine parting line runs right around the form where the two halves of the mould closed, small circular ejector pin marks are sunk into the flatter faces, a cut sprue scar sits low on one side, and a thin flash of squeezed-out plastic survives along part of one seam. The surface is really the tool's surface, lightly grained where the steel was textured and glossy where it was polished, with faint flow lines fanning out from the gate and shallow sink marks wherever the wall runs thick behind. Detail is simplified to whatever a mould can release, so undercuts are gone and every form draws cleanly away. The plastic is one saturated colour straight through, with a cavity number and a small recycling symbol moulded into a back face. Keep the subject, pose, framing and composition exactly as they are.

## Technical Details

**Material Properties:**

- Rigid polymer
- One saturated color throughout
- Grained or glossy finish (from steel tool)

**Manufacturing Evidence:**

- Parting line (mould halves)
- Ejector pin marks (circular)
- Cut sprue scar
- Flash along seams
- Flow lines from gate
- Sink marks (thick sections)
- Cavity number moulded in
- Recycling symbol

**Manufacturing Constraints:**

- No undercuts (must release from mould)
- Forms must draw cleanly away
- Detail simplified to mouldable geometry

**Surface:**

- Tool surface texture transferred
- Grained where steel was textured
- Glossy where steel was polished
