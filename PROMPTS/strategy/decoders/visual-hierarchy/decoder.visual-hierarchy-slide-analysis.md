# Reverse Engineer the Visual Hierarchy of a High-Performing Slide

## Purpose

Deconstruct high-performing slide designs to understand how visual hierarchy, design choices, and information architecture guide reader attention, create engagement patterns, and influence content consumption behavior.

## Description of Target Content or Subject

Individual slides from carousel posts, presentations, or visual content that demonstrated exceptional engagement, particularly slides that received high stop rates or prompted swipe-through behavior in multi-slide sequences.

## Discovery - Description of Resulting Output

A visual hierarchy analysis identifying what the reader sees first and the intentional attention sequence, presence of debatable/incomplete/opinion-based visual elements inviting reaction, whether the slide creates space for readers to feel safe enough to publicly agree/disagree/share experience, and identification of 3 specific comment hooks that could be added to provoke reply without forcing conversation or sounding gimmicky. Rewrites the caption or CTA using the strongest hook.

## Usage

Perfect for community engagement strategists studying visual conversation triggers, slide designers seeking to optimize for discussion, and content creators building visually-driven community interaction. Ideal for analyzing Instagram carousel slides, LinkedIn document pages, presentation slides with high engagement, and any visual content meant to spark conversation. This prompt excels at revealing how design choices influence not just consumption but active participation and dialogue.

## Prerequisites

**MANDATORY:**

- Image or description of the slide/visual post
- Context about engagement performance (likes vs comments)
- Your desired outcome for implementing similar patterns

## Prompt

You are a community engagement strategist. I will paste a post that received likes but very few comments. Analyze: (1) Does the post ask anything of the reader, implicitly or explicitly, or does it feel 'complete' and closed off to response? (2) Is there a debatable, incomplete, or opinion-based element the reader could react to, or is everything stated as settled fact? (3) Does the post make the reader feel safe enough to publicly agree, disagree, or share their own experience? (4) Identify 3 specific 'comment hooks' I could add — questions, controversial micro-claims, or open loops — that would provoke reply without feeling forced or gimmicky. Rewrite the caption/CTA using the strongest one. Post to analyze: [PASTE YOUR POST HERE]
