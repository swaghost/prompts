## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

❌ **Don't:**

- Over-exaggerate the physics (keep realistic)
- Make ball bounces too high (energy loss is real)
- Cartoon-ify the reactions (grounded comedy)
- Over-light the scene (natural soft daylight only)
- Add excessive slow-motion (use sparingly if at all)
- Make character too reactive (her calm is the joke)
- Create continuity errors between cuts
- Over-stabilize camera (some natural handheld okay in final shot)
- Add unnecessary VFX (practical physics only)

✅ **Do:**

- Maintain realistic physics throughout
- Show energy loss with each bounce
- Keep character deadpan and focused on book
- Use natural lighting (window daylight)
- Maintain consistency across all cuts
- Time comedy beats precisely
- Show realistic material interactions
- Keep camera natural (phone aesthetic)
- Make the deadpan glance brief and perfect

## Notes

This sequence is a **masterclass in deadpan physical comedy**. The humor comes from the contrast between the elaborate, physics-perfect chaos happening around the character and her complete, unwavering focus on her book. The stumble sets up the joke, but the punchline is her absolute refusal to acknowledge the chaos she's created.

**Key Success Factors:**

1. **Physics Must Be Perfect** - The more realistic the ball physics, the funnier the absurdity
2. **Character Commitment** - She never breaks, never overreacts
3. **The Glance** - Brief, deadpan, perfect timing, then back to book
4. **Environmental Consistency** - Any continuity error breaks the believability
5. **Comedy Timing** - Hold beats just long enough, not too long

The medicine ball becomes a character itself through its elaborate physics journey. Every bounce, collision, and roll must be impeccably realistic to sell the comedy.

**Inspiration:** Buster Keaton-style physical comedy where the world falls apart around an unfazed protagonist.
