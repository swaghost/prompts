# Apartment Tower Clay Material-Wave Reveal

## Classification

**Product Category:** apartment-building exterior
**Reveal Effect:** upward clay-to-material wave
**Reveal Mechanism:** one scan-like material wave converts a complete white clay tower into final facade materials
**Sequence Type:** reference-match material transformation

## Description of Resulting Video or Video Sequence

A 10-second reveal beginning with a complete matte-white clay apartment model; a single wave travels upward and converts it into the exact photoreal tower.

## Usage

For facade systems, material studies, apartment marketing, and clean architectural transformation films.

## Engines/Models

Reference-aware video model with surface-mask progression, repeated facade consistency, and final-frame adherence.

## Prerequisites / Dependencies

- Final apartment exterior as `@reference`.
- Optional matching clay-model first frame.

## Storyboard Requirement

Optional five-panel board: clay tower, ground-floor wave, mid-tower conversion, roof completion, final hero.

## Effect

### Effect Name: Clay-to-Reality Material Wave

The complete geometry exists from frame one. The wave changes only materials and detail, never floor count, proportions, balconies, or silhouette.

## Video Prompt

```text
Use @reference as the final apartment building exterior design target and exact final visual match. Create a 10-second continuous cinematic reveal.

0.0-1.2s: Begin with the complete apartment building as a simple matte-white clay architectural model matching exact reference geometry.
1.2-7.5s: One smooth material wave travels upward from ground floor to roof. Below the wave, clay becomes real concrete, stone, metal, timber, balcony glass, reflective windows, and detailed facade materials. Above it, geometry remains matte white clay.
7.5-8.8s: As the wave reaches the roof, landscaping, entrance details, and exterior lighting activate.
8.8-10.0s: The wave disappears and the fully photorealistic apartment development matches @reference exactly.

No people, text, logos, cuts, geometry construction, changing floor count, moving balconies, multiple waves, uneven boundary, material bleeding ahead, clay residue, melted surfaces, or facade drift.
```
