# PORTAL — Cinematic AI Scene Workflow

**ENVIRONMENT TRANSFORMATION / REALITY REVEAL**

---

## Platform Chain

**Nano Banana** (still generation) → **Seedance 2.5** (animation)

---

## Concept

Keep the real performance, rebuild the world around it, then add only the motion the scene actually needs. Transform environments while maintaining character consistency—from real footage to believable AI-assisted environments with restrained, believable animation.

---

## Core Philosophy

**THE RULE:** Footage first. AI second. If the scene starts feeling "designed," remove something before adding more.

**Three Critical Principles:**

1. **Lock the subject first** — Identity, hair, outfit, pose and framing should stay recognizable
2. **Make the new environment affect the person** — Through believable light, depth and atmosphere
3. **Animate fewer elements** — Real backgrounds are mostly stable

---

## Workflow

### 01 — Choose Frame

Select the key frame from your real footage that will serve as the foundation.

### 02 — Build Still (Nano Banana)

Build the approved still. Keep the person and camera language intact while redesigning the environment.

### 03 — Animate (Seedance 2.5)

Animate the approved look with restrained subject, environment, light and sound behavior.

### 04 — Final Edit

Cut and grade the final sequence.

---

## Shot 01: Burning Car — Wide

**CONCEPT:** Distant fire works best when it feels like part of the same night, not a separate effect placed behind the subject.

### Build the Look

- Use a real nighttime roadside and keep the burning car in the mid-to-far background
- Let flame, smoke and warm haze read more strongly than perfect vehicle detail
- Keep the subject visually dominant and let the fire create only restrained warm spill

### Bring it to Life

- Animate irregular flame behavior and slow smoke movement; the car itself stays completely still
- Let firelight vary gently with the flame instead of turning the whole subject orange

### Sound Design

Distant fire crackle, low night wind and quiet road ambience are enough.

### Avoid

- Perfectly sharp car
- Explosion-style fire
- Clean orange rim around the subject

---

## Shot 02: Burning Car — Close-Up

**CONCEPT:** In a close shot, the background should become more atmospheric and less descriptive.

### Build the Look

- Show only part of the distant vehicle instead of presenting the whole car clearly
- Prioritize smoke, glow and depth so the face remains the main visual anchor
- Keep the background softer and darker than the subject

### Bring it to Life

- Use small flame changes, slow smoke and a light breeze through only a few loose hair strands
- Keep the environmental light response subtle and secondary to the face

### Sound Design

Keep the fire distant in the mix. The foreground should remain intimate.

### Avoid

- Full hero car behind the face
- Heavy orange skin tone
- Smoke moving too fast

---

## Shot 03: Carnival Entrance

**CONCEPT:** The easiest way to lose the AI look is to make the carnival feel ordinary, contemporary and physically stable.

### Build the Look

- Use a real contemporary entrance with practical warm bulbs, simple booths and natural darkness
- Keep the layout slightly asymmetric and avoid making every sign or structure perfectly readable
- Let the warm practical light touch hair, leather and reflective fabric very lightly

### Bring it to Life

- Heavy signs, booths, poles and the ground remain completely rigid
- Only the light pattern around the main marquee sign moves; lightweight hanging strings may sway slightly in the breeze
- Move only a few loose hair strands. Keep the rest of the location calm

### Sound Design

Soft night wind, distant carnival activity, faint ride mechanics and a quiet electrical hum.

### Avoid

- Moving heavy structures
- All bulbs flashing together
- Fog or neon fantasy color

---

## Shot 04: Ferris Wheel

**CONCEPT:** A large object feels truly distant because of softness, atmosphere and light falloff — not just because it is huge.

### Build the Look

- Place the pale-blue Ferris wheel behind and above the subject at a believable distance
- Keep it slightly softer than the foreground and avoid a perfectly clean graphic silhouette
- Any blue influence on the subject should feel weak and environmental, not like studio lighting

### Bring it to Life

- Rotate the wheel extremely slowly with rigid geometry and stable spokes
- Keep gondolas vertical with only minimal natural sway; add only very subtle airflow in hair

### Sound Design

Distant motor hum, an occasional soft metal creak and low night wind.

### Avoid

- Crisp graphic wheel
- Strong blue rim light
- Fast rotation or bending spokes

---

## Shot 05: The Portal Door (FINALE)

**CONCEPT:** The final shot feels stronger when the impossible event is surrounded by ordinary physics.

### Build the Look

- Replace the city with a dry open field at dark twilight and place one closed freestanding door close to the woman
- Keep the pre-opening glow restrained and preserve believable hair edges, depth and environmental light
- The doorway should feel like a physical object in the field, not a graphic portal

### Bring it to Life

- The woman takes one natural step toward the door; the door opens by itself
- Reveal a bright, dimensional ocean world beyond the doorway while keeping all water on the other side
- A strong directional gust from inside affects hair, jacket edges and nearby grass in the same direction

### Sound Design

Build from quiet field ambience into door movement, strong wind and subtle distant ocean sound.

### Avoid

- Rainbow graphic beams
- Flat white portal
- Random wind directions
- Glow that erases the scene

## Realism Notes: Why Scenes Still Feel Fake

Usually the problem is not a lack of detail. It is a world that behaves too perfectly or does not physically connect to the person inside it.

### Common Problems

**TOO DESIGNED**

- Perfect symmetry, clean spacing and flawless signage quickly make a location feel generated

**TOO SHARP**

- If foreground and background are equally crisp, the image loses real lens depth

**NO INTERACTION**

- Fire, practical lights or a portal should affect nearby hair, clothing, ground and atmosphere

**EVERYTHING MOVES**

- Walls, signs and heavy structures are usually rigid. Too much motion breaks the illusion

**FAKE RIM LIGHT**

- A clean glowing outline around the person reads like compositing instead of real environmental light

**TOO MUCH ATMOSPHERE**

- Fog and glow everywhere can turn a believable location into concept art

### Fast Fix

Darken the scene slightly, reduce symmetry, soften the distance and remove unnecessary motion.

---

## Final Checklist: Before You Export

A fast pass to catch the most obvious "AI look" problems without overthinking the shot.

□ Identity and hairstyle remain consistent

□ Clothing does not morph or change unexpectedly

□ Framing and lens perspective still feel like the original shot

□ Background objects have believable distance and softness

□ Heavy structures stay rigid unless they are supposed to move

□ Wind direction is consistent across hair, fabric, smoke and grass

□ Major light sources affect nearby surfaces naturally

□ No clean glowing outline appears around the subject

□ Motion feels captured, not animated

□ Sound has believable distance and source direction

---

## Final Thought

**If an effect looks impressive but the world around it does not react, the shot will still feel fake.**

**BUILD THE WORLD. KEEP THE PERFORMANCE REAL.**

**Animation Generation:**

- Platform: Seedance 2.5
- Goal: Restrained believable motion
- Animate: Environment elements, light behavior, subtle subject movement
- Keep rigid: Heavy structures, architecture, ground

**Key Principle:**
Animate fewer elements. Real backgrounds are mostly stable.
