**Timeline/Shot Breakdown:**

0:00-0:01
The shot opens on the bare heather-grey fleece with no embroidery on it at all, nothing else in frame but cloth.

0:01-0:03
A single navy thread descends out of the air from above, its loose end hanging and swaying, and touches down on the knit. From that first contact the thread stitches the mark by itself, with nothing and no one touching it: it sinks into the fleece and rises back out over and over, laying dense satin stitch as it travels, the cloth puckering slightly around each pass, the finished stitching left solid behind it while the remaining length still floats loose above.

0:03-0:04
It fills the bold elliptical C-form first, working around its outer edge and then across its body until the shape stands solid with its crescent opening clean on the right. Then a second red thread descends and fills the half-disc inside the counter.

0:04-0:06
The navy thread then travels right and writes the script in one continuous run along the baseline, stroke following stroke in the order a hand would write it, the ascenders rising, the long tail sweeping down below the line and the final stroke flicking up at the end. The thread travels cleanly across the enclosed counters and the gaps between strokes without stitching them, leaving the bare heather-grey cloth showing through.

0:06-0:07
The last stroke completes, both threads pull taut and settle flat, the cloth relaxes and breathes slightly, and the finished mark holds, identical to the reference, for the last second of the video.
