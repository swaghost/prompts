## Pro Tips

### **Formation Design**

- Start with full group reveals to establish the crew size
- Break into smaller groups (2-4 dancers) for featured moments
- Return to full formations for maximum visual impact
- Use geometric patterns (lines, circles, triangles, V-formations)
- Create layers: foreground/midground/background dancers

### **Camera Movement Sync**

- Match camera speed to dance energy (faster for intense moments)
- Use low angles for power moves (makes them more impressive)
- Overhead shots reveal formation changes clearly
- FPV fly-throughs work best during group transitions
- Slow-motion orbits should complete 180-360 degrees

### **Lighting Rhythm**

- Pulse lighting intensity on strong beats
- Change color during musical transitions
- Use haze/fog to make light beams visible
- Create gradients across the cyclorama (not flat lighting)
- Shadows and reflections on glossy floor add depth

### **Slow-Motion Strategy**

- Place slow-motion moments at musical peaks/drops
- Each slow-mo should showcase different skill sets
- Mix group moments (Slow-Mo 01, 03, 04) with featured moments (Slow-Mo 02)
- Finale slow-motion should be the most spectacular
- Camera movement during slow-motion creates drama

### **Character Preservation**

- Use high-quality reference images (well-lit, clear facial features)
- Show full outfit in character reference (no cropping)
- Include front, 3/4 and side angles if possible
- Consistent lighting across all character references
- Verify character accuracy before long generations

### **Energy Management**

- Start strong with an impactful opening
- Build energy throughout the sequence
- Peak at the finale slow-motion moment
- Joyful expressions maintain positive energy
- Synchronized movements feel more powerful than random motion

### **Technical Quality**

- White cyclorama provides clean, professional look
- Three-color lighting scheme prevents visual chaos
- No props keeps focus on choreography
- Heavy foreground layering creates cinematic depth
- Realistic body mechanics prevent AI "floating" artifacts

## Troubleshooting

**Issue:** Characters don't maintain consistent appearance

- **Solution:** Use higher-quality reference images with clear facial features, consistent lighting, and full outfit visibility

**Issue:** Choreography looks unsynchronized

- **Solution:** Emphasize "perfectly synchronized" language, specify "every dancer moves together," use phrases like "in unison" and "synchronized timing"

**Issue:** Camera movement too chaotic

- **Solution:** Reduce number of camera movement types mentioned, emphasize "smooth" and "cinematic" rather than "rapid" and "whip pans"

**Issue:** Lighting looks flat or boring

- **Solution:** Emphasize "volumetric haze," "atmospheric fog," "light beams," and "dynamic gradients" to create visible light rays and depth

**Issue:** Slow-motion moments unclear

- **Solution:** Be very specific about when slow-motion begins/ends, describe the exact action in detail, specify camera movement during slow-motion

**Issue:** Too many dancers causing visual clutter

- **Solution:** Reduce character count to 8-15 dancers, or specify "the crew splits into smaller groups" to create visual breathing room

**Issue:** Dance styles look generic

- **Solution:** Name specific moves (Windmills, Air Freezes, Melbourne Shuffle steps), reference specific choreographers or competitions, emphasize technical precision

**Issue:** Music doesn't match choreography

- **Solution:** Specify BPM more precisely, describe rhythmic patterns, emphasize "every movement lands on the beat," describe specific musical elements
