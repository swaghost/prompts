# Multi-Character Music Video Production - Seedance 2.5

## Overview

Create premium 20-second vertical music videos featuring a charismatic lead dancer with backup performers—complete with dynamic camera work, multiple locations, choreographed transitions, and rising visual intensity from start to finish, all with photorealistic human motion and cinematic handheld energy.

**Platform:** Seedance 2.5 (1080p)  
**Format:** 9:16 vertical  
**Duration:** 20 seconds  
**Style:** High-energy commercial pop + street dance  
**Aesthetic:** Hyper-realistic, handheld music video, fast-paced with natural motion

---

## Core Concept

**What This Creates:**

A viral-style dance music video with:

- **One lead dancer** (main subject, hero of every frame)
- **6-15 backup dancers** (supporting visually, coordinated formations)
- **Multiple locations** (European streets → open plaza → crowd party → blue-hour finale)
- **Dynamic camera work** (tracking, push-ins, overhead, handheld close-ups)
- **Progressive intensity** (starts energetic, builds to explosive finale)

**Goal:**  
Feel like a real premium music video—not AI-generated, not robotic, alive and spontaneous with natural body movement, realistic clothing/hair physics, and authentic crowd energy.

## 2. Camera and Motion Rules

### 2.1 Camera Movement Vocabulary

The camera should always feel **energetic and physically present** in the dance space.

**Shot Types Used:**

1. **Wide Frontal Shots**
   - Full group visible
   - Lead centered
   - Establishes space and formation

2. **Low-Angle Medium Close-Ups**
   - Dramatic perspective
   - Lead looks powerful
   - Backup dancers visible behind

3. **Moving Close-Ups**
   - Camera tracks with subject
   - Creates momentum
   - Intimate energy

4. **Backward Tracking Shots**
   - Lead dances toward camera
   - Camera moves backward
   - Creates approaching energy

5. **Side Tracking Shots**
   - Camera moves alongside dancer
   - Walking-dance steps
   - Lateral movement energy

6. **Top-Down Overhead Shot**
   - Graphic formation view
   - Stylish and modern
   - Dancers arranged in patterns

7. **Crowd Push-In Shots**
   - Camera moves through crowd
   - Lead remains visual center
   - Immersive party energy

8. **Very Close Handheld Shots**
   - Intimate and raw
   - Slight camera shake
   - Direct eye contact
   - Face fills frame

9. **Final Hero Push-In Shot**
   - Camera comes very close to face
   - Signature ending moment
   - Confident smile into lens

---

### 2.2 Camera Motion Philosophy

**Key Rules:**

- **Never keep camera static for too long**
- Camera should move with performance and music
- Feel physically present in dance space
- Handheld, alive, dynamic

**Energy Progression:**

- First half: Slightly longer shots (2-2.5 seconds)
- Second half: Cut faster for more intensity (1-1.5 seconds)

---

### 2.3 Transition Techniques

**Use:**

- Hard cuts
- Motion-based transitions
- Body movement transitions
- Hair whip transitions (subject spins, hair covers lens)
- Natural movement blur

**Avoid:**

- Slow fades
- Digital effects
- Static cross-dissolves
- Artificial transitions

## 8. Production Tips

### Pacing Strategy

**First 10 Seconds (0:00-0:10):**

- Slightly longer shots (2-2.5 seconds)
- Establish character, location, energy
- Build momentum

**Second 10 Seconds (0:10-0:20):**

- Cut faster (1-1.5 seconds)
- Increase intensity
- Multiple locations/moments
- Peak energy
- Explosive finale

---

### Maintaining Lead Focus

**Throughout Entire Video:**

- Lead must always be hero of frame
- Even in crowd scenes, she's visual center
- Camera always finds her
- She gets all close-ups
- Backup dancers support but don't compete

**Techniques:**

- Center framing
- Depth of field (backup slightly blurred)
- Lead positioned forward
- Camera push-ins on lead only
- Lead gets signature moments

---

### Choreography Authenticity

**Natural Movement:**

- Not over-choreographed
- Moments of spontaneity (smiles, laughs)
- Natural hair movement
- Realistic body physics
- Authentic energy

**Avoid:**

- Robotic synchronization
- Stiff poses
- Unnatural timing
- Mechanical movement

---

### Camera Work Balance

**Dynamic But Not Chaotic:**

- Camera moves with purpose
- Tracks subject, pushes in, pans
- But not spinning wildly
- Handheld feel but controlled

**Match Energy:**

- Camera energy matches dance energy
- Slower moments = steadier camera
- Intense moments = more handheld shake
- Finale = aggressive push-in

## 9. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Visual Issues

❌ **Over-Polished Look**

- Too smooth, loses authenticity
- Doesn't feel like real music video

✅ **Solution:** Emphasize handheld, raw, natural motion blur

---

❌ **Static Camera**

- Camera stays still too long
- Loses energy and momentum

✅ **Solution:** Always specify camera movement, vary angles frequently

---

❌ **Backup Dancers Overpower Lead**

- Lead gets lost in crowd
- No clear hero

✅ **Solution:** Explicitly state "lead must always remain hero of frame"

---

❌ **Robotic Movement**

- Dancers look AI-generated
- Stiff, unnatural motion

✅ **Solution:** Emphasize "natural body movement," "realistic dance," "spontaneous"

---

❌ **Location Confusion**

- Not clear when/where location changes
- Jarring jumps

✅ **Solution:** Use motion transitions (hair whip), specify exact timing

---

### Timing Issues

❌ **All Shots Same Length**

- Becomes monotonous
- No intensity build

✅ **Solution:** First half longer shots, second half faster cuts

---

❌ **No Clear Finale**

- Video just stops
- No signature moment

✅ **Solution:** Explicit final hero moment (0:18.3-0:20), hard cut to black on beat
