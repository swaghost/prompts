# Mirelo — Kitchen Memory Dance Sequence

**Duration:** 30 seconds (00:00 - 00:30)  
**Format:** 1.85:1 spherical  
**Style:** Cinematic family drama with magical realism moment  
**Platform:** High-end AI video generation

---

## Technical Specifications

**Camera System:**

- Arricam LT
- Cooke S4/i primes
- 35mm Kodak Vision3 500T film stock
- T2.8 aperture
- Shallow depth of field
- Halation on highlights
- Fine organic grain
- Lifted milky blacks
- Low contrast
- No sharpening
- No HDR

**Camera Movement:**

- Handheld with micro-drift
- Never locked off
- Natural operator imperfection
- Organic breathing

**Performance:**

- Naturalistic performance
- Real dialogue sync
- No music (except diegetic song from speaker)

---

## Color Grading: "Warm Hearth Grade"

**Critical Rule:** Locked identically across every shot, never shifting with emotion.

**Color Palette:**

- **Hero Tone:** Warm 3200K tungsten on skin
- **Field Tones:** Honeyed oak worktop and cream shaker cabinetry
- **Base:** Worn oak floorboards
- **Shadow Fill:** Cool 5600K window blue holding the shadows lifted (never crushed to black)
- **Atmosphere:** Faint cooking steam and drifting flour particulate in the air

**Consistency:**

- Same white balance throughout
- Same contrast curve from first to last frame
- Same colour separation across all shots
- Grade never shifts with emotion

## Production Timeline Guidance

**Pre-Production:**

1. Cast mother and both daughters (family resemblance critical)
2. Scout kitchen location with specific lighting (warm tungsten + cool window)
3. Rehearse transformation shots extensively (motion matching)
4. Select "family song" (must feel personal, not generic)
5. Gather authentic props (nothing staged or product-like)

**Production:**

1. Shoot Shot 1 and 14 first (same setup)
2. Block transformation shots with stand-ins
3. Capture all older daughter plates
4. Capture all younger daughter plates with same camera paths
5. Shoot mother's close-up (Shot 12) last when emotion is accessible
6. Practical effects only (real flour, real steam, real smoke)

**Post-Production:**

1. Lock "Warm Hearth Grade" first across all shots
2. VFX work on transformation shots (occlusion, motion matching)
3. Sound design (diegetic only, no score)
4. Maintain 35mm film aesthetic (grain, halation, lifted blacks)
5. Final mix emphasizing naturalistic room ambience

## Conclusion

"Mirelo" is a masterclass in emotional restraint and technical precision. The magic isn't in the supernatural transformation—it's in the mother's quiet recognition that this ordinary moment of connection is extraordinary, made more precious by the knowledge that her daughter is growing up and away.

The transformation is treated as naturally as everything else in the frame because, to the mother in that instant, it feels completely real. She sees her daughter as she was and as she is, simultaneously. The film aesthetic, locked color grade, and handheld camera keep everything grounded in physical reality.

The restraint is everything: no tears fall, no music swells, the grade never shifts. Just a small smile, a faint gloss in the eyes, and the recognition that this moment—right now—is the one to remember.

The final frame mirrors the opening: same doorway, same light, same kitchen. But the empty floor is now filled with two people dancing badly, laughing hard, and the ordinary evening has become something neither will forget.

**Core Philosophy:** Magic exists in the ordinary. Transformation happens in the mundane. The most powerful moments are the ones we almost miss—until we choose to see them.
