## Overview

**Metro Reel** is a comprehensive AI video generation system for creating ultra-cinematic urban fashion films using tools like **Seedance 2.5**, **Higgsfield**, **GPT Image 2**, and **Nano Banana Pro**.

### What Makes Metro Reel Unique

- **Character Consistency**: Multi-angle character sheets ensure perfect consistency across all shots
- **Cinematic Storytelling**: 13-panel grid structure creates narrative flow and visual rhythm
- **Documentary Realism**: Handheld aesthetic with real fabric movement, grain, and authentic lighting
- **Teal + Orange Grade**: Professional cinematic color palette for social media impact
- **15-Second Format**: Optimized for Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts

### Core Workflow

```
Character Sheet (4 angles) → Storyboard (13 panels) → Video Generation (15 seconds)
```

### AI Tools Used

| Tool                         | Purpose                          | Strengths                                                                                |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Higgsfield + GPT Image 2** | Character sheet generation       | Consistent 2×2 grid, neutral studio backgrounds, fashion editorial quality               |
| **Seedance 2.5**             | Video generation from storyboard | Smooth motion, 15-second clips, character alive throughout, handheld documentary realism |
| **Nano Banana Pro**          | Alternative video generation     | Fast iteration, stylized motion                                                          |
| **GPT Image 2**              | Storyboard frame generation      | Dark cinematic editorial style, teal + orange palette                                    |

## Master Character Sheet System

### Purpose

Character sheets ensure **perfect consistency** across all 13 video shots by establishing:

- Exact physical appearance
- Outfit details (every garment, accessory, color)
- Facial features, hair, skin tone, tattoos
- Body type and proportions
- Jewelry and accessories

### Character Sheet Format

**Format**: 2×2 Grid  
**Angles**: Front • Back • Left Side Profile • Right Side Profile  
**Palette**: White Studio • Neutral  
**Style**: Fashion Editorial  
**Tool**: Higgsfield - GPT Image 2

## 13-Panel Cinematic Storyboard

### Storyboard Overview

**Format**: 13-Panel Grid  
**Style**: Dark Cinematic Editorial  
**Duration**: 15 Seconds  
**Palette**: Teal + Orange  
**Tool**: GPT Image 2

### Panel Breakdown (Shot-by-Shot)

#### **SHOT 1 — SILHOUETTE**

- **Framing**: Side profile walking LEFT TO RIGHT past backlit glass wall
- **Action**: Phone in hand
- **Lighting**: Teal rim light only
- **Mood**: Mystery, arrival
- **Duration**: ~1 second

#### **SHOT 2 — ESCALATOR FROM BELOW**

- **Framing**: LOW angle looking UP
- **Action**: She rides escalator DOWN toward camera
- **Environment**: Teal escalator railings, deep teal, reflective floor
- **Mood**: Descent into urban underworld
- **Duration**: ~1 second

#### **SHOT 3 — ESCALATOR FACE-ON**

- **Framing**: Camera at top shooting DOWN
- **Action**: She faces camera front-on, arms on railings
- **Composition**: Wide angle symmetrical, tunnel below
- **Mood**: Confrontation, confidence
- **Duration**: ~1.5 seconds

#### **SHOT 4 — ESCALATOR BACK**

- **Framing**: Full body back shot, camera behind her
- **Movement**: Camera pushes forward slowly
- **Details**: Hair cascades down, tunnel swallows frame
- **Mood**: Journey into depth
- **Duration**: ~1.5 seconds

#### **SHOT 5 — MIRROR REFLECTION**

- **Framing**: Curved convex security mirror near turnstile, camera over right shoulder
- **Action**: Her reflection visible — she glows, tilts head, studies herself
- **Environment**: Green exit signs glow in background
- **Mood**: Self-awareness, introspection
- **Duration**: ~1 second

#### **SHOT 6 — BENCH SITTING**

- **Framing**: Camera LOW front-on
- **Action**: Sits legs apart, forearms on knees, hands clasped. Stares at ground then looks up at camera
- **Environment**: Dark metal mesh wall behind, single overhead light pools on her
- **Mood**: Pensive, tired but strong
- **Duration**: ~1.5 seconds

#### **SHOT 7 — PLATFORM PHONE**

- **Framing**: Camera SIDE ANGLE, full body
- **Action**: Orange train rushes LEFT TO RIGHT in full motion blur. Wind hits — hair, shirt moves, trousers billow. She stays still
- **Mood**: Stillness amidst chaos
- **Duration**: ~1 second

#### **SHOT 8 — TRAIN BLUR**

- **Framing**: Camera LOW ANGLE looking UP
- **Action**: Full body. Orange train pulls away RIGHT TO LEFT in wide arc. 40% slow motion
- **Details**: Trousers and shirt swirl, hair fans out
- **Mood**: Wind, motion, freedom
- **Duration**: ~1.5 seconds

#### **SHOT 9 — DOOR FRAME HERO**

- **Framing**: Dead centre in open orange train doors
- **Action**: Teal carriage behind, arms loose, extremely slow push in
- **Expression**: Zero expression, total presence
- **Composition**: Orange door frame surrounds her like a portrait
- **Mood**: Iconic, powerful
- **Duration**: ~2 seconds

#### **SHOT 10 — INSIDE TRAIN**

- **Framing**: Camera handheld LOW across from her
- **Action**: Sits on teal bench, leans on window. Tunnel lights strobe across face as train moves. Touches necklace absently
- **Mood**: Solitude, reflection
- **Duration**: ~1.5 seconds

#### **SHOT 11 — PLATFORM TWIRL**

- **Framing**: Camera WIDE low angle
- **Action**: Orange train pulls away RIGHT TO LEFT. She turns — hair fans out in wide arc. SLOW MOTION 40%. Trousers and shirt swirl. Ends facing camera
- **Mood**: Graceful, cinematic
- **Duration**: ~2 seconds

#### **SHOT 12 — FACE CLOSEUP**

- **Framing**: Extreme macro — eyes, nose, necklace fill entire frame
- **Action**: Looks LEFT then stares directly into lens. One slow blink
- **Details**: Teal catchlight in eyes, real skin texture
- **Mood**: Intimacy, connection
- **Duration**: ~1 second

#### **SHOT 13 — EXIT WALK**

- **Framing**: Camera FLOOR LEVEL looking UP
- **Action**: She steps forward toward camera with authority. Orange train alongside. Walks toward lens, fills frame. FADES TO BLACK
- **Mood**: Purpose, departure, closing statement
- **Duration**: ~1.5 seconds

### Storyboard Narrative Arc

```
1. Arrival (Silhouette)
2-4. Descent (Escalator sequence)
5-6. Pause (Reflection, waiting)
7-8. Transit (Train motion)
9. Hero Moment (Door frame portrait)
10. Solitude (Inside train)
11-12. Climax (Twirl + closeup)
13. Exit (Walk to black)
```

## Platform Workflows

### Higgsfield + GPT Image 2 (Character Sheet)

**Purpose**: Generate consistent 2×2 character reference grid

**Workflow**:

1. Open Higgsfield
2. Select **GPT Image 2** model
3. Input character sheet prompt (see Master Character Sheet Prompt Template)
4. Generate 2×2 grid
5. Download high-resolution version
6. Use as reference for all subsequent video generation

**Settings**:

- Aspect Ratio: 1:1 (square for 2×2 grid)
- Quality: Maximum
- Style: Fashion editorial, clean studio

**Pro Tip**: Generate multiple character sheet variations with different outfits, then mix and match for different Metro Reel stories.

---

### GPT Image 2 (Storyboard Generation)

**Purpose**: Generate 13-panel cinematic storyboard grid

**Workflow**:

1. Open GPT Image 2 (via ChatGPT or API)
2. Input storyboard grid prompt with all 13 shot descriptions
3. Specify: "13-panel grid layout, dark cinematic editorial style, teal + orange color palette"
4. Generate storyboard
5. Download and attach as reference for Seedance

**Settings**:

- Style: Dark cinematic editorial
- Palette: Teal + Orange
- Format: Multi-panel grid (13 shots)

**Pro Tip**: Request "numbered panels" in the prompt to clearly identify each shot.

---

### Seedance 2.5 (Video Generation)

**Purpose**: Generate 15-second continuous video from storyboard reference

**Workflow**:

1. Open Seedance 2.5
2. **Attach References**:
   - Upload 13-panel storyboard as primary reference
   - Upload character sheet for consistency
3. **Input Master Video Prompt**:

   ```
   15-SECOND CINEMATIC URBAN FASHION FILM

   Location: Dark realistic underground metro subway
   Camera: Sony FX3 • 35mm • f1.8 • Shallow DOF • Film grain • Teal-orange LUT
   Style: Handheld documentary • EVERY shot different angle • Character ALIVE throughout
   ```

4. **Add Shot-by-Shot Details**: Paste all 13 shot prompts in sequence
5. **Add Color Grade Instructions**:
   ```
   COLOR GRADE: Teal shadows • Orange highlights • Crushed blacks • Film grain ISO 3200
   Zero beauty filter • Real skin texture • Real fabric movement • Documentary realism
   ```
6. **Generate**: Set duration to 15 seconds, generate
7. **Review**: Check character consistency, motion smoothness, color grade
8. **Iterate**: Regenerate specific shots if needed

**Seedance 2.5 Settings**:

- Duration: 15 seconds
- Motion: Medium to high (for handheld documentary feel)
- Consistency: High (use character sheet reference)
- Style: Documentary realism, handheld throughout

**Pro Tip**: If Seedance struggles with 13 shots in 15 seconds, split into 3 clips:

- Clip 1: Shots 1-4 (Arrival & descent) — 5 seconds
- Clip 2: Shots 5-9 (Pause & hero moment) — 5 seconds
- Clip 3: Shots 10-13 (Solitude & exit) — 5 seconds
  Then stitch together in post.

## Camera Movement Library

### Static Shots

#### **Locked-Off Frame**

- Camera completely still
- Subject moves through frame
- Best for: Establishing shots, hero moments (Shot 9 door frame)

#### **Handheld Static**

- Slight natural camera shake
- Subject relatively still
- Best for: Documentary realism, intimate moments (Shot 6 bench)

---

### Dynamic Movements

#### **Slow Push In**

- Camera slowly moves toward subject
- Builds tension and focus
- Best for: Emotional climax, hero moments (Shot 9)

#### **Slow Pull Out**

- Camera slowly moves away from subject
- Reveals environment, creates isolation
- Best for: Establishing context, ending shots

#### **Dolly/Track Left or Right**

- Camera moves laterally
- Follows subject or reveals space
- Best for: Walking shots, transitions

#### **Crane/Pedestal Up or Down**

- Camera moves vertically
- Dramatic reveal or perspective shift
- Best for: Escalator shots (Shots 2, 3, 4)

#### **Arc Around Subject**

- Camera circles around subject
- 360° reveal, dynamic energy
- Best for: Hero moments, fashion showcase

---

### Handheld Movements

#### **Documentary Follow**

- Camera moves naturally with subject
- Slight shake, human feel
- Best for: Inside train (Shot 10), immersive shots

#### **Handheld Whip Pan**

- Fast horizontal camera movement
- Motion blur transition
- Best for: Cutting between shots, energy

## Expression & Performance Guide

### Expression Palette (13 Key Expressions)

#### **1. Absorbed (Shot 1 - Silhouette)**

- Eyes down or straight ahead
- Neutral mouth
- In her own world, unaware of camera
- **Direction**: "You're lost in thought, phone in hand, just passing through."

#### **2. Distracted, Glances Up (Shot 2 - Escalator Down)**

- Eyes on phone, then lifts gaze with curiosity
- Slight eyebrow raise

### Performance Notes for AI Video Generation

When specifying expressions in prompts:

✅ **DO**:

- Use specific emotional descriptors ("pensive," "introspective," "unbothered")
- Describe physical cues ("eyes lift to camera," "hand touches necklace," "slight chin lift")
- Reference body language ("statue-like stillness," "determined stride")

❌ **DON'T**:

- Use vague terms ("looks good," "appears nice")
- Over-specify micro-expressions AI may struggle with ("left eyebrow raised 2mm")
- Request complex sequential emotions within one shot

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

### Issue 1: Character Inconsistency Across Shots

**Problem**: Face or outfit changes between shots  
**Solution**:

- Ensure character sheet is attached as reference for every generation
- Use same seed/consistency token if platform supports it
- Regenerate inconsistent shots individually with stronger character description
- Consider generating in batches (Shots 1-4, 5-9, 10-13) with same session

---

### Issue 2: Motion Blur Too Aggressive or Absent

**Problem**: Train blur (Shots 7, 8, 11) looks static or over-blurred  
**Solution**:

- Specify exact motion blur level: "full motion blur, 100% speed" or "40% slow motion"
- Add reference to shutter speed: "180° shutter at 25fps for natural motion blur"
- If too blurred: specify "subject sharp, background blurred"
- If not blurred enough: specify "train rushing past at full speed, complete motion blur"

---

### Issue 3: Color Grade Not Matching Teal + Orange

**Problem**: Colors look default or wrong palette  
**Solution**:

- Re-emphasize in prompt: "COLOR GRADE: Deep teal shadows (#0A3A3A), warm orange highlights (#FF8040)"
- Add: "Crushed blacks, high contrast, cinematic LUT applied"
- Use reference image of correct color grade
- Specify "zero beauty filter, real skin texture" to avoid overly smooth/pink skin

---

### Issue 4: Camera Angle Wrong

**Problem**: Low angle shot rendered as eye-level, etc.  
**Solution**:

- Use precise language: "Camera FLOOR LEVEL looking UP" instead of "low angle"
- Add camera height specification: "camera at 0.3 meters above ground"
- Reference real-world camera positions: "fisheye lens 6 inches above floor"

---

### Issue 5: Expression Too Neutral or Wrong Emotion

**Problem**: Subject expression doesn't match shot intent  
**Solution**:

- Use specific emotional descriptors: "pensive, tired but strong" not just "looks at camera"
- Describe physical cues: "eyes lift slowly to camera, slight furrow in brow"
- Add performance direction: "she stares into lens with quiet intensity, one slow blink"
- Reference acting: "documentary portrait, raw, unposed"

---

### Issue 6: Environment Looks Generic or Wrong

**Problem**: Doesn't look like metro/subway, or too clean/modern  
**Solution**:

- Add specific details: "dark realistic underground metro subway, teal tile walls, reflective concrete floor"
- Reference real locations: "NYC subway aesthetic" or "London Underground style"
- Specify wear and tear: "industrial fluorescent lighting, metal mesh walls, worn benches"

---

### Issue 7: Slow-Motion Not Working

**Problem**: Shots 8 and 11 don't appear slowed down  
**Solution**:

- Specify exact slow-mo percentage: "40% slow motion" or "100fps played back at 25fps"
- Describe visual result: "hair fans out in wide arc, trousers swirl, train pulls away slowly"
- Avoid term "slow motion" alone—add visual descriptors of what slow-mo achieves

---

### Issue 8: Video Duration Wrong

**Problem**: Generated clip too short or too long  
**Solution**:

- Specify exact duration per shot in prompt (e.g., "SHOT 1 — 1sec")
- If platform limits to shorter clips, split into segments and stitch in post
- For 15-second total: confirm all 13 shot durations add up correctly

## Pro Tips & Best Practices

### 1. Always Generate Character Sheet First

Never skip this step. Character consistency is the foundation of Metro Reel quality.

### 2. Use Numbered Storyboard Panels

Request "numbered panels 1-13" in storyboard generation so you can reference specific shots easily.

### 3. Attach Both References to Seedance

Character sheet + storyboard = maximum consistency and accuracy.

### 4. Specify Expression in Every Shot

Don't leave it to chance. "Absorbed, in her own world" is better than "looking forward."

### 5. Separate Static from Motion Shots

Clearly mark which shots are locked-off (1, 3, 5, 6, 7) vs. moving camera (4, 9, 10).

### 6. Use Real Camera Terminology

"Sony FX3, 35mm, f/1.8" triggers cinematic aesthetic in AI models better than "good camera."

### 7. Emphasize "ALIVE Throughout"

Add "character ALIVE, natural movement, not posed" to prevent stiff, mannequin-like results.

### 8. Crushed Blacks Are Key

Specify "crushed blacks" not just "dark shadows" for true cinematic look.

### 9. Film Grain Adds Realism

"ISO 3200 film grain" prevents overly-smooth AI skin texture.

### 10. Test Color Grade Early

Generate one shot first to verify color grade matches intent before rendering all 13.

### 11. Split Long Videos if Needed

If 15 seconds struggles, do 3×5-second clips and stitch in post (CapCut, Premiere, etc.).

### 12. Sound Design Matters

Add metro ambience, train sounds, footsteps in post—audio makes or breaks realism.

### 13. Slow-Mo Shots Need Extra Time

Shots 8 and 11 (slow-motion) may need longer duration to appear slowed down properly.

### 14. Reference Real Metro Stations

Search "NYC subway photography" or "Tokyo metro" for lighting and composition inspiration.

### 15. Avoid Over-Prompting

Too many details confuse AI. Prioritize: character, action, camera, lighting, color.

### 16. Regenerate Problem Shots Individually

Don't regenerate entire 15-second video—just replace the 1-2 shots that didn't work.

### 17. Save Successful Seeds/Settings

If a generation works perfectly, save all settings for future replication.

### 18. Iterate Expressions Separately

Test different expressions for key shots (9, 12) with quick iterations before final render.

### 19. Lighting Consistency Is Visual Glue

Make sure overhead fluorescent + teal tone appears in every metro shot.

### 20. Use "Zero Beauty Filter"

Specify "zero beauty filter, real skin texture" to avoid AI over-smoothing faces.

### 21. Wide Shots Establish, Closeups Reveal

Balance wide environmental shots (1, 3, 11) with intimate closeups (12).

### 22. Train Motion = Energy

Shots with train blur (7, 8, 11) add energy—use them for climax moments.

### 23. Negative Space = Cinematic

Don't fill every frame—let environment breathe around subject.

### 24. End Strong

Shot 13 (exit walk, fade to black) is your closing statement—make it authoritative.

### 25. Color Grade in Post if Needed

If AI color isn't perfect, use LUTs in post (DaVinci Resolve, Premiere Pro).

### 26. Match Audio to Visuals

Teal+orange grade pairs well with electronic/ambient music. Adjust audio genre to match color mood.

### 27. Add Text Overlays Sparingly

If adding text (captions, brand name), keep it minimal—don't compete with visuals.

### 28. Test on Mobile First

Most viewers watch vertical video on phones—preview on mobile before publishing.

### 29. Thumbnail = Shot 9

Use the hero door frame shot (9) as thumbnail—most iconic frame.

### 30. Version Control Your Prompts

Save every prompt variation in a document. Track what works for future projects.
