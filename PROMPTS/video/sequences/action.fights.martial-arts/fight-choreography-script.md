# Martial Arts Fight Choreography Script

## Description

Create ultra-detailed, frame-by-frame martial arts fight choreography scripts with precise timestamp breakdowns. This prompt generates comprehensive fight sequences with specific character actions, combat techniques, injuries, environmental details, and dramatic progression—perfect for action filmmaking, animation storyboarding, game cinematics, or martial arts content planning.

## Usage

Ideal for planning realistic fight sequences with multiple opponents, progressive injury accumulation, environmental storytelling, and cinematic pacing. Each timestamp segment contains specific character positions, techniques executed, impact descriptions, injuries sustained, and scene atmosphere changes. Perfect for directors, fight coordinators, animators, and game designers.

## Prerequisites

- Clear fight scenario (characters, setting, objectives)
- Desired fight duration and pacing
- Character capabilities and fighting styles
- Environment details and props
- Outcome and dramatic arc

## Example Fight Script

**Scene:** Industrial warehouse fight - Main character vs. three opponents (Striker, Pipe, Bruiser)
**Duration:** 15 seconds
**Style:** Realistic martial arts with progressive injuries
**Atmosphere:** Orange dust-filled darkness, tense survival

### [00:00-00:01]

Main character stands center.
Striker stands three meters ahead.
Both breathe calmly.
Dust fills orange darkness.
Pipe is not visible.
Bruiser is not visible.

### [00:01-00:03]

Striker rushes forward.
Main character pivots left foot.
Main character performs spinning kick.
Bare foot hits jaw.
Striker flies backward away.
Striker exits the frame.
Striker never appears again.

### [00:03-00:04]

Pipe enters from side.
He swings rusty pipe.
Pipe hits left ribs.
Shirt tears at ribs.
Red scrape appears there.
No new face wound.

### [00:04-00:06]

Main character staggers backward hard.
He drops one knee.
He holds injured ribs.
His breathing becomes heavy.
He slowly lifts head.
Determined eyes look forward.
He stands up again.
Only main character visible.

### [00:06-00:08]

Pipe attacks again.
Main character steps forward quickly.
Main character knees Pipe's stomach.
Metal pipe falls down.
Main character follows spinning kick.
Kick hits Pipe's jaw.
Pipe flies out frame.
Pipe never appears again.

### [00:08-00:09]

Bruiser appears first time.
He exits dark doorway.
Main character turns toward him.
Main character holds injured ribs.
Only main character and Bruiser.

### [00:09-00:12]

Bruiser throws three punches.
First punch hits cheek.
Left cheek gets scraped.
Second punch hits ribs.
Right ribs get scraped.
Third uppercut hits jaw.
Small jaw mark appears.
Main character falls backward down.
He hits concrete floor.
Wounds stay correctly placed.

### [00:12-00:14]

Main character pushes himself up.
He wipes his face.
Main character stands up again.
He steps forward firmly.
Right knee rises first.
Main character jumps into air.
He rotates 180 degrees.
Taekwondo flying back kick.
Right foot hits Bruiser.
Impact strikes left jaw.

### [00:14-00:15]

Bruiser flies backward hard.
He crashes into wall.
He slides down unconscious.
Main character lands and stands.
He breathes very heavily.
All wounds remain visible.
Main character looks at corner.
Scene ends in silence.

## Prompt Template

Create a detailed fight choreography script for [CHARACTER] fighting [NUMBER] opponents in [SETTING]. The fight should last [DURATION] and follow [STYLE] martial arts. Break down the sequence into precise timestamp segments, describing:

**For Each Timestamp Segment ([MM:SS-MM:SS]):**

1. **Character Positions** - Where each character stands or moves
2. **Initiating Action** - Who attacks/defends and how
3. **Technique Execution** - Specific martial arts techniques (spinning kick, uppercut, flying back kick, etc.)
4. **Impact Description** - Where strikes land (jaw, ribs, stomach, cheek)
5. **Physical Results** - How opponent reacts (flies backward, staggers, falls, etc.)
6. **Injuries Sustained** - Specific wounds that appear (scrapes, marks, tears) and their locations
7. **Injury Continuity** - All previous wounds remain visible and correctly placed
8. **Character State** - Breathing, posture, determination, exhaustion
9. **Environmental Details** - Lighting, atmosphere, props, dust, darkness
10. **Exit/Elimination** - When opponents are defeated and leave frame permanently
11. **Frame Presence** - Which characters are visible in current moment

**Fight Progression Requirements:**

- Progressive fatigue and injury accumulation for main character
- Realistic impact physics (characters fly back, stagger, fall)
- Wound continuity throughout entire sequence
- Specific martial arts technique names
- Clear opponent elimination (exits frame, never returns)
- Environmental storytelling (dust, darkness, doorways)
- Breathing and physical state changes
- Determined recovery moments
- Silent dramatic pauses
- Realistic combat pacing

**Technical Specifications:**

- Timestamp format: [MM:SS-MM:SS]
- Duration segments: 1-4 seconds each
- Action clarity: One action per line
- Injury tracking: Cumulative and persistent
- Character focus: Clear visibility notes
- Spatial awareness: Positions and distances
- Prop interaction: Objects enter/exit/fall
- Lighting atmosphere: Consistent tone

- **Realistic Gritty:** Heavy breathing, exhaustion, messy impacts
- **Martial Arts Cinema:** Clean techniques, stylized movements, heroic recovery
- **Brutal Survival:** Desperate moves, improvised weapons, no rules

**Injury Detail Levels:**

- **Minimal:** Basic impact notation only
- **Moderate:** Visible marks, scrapes, bruising (default in example)
- **Detailed:** Blood, swelling, torn clothing, progressive damage
- **Extreme:** Graphic wounds, broken bones, realistic trauma (mature content)
- **None:** Clean fight with no visible damage

## Best Practices

**For Realistic Fight Choreography:**

1. **Respect Physics and Timing**
   - Strikes take time to execute
   - Bodies react realistically to impact
   - Recovery requires moments
   - Fatigue accumulates progressively
   - Distance and positioning matter

2. **Maintain Injury Continuity**
   - Track all wounds from their appearance
   - Injuries affect subsequent performance
   - Pain shows in breathing and movement
   - Wounds remain visible throughout
   - Cumulative damage tells story

3. **Use Specific Technique Names**
   - "Spinning kick" not just "kick"
   - "Uppercut" not just "punch"
   - "Flying back kick" not "jump attack"
   - "Taekwondo" style specification
   - Authentic martial arts terminology

4. **Control Character Presence**
   - Note who is visible each moment
   - Track opponent eliminations clearly
   - Manage entrances and exits
   - Maintain spatial awareness
   - Avoid confusion with multiple fighters

5. **Build Dramatic Arc**
   - Start calm, build tension
   - Show struggle and determination
   - Include recovery moments
   - Progressive difficulty increase
   - Satisfying resolution or cliffhanger

6. **Environmental Integration**
   - Use setting details consistently
   - Props enter, interact, and exit clearly
   - Lighting and atmosphere remain stable
   - Environmental damage can occur
   - Space affects fighting options

**Timestamp Breakdown Tips:**

- **1-second segments:** Single decisive actions (knockout blow)
- **2-second segments:** Quick exchanges (punch combo, dodge and counter)
- **3-second segments:** Complex techniques (spinning kick setup and execution)
- **4-second segments:** Multi-step sequences (opponent enters, attacks, gets countered)

**Common Pitfalls to Avoid:**

- Vague technique descriptions ("he hits him")
- Inconsistent wound placement
- Unrealistic recovery speeds
- Confusing character positions
- Too many simultaneous actions
- Missing injury continuity
- Unclear opponent status
- Impossible timing compression

## Advanced Techniques

**Multi-Opponent Management:**

Stagger opponent appearances to maintain focus:

- **Wave 1:** Striker attacks first (00:01-00:03)
- **Wave 2:** Pipe enters while Striker eliminated (00:03-00:08)
- **Wave 3:** Bruiser appears after Pipe defeated (00:08-00:15)

**Camera Direction Suggestions:**

Though not explicitly included in script, consider noting:

- Close-up during facial impacts
- Wide shot during flying kicks
- Medium during struggle moments
- Low angle for dramatic standing up
- Tracking shot during rapid exchanges

**Sound Design Cues:**

Implicit in choreography but can be noted:

- Heavy breathing sounds
- Impact sounds (crack, thud, crash)
- Pipe clanging as it hits and falls
- Body hitting concrete floor
- Unconscious body sliding down wall
- Silence for dramatic punctuation

**Editing Rhythm:**

Timestamp segments suggest cut points:

- Quick cuts during rapid exchanges (1-2 sec segments)
- Held shots during recovery (3-4 sec segments)
- Dramatic pause after final blow
- Slow-motion opportunities (flying kick)

## Technical Notes

**Fight Duration Planning:**

- **5-10 seconds:** Single opponent, decisive victory
- **10-15 seconds:** 2-3 opponents, challenging fight (example above)
- **15-25 seconds:** 3-5 opponents or difficult single opponent
- **25-40 seconds:** Extended battle, multiple waves, endurance test
- **40+ seconds:** Epic confrontation, boss fight, climactic showdown

**Realistic Timing Benchmarks:**

- Punch: 0.2-0.5 seconds execution
- Kick: 0.5-1.0 seconds execution
- Flying kick: 1.0-1.5 seconds execution
- Recovery from hit: 1-3 seconds minimum
- Standing up from ground: 2-4 seconds
- Knockout opponent exit: 1-2 seconds

**Martial Arts Style Characteristics:**

- **Taekwondo:** High kicks, spinning techniques, aerial moves
- **Boxing:** Punches only, footwork, head movement
- **Muay Thai:** Eight-point striking, clinch, elbows, knees
- **MMA:** Mixed techniques, ground game, submissions
- **Kung Fu:** Diverse styles, flowing movements, animal forms
- **Karate:** Linear strikes, powerful impacts, katas
- **Capoeira:** Acrobatic, rhythmic, disguised as dance
