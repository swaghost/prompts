# Cinematic Composition Rules Reference

Composition is the organization of subjects, negative space, lines, depth, scale, and visual weight inside the frame. Use a rule to direct attention and express spatial or emotional meaning; treat the rules as flexible tools rather than mandatory formulas.

## Composition Prompt Formula

`composition rule` + `subject placement and scale` + `foreground/middle/background structure` + `leading lines or geometry` + `negative space and visual weight` + `camera angle and aspect ratio` + `story purpose`.

Place the primary subject or horizon near a thirds intersection or dividing line. Leave directional space for gaze or movement.

**Prompt:** `Compose using the rule of thirds, place the subject on the left or right third, reserve directional negative space, align the horizon deliberately, balanced cinematic framing.`

![Golden ratio SVG reference](COMPOSITION.GOLDEN-RATIO.svg)

![Golden ratio explanatory graphic](COMPOSITION.GOLDEN-RATIO.EXPL.jpeg.jpeg)

Arrange the dominant subject and supporting visual path along a proportional spiral or golden-section flow.

**Prompt:** `Use golden-ratio composition, place the subject at the visual convergence point, guide the eye through a curved proportional flow, elegant layered framing, controlled negative space.`

### Centered Composition

![Centered composition example](COMPOSITION.CENTERED.jpg)

![Centered composition SVG reference](COMPOSITION.CENTERED.svg)

Place the main subject on the central vertical axis when authority, ritual, confrontation, symmetry, or iconic stillness is desired.

**Prompt:** `Use centered cinematic composition, subject aligned to the vertical axis, deliberate symmetry, stable horizon, controlled headroom, iconic stillness.`

### Symmetry

![Symmetry example](COMPOSITION.SYMMETRY.jpg)

![Symmetry SVG reference](COMPOSITION.SYMMETRY.svg)

![Symmetry explanatory graphic](COMPOSITION.SYMMETRY.EXPL.jpeg)

Mirror or balance the left and right sides of the frame through architecture, bodies, light, or repeated shapes.

**Prompt:** `Use strong bilateral symmetry, mirror the architecture and visual weight around the subject, centered perspective, precise geometry, restrained movement.`

### Asymmetry

![Asymmetry example](COMPOSITION.ASYMMETRY.jpg)

![Asymmetry SVG reference](COMPOSITION.ASYMMETRY.svg)

Use unequal visual weight, off-center placement, and deliberate imbalance to create tension, instability, or openness.

**Prompt:** `Use deliberate asymmetrical composition, place the subject off-center, counterbalance with a smaller distant element, preserve visual tension and intentional empty space.`

### Balance

![Balance example](COMPOSITION.BALANCE.jpg)

![Balance SVG comparison](COMPOSITION.BALANCE-COMPARISON.svg)

Distribute visual weight through size, contrast, color, texture, and position so the frame feels stable or intentionally unstable.

**Prompt:** `Balance the frame through subject scale, tonal contrast, color weight, and counterpoint objects; create stable visual equilibrium without flattening depth.`

### Visual Weight

![Visual weight example](COMPOSITION.VISUAL-WEIGHT.jpg)

![Visual weight SVG map](COMPOSITION.VISUAL-WEIGHT-MAP.svg)

Control where attention lands by adjusting brightness, saturation, scale, sharpness, faces, contrast, and proximity to the frame edge.

**Prompt:** `Prioritize visual weight on the subject's face and hands through sharpness, contrast, and brightness; subordinate secondary elements with lower contrast and softer detail.`

### Leading Lines

![Leading lines example](COMPOSITION.LEADING-LINES.jpg)

![Leading lines SVG reference](COMPOSITION.LEADING-LINES.svg)

Use roads, rails, architecture, shadows, limbs, or perspective edges to lead the eye toward the subject or destination.

**Prompt:** `Use strong leading lines from the foreground toward the subject, converge architectural edges near the face, preserve depth and directional flow, cinematic perspective.`

### Diagonal Lines

![Diagonal lines example](COMPOSITION.DIAGONAL-LINES.jpg)

### S-Curve

![S-curve example](COMPOSITION.S-CURVE.jpg)

![S-curve SVG reference](COMPOSITION.S-CURVE.svg)

Use a winding path, body line, river, road, or visual sequence that guides the eye through an elegant curved route.

**Prompt:** `Use an S-curve composition to guide the eye from foreground to subject and into the background, graceful spatial rhythm, layered depth, controlled visual flow.`

### Triangular Composition

![Triangular composition example](COMPOSITION.TRIANGULAR_COMPOSITION.jpg)

![Triangular composition SVG reference](COMPOSITION.TRIANGULAR.svg)

Arrange three subjects or visual anchors as a triangle to stabilize relationships, hierarchy, or conflict.

### Depth

![Depth example](COMPOSITION.DEPTH.jpg)

![Depth SVG three-plane reference](COMPOSITION.DEPTH-THREE-PLANES.svg)

Separate foreground, middle ground, and background to create spatial volume and narrative layering.

**Prompt:** `Build strong cinematic depth with a soft foreground frame, sharply staged middle-ground subject, and readable background action; maintain clear spatial hierarchy.`

### Layering

![Layering example](COMPOSITION.LAYERING.jpg)

![Layering SVG reference](COMPOSITION.LAYERING.svg)

**Prompt:** `Use layered composition with distinct foreground, middle-ground, and background actions, preserve readable overlaps, let secondary information remain discoverable without competing with the subject.`

### Minimalism

![Minimalism example](COMPOSITION.MINIMALISM.jpg)

Reduce competing forms, colors, textures, and actions so one subject or gesture carries the frame.

**Prompt:** `Use minimalist composition with one dominant subject, restrained palette, uncluttered background, precise silhouette, generous breathing room, every remaining object intentional.`

Allow the subject, texture, or action to press toward the frame edges, increasing intimacy, pressure, or sensory immersion.

**Prompt:** `Fill the frame with the subject, crop close to the edges without cutting essential features, emphasize texture and expression, high visual immediacy, minimal empty space.`

## Definitions And New Graphics For Previously Missing Rules

### Headroom And Lead Room

![Headroom and lead room diagram](COMPOSITION.HEADROOM-LEAD-ROOM.svg)

**Headroom** is the intentional space between the top of a subject's head and the upper frame edge. **Lead room** is the space in front of a subject's gaze or movement. Too little headroom feels cramped; too little lead room makes a moving or looking subject appear blocked.

**Prompt:** `Maintain intentional headroom above the head and generous lead room in the direction of gaze or movement; keep the subject comfortably inside the frame without crowding.`

### Look Room / Nose Room

![Look room and nose room diagram](COMPOSITION.LOOK-ROOM-NOSE-ROOM.svg)

Look room, also called nose room, is the visual space placed in front of a person's face and gaze. It gives the viewer a destination for the character's attention and prevents the face from pressing into the frame edge.

**Prompt:** `Place the subject with clear look room in front of the eyes and nose, preserve directional psychological space, and avoid crowding the face against the frame edge.`

### Horizon Line Placement

![Horizon line placement diagram](COMPOSITION.HORIZON-LINE.svg)

The horizon line controls the balance between sky and ground and can alter perceived scale, stability, and power. A high horizon emphasizes the ground or foreground; a low horizon gives the sky and subject more grandeur; a tilted horizon introduces instability.

**Prompt:** `Place the horizon deliberately at the upper third, lower third, or eye level according to the story; keep it level for stability or intentionally tilted for disorientation.`

### Eye-Line Match

![Eye-line match diagram](COMPOSITION.EYE-LINE-MATCH.svg)

An eye-line match aligns the apparent gaze direction between characters or between a character and an offscreen object. It establishes spatial relationship; a broken or mismatched eye-line can create confusion, alienation, or deliberate psychological rupture.

**Prompt:** `Align the characters' eye-lines across the composition to establish a clear spatial relationship; use a deliberate mismatch only to create disorientation or emotional separation.`

### Screen Direction / 180-Degree Axis

![180-degree axis and screen direction diagram](COMPOSITION.SCREEN-DIRECTION-180-DEGREE.svg)

The 180-degree rule keeps the camera on one side of an implied action axis so subjects maintain consistent screen direction. Crossing the axis reverses positions and can confuse geography unless the crossing is shown or deliberately motivated.

**Prompt:** `Maintain the 180-degree axis and consistent screen direction across the shot sequence; cross the line only through a visible camera move or a motivated psychological rupture.`

### Size Contrast / Scale Hierarchy

![Scale hierarchy diagram](COMPOSITION.SCALE-HIERARCHY.svg)

Scale hierarchy uses relative size, distance, and framing to establish importance, power, intimacy, or social distance. A dominant foreground figure can overpower a smaller background figure without dialogue.

**Prompt:** `Create a clear scale hierarchy with a dominant foreground subject, a secondary mid-ground figure, and a smaller distant figure; use size and distance to express power and relationship.`

### Figure-Ground Separation

![Figure-ground separation diagram](COMPOSITION.FIGURE-GROUND.svg)

Figure-ground separation makes the primary subject readable against its environment through contrast, edge light, color difference, depth of field, silhouette, or simplified background detail.

**Prompt:** `Separate the subject from the background through tonal contrast, edge light, color contrast, silhouette, and depth of field; keep the figure immediately readable without a cutout effect.`

### Open And Closed Composition

![Open and closed composition diagram](COMPOSITION.OPEN-CLOSED-FRAME.svg)

An **open composition** suggests that the world continues beyond the frame, often allowing subjects or movement to enter and leave. A **closed composition** contains the important information within the borders and can feel controlled, trapped, complete, or self-contained.

**Prompt:** `Choose an open composition with elements entering or leaving the frame for expansion and uncertainty, or a closed composition that contains the visual information for control and confinement.`

### Tangent Control

![Tangent control diagram](COMPOSITION.TANGENT-CONTROL.svg)

A tangent occurs when a line, edge, prop, or another body appears to touch or merge awkwardly with a subject's head, limbs, or silhouette. Tangents flatten depth and create accidental visual confusion.

**Prompt:** `Audit the frame for tangents; keep architecture, props, and background lines from touching the subject's head or limbs accidentally, using clean separation or deliberate overlap only.`

### Chiaroscuro Composition

![Chiaroscuro composition hierarchy diagram](COMPOSITION.CHIAROSCURO-HIERARCHY.svg)

Chiaroscuro composition uses the geometry of light and shadow to establish hierarchy. The illuminated zone attracts attention while shadow becomes negative space, concealment, or a competing emotional force.

**Prompt:** `Compose through a deliberate light-dark structure, place the primary subject in the controlled zone of illumination, use shadow as negative space, and preserve readable tonal hierarchy.`

## Practical Selection Guide

| Story objective           | Start with                                                     |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Authority or ritual       | Centered composition, symmetry, visual weight                  |
| Vulnerability or solitude | Negative space, asymmetry, small scale, depth                  |
| Movement or danger        | Diagonal lines, leading lines, S-curve, fill the frame         |
| Relationship and conflict | Triangular composition, eye-line match, layering, rule of odds |
| Discovery or revelation   | Framing within frame, depth, background layers, golden ratio   |
| Calm or contemplation     | Balance, minimalism, centered composition, open space          |
| Crowds or social systems  | Patterns, repetition, rule of odds, layering                   |

## Hard Negatives

`No accidental tangents, no clipped faces or hands unless intentional, no empty space on the wrong side of gaze or motion, no competing focal points, no broken screen direction, no horizon intersecting the subject awkwardly, no arbitrary rule application without story purpose.`
