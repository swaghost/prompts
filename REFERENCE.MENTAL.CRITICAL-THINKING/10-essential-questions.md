# 10 Essential Critical Thinking Questions

A comprehensive framework of 10 fundamental questions to develop rigorous critical thinking and analytical reasoning.

## The Questions

### 1. What do you mean by that?

**Purpose:** Clarification and definition

- Seek precise meaning
- Avoid ambiguity
- Define key terms
- Ensure shared understanding

**Usage:**

- When terms are vague or unclear
- To prevent talking past each other
- To establish common ground
- Before proceeding with argument

**Example:**
"When you say 'success,' do you mean financial wealth, personal fulfillment, or something else?"

---

### 2. How do you know that's true?

**Purpose:** Evaluate evidence and justification

- Assess basis for belief
- Examine sources
- Check reasoning
- Verify claims

**Usage:**

- Challenge unsupported assertions
- Distinguish knowledge from opinion
- Evaluate credibility
- Test epistemic grounds

**Example:**
"You claim X causes Y—what evidence supports this causal relationship?"

---

### 3. What's the counter-argument?

**Purpose:** Consider opposing views

- Intellectual honesty
- Steel-man opposition
- Avoid one-sided thinking
- Strengthen own position

**Usage:**

- Before concluding
- To test robustness
- To find weak points
- To demonstrate fairness

**Example:**
"What would someone who disagrees with this policy say, and how would they justify their view?"

---

### 4. What are you assuming here?

**Purpose:** Expose hidden premises

- Identify unstated beliefs
- Question presuppositions
- Reveal conceptual framework
- Check for granted assumptions

**Usage:**

- When something doesn't follow
- To find hidden premises
- To challenge frameworks
- To reveal worldviews

**Example:**
"You're assuming people are rational actors—but what if they're not?"

---

### 5. What evidence supports this?

**Purpose:** Demand empirical grounding

- Require proof
- Distinguish speculation from fact
- Assess data quality
- Proportion belief to evidence

**Usage:**

- Evaluate factual claims
- Check for cherry-picking
- Assess strength of support
- Avoid anecdotal reasoning

**Example:**
"What studies demonstrate this effect? How large was the sample? Has it been replicated?"

---

### 6. What are the implications?

**Purpose:** Trace consequences

- Follow logic forward
- Second-order effects
- Unintended consequences
- Practical outcomes

**Usage:**

- Test consistency
- Reveal absurd conclusions
- Consider side effects
- Think strategically

**Example:**
"If we accept this principle, what else follows? Are we willing to accept those implications?"

---

### 7. Why does this matter?

**Purpose:** Establish relevance and significance

- Connect to what's important
- Assess practical value
- Distinguish trivial from crucial
- Motivate inquiry

**Usage:**

- Prioritize focus
- Justify effort
- Connect to values
- Show stakes

**Example:**
"Even if true, why should we care? What difference does it make to the decision at hand?"

---

### 8. Is there another way to see this?

**Purpose:** Encourage perspectivism

- Multiple viewpoints
- Reframe the issue
- Creative alternatives
- Escape mental ruts

**Usage:**

- Break impasses
- Generate options
- Challenge frames
- Find synthesis

**Example:**
"You see this as a conflict, but could we view it as a misunderstanding? As an opportunity?"

---

### 9. What would change your mind?

**Purpose:** Test falsifiability and openness

- Avoid dogmatism
- Ensure epistemic humility
- Identify decision criteria
- Distinguish belief from faith

**Usage:**

- Check for rationality
- Test genuine belief
- Find crux of disagreement
- Establish falsifiability

**Example:**
"What evidence, if you encountered it, would lead you to abandon this view?"

---

### 10. How could this be improved?

**Purpose:** Constructive critique

- Move beyond criticism
- Generate better alternatives
- Refine and iterate
- Problem-solving orientation

**Usage:**

- After critique
- To be constructive
- To collaborate
- To innovate

**Example:**
"Rather than just rejecting this proposal, how could we modify it to address the concerns?"

---

## Using the Framework

### Sequential Application

### Selective Use

### Iterative Process

Cycle through multiple times, each revealing deeper insights

### Dialogue Tool

Use in discussions to elevate conversation quality

### Self-Examination

Apply to own thinking to avoid bias

## Key Principles

**1. Question Everything**
No claim is above scrutiny

**2. Precision Matters**
Vague thinking leads to vague conclusions

**3. Evidence Required**
Assertions need support

**4. Consider Opposition**
Steel-man, don't straw-man

**5. Follow Implications**
Logic has consequences

**6. Significance Varies**
Not all truths matter equally

**7. Multiple Perspectives**
Truth is multi-faceted

**8. Stay Falsifiable**
Unfalsifiable beliefs are dogma

**9. Improve, Don't Just Critique**
Build better alternatives

**10. Stay Humble**
You could be wrong

## Red Flags These Questions Catch

**Question 1 catches:** Vagueness, ambiguity, equivocation
**Question 2 catches:** Unsupported assertions, appeals to authority
**Question 3 catches:** One-sided thinking, confirmation bias
**Question 4 catches:** Hidden premises, question-begging
**Question 5 catches:** Anecdotal reasoning, insufficient evidence
**Question 6 catches:** Inconsistency, reductio ad absurdum
**Question 7 catches:** Red herrings, irrelevance
**Question 8 catches:** False dichotomies, tunnel vision
**Question 9 catches:** Dogmatism, unfalsifiability
**Question 10 catches:** Purely negative criticism, defeatism

## Combining Questions

Powerful question combinations:

**Deep Understanding:**
1 → 4 → 2 (Clarify → Expose assumptions → Verify)

**Robust Evaluation:**
2 → 5 → 3 (Justification → Evidence → Opposition)

**Strategic Thinking:**
6 → 7 → 10 (Implications → Significance → Improvement)

**Epistemic Humility:**
2 → 9 → 8 (How know? → What would change mind? → Other views?)

## Mental Checklist

Before concluding anything:

✓ Have I defined my terms? (Q1)
✓ Have I verified my evidence? (Q2, Q5)
✓ Have I considered objections? (Q3)
✓ Have I checked my assumptions? (Q4)
✓ Have I traced implications? (Q6)
✓ Have I established relevance? (Q7)
✓ Have I considered alternatives? (Q8)
✓ Am I being dogmatic? (Q9)
✓ Can I improve this? (Q10)
