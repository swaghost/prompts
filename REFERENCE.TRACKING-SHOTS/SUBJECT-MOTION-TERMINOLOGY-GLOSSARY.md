# 🧾 **1. Full Glossary of Subject‑Motion Terminology**

A comprehensive, film‑school‑level vocabulary list for describing actors, objects, and environmental elements inside the frame.

---

## 🧍‍♂️ **A. Human Motion (Actors)**

### **Basic Movement**

- **walks**, **runs**, **jogs**, **rushes**, **charges**
- **stumbles**, **trips**, **collapses**, **falls**
- **approaches**, **retreats**, **sidesteps**, **circles**
- **enters**, **exits**, **crosses frame**, **passes foreground**

### **Micro‑Movement**

- **glances**, **looks over**, **peers**, **squints**
- **nods**, **shakes head**, **tilts head**
- **fidgets**, **taps**, **twitches**, **shifts weight**
- **inhales sharply**, **exhales slowly**, **holds breath**

### **Gesture & Expression**

- **reaches**, **grasps**, **clutches**, **releases**
- **points**, **waves**, **signals**
- **tightens jaw**, **softens gaze**, **furrows brow**
- **smiles faintly**, **smirks**, **grimaces**

### **Posture & Body Language**

- **stands tall**, **slumps**, **hunches**, **straightens**
- **leans in**, **leans back**, **recoils**
- **opens posture**, **closes off**, **turns away**

---

## 🎭 **B. Emotional Behavior (Performance Language)**

### **Internal State Made External**

- **hesitates**, **lingers**, **wavers**
- **commits**, **asserts**, **confronts**
- **withdraws**, **shrinks**, **avoids**
- **searches**, **calculates**, **plots**

### **Energy Level**

- **tense**, **relaxed**, **agitated**, **detached**
- **frantic**, **calm**, **deliberate**, **impulsive**

---

## 🎨 **C. Spatial & Blocking Terms**

### **Position in Frame**

- **foreground**, **midground**, **background**
- **center‑framed**, **edge‑weighted**, **profile**, **three‑quarter**

### **Movement Relative to Camera**

- **moves toward camera**, **backs away**
- **crosses left to right**, **crosses right to left**
- **holds still while camera moves**, **moves independently of camera**

### **Relational Blocking**

- **dominates frame**, **is dwarfed by environment**
- **isolated in negative space**, **crowded by objects**
- **framed against window**, **silhouetted in doorway**

# 🎭 **3. How Subject Motion Shapes Tone, Pacing, and Emotional Impact**

This is the deeper layer—the psychology of movement.

---

## ⚡ **A. Tone**

### **Slow, deliberate motion**

- Creates **intimacy**, **melancholy**, **reflection**
- Subjects feel **vulnerable**, **thoughtful**, **exposed**

### **Fast, erratic motion**

- Creates **chaos**, **urgency**, **fear**, **excitement**
- Subjects feel **unstable**, **energized**, **threatened**

### **Stillness**

- Creates **tension**, **power**, **anticipation**
- Stillness is often more dramatic than motion.

---

## ⏱️ **B. Pacing**

### **Frequent subject movement**

- Speeds up perceived pacing
- Makes scenes feel active even without camera motion

### **Minimal subject movement**

- Slows pacing
- Forces viewer attention onto micro‑expressions and environment

### **Movement synchronized with camera**

- Creates smooth, controlled pacing
- Feels intentional and choreographed

### **Movement independent of camera**

- Creates unpredictability
- Feels raw, documentary‑like, or chaotic

---

## ❤️ **C. Emotional Impact**

### **Approaching camera**

- Builds connection
- Feels intimate, confrontational, or vulnerable

### **Retreating from camera**

- Creates emotional distance
- Feels isolating, rejecting, or tragic

### **Crossing frame**

- Suggests transition, change, indecision
- Often used to show characters “moving through” emotional states

### **Sudden motion**

- Shocks viewer
- Signals danger, revelation, or emotional break

### **Lingering motion**

- Builds suspense
- Suggests contemplation or unresolved tension
