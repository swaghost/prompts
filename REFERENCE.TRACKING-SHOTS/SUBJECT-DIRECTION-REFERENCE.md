🎭 1. Physical Action Verbs (the core vocabulary)
These describe what the subject does in the frame. They’re simple, direct, and always literal.

walks, runs, rushes, stumbles, collapses

turns, glances, looks over, whips around

reaches, grabs, throws, drops

leans, slumps, straightens, paces

enters, exits, crosses frame, passes foreground

These verbs are used in screen direction, blocking notes, and shot lists.

🧍‍♂️ 2. Blocking Terms (where the subject is in space)
Blocking describes position, movement, and relationship to the camera.

Spatial Position
foreground, midground, background

center frame, off‑center, profile, three‑quarter, over‑the‑shoulder

Movement Through Space
moves toward camera, backs away, crosses left to right, crosses right to left

approaches, retreats, circles, drifts, sidesteps

Relational Position
dominates frame, is dwarfed by environment, is framed against window, is isolated in negative space

This is the language directors and cinematographers use when discussing staging.

🎭 3. Performance Language (emotional and psychological description)
This describes how the subject behaves, not just what they do.

hesitant, confident, nervous, detached, agitated

searching, calculating, lost, determined

holds tension, softens, breaks, withdraws, opens up

Often paired with physical verbs:

She hesitates, then steps forward.  
He retreats, shoulders tight, avoiding eye contact.

🎨 4. Compositional Language (how subjects interact with the frame)
This is the vocabulary of visual storytelling.

Framing
framed tightly, framed loosely, center‑punched, edge‑weighted

silhouetted, backlit, obscured, partially hidden

Scale
close, intimate, distant, overwhelmed, monumental

Focus
in sharp focus, soft focus, falls out of focus, emerges into focus

Presence
dominant, submissive, neutral, threatening, vulnerable

This is the language used in cinematography analysis and visual design.

🎬 5. Object Motion (props, vehicles, environmental elements)
Objects are described with the same verbs as actors, but with more mechanical specificity.

slides, rolls, swings, drops, clatters, shatters

drifts, floats, sways, ripples, billows

accelerates, brakes, veers, skids (vehicles)

Environmental motion is often described poetically:

wind pushes through the trees

smoke curls upward

light flickers across the wall

🎥 6. Interaction With Camera Motion (combined language)
Subjects and camera often move together, and the language reflects that relationship.

She walks into the push‑in

He crosses frame as the camera pans with him

The subject holds still while the camera orbits

The object drops out of frame as the camera tilts down

This is the “dance” between subject and camera.
