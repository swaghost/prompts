# Cinematic Motion Taxonomy (Camera + Subject + Environment)

A unified system describing how all three motion layers interact.

---

## A. Camera Motion Layer

- **Pan / Tilt** — rotational reveal
- **Dolly / Truck / Track** — physical movement
- **Push-In / Pull-Out** — emotional emphasis
- **Crane / Jib** — vertical scale
- **Orbit / Arc** — dynamic perspective
- **Zoom** — optical tension
- **Static** — intentional stillness

---

## B. Subject Motion Layer

### Independent Motion

Subject moves while camera stays static.  
_Creates realism, unpredictability, documentary feel._

### Synchronized Motion

Camera moves with subject.  
_Creates smoothness, choreography, control._

### Counter-Motion

Camera moves opposite subject.  
_Creates tension, conflict, emotional friction._

### Still Subject + Moving Camera

Subject holds still while camera moves.  
_Creates power, focus, psychological intensity._

---

## C. Environmental Motion Layer

- **Atmospheric** — smoke, fog, dust, mist
- **Natural** — wind, water, foliage
- **Mechanical** — vehicles, machinery, lights
- **Ambient** — reflections, shadows, flickers

---

## D. Combined Motion Archetypes

### 1. Pursuit Motion

- Camera tracks with subject
- Subject moves rapidly
- Environment blurs

**Tone:** urgency, danger, adrenaline

### 2. Isolation Motion

- Camera pulls out
- Subject remains still
- Environment expands

**Tone:** loneliness, vulnerability

### 3. Revelation Motion

- Camera pushes in
- Subject reacts subtly
- Environment remains static

**Tone:** discovery, realization, emotional shift

### 4. Chaos Motion

- Camera handheld or erratic
- Subjects move unpredictably
- Environment flickers or shifts

**Tone:** panic, instability, conflict

### 5. Power Motion

- Camera cranes up or pushes in
- Subject stands still or rises
- Environment frames subject

**Tone:** dominance, transformation, heroism
