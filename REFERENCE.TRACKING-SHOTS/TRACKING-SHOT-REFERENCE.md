1. Static Shot — Stillness, entrapment, focus
   A shot with zero movement, camera locked off. Used to trap characters, emphasize performance, or create painterly compositions. Stillness itself becomes a dramatic choice.

2. Pan — Horizontal reveal, connection, scanning
   Camera rotates left or right on a fixed axis.
   Used to reveal information, connect subjects, or follow action.
   Language: slow pan, whip pan, motivated pan, reveal pan.

3. Tilt — Vertical power dynamics, scale, discovery
   Camera rotates up or down from a fixed point.
   Tilting up conveys dominance or awe; tilting down conveys vulnerability or scrutiny.
   Language: tilt up, tilt down, vertical reveal.

4. Dolly — Physical movement through space, intimacy, isolation
   Camera moves forward, backward, or laterally on a track.
   A dolly-in creates intimacy or urgency; a dolly-out signals isolation or resolution.
   Language: dolly in, dolly out, tracking dolly, motivated dolly.

5. Tracking / Trucking — Following action, spatial orientation
   Camera moves sideways or alongside a subject.
   Used to follow movement or maintain spatial continuity.
   Language: tracking shot, trucking shot, lateral move.

6. Push-In / Pull-Out — Emphasis vs. detachment
   A push-in moves the camera toward the subject: “this matters.”
   A pull-out moves away, creating emotional distance or revealing context.
   Language: slow push, dramatic push, context pull, abandoning pull.

7. Zoom — Optical tension, unnatural focus shift
   Changes focal length without moving the camera.
   Feels unnatural because human eyes don’t zoom; often used in horror or documentary.
   Language: slow zoom, snap zoom, whip zoom, vertigo effect (dolly + zoom).

8. Crane / Boom / Jib — Vertical lift, grandeur, spatial mastery
   Camera rises or descends through space.
   Used for sweeping reveals, transitions, or establishing scale.
   Language: crane up, crane down, jib rise, hero lift.

9. Orbit / Arc Shot — Dynamic perspective shift, emotional circling
   Camera moves around a subject in a curved path.
   Used to create energy, disorientation, or relational tension.
   Language: orbit shot, arc move, 360 move.

10. Dutch Angle (with or without movement) — Psychological imbalance
    Camera is tilted on its roll axis, sometimes combined with motion.
    Language: dutch tilt, canted angle, disorientation cue.
